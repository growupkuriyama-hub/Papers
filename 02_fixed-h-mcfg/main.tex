\begin{abstract}
Fix a surjective finite-semigroup morphism $s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$
and a fan-out bound $f$.  We study positive-data learning of multiple
context-free languages under componentwise $s$-typed substitutability.
A canonical fresh-identity completion $\widehat s:\Sigma^*\to\mathsf S^\sharp$
evaluates grammar yields, with empty words treated separately. We specialize Yoshinaka's signature-based positive-data learner
to fixed finite-semigroup typing, make the set-driven reconstruction
explicit, and analyze characteristic samples beyond their cardinality.
At bounded rule rank the hypothesis updates are polynomial-time.

Our main quantitative measure is the total length of characteristic positive
data relative to the size $\|G\|$ and original rule thickness $\theta_G$ of
a reduced good MCFG presentation. At fixed fan-out and rank, boundary-window
typings admit $O(\|G\|^2\bar\theta_G)$ characteristic data; combining a
window with any fixed finite group yields $O(\|G\|^3\bar\theta_G)$, including
noncommutative groups. We refine the abelian constants via zero-sum theory
and extend the cubic bound to typings with positive-image semigroup in
the pseudovariety $\mathbf{LG}$ of local groups. For rank-one rules,
every fixed finite typing has a grammar-size polynomial bound.
A distinct language-based condition gives polynomial data for
abelian-filtered targets with bounded-rank good presentations, even
when the positive-image semigroup lies outside $\mathbf{LG}$.

We provide a computable rule-only witness budget and a separate
distributional-representative upper bound. At the pseudovariety level,
the local-group upper boundary has lower-bound obstructions at fan-out one.
We also give a boundary-typed non-context-free example
with intrinsic rule rank two. The general higher-fan-out
superpolynomial data lower-bound problem remains open.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} grammatical inference; multiple context-free grammars; positive data; finite-semigroup typing; multidimensional substitutability; Gold identification; rule rank.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Positive-data grammatical inference asks when a language can be recovered from
an enumeration of its positive examples alone \cite{Gold1967,Angluin1980}.
Distributional approaches make this possible for restricted grammar classes by
turning observed interchangeability of substrings or tuples into grammar
nonterminals.  For context-free grammars (CFGs) this includes substitutable
languages \cite{ClarkEyraud2007} and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$ hierarchy
\cite{Yoshinaka2008}.  Yoshinaka extended the idea to multiple context-free
grammars (MCFGs), whose nonterminals generate tuples and whose substitutions
are tested by multi-hole sentence contexts \cite{Yoshinaka2009,Yoshinaka2011}.
MCFGs are a standard mildly context-sensitive formalism for discontinuous and
cross-serial dependencies \cite{SekiEtAl1991,Kallmeyer2010}.

This paper fixes, as part of the learning bias, a surjective finite-semigroup
homomorphism $s:\Sigma^+\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf S$ and compares observed
\emph{nonempty} tuple components only when their $s$-values agree.  The
canonical fresh-identity completion $\widehat s:\Sigma^*\to\mathsf S^\sharp$ is
used only to evaluate grammar templates, including empty terminal gaps.  The
typing and the fan-out bound are fixed before any data are seen, separating the
choice of a finite bias from learning under that bias~\cite{CosteTyping2004};
prefix--suffix windows and DFA transition morphisms are typical instances.
Other biases restrict derivations by regular control sets
\cite{Takada1988,Takada1995} or restrict the evidence used to compare factors
\cite{CosteGaretNicolas2012,CosteNicolas2019}; positive-data learning of other
mildly context-sensitive formalisms is studied in \cite{OatesEtAl2006}, and
MCFG learning with membership queries or dual context sets in
\cite{Yoshinaka2010,ClarkYoshinaka2014,KanazawaYoshinaka2023}.  A recent
substitution lemma for MCFLs \cite{DuncanEtAl2026Substitution} is a necessary
condition for membership, not a learning criterion.

\paragraph{Prior work and what is not claimed.}
The qualitative learning mechanism is not new.  The reconstruction operator of
Section~\ref{sec:learner} is Yoshinaka's observed-tuple grammar
\cite[Section~4.1]{Yoshinaka2011} with two changes: substitution links are
guarded by equality of componentwise types, and $\eps$ is admitted by a separate
start rule.  Its soundness and completeness
proofs are typed transcriptions of \cite[Lemmas~3--5]{Yoshinaka2011}, and the
unbounded-rank variant is the typed form of \cite[Corollary~2]{Yoshinaka2011}.
At bounded rule rank the typed class is, moreover, the substitutable class of a
finite sort refinement in Yoshinaka's signature framework
(Proposition~\ref{prop:yoshinaka2015-embedding}), so identifiability, polynomial
updates and characteristic sets of bounded \emph{cardinality} follow from
\cite[Theorem~1 and Lemma~3]{Yoshinaka2015General}; that theorem is proved there
for the context-indexed construction $\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm D}$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:substp} relates our operator to the substructure-indexed
$\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}$.  Polynomial characteristic data for fixed
prefix--suffix typing of CFGs is due to Yoshinaka~\cite{Yoshinaka2008}; the
companion CFG paper~\cite{kuriyamaCFG} (arXiv v5, with a frozen TCS R1 source)
treats fan-out one, including a window--group bound and an operator-independent
semilattice lower bound.  The non-branching lower bound used in
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} is due to Gallot~\cite{Gallot2021}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our object is the \emph{total encoded length} of characteristic positive data
relative to an MCFG presentation, for typed \emph{tuples} at higher rule rank,
where several component boundaries must be preserved simultaneously.  For a
reduced good MCFG $G$ put $\bar\theta_G=\max\{1,\theta_G\}$, where $\theta_G$ is
rule thickness (Section~\ref{sec:char-data}).  With alphabet, fan-out,
rule-rank bound and typing fixed:

The group result includes noncommutative groups; the abelian case has sharper
constants (Theorem~\ref{thm:window-abelian-polydata}).  The $\mathbf{LG}$
hypothesis concerns the positive-image semigroup; its known algebraic
characterization~\cite{AlmeidaEtAl2009} is distinct from the learning bound,
and the matching pseudovariety-level obstruction is established only at fan-out
one (Corollary~\ref{cor:pseudovariety-dichotomy}).  We also give a computable
rule-only data budget (Proposition~\ref{prop:computed-witness-budget}), a
language-based bound for abelian-filtered targets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mcfg-abelian-filter-data}), and a boundary-typed
non-context-free target of intrinsic fan-out and rule rank two
(Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-ranktwo-witness}).  Characteristic data concern
language-level exactness of a set-driven operator; syntactic stabilization is
supplied by a separate conservative learner.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $\Sigma$ is a finite alphabet, $\eps$ the empty word, and
$\Sigma^+:=\Sigma^*\setminus\{\eps\}$.  If $\Sigma=\emptyset$, the only
languages are $\emptyset$ and $\{\eps\}$ and are handled directly.

\subsection{Positive-Data Learning}

\begin{definition}[Text, TxtEx identification, conservativeness]
Fix a pause symbol $\pausesym\notin\Sigma$.  A \emph{text} for
$L\subseteq\Sigma^*$ is an infinite sequence over $L\cup\{\pausesym\}$ in which
every element of $L$ occurs; thus $\pausesym,\pausesym,\ldots$ is the text for
$\emptyset$.  The \emph{content} of a finite prefix is the set of elements of
$\Sigma^*$ occurring in it.  A \emph{learner} is a computable map from finite
prefixes to grammars.  It \emph{TxtEx-identifies} $L$~\cite{Gold1967} if on
every text for $L$ its outputs are, from some stage on, one fixed grammar $H$
with $L(H)=L$, and it TxtEx-identifies a class if it does so for each member.
A learner is \emph{conservative} if it changes its hypothesis only on a datum
$w\in\Sigma^*$ not generated by the current hypothesis.  We fix a hypothesis
$H_0$ with $L(H_0)=\emptyset$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Set-driven operator and characteristic sample]
\label{def:characteristic-sample}
A \emph{set-driven reconstruction operator} $\mathcal B$ maps each finite
$K\subseteq\Sigma^*$ to a grammar $\mathcal B(K)$ depending only on the set
$K$; it is \emph{sample-consistent} if $K\subseteq L(\mathcal B(K))$.  For a
language $L$, a finite $K^\star\subseteq L$ is a \emph{characteristic sample
for $\mathcal B$} if $K^\star\subseteq K\subseteq L$ implies
$L(\mathcal B(K))=L$ for every finite $K$ (cf.~\cite{deLaHiguera1997}).  Unlike
TxtEx identification this is a statement about generated languages: $\mathcal
B(K)$ need not stabilize syntactically as $K$ grows.
\end{definition}

For finite $K\subseteq\Sigma^*$ put $\|K\|_+:=\sum_{w\in K}(|w|+1)$, and for a
tuple $\tuple u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)$ put $\|\tuple u\|_1:=\sum_i|u_i|$.  Every
polynomial bound below states whether it concerns cardinality or $\|\cdot\|_+$.

\subsection{Fixed Finite-Semigroup Typing}
\label{subsec:semigroup-typing}

Following the companion CFG paper~\cite{kuriyamaCFG}, the learning bias is a
surjective semigroup morphism $s:\Sigma^+\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf S$ onto a
finite semigroup, given by its multiplication table and the letter images (so
$s(w)$ is computable in linear time).  A finite codomain can always be replaced
by the positive image, so surjectivity loses no generality.

\begin{definition}[Fresh completion, tuple types, refinement]
\label{def-monoid-morphism}
\label{def:semigroup-typing}
\label{def:h-refinement}
Let $\mathsf S^\sharp=\mathsf S\uplus\{\mathbf 1_\sharp\}$ be obtained by
adjoining a \emph{new} identity (even if $\mathsf S$ has one), and let
$h=\widehat s:\Sigma^*\to M:=\mathsf S^\sharp$ be the monoid morphism with
$\widehat s(\eps)=\mathbf 1_\sharp$ and $\widehat s|_{\Sigma^+}=s$.  For
$\tuple w=(w_1,\ldots,w_d)$ put
$h^{(d)}(\tuple w)=(h(w_1),\ldots,h(w_d))$; on $(\Sigma^+)^d$ this is the
semigroup tuple type $s^{(d)}$.  The value $\mathbf 1_\sharp$ only evaluates
empty template factors; it is not a type of the bias.  For typings $s,t$ on
$\Sigma^+$ write $s\preceq t$ if $\ker t\subseteq\ker s$ on $\Sigma^+$
(equivalently, $s$ factors through the positive image of $t$), and let $(s,t)$
denote $w\mapsto(s(w),t(w))$ restricted to its positive image.
\end{definition}

\emph{Monoid implementations.}  A finite-monoid morphism $h:\Sigma^*\to M$ is
used as a presentation of the typing $h|_{\Sigma^+}$ onto its positive image;
accordingly $h_0\preceq h_1$ means $h_0|_{\Sigma^+}\preceq h_1|_{\Sigma^+}$,
products of implementations are formed componentwise, and
$\Cmcf{f}{h}$, $\mathcal B_{f,h}$, $\mathcal A_{f,h}$ mean
$\Cmcf{f}{h|_{\Sigma^+}}$, $\mathcal B_{f,\widehat s}$,
$\mathcal A_{f,\widehat s}$ with $s=h|_{\Sigma^+}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:semigroup-transfer} shows that nothing below depends on the
choice of implementation.  Bounds in terms of $|M|$ refer to
$M=\mathsf S^\sharp$, so $|M|=|\mathsf S|+1$; algebraic conditions (such as
$\mathbf{LG}$) always refer to the positive image $\mathsf S$.  The trivial
typing $s_{\mathrm{triv}}$ has one positive type and is implemented by
$h_{\mathrm{triv}}:\Sigma^*\to\{1\}$.

\begin{definition}[Prefix--suffix typing]
\label{def:prefix-suffix-typing}
For $k,\ell\ge0$ put $m_0:=\max\{1,k+\ell\}$, and for $|w|\ge m_0$ let
$\operatorname{pref}_k(w)$, $\operatorname{suf}_\ell(w)$ be the prefix and
suffix of the indicated lengths (length-zero windows are $\eps$).  Define
\[
 h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(w)=
 \begin{cases}
   w, & |w|<m_0,\\
   \langle\operatorname{pref}_k(w),\operatorname{suf}_\ell(w)\rangle,
      & |w|\ge m_0,
 \end{cases}
\]
with disjoint tags for short words and pairs, and put
$M_{k,\ell}:=h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(\Sigma^*)$.  For short $x,y$ and pairs
$\langle u,v\rangle,\langle u',v'\rangle$ define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x\cdot y&:=h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(xy), &
 x\cdot\langle u,v\rangle&:=\langle\operatorname{pref}_k(xu),v\rangle,\\
 \langle u,v\rangle\cdot y&:=\langle u,\operatorname{suf}_\ell(vy)\rangle, &
 \langle u,v\rangle\cdot\langle u',v'\rangle&:=\langle u,v'\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

A case check gives $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(xy)=h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(x)\cdot
h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(y)$; since $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ is onto $M_{k,\ell}$,
associativity is inherited from concatenation and $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}(\eps)$ is
the identity.  Thus $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ is a finite-monoid morphism; its
positive restriction $s_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ is the boundary typing, and since the
short-word tag separates $\eps$ from $\Sigma^+$, $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ is
isomorphic to $\widehat{s_\Sigma^{k,\ell}}$.  A typing $h$ with
$h\preceq h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ is called \emph{$(k,\ell)$-boundary-determined},
or briefly a \emph{boundary typing}.

\subsection{Multiple Context-Free Grammars}

\begin{definition}[Standard MCFG presentation]
\label{def:standard-mcfg}
An MCFG \cite{SekiEtAl1991,Kallmeyer2010} is
$G=(V,\Sigma,P,S,\fan)$ with finite $V$, $S\in V$, $\fan(S)=1$ and
$\fan(A)\ge1$.  A rule of rank $r\ge0$ is
$A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_r)$ with template
$\rho=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d_0})$, $d_0=\fan(A)$, $d_j=\fan(B_j)$, where
each $\alpha_i$ is a word over
$\Gamma_\rho:=\Sigma\uplus\Xi_1\uplus\cdots\uplus\Xi_r$,
$\Xi_j:=\{\xi_j^1,\ldots,\xi_j^{d_j}\}$, and every variable occurs at most once
in $\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_{d_0}$ (\emph{linearity}); the rule is
\emph{nondeleting} if every variable occurs exactly once (derived components
may still be empty).  For $\tuple u_j\in(\Sigma^*)^{d_j}$,
$\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r)$ substitutes $u_{j,\kappa}$ for
$\xi_j^\kappa$; for $r=0$ the rule is a constant rule.  The tuple languages
$L_A(G)$ form the least family closed under the rules, and
$L(G):=\{w:(w)\in L_S(G)\}$.  We write $A\Rightarrow_G^*\tuple u$ for a
\emph{terminal derivation} (a finite derivation tree rooted at $A$ evaluated
bottom-up); one from $S$ is \emph{successful}.  $G$ is an $f$-MCFG if
$\fan(A)\le f$ for all $A$, and has rule-rank cap $r_{\max}$ if all ranks are
at most $r_{\max}$.
\end{definition}

We write $\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$ for the languages of MCFGs of fan-out $\le f$
and rule rank $\le r$, so that
$f\text{-}\mathrm{MCFL}=\bigcup_{r<\infty}\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$.  Minimum rule
rank of a language refers to all presentations, not to a given grammar.
$\|G\|$ is the length of a fixed symbol-level encoding counting nonterminal and
rule records and every terminal or variable occurrence in templates.  For
nonterminals of equal fan-out $d$, $A\to B$ abbreviates the identity-template
rule $A\to(\xi_1^1,\ldots,\xi_1^d)(B)$, and $A\to\tuple c$ a constant rule.
Rule variables are always $\xi_j^\kappa$; terminals in examples are in
\texttt{typewriter} font.

A nondeleting rule is \emph{nonpermuting} if, for each $j$,
$\xi_j^1,\ldots,\xi_j^{d_j}$ occur in this order in $\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_{d_0}$;
following \cite[Section~2.3]{Yoshinaka2011} it is \emph{$\lambda$-free} if no
$\alpha_i$ is empty, and a nonpermuting rule is \emph{nonmerging} if no
$\alpha_i$ contains a factor $\xi_j^\kappa\xi_j^{\kappa+1}$.  A rule, template
or presentation is \emph{good} if it is $\lambda$-free, nondeleting,
nonpermuting and nonmerging.  By induction on derivations, every component of
every tuple derived from a nonterminal of a $\lambda$-free grammar is
nonempty.  A nonterminal is \emph{reachable} if it is reachable from $S$ in the
graph with an edge $A\to B$ whenever $B$ is a child in a rule for $A$, and
\emph{productive} if $L_A(G)\ne\emptyset$; $G$ is \emph{reduced} if all
nonterminals are both.

\begin{proposition}[Good normalization with rank preservation]
\label{prop:good-normalization}
Let $G_{\mathrm{in}}$ be a finite $f$-MCFG of rule rank at most $r_{\max}$.
There is a finite good $f$-MCFG $G_{\mathrm{good}}$ of rule rank at most
$r_{\max}$ with $L(G_{\mathrm{good}})=L(G_{\mathrm{in}})\setminus\{\eps\}$ and,
for fixed $f,r_{\max}$, $\|G_{\mathrm{good}}\|=O_{f,r_{\max}}(\|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Trim; apply the nondeleting construction (f3) of
\cite[proof of Lemma~2.2]{SekiEtAl1991} by retaining subsets of output
components; decorate nonterminals by component permutations; decorate them by
realizable nonempty-component patterns and project away empty components (this
removes $\eps$ and makes all templates $\lambda$-free); finally apply
Yoshinaka's nonmerging conversion \cite[Section~2.3, Lemma~1]{Yoshinaka2011}.
No step increases fan-out or rule rank, and with $f,r_{\max}$ fixed each step
creates $O_{f,r_{\max}}(1)$ copies of each nonterminal and rule (at most $2^f$,
$f!$ and $2^{fr_{\max}}$ decorations).  The derivation correspondences and the
size bookkeeping are given in Appendix~\ref{app:normalization-details}; cf.\
also \cite[Section~2.1]{Kanazawa2019Ogden} and
\cite[Section~2.3, after Example~2]{Yoshinaka2011}.
\end{proof}

The proposition controls size, fan-out and rank, not thickness; thickness
transfer for reduced $\lambda$-free inputs is
Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-good-thickness}.  Fan-out and rule rank are
distinct resources: there is no rank reduction preserving an arbitrary fixed
fan-out \cite{RambowSatta1999,GomezRodriguezEtAl2009}.  The reconstruction
below therefore imposes neither a binary normal form nor a known rank cap.

\subsection{Tuple Contexts and Substitutability}

A \emph{sentence context of arity $d$} is a word
$E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots v_{d-1}\blank_dv_d$ with each hole occurring once,
$v_0,v_d\in\Sigma^*$ and $v_1,\ldots,v_{d-1}\in\Sigma^+$; such contexts are
\emph{admissible}, and $\mathcal E_d$ denotes their set
(\cite[Section~3.1]{Yoshinaka2011}).  $E[\tuple w]$ fills $\blank_i$ by $w_i$,
$|E|_{\mathrm{fix}}:=\sum_{i=0}^d|v_i|$, and $E$ \emph{accepts} $\tuple w$ in
$L$ if $E[\tuple w]\in L$.

\begin{definition}[Tuple occurrence]
\label{def:tuple-occurrence}
For $w=a_1\cdots a_n$ number the cut positions
$\cutpos{0}a_1\cutpos{1}\cdots a_n\cutpos{n}$ and write
$w[i:i']:=a_{i+1}\cdots a_{i'}$.  An \emph{arity-$d$ tuple occurrence} in $w$
is a sequence $0\le i_1<i_1'<i_2<i_2'<\cdots<i_d<i_d'\le n$; it determines
$\tuple x=(w[i_1:i_1'],\ldots,w[i_d:i_d'])\in(\Sigma^+)^d$ and the context
$E\in\mathcal E_d$ obtained by replacing these intervals by
$\blank_1,\ldots,\blank_d$, so that $E[\tuple x]=w$.  For a finite $K$,
$\mathrm{Obs}_d(K)$ is the set of tuples $\tuple x$ arising from occurrences in
words of $K$, i.e.\ $\tuple x\in(\Sigma^+)^d$ with $E[\tuple x]\in K$ for some
$E\in\mathcal E_d$, and $\mathrm{Obs}(K):=\bigcup_{d\le f}\mathrm{Obs}_d(K)$.
\end{definition}

For $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$ and $\tuple x\in(\Sigma^*)^d$ the
\emph{$d$-tuple distribution} is
$\D_L^{(d)}(\tuple x):=\{E\in\mathcal E_d:E[\tuple x]\in L\}$.

\begin{definition}[$(f,s)$-tuple substitutability]
\label{def:tuple-substitutable}
Let $f\ge1$ and $s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$ a finite-semigroup morphism.  A language
$L\subseteq\Sigma^*$ is \emph{$(f,s)$-tuple-substitutable} if for every
$1\le d\le f$ and all $\tuple x,\tuple y\in(\Sigma^+)^d$,
\[
 s^{(d)}(\tuple x)=s^{(d)}(\tuple y)\ \text{ and }\
 \D_L^{(d)}(\tuple x)\cap\D_L^{(d)}(\tuple y)\ne\emptyset
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \D_L^{(d)}(\tuple x)=\D_L^{(d)}(\tuple y).
\]
\end{definition}

Empty components are never compared (Yoshinaka's convention
\cite[Section~3.1 and footnote~3]{Yoshinaka2011}; see \cite{Clark2013} for
CFGs); $\eps\in L$ is handled at the start symbol.  The restriction matters
already at fan-out one: $\mathtt a$ and $\eps$ share a context in
$\mathtt a^+$ but have different distributions.  At fan-out one,
$\D_L^{(1)}(x)$ is the two-sided distribution $\{(u,v):uxv\in L\}$.  For
$\tuple x,\tuple y\in(\Sigma^+)^d$ write $\tuple x\equiv_{L,h}^d\tuple y$
(or $\tuple x\equiv_{L,h}\tuple y$) if $h^{(d)}(\tuple x)=h^{(d)}(\tuple y)$
and $\D_L^{(d)}(\tuple x)=\D_L^{(d)}(\tuple y)$; this is an equivalence
relation.

\begin{definition}[The target class]
\label{def:class-cmcf}
$\Cmcf{f}{s}$ is the class of $(f,s)$-tuple-substitutable $f$-MCFLs.  A target
in $\Cmcf{f}{h}$ for a boundary typing $h$ is called \emph{boundary-typed}.
\end{definition}

If $h\preceq h'$ then $\Cmcf{f}{h}\subseteq\Cmcf{f}{h'}$, since equal finer
types are equal coarser types; in particular
$\Cmcf{f}{h_{\mathrm{triv}}}\subseteq\Cmcf{f}{h}$.  The class is not monotone
in $f$: only $\Cmcf{f+1}{h}\cap f\text{-}\mathrm{MCFL}\subseteq\Cmcf{f}{h}$
holds, and already for trivial typing
$\{w\mathtt{\#}w^R\}$ is $1$- but not $2$-dimensionally substitutable
\cite[Section~3.1, Example~5]{Yoshinaka2011}; Yoshinaka notes that
$\mathsf{SL}(p,r)$ and $\mathsf{SL}(p+1,r)$ are incomparable for $r\ge1$
\cite[Section~4.1]{Yoshinaka2011}.  Thus $f$ is part of the bias.  For every
$\mathsf{Acc}\subseteq M$, $h^{-1}(\mathsf{Acc})\in\Cmcf{f}{h}$: it is regular,
and equal componentwise types give equal total types in every context.

\begin{proposition}[Intersection and abelian filtering]
\label{prop:abelian-coset-filter}
Fix $f$ and $h$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item If $L_1,L_2$ are $(f,h)$-tuple-substitutable, so is $L_1\cap L_2$.
\item If $L$ is $(f,h)$-tuple-substitutable, $\mathbb A$ is an abelian group,
$\varphi:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb A$ a monoid morphism, $\mathcal H\le\mathbb A$ and
$\gamma\in\mathbb A$, then $L\cap\varphi^{-1}(\gamma+\mathcal H)$ is
$(f,h)$-tuple-substitutable.
\end{enumerate}
Whenever the intersection is an $f$-MCFL, it belongs to $\Cmcf{f}{h}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) A context accepting equal-type $\tuple x,\tuple y$ in $L_1\cap L_2$ accepts
both in each $L_i$, so their distributions agree in each $L_i$, hence in the
intersection.  (ii) If a context accepts $\tuple x,\tuple y$ in
$\varphi^{-1}(\gamma+\mathcal H)$, then
$\delta:=\sum_i(\varphi(y_i)-\varphi(x_i))\in\mathcal H$; by commutativity
$\varphi(E'[\tuple y])-\varphi(E'[\tuple x])=\delta$ for every $E'$, so coset
membership transfers in both directions.  Thus
$\varphi^{-1}(\gamma+\mathcal H)$ is $(f,h_{\mathrm{triv}})$-, hence
$(f,h)$-tuple-substitutable; apply (i).
\end{proof}

Consequently every $f$-MCFL of the form
$h^{-1}(\mathsf{Acc})\cap\varphi^{-1}(\gamma+\mathcal H)$ lies in
$\Cmcf{f}{h}$; such factorizations are not supplied to the learner.

All interaction between templates and contexts below rests on one observation.

\begin{lemma}[Induced child contexts]
\label{lem:filling-identity}
Let $\rho=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_e)$ be a good template of rank $r$ with child
arities $d_1,\ldots,d_r$, let $E\in\mathcal E_e$, let $1\le j\le r$, and fix
$\tuple u_i\in(\Sigma^+)^{d_i}$ for $i\ne j$.  Let $E_j$ be the word obtained
from $E[(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_e)]$ by replacing $\xi_j^\kappa$ by
$\blank_\kappa$ and every other variable $\xi_i^\iota$ by $u_{i,\iota}$.  Then
$E_j\in\mathcal E_{d_j}$ and
$E_j[\tuple x]=E[\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_{j-1},\tuple x,
\tuple u_{j+1},\ldots,\tuple u_r)]$ for every
$\tuple x\in(\Sigma^*)^{d_j}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity holds because substitutions commute.  By linearity and
nondeletion each $\blank_\kappa$ occurs exactly once in $E_j$, and by
nonpermutation in the order $\kappa=1,\ldots,d_j$, since $E$ places
$\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_e$ from left to right.  Consider $\xi_j^\kappa$ and
$\xi_j^{\kappa+1}$.  If both lie in one $\alpha_i$, nonmerging puts a terminal
or a variable $\xi_i^\iota$ with $i\ne j$ between them, which becomes a
nonempty word.  Otherwise they lie in $\alpha_i,\alpha_{i'}$ with $i<i'$, and
the material between them contains the separator $v_i\in\Sigma^+$ of $E$.  So
every internal separator of $E_j$ is nonempty.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Reconstruction from Positive Data}
\label{sec:learner}

The reconstruction operator is Yoshinaka's observed-tuple grammar
\cite[Section~4.1]{Yoshinaka2011} with type-guarded substitution links; its
soundness and completeness proofs (Sections~\ref{sec:soundness}
and~\ref{sec:completeness}) transcribe \cite[Lemmas~3--5]{Yoshinaka2011}.
They are given in full because they also cover unbounded rank and because the
explicit witness set of Definition~\ref{def:witness-set} is what
Section~\ref{sec:char-data} measures.  Section~\ref{sec:general-framework}
places the bounded-rank case in Yoshinaka's signature framework.

\subsection{Set-Driven Reconstruction}

\begin{definition}[Composition witness]
\label{def:composition-witness}
Let $K$ be finite and $r\ge1$.  A \emph{composition witness of rank $r$} in $K$
is a triple $(\tuple z,\rho,(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r))$ with
$\tuple z\in\mathrm{Obs}(K)$, $\rho$ a good template of rank $r$ whose output
and child arities are at most $f$, $\tuple u_j\in(\Sigma^+)^{d_j}$, and
$\tuple z=\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r)$.
\end{definition}

Since each $\alpha_i(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r)=z_i$ fixes the positions of
all child components inside $\tuple z$, a composition witness is the same as a
segmentation of an observed occurrence of $\tuple z$ into terminal gaps and
labelled child intervals.  If $E[\tuple z]\in K$ with $E\in\mathcal E_e$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:filling-identity} shows that every $\tuple u_j\in\mathrm{Obs}(K)$.
Linearity and nondeletion make the children contribute disjointly to
$\tuple z$, and each contributes at least one letter, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sample-rank}
 r\le\textstyle\sum_j\|\tuple u_j\|_1\le\|\tuple z\|_1\le\max_{w\in K}|w|
 \le\|K\|_+ .
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Capped and uncapped reconstruction operators]
\label{def:reconstruction}
Fix $f$ and $h=\widehat s$.  For $r_{\max}\ge1$ let
$\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}(K)=\widehat G_{\le r_{\max}}(K)$ be the
grammar with start symbol $\widehat S$, a nonterminal $[\tuple x]$ of arity
$d$ for each $\tuple x\in\mathrm{Obs}_d(K)$, $d\le f$, and the rules
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item[\textnormal{(Start)}] $\widehat S\to[(w)]$ for $w\in K\cap\Sigma^+$, and
      $\widehat S\to\eps$ if $\eps\in K$;
\item[\textnormal{(Const)}] $[\tuple x]\to\tuple x$ for every observed
      $\tuple x$;
\item[\textnormal{(Comp)}] $[\tuple z]\to\rho([\tuple u_1],\ldots,[\tuple u_r])$
      for every composition witness of rank $1\le r\le r_{\max}$;
\item[\textnormal{(Link)}] $[\tuple x]\to[\tuple y]$ for observed
      $\tuple x,\tuple y$ of the same arity $d$ with
      $h^{(d)}(\tuple x)=h^{(d)}(\tuple y)$ and $E[\tuple x],E[\tuple y]\in K$
      for some $E\in\mathcal E_d$.
\end{enumerate}
For $K\ne\emptyset$ put $\mathcal B_{f,h}(K):=\widehat G(K):=
\widehat G_{\le\|K\|_+}(K)$; $\mathcal B_{f,h}(\emptyset)$ has no rules.
Fixed encoding orders make both operators deterministic and set-driven.
\end{definition}

By \eqref{eq:sample-rank}, $\mathcal B_{f,h}(K)$ contains a Comp rule for every
composition witness in $K$.  Both operators are sample-consistent, by
$\widehat S\to[(w)]\to(w)$ and $\widehat S\to\eps$.  Every $[\tuple x]$
derives only tuples with nonempty components, since Comp templates are
$\lambda$-free; so $\widehat S\Rightarrow^*\eps$ only by the rule
$\widehat S\to\eps$.  For trivial typing and $\eps\notin K$,
Comp/Const, Link and Start are exactly the rules of Types~I, II and III of
\cite[Section~4.1]{Yoshinaka2011}, and $\mathcal B_{f,h}$ is the operator of
Yoshinaka's unbounded-rank learner $\mathcal A(p,*)$
\cite[Corollary~2]{Yoshinaka2011}.

\subsection{Soundness}
\label{sec:soundness}

\begin{lemma}[Composition preserves equivalence]
\label{lem:witnessed-composition}
Let $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$, let $\rho$ be a good template of rank $r$, and let
$\tuple x_j,\tuple u_j\in(\Sigma^+)^{d_j}$ satisfy
$\tuple u_j\equiv_{L,h}\tuple x_j$ for every $j$.  Then
$\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r)\equiv_{L,h}\rho(\tuple x_1,\ldots,\tuple x_r)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each output component is the concatenation of the terminals of $\rho$ and of
child components in a fixed order, and is nonempty by $\lambda$-freeness;
since $h$ is a monoid morphism, the parent types agree.  Fix
$E\in\mathcal E_e$ and put
$\tuple p_j:=\rho(\tuple x_1,\ldots,\tuple x_j,\tuple u_{j+1},\ldots,\tuple u_r)$
for $0\le j\le r$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:filling-identity} with siblings
$\tuple x_1,\ldots,\tuple x_{j-1},\tuple u_{j+1},\ldots,\tuple u_r$ gives
$E_j\in\mathcal E_{d_j}$ with $E[\tuple p_{j-1}]=E_j[\tuple u_j]$ and
$E[\tuple p_j]=E_j[\tuple x_j]$; as
$\D_L(\tuple u_j)=\D_L(\tuple x_j)$, $E[\tuple p_{j-1}]\in L$ iff
$E[\tuple p_j]\in L$.  Chaining over $j$ gives
$\D_L(\tuple p_0)=\D_L(\tuple p_r)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of every hypothesis]
\label{prop:reconstruction-soundness}
Let $L$ be $(f,s)$-tuple-substitutable and $K\subseteq L$ finite.  For every
$r_{\max}\ge1$, if $[\tuple x]\Rightarrow^*\tuple u$ in
$\widehat G_{\le r_{\max}}(K)$, then $\tuple u\equiv_{L,h}\tuple x$.
Consequently $L(\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}(K))\subseteq L$ and
$L(\mathcal B_{f,h}(K))\subseteq L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Lemmas~3--4]{Yoshinaka2011} with typed links.  Induct on the
derivation, according to its first rule.  Const: $\tuple u=\tuple x$.  Link
$[\tuple x]\to[\tuple y]$: $\tuple x,\tuple y$ have equal types and share a
context in $K\subseteq L$, so substitutability gives
$\tuple x\equiv_{L,h}\tuple y$, and $\tuple u\equiv_{L,h}\tuple y$ by
induction.  Comp $[\tuple z]\to\rho([\tuple u_1],\ldots,[\tuple u_r])$ with
$[\tuple u_j]\Rightarrow^*\tuple v_j$: by induction
$\tuple v_j\equiv_{L,h}\tuple u_j$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:witnessed-composition} gives
$\rho(\tuple v_1,\ldots,\tuple v_r)\equiv_{L,h}\tuple z$.  A nonempty word $w$
is derived from $\widehat S$ via some $[(w_0)]$ with $w_0\in K$; then
$(w)\equiv_{L,h}(w_0)$ and $\blank_1\in\D_L((w_0))$, so $w\in L$; and $\eps$
is derived only if $\eps\in K\subseteq L$.  The uncapped operator is the capped
one with $r_{\max}=\|K\|_+$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Yield-Typed Refinement and Witnesses}
\label{sec:refinement}

\begin{definition}[Yield-type refinement]
\label{def:output-refinement}
Let $G=(V,\Sigma,P,S,\fan)$ be a finite nondeleting MCFG and $h:\Sigma^*\to M$
a finite-monoid morphism.  For a template $\rho$ and
$\mathbf t_j\in M^{d_j}$, let $\out_\rho(\mathbf t_1,\ldots,\mathbf t_r)\in
M^{d_0}$ have $i$th component the image of $\alpha_i$ under the monoid
morphism $\Gamma_\rho^*\to M$ extending $a\mapsto h(a)$ and
$\xi_j^\kappa\mapsto(\mathbf t_j)_\kappa$.  The \emph{full yield-type
refinement} $G^h$ has a fresh start symbol $\widetilde S$, nonterminals
$A_{\mathbf t}$ for $A\in V$ and $\mathbf t\in M^{\fan(A)}$, start rules
$\widetilde S\to S_{(\eta)}$ for $\eta\in M$, and for every rule
$A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_r)$ (including $r=0$) and all
$\mathbf t_j\in M^{\fan(B_j)}$ the rule
$A_{\out_\rho(\mathbf t_1,\ldots,\mathbf t_r)}\to
\rho(B_{1,\mathbf t_1},\ldots,B_{r,\mathbf t_r})$.  The \emph{trimmed
refinement} $\widetilde G_0$ keeps exactly the typed nonterminals and rules
occurring in some successful derivation from $\widetilde S$.  Unless $G^h$ is
named, \emph{typed nonterminal} and \emph{typed rule} refer to the
non-start nonterminals and rules of $\widetilde G_0$, and $L_X:=\{\tuple u:X\Rightarrow_{\widetilde
G_0}^*\tuple u\}$.
\end{definition}

Typed rules have the templates of their sources, so $\widetilde G_0$ is good
whenever $G$ is; each $L_X$ is nonempty, with nonempty components if $G$ is
$\lambda$-free.

\begin{proposition}[Yield-type invariants]
\label{prop:output-invariants}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item If $A_{\mathbf t}\Rightarrow^*\tuple u$ in $G^h$, then
$h^{(\fan(A))}(\tuple u)=\mathbf t$, and erasing indices gives a
$G$-derivation of $\tuple u$.
\item Every terminal derivation $A\Rightarrow_G^*\tuple u$ lifts uniquely to
$A_{h^{(\fan(A))}(\tuple u)}\Rightarrow_{G^h}^*\tuple u$; a successful
derivation of $w$ lifts uniquely through $\widetilde S\to S_{(h(w))}$.
\item $L(\widetilde G_0)=L(G^h)=L(G)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At every rule application
$h^{(\fan(A))}(\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_r))=\out_\rho(h^{(d_1)}(\tuple
u_1),\ldots,h^{(d_r)}(\tuple u_r))$, because $h$ is a monoid morphism.
Induction on derivation height gives (i) and the unique lifting (ii).  Erasing
indices gives $L(G^h)\subseteq L(G)$, (ii) the converse, and trimming keeps
every rule of every successful derivation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Monoid implementations]
\label{lem:semigroup-transfer}
Let $h_0:\Sigma^*\to M_0$ be a finite-monoid morphism,
$S_0:=h_0(\Sigma^+)$ and $s_0:=h_0|_{\Sigma^+}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For $1\le d\le f$ and $\tuple x,\tuple y\in(\Sigma^+)^d$, the equalities
$h_0^{(d)}(\tuple x)=h_0^{(d)}(\tuple y)$,
$s_0^{(d)}(\tuple x)=s_0^{(d)}(\tuple y)$ and
$\widehat{s_0}^{(d)}(\tuple x)=\widehat{s_0}^{(d)}(\tuple y)$ are equivalent.
Hence $\Cmcf{f}{h_0}=\Cmcf{f}{s_0}$, and the reconstruction operators built
with $h_0$ and with $\widehat{s_0}$ return the same grammar on every sample.
\item For every good MCFG $G$, the trimmed refinements of $G$ under $h_0$ and
under $\widehat{s_0}$ coincide (the untrimmed ones need not); hence the same
canonical tuples, contexts and witness words may be chosen for both.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) The three maps agree on $\Sigma^+$ as maps into $S_0$, and the class and
the Link guard compare types of nonempty components only.  (ii) $S_0$ is a
subsemigroup of $M_0$ and of $S_0^\sharp$ with the same multiplication.  By
$\lambda$-freeness and Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(i), every
typed nonterminal occurring in a successful derivation under either morphism
is some $A_{\mathbf t}$ with $\mathbf t\in S_0^{\fan(A)}$, and the start links
used are $\widetilde S\to S_{(\eta)}$ with $\eta\in S_0$.  For child indices in
$S_0$, every component $\alpha_i$ of a good template is a nonempty word whose
letters and variables are evaluated in $S_0$ identically under both morphisms,
so both give the same $\out_\rho$.  Thus the two full refinements contain the
same rules among $S_0$-indexed symbols, which carry all successful derivations.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical contexts for typed nonterminals]
\label{lem:identity-canonical-contexts}
Let $G$ be good.  Every typed nonterminal $X$ of arity $d$ has a context
$\chi(X)\in\mathcal E_d$ with $\chi(X)[\tuple u]\in L(G)$ for all
$\tuple u\in L_X$; if $\widetilde S\to X$ is in $\widetilde G_0$, one may take
$\chi(X)=\blank_1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take a successful typed derivation containing $X$, and follow the path from
the root to an occurrence of $X$, starting with $\blank_1$.  At each rule on
the path apply Lemma~\ref{lem:filling-identity} with the (nonempty) yields of
the sibling subderivations fixed; this gives $\chi(X)\in\mathcal E_d$.  The
rules on the path depend only on the label $X$, so replacing the subtree of
$X$ by any $X$-derivation yields a successful derivation of
$\chi(X)[\tuple u]$.  If $\widetilde S\to X$ is a rule, use the path of length
zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Typed nonterminals are distributionally homogeneous]
\label{lem:typed-homogeneity}
Let $G$ be good with $L:=L(G)\in\Cmcf{f}{s}$.  For every typed nonterminal $X$
of arity $d$, $L_X$ lies in a single $\equiv_{L,h}^d$-class, denoted $[X]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\tuple u,\tuple v\in L_X$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(i) gives equal types and
$\chi(X)$ accepts both; substitutability gives
$\D_L(\tuple u)=\D_L(\tuple v)$.
\end{proof}

This parallels, but is weaker than, the homogeneity of congruential MCFGs
\cite{ClarkYoshinaka2016}, whose contexts may have empty separators.

\begin{definition}[Canonical tuples, contexts and the witness set]
\label{def:target-canonical}
\label{def:witness-set}
Let $G$ be good.  For every typed nonterminal $X$ fix a \emph{canonical tuple}
$\omega(X)\in L_X$ and a \emph{canonical context} $\chi(X)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:identity-canonical-contexts}, with $\chi(X)=\blank_1$ whenever
$\widetilde S\to X$ is in $\widetilde G_0$.  Let $\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$
consist of the words
\[
 \chi(X)[\omega(X)]\ \ (X\text{ typed}),\qquad
 \chi(X)[\rho(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_q))]\ \
 (X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)\text{ typed},\ q\ge0).
\]
\end{definition}

Since $\omega(X)$ and $\rho(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_q))$ belong to $L_X$,
$\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$ is a finite subset of $L(G)$, nonempty if
$L(G)\ne\emptyset$.  Components of canonical tuples are nonempty, so
$\|\omega(X)\|_1\ge\fan(X)$.  Canonical objects are target witnesses, not
inputs of the operator; qualitatively any choice works, while
Section~\ref{sec:char-data} chooses short ones.

\begin{proposition}[Cardinality of the witness set]
\label{prop:witness-cardinality}
Let $G=(V,\Sigma,P,S,\fan)$ be good of fan-out $\le f$ and rule rank
$\le r_{\max}$, and let $n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ and $n_{\mathrm{rule}}$ be the numbers
of typed nonterminals and typed rules of $\widetilde G_0$.  Then
\[
 |\Wit(\widetilde G_0)|\le n_{\mathrm{typ}}+n_{\mathrm{rule}}
 \le|V||M|^f+|P||M|^{fr_{\max}} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One word is selected per typed nonterminal and per typed rule.  A typed
nonterminal is some $A_{\mathbf t}$ with $\mathbf t\in M^{\fan(A)}$, and a typed
rule is determined by its source rule and the child indices
$\mathbf t_j\in M^{\fan(B_j)}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completeness and Exact Reconstruction}
\label{sec:completeness}

The completeness argument \cite[Lemma~5]{Yoshinaka2011} needs only that the
sample exposes, for every typed rule, one same-type link and one
decomposition. We state it for arbitrary typed representatives, so that it
also applies to the relaxed witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:char-data}.
No assumption that $\iota(X)\in L_X$ is made: the two required witnessed
sentence words are instead explicit hypotheses.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness from a witness assignment]
\label{prop:completeness}
Let $G$ be a finite good MCFG of fan-out $\le f$, let $r_G$ be the maximum rank
of a typed rule of $\widetilde G_0$ ($r_G:=0$ if none), and assign to every
typed nonterminal $X=A_{\mathbf t}$ a tuple $\iota(X)\in(\Sigma^+)^{\fan(A)}$
with $h^{(\fan(A))}(\iota(X))=\mathbf t$ and a context
$\chi(X)\in\mathcal E_{\fan(A)}$, where $\chi(X)=\blank_1$ whenever
$\widetilde S\to X$ is in $\widetilde G_0$.  If a finite sample $K$ contains
$\chi(X)[\iota(X)]$ for every $X$ and
$\chi(X)[\rho(\iota(Y_1),\ldots,\iota(Y_q))]$ for every typed rule
$X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)$, $q\ge0$, then
\[
 L(G)\subseteq L\bigl(\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}(K)\bigr)
 \ \ (r_{\max}\ge\max\{1,r_G\}),
 \qquad
 L(G)\subseteq L\bigl(\mathcal B_{f,h}(K)\bigr).
\]
Substitutability is not assumed.  The canonical choice $\iota=\omega$ gives
the hypothesis $\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\subseteq K$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We show that $X\Rightarrow_{\widetilde G_0}^*\tuple u$ implies
$[\iota(X)]\Rightarrow^*\tuple u$ in both grammars, by induction on the
derivation.  Each $\iota(X)$ is observed, since $\chi(X)[\iota(X)]\in K$.  Let
the derivation start with $X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)$, followed by
$Y_j\Rightarrow^*\tuple u_j$, and put
$\tuple z:=\rho(\iota(Y_1),\ldots,\iota(Y_q))$.  Then $\tuple z$ is observed,
since $\chi(X)[\tuple z]\in K$, and
$h(\tuple z)=\out_\rho(\text{indices of the }Y_j)$ is the index of $X$, which
is $h(\iota(X))$; as $\chi(X)$ accepts both in $K$, the rule
$[\iota(X)]\to[\tuple z]$ is a Link.  If $q=0$, Const gives
$[\tuple z]\to\tuple z=\tuple u$.  If $q\ge1$,
$(\tuple z,\rho,(\iota(Y_j))_j)$ is a composition witness of rank
$q\le r_G\le r_{\max}$, and $q\le\|K\|_+$ by \eqref{eq:sample-rank}, so Comp
gives $[\tuple z]\to\rho([\iota(Y_1)],\ldots,[\iota(Y_q)])$ in both
operators; the induction hypothesis completes
$[\iota(X)]\Rightarrow^*\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_q)$.  Finally a
successful derivation begins with $\widetilde S\to X$, where
$\chi(X)=\blank_1$, so $\iota(X)=(w_0)$ with $w_0\in K\cap\Sigma^+$ and
$\widehat S\to[\iota(X)]$ is a Start rule; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(iii) this covers $L(G)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact reconstruction for a fixed typing]
\label{thm:exact-reconstruction}
Fix $f\ge1$ and $s:\Sigma^+\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf S$, and put
$h=\widehat s$.  Let $L\in\Cmcf{f}{s}$.  If $L\cap\Sigma^+=\emptyset$ put
$K_L^\star:=L$ and $r_G:=0$.  Otherwise let $G$ be a good presentation of
$L\cap\Sigma^+$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}), and put
$K_L^\star:=\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\cup(\{\eps\}\cap L)$.  Then
$K_L^\star\subseteq L$ is finite, and every finite $K$ with
$K_L^\star\subseteq K\subseteq L$ satisfies
\[
 L(\mathcal B_{f,s}(K))=L,\qquad
 L(\mathcal B_{f,s}^{\le r_{\max}}(K))=L\quad
 \text{for every }r_{\max}\ge\max\{1,r_G\}.
\]
If $L\in\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$, $G$ can be chosen of rank $\le r$, so
$r_{\max}=\max\{1,r\}$ suffices (also when $r=0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $L\cap\Sigma^+=\emptyset$ then $K=L\in\{\emptyset,\{\eps\}\}$ and the claim
is immediate from Definition~\ref{def:reconstruction}.  Otherwise
$K_L^\star\subseteq L$ is finite by the remark after
Definition~\ref{def:witness-set}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:completeness} with
$\iota=\omega$ (admissible by Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(i))
gives $L\cap\Sigma^+\subseteq L(\mathcal B(K))$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction-soundness} gives
$L(\mathcal B(K))\subseteq L$, for both operators.  The hypothesis contains
$\eps$ iff $\eps\in K$, iff $\eps\in L$, because $K_L^\star\subseteq K\subseteq
L$.  Rank-preserving normalization gives the last sentence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Effective Reconstruction and Conservative Learning}
\label{sec:poly-time}
\begin{theorem}[Polynomial construction under a rule-rank cap]
\label{thm:poly}
Fix $f,r_{\max}\ge1$ and $s$, and implement type tests by $h=\widehat s$.  The
encoding of $\widehat G_{\le r_{\max}}(K)$ is computable in time
$(1+\|K\|_+)^{O(fr_{\max})}$; its fan-out is at most $f$ and its rank at most
$r_{\max}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $n_K=\max\{1,\|K\|_+\}$.  An occurrence is a sample word with at most $2f$
cut positions, so there are $n_K^{O(f)}$ occurrences, typed by fixed-monoid
products; grouping occurrences by context and type lists the Link rules.  A
composition witness of rank $r\le r_{\max}$ is determined by a parent
occurrence and at most $2fr_{\max}$ further cut positions inside it, with a
labelling whose number depends only on $f,r_{\max}$; goodness is checked
directly.  This gives $n_K^{O(fr_{\max})}$ candidate Comp rules; the remaining
rules have rank at most one, and deduplication leaves an encoding of the same
size.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Conservative sequential learner]
\label{def:conservative-learner}
The learner $\mathcal A_{f,h}$ (respectively $\mathcal A_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}$)
starts with $H_0$, keeps its hypothesis on $\pausesym$ and on every datum the
hypothesis generates, and otherwise replaces it by $\mathcal B_{f,h}(K)$
(respectively $\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}(K)$), where $K$ is the content
read so far.  This is the policy of \cite[Algorithm~1]{Yoshinaka2011}.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Gold identification]
\label{cor:gold-explanatory}
\label{cor:capped-gold-poly}
Fix $f\ge1$ and $s$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\mathcal A_{f,s}$ TxtEx-identifies $\Cmcf{f}{s}$.
\item For fixed $r_{\max}$, $\mathcal A_{f,s}^{\le r_{\max}}$ TxtEx-identifies
$\Cmcf{f}{s}\cap\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r_{\max})$, in time per datum polynomial in the
size of the content read so far.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The learners are computable: hypotheses are computable and MCFG membership is
decidable~\cite{SekiEtAl1991}.  Let $L$ be in the class and fix a text; let
$K_L^\star$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-reconstruction}, with
$r_{\max}\ge r_G$ in case (ii), and let $n_0$ be a stage whose content
contains $K_L^\star$.  Every hypothesis $H$ satisfies $L(H)\subseteq L$, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction-soundness} or because $H=H_0$.  A rebuild
at a stage $\ge n_0$ yields an exact hypothesis, which is never changed
afterwards since all later data lie in $L$.  If no rebuild occurs after $n_0$,
let $H$ be the hypothesis current at $n_0$.  If $H=H_0$, no positive datum was
ever read, so $L=\emptyset=L(H)$.  Otherwise $H$ was built from the content of
some stage $m$; it generates that content by sample-consistency and every
later datum by conservativeness, hence all of $L$, so $L(H)=L$.  In both cases
the outputs stabilize on a correct grammar.  For (ii),
Theorem~\ref{thm:poly} bounds rebuilding, and membership of a datum $w$ in a
hypothesis $\widehat G$ of fan-out $f$ and rank $r_{\max}$ takes
$O(\|\widehat G\|^2|w|^{f(r_{\max}+1)})$ time
\cite[Proposition~4]{Yoshinaka2011}.
\end{proof}

Every CFG has a rank-two presentation, so $\mathcal A_{1,h}^{\le2}$ covers
$\Cmcf{1}{h}$; this does not imply polynomial characteristic-data length at
rank two (Section~\ref{sec:char-data}).

\subsection{Specialization of Yoshinaka's 2015 Signature}
\label{sec:general-framework}

We recall from \cite[Sections~4 and~6.1]{Yoshinaka2015General} the notions used.
A \emph{signature} consists of pairwise disjoint sorts (sets of objects), a
distinguished sort $O_*$, and typed function names; a grammar over it has
rules $X_0\leftarrow g\langle X_1,\ldots,X_n\rangle$ respecting sorts and
initial symbols of sort $O_*$.  $\mathcal S_O$ and $\mathcal C_O$ are the
substructures and contexts (functions $O\to O_*$) of sort $O$ realized in some
grammar over the signature, and a language is \emph{substitutable} if
$c_1\odot s_1,c_1\odot s_2,c_2\odot s_1\in L$ imply $c_2\odot s_2\in L$ for all
sorts $O$, $c_i\in\mathcal C_O$, $s_i\in\mathcal S_O$.

Fix $f,r\ge1$ and $s:\Sigma^+\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf S$, with $h=\widehat s$.
The signature $\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$ has the sort $O_*$, a tagged copy
$\{\overline w:w\in\Sigma^*\}$ of $\Sigma^*$, and the tagged sorts
$T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}=\{\tuple w\in(\Sigma^+)^d:s^{(d)}(\tuple w)=
\boldsymbol\mu\}$ for $1\le d\le f$ and $\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathsf S^d$.  Its
functions are: every good template $\rho$ of rank $q\le r$ with arities at most
$f$, typed as
$T_{d_1,\boldsymbol\mu_1}\times\cdots\times T_{d_q,\boldsymbol\mu_q}\to
T_{d_0,\out_\rho(\boldsymbol\mu_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol\mu_q)}$ for all
$\boldsymbol\mu_j$ (the index lies in $\mathsf S^{d_0}$ by
$\lambda$-freeness); promotions $\pi_\mu:T_{1,\mu}\to O_*$, $(w)\mapsto
\overline w$; and a constant $\overline\eps$ of sort $O_*$.  Forgetting types,
this is a finite sort refinement in the sense of
\cite[Section~8]{Yoshinaka2015General} of the signature of good MCFG
operations on nonempty tuples, a nonempty-component variant of
\cite[Example~3]{Yoshinaka2015General}; at fan-out one the prefix--suffix
case corresponds to \cite[Example~4]{Yoshinaka2015General}.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-rank coverage by Yoshinaka's 2015 framework]
\label{prop:yoshinaka2015-embedding}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item $\mathcal S_{T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}}=T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}$,
$\mathcal C_{T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}}=\{\tuple x\mapsto\overline{E[\tuple x]}:
E\in\mathcal E_d\}$ as sets of functions on $T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}$, and
$\mathcal C_{O_*}$ contains only the identity.
\item $\mathsf{Subst}(\mathfrak S_{f,r,s})=\Cmcf{f}{s}\cap\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$.
\item For fixed $f,r,s$, $\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$ satisfies Assumptions~1--3 of
\cite{Yoshinaka2015General}.  Hence, by \cite[Theorem~1]{Yoshinaka2015General}
(proved there for $\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm D}$ via its Lemmas~2--3),
$\Cmcf{f}{s}\cap\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$ is identifiable in the limit from positive
data with polynomial-time updates, and for a target grammar without useless
symbols, with $N$ nonterminals and $P$ rules, $\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm D}$ has
a characteristic set of cardinality at most $|N|+|P|$ (the bound stated after
\cite[Lemma~3]{Yoshinaka2015General}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Constants (good rank-zero templates, i.e.\ tuples of nonempty words) give
$\mathcal S_T=T$.  No function takes an argument of sort $O_*$ and only
promotions and $\overline\eps$ have codomain $O_*$; so $\mathcal C_{O_*}$ is
the identity, and the context of a non-start nonterminal is obtained from the
argument context $\blank_1$ of a promotion by descending through good
templates whose sibling arguments are nonempty tuples.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:filling-identity} each step yields an admissible context, so
$\mathcal C_T\subseteq\{E[\cdot]:E\in\mathcal E_d\}$.  Conversely
$E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots\blank_dv_d\in\mathcal E_d$ is the context of $Z$ in the
grammar $X_*\leftarrow\pi_\nu\langle Y\rangle$,
$Y\leftarrow(v_0\xi_1^1v_1\cdots\xi_1^dv_d)\langle Z\rangle$,
$Z\leftarrow\tuple x_0$ for any $\tuple x_0\in T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}$; the
unary template is good because $v_1,\ldots,v_{d-1}\ne\eps$ and its output is
nonempty, and $\nu$ is its output type.

(ii) By (i), Yoshinaka's condition for the sort $T_{d,\boldsymbol\mu}$ says:
for $E_1,E_2\in\mathcal E_d$ and $\tuple x,\tuple y$ of type
$\boldsymbol\mu$, $E_1[\tuple x],E_1[\tuple y],E_2[\tuple x]\in L$ imply
$E_2[\tuple y]\in L$; for $O_*$ it is vacuous.  Over all $d\le f$ and
$\boldsymbol\mu$ this is $(f,s)$-tuple substitutability.  It remains to see
that $\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$-grammars generate exactly $\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$.
Erasing tags turns such a grammar into an MCFG of fan-out $\le f$ and rank
$\le r$ (promotions become identity rules into a fresh start symbol,
$\overline\eps$ a constant rule).  Conversely, for $L\in\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$
with $L\cap\Sigma^+\ne\emptyset$, take a good presentation $G$ of
$L\cap\Sigma^+$ of rank $\le r$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}).
In $\widetilde G_0$ every non-start index lies in $\mathsf S^{\fan(A)}$ and
every typed rule is an instance of a function of $\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$; replace
each start rule $\widetilde S\to S_{(\eta)}$ by a promotion $\pi_\eta$ and add
$\overline\eps$ if $\eps\in L$.  The result generates $L$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(iii).  The case $L\subseteq\{\eps\}$
is trivial.

(iii) Assumption~2 holds since arities are at most $r$.  For Assumption~1,
sort membership, function evaluation, recognizing admissible contexts (by
(i)), plugging, and membership in the language of a grammar over
$\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$ are polynomial; the last is bounded fan-out and rank MCFG
membership after erasing tags \cite[Proposition~4]{Yoshinaka2011}.  For
Assumption~3, let $d\in\Sigma^*$.  By (i) the substructures of non-start sorts
occurring in $d$ are the tuples of occurrences of arity $\le f$ in $d$, typed
by $s$, and their contexts are the corresponding admissible contexts; at
$O_*$ they are $\overline d$ and the identity.  A typed template $\rho$ with
$c\odot\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_q)=\overline d$ yields an occurrence
of $\tuple z=\rho(\tuple u_1,\ldots,\tuple u_q)$ in $d$, i.e.\ a composition
witness on it (a constant if $q=0$); promotions and $\overline\eps$ occur only
for $d\ne\eps$ and $d=\eps$, respectively.  Crucially, a function
\emph{name} includes its output sort and the ordered list of input sorts:
for every candidate factorization the componentwise $s$-types of the
observed child tuples determine these sorts, and the output sort is computed
by $\out_\rho$. Thus the enumeration records each typed instance, not only
the untyped template, including the well-typed unary identity instances.
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:poly}, all these sets are computable in time
$(|d|+1)^{O(f(r+1))}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Substructure-indexed and guarded reconstruction]
\label{lem:substp}
Let $\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}$ be Yoshinaka's substructure-indexed
construction \cite[Section~6.1]{Yoshinaka2015General} for
$\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$ with the sets of the preceding proof.  For every finite
$K\subseteq\Sigma^*$,
$L(\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}(K))=L(\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,s}(K))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}(K)$ has a nonterminal $[\![x]\!]$ for each
substructure $x$ occurring in $K$, initial symbols $[\![\overline w]\!]$ for
$w\in K$, and a rule $[\![x_0]\!]\leftarrow g\langle[\![x_1]\!],\ldots,
[\![x_n]\!]\rangle$ for each function $g$ occurring in $K$ and occurring
substructures $x_i$ of matching sorts whenever some context $c$ occurring in
$K$ has $c\odot x_0,c\odot g(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in K$.  On non-start sorts
identify $[\![\tuple x]\!]$ with $[\tuple x]$; these range over
$\mathrm{Obs}(K)$.

Such a rule with $g=\rho$ of rank $n$ is simulated in
$\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,s}(K)$: put $\tuple z:=\rho(\tuple x_1,\ldots,\tuple
x_n)$; by (i), $c=E[\cdot]$ with $E\in\mathcal E_{d}$ and
$E[\tuple x_0],E[\tuple z]\in K$, so $\tuple z$ is observed and has the sort,
hence the type, of $\tuple x_0$; thus $[\tuple x_0]\to[\tuple z]$ is a Link,
followed by Const $[\tuple z]\to\tuple z$ if $n=0$ or by the Comp rule of the
composition witness $(\tuple z,\rho,(\tuple x_j)_j)$ if $n\ge1$.  Conversely,
each non-start rule of $\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,s}(K)$ is a rule of
$\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}(K)$: Const $[\tuple x]\to\tuple x$ and Comp
$[\tuple z]\to\rho(\ldots)$ use $x_0=\tuple x$, resp.\ $x_0=\tuple z$, and the
context of an occurrence of $x_0$; Link $[\tuple x]\to[\tuple y]$ uses the
identity template of the common sort, which occurs in $K$, and the shared
context.  For the Link case, the unary identity has a genuinely
observed function instance: if $E[\tuple y]\in K$, then
$E[\operatorname{id}(\tuple y)]\in K$; hence its typed function name
belongs to $\mathrm{FUN}(K)$.  Likewise, each Comp uses its witnessed
observed parent and child factorization, and each Const its rank-zero
instance. By induction on derivations, $[\![\tuple x]\!]$ and
$[\tuple x]$ derive the same tuples.  At $O_*$ the only context is the identity and only
promotions and $\overline\eps$ have codomain $O_*$; so for $K\ne\emptyset$ the
rules for initial symbols are $[\![\overline w]\!]\leftarrow\pi\langle
[\![(w')]\!]\rangle$ ($w,w'\in K$, $w'\ne\eps$) and
$[\![\overline w]\!]\leftarrow\overline\eps$ ($w\in K$, $\eps\in K$), and both
grammars generate $\bigcup_{w'\in K\cap\Sigma^+}L([(w')])\cup(\{\eps\}\cap
K)$; for $K=\emptyset$ both languages are empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not inherited]
\label{rem:yoshinaka-inheritance}
(a) Yoshinaka proves \cite[Theorem~1]{Yoshinaka2015General} for
$\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm D}$ and states without separate proof that his
Algorithm~1 also works with $\mathrm{SUBST}_{\mathrm P}$
\cite[Sections~6.1 and~7]{Yoshinaka2015General}.  For
$\mathfrak S_{f,r,s}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:substp} together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction-soundness} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-reconstruction} supplies that verification;
Lemma~\ref{lem:substp} asserts equality of generated languages, not of
grammars.  (b) Cardinality bounds, whether $|N|+|P|$ or
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness-cardinality}, do not bound total length; this is
the subject of Section~\ref{sec:char-data}.  (c) Assumption~2 bounds arity in
each signature, so Proposition~\ref{prop:yoshinaka2015-embedding} covers
$\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$ only for fixed $r$; unbounded rank is covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-reconstruction}, extending
\cite[Corollary~2]{Yoshinaka2011} to typed links.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Thickness and reusable canonical contexts}
\label{subsec:thickness-contexts}

\paragraph{Rule thickness and Yoshinaka's efficiency criterion.}
For the quantitative results, presentations are reduced and \emph{good}, as in
Yoshinaka's fixed-dimension, fixed-rank good form
\cite[Section~2.3, after Example~2]{Yoshinaka2011}.  For a rule $R$ of $G$ let
$\theta_G(R)$ be the minimum length of a successful sentence derivation using
$R$, put $\theta_G:=\max_{R\in P}\theta_G(R)$, and define
\(t_G:=\max_A\min\{\|\tuple w\|_1:\tuple w\in L_A(G)\}.\)
Thus $\theta_G$ is rule thickness and $t_G$ nonterminal thickness.
Yoshinaka writes these quantities as $\tau_G$ and $t_G$, respectively, with
$\tau_G\le\|G\|t_G$ in his notation
\cite[Section~4.3]{Yoshinaka2011}.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial equivalence of the two thickness parameters]
\label{lem:thickness-equivalence}
For every reduced good presentation $G$,
\(t_G\le\theta_G \qquad\text{and}\qquad \theta_G\le 2\|G\|\max\{1,t_G\}.\)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $t_G\le\theta_G$, choose any rule with left-hand side $A$ and place it
in a successful derivation.  Nondeletion preserves the tuple produced at that
occurrence of $A$, so its total length is bounded by the final sentence.
Minimize for $A$ and maximize over $A$.

For the converse, fix a rule $R$ with left-hand side $A$ and follow a shortest
dependency path from the start symbol to $A$.  The path repeats no
nonterminal.  Keep the path child open, complete each sibling by a shortest
terminal tuple, then apply $R$ and complete its children likewise.  Across the
distinct rules on this construction, both explicit terminal material and
child-variable occurrences are bounded by $\|G\|$, giving a successful
sentence of length at most
$\|G\|+\|G\|t_G\le2\|G\|\max\{1,t_G\}$.
\end{proof}

Hence the two thickness parameters are polynomially equivalent on reduced good
presentations.  Yoshinaka's efficiency criterion asks for polynomial
hypothesis construction, characteristic-set cardinality, and total positive
data in target size and rule thickness
\cite[Definition~1]{Yoshinaka2011}; see also
\cite{EyraudHeinzYoshinaka2016}.  Here characteristic-data bounds concern the
capped set-driven reconstruction, while TxtEx stabilization is supplied by the
conservative learner.  Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-good-thickness}
transfers the size--thickness scale for reduced $\lambda$-free inputs; genuine
empty components may instead cause the exponential growth of
Proposition~\ref{prop:empty-component-blowup}.

\begin{lemma}[Reusable canonical-context bound]

\label{lem:reusable-context-bound}
Let $\mathcal G_{\mathrm t}$ be a trimmed good typed MCFG
with $n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ non-start typed nonterminals and maximum non-start rule rank at most
$r_0\ge1$.  Suppose that every non-start typed nonterminal $Y$ has a canonical tuple
$\omega(Y)\in L_Y(\mathcal G_{\mathrm t})$ with
$\|\omega(Y)\|_1\le b_{\mathrm{can}}$, and that every non-start typed rule writes at
most $b_{\mathrm{rule}}$ terminal symbols explicitly.  Then the
canonical contexts may be chosen so that
\[
  |\chi(X)|_{\mathrm{fix}}
  \le n_{\mathrm{typ}}\bigl(b_{\mathrm{rule}}+(r_0-1)b_{\mathrm{can}}\bigr)
\]
for every non-start typed nonterminal $X$.  With these contexts and canonical tuples,
every selected witness word has length at most
\[
  n_{\mathrm{typ}}\bigl(b_{\mathrm{rule}}+(r_0-1)b_{\mathrm{can}}\bigr)+b_{\mathrm{rule}}+r_0b_{\mathrm{can}}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\widetilde S\to X$ is present take $\chi(X)=\blank_1$; otherwise follow a
shortest typed-nonterminal dependency path to $X$.  It has at most
$n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ non-start steps.  Keeping the path child open and filling
each sibling canonically costs at most
$b_{\mathrm{rule}}+(r_0-1)b_{\mathrm{can}}$ terminals per step.  Goodness
preserves the selected components, their order, and nonempty separators,
giving the context bound.  Adding either one canonical tuple or one rule
template with at most $r_0$ child tuples gives the displayed witness bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Refined-Thickness Baseline}
\label{subsec:typed-thickness}

For a reduced good presentation $G$ of an $\eps$-free target, let
$\widetilde G_0$ be its trimmed full yield-type refinement and put
\(\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}} :=\max\{1,\theta_{\widetilde G_0}\},\)
where $\theta_{\widetilde G_0}$ is rule thickness computed in the refined
grammar.  No comparison with the original untyped thickness is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Refined-thickness baseline]
\label{lem:typed-thickness-baseline}
Fix $f\ge1$, $r_{\max}\ge1$, a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, and a finite
typing $h:\Sigma^*\to M$.  Let $G$ be a reduced good fan-out-$\le f$,
rank-$\le r_{\max}$ presentation of an $\eps$-free language in
$\Cmcf{f}{h}$, and let $\widetilde G_0$ be its trimmed yield-type refinement.
Then the target witness set has cardinality polynomial in $\|G\|$ and total
positive length polynomial in $\|G\|$ and
$\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}}:=\max\{1,\theta_{\widetilde G_0}\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The trimmed refinement $\widetilde G_0$ remains reduced and good.  A
minimum-yield successful derivation using a typed rule with left-hand side
$X$ contains an $X$-tuple of total length at most
$\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}}$, so use such tuples canonically.  If
$\widetilde S\to X$ exists take $\chi(X)=\blank_1$; otherwise remove the
chosen $X$-subtree from the same derivation to obtain a canonical context with
fixed material at most $\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}}$.  Each typed rule writes at
most that many terminals and has at most $r_{\max}$ canonical children, so
every selected witness has length at most
$(r_{\max}+2)\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}}$.  Therefore
\[
 |\Wit(\widetilde G_0)|
 \le |V||M|^f+|P||M|^{fr_{\max}},
 \qquad
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+
 \le
 (|V||M|^f+|P||M|^{fr_{\max}})
 (1+(r_{\max}+2)\bar\theta_{\mathrm{typ}}),
\]
which is polynomial for fixed $f,r_{\max},h$.
\end{proof}

For trivial typing, no nonterminal is split by
yield type; consequently the original
thickness scale is retained. This recovers
Yoshinaka's untyped characteristic-data
estimate~\cite[Lemma~7]{Yoshinaka2011}.
Without this uniqueness, the refinement
may have much larger thickness, as the next
proposition demonstrates.

\begin{proposition}[Exponential blow-up of full yield-type refinement]
\label{prop:typed-thickness-exponential}
There are a fixed two-element typing $h_{\mathtt c}$, reduced good fan-out-one rank-two
grammars $G_n$ of size $O(n)$, and a constant bound
$\theta_{G_n}\le2$, such that $L(G_n)=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt c\}^+$ and the
trimmed full yield-type refinement $\widetilde G_{n,0}$ satisfies
\(\theta_{\widetilde G_{n,0}}\ge 2^n.\)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $M_{\mathtt c}=\{1,\zeta\}$ with $\zeta^2=\zeta$,
$h_{\mathtt c}(\mathtt a)=1$, and
$h_{\mathtt c}(\mathtt c)=\zeta$.  For $n\ge1$, view
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S&\to SS\mid\mathtt a\mid\mathtt c\mid A_0\mid\cdots\mid A_n,\\
 A_0&\to\mathtt a,\qquad
 A_i\to A_{i-1}A_{i-1}\mid\mathtt c\quad(1\le i\le n)
\end{aligned}
\]
as a fan-out-one MCFG.  It is reduced and good, has size $O(n)$ and rule rank
two, generates $\{\mathtt a,\mathtt c\}^+$, and every rule has a successful
use of length at most two, so $\theta_{G_n}\le2$.

In the full refinement, the retained copy $(A_n)_1$ can derive only words
without $\mathtt c$; recursively the unique such yield is
$\mathtt a^{2^n}$.  Hence the typed rule
$S_{(1)}\to(A_n)_1$ has successful thickness at least $2^n$.
\end{proof}

Thus full yield-type refinement admits no bound polynomial in
$\|G\|\max\{1,\theta_G\}$ for arbitrary fixed typing, already at fan-out one
and rank two.  This is a limitation of the full-refinement route, not of the
language $\{\mathtt a,\mathtt c\}^+$ itself.

\paragraph{Prior lower bound at fan-out one.}
The revised companion CFG paper~\cite{kuriyamaCFG}
(whose frozen TCS R1 source is linked in the bibliography) establishes an
operator-independent exponential characteristic-data
lower bound for fixed two-element semilattice typing,
already at fan-out one and rule rank two
\cite{kuriyamaCFG}.
We do not reproduce this prior CFG result.
It does not automatically extend to higher fan-out,
whose tuple substitutability tests more arities.

\subsection{Rule Rank One: Grammar-Size Polynomial Data}
\label{subsec:rankone-polydata}

Unary derivation chains admit a grammar-size bound
independent of thickness, even under arbitrary fixed
componentwise typing.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-one polynomial construction and characteristic data]
\label{thm:rankone-polydata}
Fix $f\ge1$, finite $\Sigma$, and a finite-semigroup typing
$s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$. Put $h=\widehat s$ and $M=\mathsf S^\sharp$.
For any finite standard fan-out-$\le f$,
rank-$\le1$ grammar $G_{\mathrm{in}}$
of $L\in\Cmcf{f}{s}$,
$\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le1}$ is polynomial-time in
$\|K\|_+$ and has a characteristic sample of
total length $\operatorname{poly}_{f,s}(\|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|)$.
The direct-start datum $\eps$ is added when
$\eps\in L$ without affecting this bound.
The conservative learner
$\mathcal A_{f,h}^{\le1}$ consequently
TxtEx-identifies
$\Cmcf{f}{s}\cap\mathsf{MCFL}(f,1)$
with polynomial time per datum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalize without increasing rank
(Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}),
and trim the typed refinement.
Write $n_{\mathrm{typ}}\le|V||M|^f$ for its
number of non-start typed nonterminals and
$b_G\le\max\{1,\|G\|\}$ for the largest explicit
terminal count of a rule, taking $b_G\ge1$.
Choose a typed terminal unary derivation that
minimizes first total yield length and then the
number of rules. No typed nonterminal can repeat:
splicing in the later suffix would not increase
yield length and would strictly shorten the
chain. Therefore
$\|\omega(X)\|_1\le n_{\mathrm{typ}}b_G$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:reusable-context-bound} bounds
each witness word by $(2n_{\mathrm{typ}}+1)b_G$,
while Proposition~\ref{prop:witness-cardinality}
gives at most $n_{\mathrm{typ}}+|P||M|^f$
witnesses. Hence
\[
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+
 \le(n_{\mathrm{typ}}+|P||M|^f)
        \bigl(1+(2n_{\mathrm{typ}}+1)b_G\bigr).
\]
The polynomial overhead of normalization and the
time and convergence bounds follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:poly} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:capped-gold-poly}.
\end{proof}

This extends the rank-one untyped scale
\cite[Lemma~7]{Yoshinaka2011} and the fan-out-one
argument of~\cite{kuriyamaCFG} to arbitrary
fixed finite typing at every fixed fan-out.
Rank two cannot generally yield a
grammar-size-only bound: the size-$O(n)$
grammar $A_0\to\mathtt c$,
$A_i\to A_{i-1}A_{i-1}$, $S\to A_n$
generates the singleton $\{\mathtt c^{2^n}\}$,
which is $(f,h)$-tuple-substitutable for
every fixed $f,h$: over a one-letter alphabet,
sharing a sentence context forces equal total
component lengths, so every context accepts
both tuples or neither. Every characteristic
sample for this singleton target contains a word
of length at least $2^n$.

\subsection{Prefix--Suffix-Determined Typings: Thickness-Sensitive Data}
\label{subsec:boundary-polydata}

Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-thickness-baseline} gives an arbitrary-$h$
characteristic-data bound in terms of the thickness of the refined typed
presentation.  Proposition~\ref{prop:typed-thickness-exponential} shows that
this refined thickness need not be polynomially controlled by the original
good presentation.  A stronger transfer is nevertheless possible whenever the
finite typing is determined by a fixed prefix--suffix window.  We formulate
this boundary-determined regime in the same thickness-sensitive representation
model used by Yoshinaka~\cite[Section~4.3]{Yoshinaka2011}.

Let $G=(V,\Sigma,P,S,\fan)$ be a reduced good MCFG and put
\(\bar\theta_G:=\max\{1,\theta_G\}.\)
Here $\theta_G$ is the rule-thickness parameter defined above.  The argument is stated for the
$\lambda$-free good core.  If the target also contains $\eps$, apply
the argument to $L\setminus\{\eps\}$ and add the single datum $\eps$ at
the start symbol, exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-reconstruction}; this
adds only one length-zero example and does not affect either polynomial bound.

\begin{lemma}[Rule thickness bounds local terminal material]
\label{lem:thickness-local-bounds}
Let $G$ be reduced and good.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item for every rule $R$, the total number of terminal symbols written
explicitly in the template of $R$ is at most $\theta_G$;
\item for every nonterminal $A$, there exists a terminal tuple
$\tuple u\in L_A(G)$ with $\|\tuple u\|_1\le\theta_G$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reducedness places every rule in some successful derivation.  Because a good
grammar is nondeleting, every terminal written by that rule and every component
of the tuple produced at its left-hand side survives exactly once to the final
sentence.  A minimum-yield successful derivation using the rule therefore bounds both
its explicit terminal material and the total length of that occurrence's
left-hand-side tuple.  Maximizing over rules gives (i) and (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Untyped rule thickness controls canonical contexts]
\label{lem:untyped-contexts}
Let $G=(V,\Sigma,P,S,\fan)$ be a reduced good
MCFG and $\widetilde G_0$ its trimmed
yield-type refinement for any fixed finite typing
$h$. For each $A\in V$ of fan-out $d$ there is
an admissible sentence context $E_A\in\mathcal E_d$
such that
\[
 |E_A|_{\mathrm{fix}}\le\bar\theta_G,
 \qquad E_A[\tuple u]\in L(G)
 \quad\text{for all }\tuple u\in L_A(G),
\]
and $E_S=\blank_1$ may be chosen.
Thus for every non-start typed nonterminal
$X=A_{\mathbf t}$ the choice
$\chi(X):=E_A$ meets
Definition~\ref{def:target-canonical}.
With canonical tuples satisfying
$\|\omega(X)\|_1\le b_{\mathrm{can}}$
and maximum rule rank $r_{\max}$,
every word in $\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$
has length at most
\(2\bar\theta_G+r_{\max}b_{\mathrm{can}}.\)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take $E_S=\blank_1$. For $A\ne S$ choose
a source rule $R$ with left-hand side $A$.
Because $G$ is reduced, there is a successful
sentence derivation using $R$ and having
length $\theta_G(R)\le\theta_G$.
Remove the subtree rooted at that occurrence
of $A$, replacing its $d$ components by
ordered holes. Lemma~\ref{lem:filling-identity},
iterated along the path to the root, and
goodness show that the resulting
$E_A$ belongs to $\mathcal E_d$.
Its fixed terminal material is part of the
selected sentence, hence has length at most
$\theta_G(R)$. Substituting any tuple from
$L_A(G)$ gives another successful source
derivation, so $E_A$ accepts every such tuple.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(i),
$L_X\subseteq L_A(G)$; consequently $E_A$
accepts every tuple of the typed $X$.
The surrounding source derivation can also
be lifted to the full typed refinement by
Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(ii):
the type of $X$ determines all ancestral
types given the fixed sibling derivations,
and the fresh start symbol admits every
start type. In particular, $\chi(X)$
has the required common-context property.
For a direct start link, $X=S_{\mathbf t}$
and the chosen context is $\blank_1$.

A witness is $\chi(X)[\omega(X)]$,
$\chi(X)[\tuple c]$, or
$\chi(X)[\rho(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_r))]$.
Its context contributes at most
$\bar\theta_G$ terminals; the source rule
writes at most $\bar\theta_G$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:thickness-local-bounds}(i),
and it has at most $r_{\max}$ child tuples.
The displayed bound follows, with the
rank-zero case included.
\end{proof}

Fix $k,\ell\ge0$ and let
$h:\Sigma^*\to M$ be any finite typing with
$h\preceq h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$.  Thus $h$ factors through the surjective prefix--suffix typing
$h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$.  Let $G$ have fan-out at most $f$ and
rule rank at most a fixed $r_{\max}\ge1$, and let $\widetilde G_0$ be its
trimmed full yield-type refinement under $h$.  Let $n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ be the
number of retained non-start $h$-typed nonterminals.

\begin{lemma}[Short prefix--suffix-typed canonical tuples]
\label{lem:boundary-short-canonical}
For every non-start typed nonterminal $X$ of $\widetilde G_0$, the
canonical tuple may be chosen so that
\(\|\omega(X)\|_1 \le f(k+\ell+1)n_{\mathrm{typ}}(r_{\max}+1)\bar\theta_G.\)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose, among all terminal derivations from $X$ in the trimmed typed grammar
$\widetilde G_0$, one with the minimum number of grammar-rule nodes, and then mark the root terminals needed to
determine its componentwise $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$-type: all terminals of a
component shorter than $m_0=\max\{1,k+\ell\}$ from
Definition~\ref{def:prefix-suffix-typing}, and otherwise its first $k$ and
last $\ell$ terminals (one arbitrary terminal when $k=\ell=0$).  There are at most $f(k+\ell+1)$ marked terminals.

No typed nonterminal can occur twice on a root-to-leaf grammar path of this minimal
derivation.  If an upper and a proper lower occurrence had the same typed
label, the lower subtree would itself be a valid typed terminal derivation from
the same typed nonterminal, and replacing the upper subtree by it would strictly reduce
the number of grammar-rule nodes.  Every typed nonterminal occurring in this derivation is retained in $\widetilde G_0$; hence every root-to-marked-terminal path
contains at most $n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ grammar nodes.  Their union therefore contains at most
$f(k+\ell+1)n_{\mathrm{typ}}$ grammar nodes.

Keep the union of these marked root-to-leaf paths as the retained subderivation.  By linearity and nondeletion, each component
of every omitted child contributes a contiguous interval to exactly one root
component; it is never split between root components.  Replace every omitted
child subtree by a shortest terminal tuple of its underlying untyped
nonterminal.  Lemma~\ref{lem:thickness-local-bounds}(ii) bounds each such tuple
by $\bar\theta_G$, and part~(i) bounds the explicit terminal material of each
retained rule by $\bar\theta_G$.  Because the grammar is good, every
replacement component is nonempty.  An omitted child cannot contribute to a
short root component: every terminal of such a component was marked, so any
nonempty contribution from that child would contain a marked terminal and the
corresponding root-to-terminal path would place the child on the retained
skeleton.  In a long root component no replaced block crosses a protected
prefix or suffix position.
Hence every replacement lies wholly in the unmarked interior of that component:
the marked first $k$ and last $\ell$ terminals remain literal prefixes and
suffixes, and the component stays on the long-word side of the threshold.
Thus the root prefix--suffix type is unchanged (and when $k=\ell=0$ the single
marked terminal preserves nonemptiness).  Applying the factor map supplied by
$h\preceq h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ shows that its componentwise $h$-type is
unchanged as well.
Each retained node contributes at most $\bar\theta_G$ explicit terminals and
has at most $r_{\max}$ omitted child subtrees.  The resulting root tuple has
length at most
\(f(k+\ell+1)n_{\mathrm{typ}}(r_{\max}+1)\bar\theta_G.\)
It therefore has the $h$-type indexing $X$.  The replacements may change
internal typed labels of the original typed derivation; this is harmless.  We
retain only the underlying $G$-derivation and the preserved root type, and then
Proposition~\ref{prop:output-invariants}(ii) uniquely lifts that derivation back
to $G^h$ with root $X$.  Since $X$ belongs to $\widetilde G_0$, the lifted
derivation can be inserted into a successful derivation through $X$ and hence
survives trimming.  Its root tuple may therefore be chosen as $\omega(X)$.
\end{proof}

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:untyped-contexts}
with
\(b_{\mathrm{can}} =f(k+\ell+1)n_{\mathrm{typ}} (r_{\max}+1)\bar\theta_G.\)
Every selected witness word then has length at most
$2\bar\theta_G+r_{\max}b_{\mathrm{can}}
 =O_{f,r_{\max},k,\ell,h,\Sigma}
   (\|G\|\bar\theta_G)$, since
$n_{\mathrm{typ}}\le|V||M|^f$.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial data for prefix--suffix-determined typings]
\label{thm:boundary-polydata}
Fix $f\ge1$, $r_{\max}\ge1$, $k$, $\ell$, a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, and a
finite-semigroup typing $s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$ satisfying
$s\preceq s_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$; put $h=\widehat s$.
Let $L\in\Cmcf{f}{s}$ and let $G$ be a reduced good
fan-out-$\le f$, rank-$\le r_{\max}$ grammar with
$L(G)=L\cap\Sigma^+$. Then
$\mathcal B_{f,h}^{\le r_{\max}}$ is polynomial-time in the positive sample.
A characteristic sample for $L$ is
$K=\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\cup(\{\eps\}\cap L)$, with
\[
 |\Wit(\widetilde G_0)|=O_{f,r_{\max},s}(\|G\|),
 \qquad
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+
 =O_{f,r_{\max},k,\ell,s,\Sigma}
   (\|G\|^2\bar\theta_G),
\]
where $\bar\theta_G=\max\{1,\theta_G\}$.  For a reduced $\lambda$-free input
presentation $G_{\mathrm{in}}$ (deleting rules allowed), the structural
good-form conversion of Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-good-thickness}
yields the explicit presentation-relative bound
\[
 O_{f,r_{\max},k,\ell,s,\Sigma}\!\left(
 \|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|^3\max\{1,t_{G_{\mathrm{in}}}\}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $h$-refinement has at most $|V||M|^f$ typed nonterminals and
$|P||M|^{fr_{\max}}$ typed rule instances.
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-short-canonical} applies because preserving the finer
$h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ boundary type preserves the coarser $h$-type.  Lemma~\ref{lem:untyped-contexts} now
bounds every selected witness word by
$O_{f,r_{\max},k,\ell,h,\Sigma}
(\|G\|\bar\theta_G)$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:witness-cardinality} gives
\(|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)| \le n_{\mathrm{typ}}+|P||M|^{f r_{\max}},\)
with $n_{\mathrm{typ}}\le |V||M|^f$.  Multiplying the polynomial number of
witnesses by the preceding word-length bound, and adding at most one datum for
$\eps$, proves the asserted total-positive-length bound.  The same successful
witnesses also imply a polynomial bound on the refined typed rule thickness,
but that intermediate parameter is not needed for the estimate.  Theorem~\ref{thm:poly} gives the hypothesis-construction claim.
\end{proof}

The input-presentation clause follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-good-thickness}; nonterminal thickness is
used because deleting source rules may erase material before good-form
conversion.  Corollary~\ref{cor:capped-gold-poly} then gives polynomial time
per processed datum for the corresponding capped conservative learner.

At fan-out one, this polynomial boundary-typing bound
is not new: Yoshinaka's learner for $(k,\ell)$-substitutable
context-free languages already yields polynomial
characteristic data in grammar size and
thickness~\cite[Theorem~1]{Yoshinaka2008},
and the revised companion CFG manuscript extends
the window result to finite-group
refinements~\cite{kuriyamaCFG}.
The contribution of Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-short-canonical}
is to preserve boundary information simultaneously
across \emph{multiple} tuple components, giving the
same kind of bound at every fixed fan-out $f\ge2$.

The bound is relative to a reduced good bounded-rank presentation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-ranktwo-witness} below shows that this higher-rank
regime is needed at the language level: it gives a non-context-free
$(1,1)$-typed target of minimum fan-out two and minimum rule rank two even
when competing presentations may use arbitrary finite fan-out.
Of the examples below, $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ falls under the window theorem,
whereas the rank-one languages $L_{\mathrm{cd}}$ and $L_\star$ fall under
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankone-polydata}; thus each separation is compatible with
a quantitative learning bound.


\subsection{An Algebraic Criterion for Polynomial Characteristic Data}
\label{subsec:algebraic-typed-data}

Write $\mathbf{LI}$ for the pseudovariety of finite locally trivial
semigroups and $\mathbf{Grp}$ for the pseudovariety of finite groups.
Their join $\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}$ consists of divisors of
finite products $S\times A$, with $S\in\mathbf{LI}$ and $A$ a finite group
\cite{Costa2002LI,Costa2002LIG}.  The following criterion makes
the window--group hypothesis intrinsic to the positive image of
the fixed typing.  Its finite-semigroup ingredient also occurs in
the companion CFG manuscript~\cite{kuriyamaCFG}.

\begin{lemma}[Locally trivial--group typings admit window--group refinements]
\label{lem:mcfg-li-group-window}
Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty finite alphabet and let
$s:\Sigma^+\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf S$ be a finite-semigroup typing;
put $h=\widehat s$. If $\mathsf S\in\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}$, then
there exist $k,\ell\ge0$, a finite group $A$, and a
morphism $g:\Sigma^*\to A$ such that
\[
 h\preceq (h_\Sigma^{k,\ell},g)
 \quad\text{on all of }\Sigma^*.
\]
If $\mathsf S\in\mathbf{LI}$, the group factor can
be taken trivial, so $h\preceq h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a finite locally trivial semigroup $S$, a finite group
$A$, a subsemigroup $T\subseteq S\times A$, and an onto
semigroup morphism $\pi:T\to\mathsf S$.  For each
$a\in\Sigma$ lift $s(a)$ to a pair $(s_a,g_a)\in T$.
Multiplying these pairs extends the lifts to a morphism
$\rho:\Sigma^+\to T$ with $\pi\rho=s$.
Let $\sigma:\Sigma^+\to S$ and $g:\Sigma^*\to A$ denote the two
coordinate morphisms, with $g(\eps)=1_A$.

The positive image $S_0=\sigma(\Sigma^+)$ is locally trivial.
Put $q=|S_0|+1$.  The standard finite locally trivial
identities $S_0^q=S_0E(S_0)S_0$ and
$euf=ef$ for idempotents $e,f\in S_0$ and $u\in S_0$
\cite{Pin1986,kuriyamaCFG}
imply that $\sigma(w)$ is determined by its length-$q$
prefix and suffix whenever $|w|\ge2q$:
writing $w=pxz$ with $|p|=|z|=q$,
choose $\sigma(p)=aeb$ and $\sigma(z)=cfd$; writing
$\sigma^1(\eps)=1$ in the adjoined identity monoid, we obtain
\[
 \sigma(pxz)=ae\,(b\,\sigma^1(x)\,c)\,fd=aefd=\sigma(pz).
\]
The parenthesized factor belongs to $S_0$ even if $x=\eps$,
because $b,c\in S_0$.  Words shorter than $2q$
are recorded individually by $h_\Sigma^{q,q}$.
Therefore equality of $h_\Sigma^{q,q}$-values on
$\Sigma^+$ implies equality of $\sigma$-values.  Equality of
both window and $g$ values then implies equality of
$\rho$ values and hence of $h$ values.

The window typing distinguishes $\eps$ from every nonempty
word, so the kernel inclusion extends to all of $\Sigma^*$.
Taking $k=\ell=q$ proves the first assertion.
If $\mathsf S$ itself is locally trivial, use
$S=\mathsf S$, its identity projection for $\pi$,
and the trivial group.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Algebraic sufficient conditions for polynomial data]
\label{cor:mcfg-li-group-polydata}
Fix a nonempty finite $\Sigma$, a fan-out cap $f\ge1$,
a rule-rank cap $r_{\max}\ge1$, and a finite-semigroup typing
$s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$; put $h=\widehat s$.
Let $G$ be any reduced good fan-out-$\le f$,
rank-$\le r_{\max}$ presentation of a target in $\Cmcf{f}{s}$,
and put $\bar\theta_G=\max\{1,\theta_G\}$.
The capped reconstruction operator has a characteristic sample
of cardinality $O_{f,r_{\max},h}(\|G\|)$ with:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+
 =O_{f,r_{\max},h,\Sigma}(\|G\|^2\bar\theta_G),
 &\text{if }\mathsf S\in\mathbf{LI},\\[2pt]
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+
 =O_{f,r_{\max},h,\Sigma}(\|G\|^3\bar\theta_G),
 &\text{if }\mathsf S\in\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}.
\end{array}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $\mathbf{LI}$, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:mcfg-li-group-window}
with trivial group and Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-polydata}.
For $\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}$, use the finite window--group
refinement from that lemma and
Theorem~\ref{thm:window-group-polydata}.
The parameters of the refining morphisms depend only on the fixed
typing and alphabet, not on $G$.  The bound on cardinality
holds in both cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
These are \emph{sufficient} algebraic conditions, not a
characterization of all typings admitting polynomial
original-thickness data.  The lower-bound question beyond
fan-out one in Open Problem~\ref{op:fanout-two} remains open.
The empty-alphabet case has only the word $\eps$ and is trivial.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Computing Short Characteristic Data}
\label{subsec:effective-char-data}

The bounds above are existential: each proof exhibits a short
tuple or context.  The shortest ones can be computed directly.
Call a sentence context of a typed nonterminal $X$
\emph{derivational} if it arises, as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:identity-canonical-contexts}, from a successful
typed derivation by deleting one occurrence of the subtree
rooted at $X$.  Every such context is admissible for $X$.
The contexts $E_A$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:untyped-contexts}, lifted
to $X=A_{\mathbf t}$, and those of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reusable-context-bound} are derivational.

\begin{proposition}[Effective short characteristic data]
\label{prop:effective-char-data}
Fix $f,r_{\max}$ and a finite typing $h:\Sigma^*\to M$.  Given a
reduced good MCFG $G$ of fan-out at most $f$ and rule rank at most
$r_{\max}$, the following can be computed in time polynomial in
$\|G\|$, with lengths written in binary:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item $\theta_G(R)$ for every rule $R$, and hence $\theta_G$ and $t_G$;
\item the trimmed refinement $\widetilde G_0$ and, for each non-start
typed nonterminal $X$, a canonical tuple $\omega(X)\in L_X$ of
minimum total length and a derivational context $\chi(X)$ of
minimum fixed length, with $\chi(X)=\blank_1$ whenever
$\widetilde S\to X$ belongs to $\widetilde G_0$;
\item derivation DAGs of size polynomial in $\|G\|$ representing
these tuples and contexts, and hence every word of the
witness set $\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:witness-set} built from them.
\end{enumerate}
Every word-length and total-length bound proved in this section
and in Appendices~\ref{app:window-abelian},
\ref{app:window-group} and~\ref{app:local-group} holds for this computed witness set.
Its words can be written out explicitly in time polynomial in
$\|G\|+\|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $f,r_{\max},h$ the full refinement $G^h$ has at most
$|V||M|^f$ typed nonterminals and $|P||M|^{fr_{\max}}$ typed rules,
and trimming is a linear-time productivity and reachability
computation.  View the typed rules as weighted deduction steps.
A rule $X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)$ maps child lengths $x_j$ to
$c_\rho+\sum_jx_j$, where $c_\rho$ is the number of terminals
written by $\rho$; this function is superior.  To break
zero-length ties without cyclic predecessor pointers, minimize the
lexicographic pair (terminal length, derivation-node count), adding
$(c_\rho,1)$ at each rule.  The pair-valued operation is strictly
superior to each child even when no terminal is written.
The shortest-derivation algorithm in the weighted deductive
form of Nederhof~\cite{Nederhof2003}, building on Knuth's
generalization of Dijkstra's method for the grammar problem
(see also Ramalingam and Reps~\cite{RamalingamReps1996}),
therefore computes the
minimum length $\lambda(X)=\min\{\|\tuple u\|_1:\tuple u\in L_X\}$
of every typed nonterminal together with an acyclic choice of
minimizing rules.  These choices form a derivation DAG for
$\omega(X)$, with shared subderivations.  A second Dijkstra
pass computes outside lengths, breaking equal-length ties by
the number of edges.  It
starts with $\mu(X)=0$ when $\widetilde S\to X$ is present.  For
every typed rule $X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)$ and every $j$ it adds
an edge from $X$ to $Y_j$ of weight
$c_\rho+\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda(Y_i)$.  The value $\mu(X)$ is the
minimum fixed length of a derivational context of $X$, and the
shortest-path tree supplies its DAG.  The same two passes on $G$
itself give $t_G=\max_A\lambda(A)$ and
$\theta_G(R)=\mu(A)+c_\rho+\sum_j\lambda(B_j)$ for
$R:A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_r)$.

Each proof in this section and in the cited appendices bounds a
witness word by bounding the length of its context, its rule
template, and its canonical child tuples separately.  It does so
by exhibiting some tuple in $L_X$ and some derivational context
of length within the stated bound.  The computed minima are no
longer, so every stated bound persists.  Expanding a DAG takes
time linear in the length of the word it represents.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rule-only characteristic witnesses]
\label{prop:rule-only-witnesses}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-char-data},
choose all canonical tuples by the consistent acyclic
minimum-weight derivations described there.
For each non-start typed nonterminal $X$, let
$R_X:X\to\rho_X(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)$ be the first rule in
its chosen minimizing derivation (including $q=0$).
Then
\[
 \omega(X)=\rho_X(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_q)).
\]
Consequently the witness set of
Definition~\ref{def:witness-set} equals the set of
rule-witness words alone:
\[
 \Wit(\widetilde G_0)=
 \bigl\{
 \chi(\operatorname{lhs}R)
 [\rho_R(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_q))]:
 R\in\mathcal R
 \bigr\},
\]
where $\mathcal R$ denotes the non-start typed rules
and rank-zero rules are included.
In particular,
\[
 |\Wit(\widetilde G_0)|\le|\mathcal R|
 \le |P||M|^{fr_{\max}},
\]
for a fixed typing and rule-rank cap.
The set remains characteristic for
$\mathcal B^{\le r_{\max}}_{f,h}$ whenever the
target is in $\Cmcf{f}{h}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each typed nonterminal $X$, an optimal derivation
starts with a rule $R_X$. Every child derivation must
have minimum weight: replacing a nonoptimal child by
the chosen minimum would decrease the lexicographic
weight of the whole derivation. The additional
derivation-node tie-breaker makes the simultaneous
choice of minimizing rules acyclic. Hence the displayed
identity for $\omega(X)$ holds, also for rank zero.

It follows that $\chi(X)[\omega(X)]$ is
\emph{identical} to the witness word of $R_X$.
Every other term in Definition~\ref{def:witness-set}
is already a rule witness. Thus the two sets coincide
as sets of terminal words; in particular, the
completeness and characteristic-sample arguments using
$\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$ are unchanged.
There is at most one selected word per rule instance.
The refinement bound on the number of typed rules
gives the displayed cardinality estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A computable presentation-sensitive data budget]
\label{prop:computed-witness-budget}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-char-data},
let $\mathcal R$ be the non-start typed rules
of $\widetilde G_0$.
Let $\lambda(X)$ be the minimum total yield length from $X$,
and let $\mu(X)$ be the minimum fixed length of a derivational
sentence context of $X$ (with $\mu(X)=0$ for a direct start link).
For $R:X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)$, put
\[
 b(R):=c_\rho+\sum_{j=1}^q\lambda(Y_j),
 \qquad
 \mathsf B_{\mathrm{rule}}(G,h):=
 \sum_{R\in\mathcal R}
 \bigl(1+\mu(\operatorname{lhs}R)+b(R)\bigr).
\]
There is a computable witness set $\Wit(\widetilde G_0)$ with
\[
 \|\Wit(\widetilde G_0)\|_+\le\mathsf B_{\mathrm{rule}}(G,h).
\]
The integer $\mathsf B_{\mathrm{rule}}(G,h)$ is computable in polynomial time in
$\|G\|$ when $f,r_{\max},h$ are fixed, with its value represented in
binary.  If $L(G)$ also contains the empty word and its nonempty
core is treated separately, the optional $\eps$ datum adds at most one
to the encoded budget.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the minimizing canonical tuples and derivational contexts
constructed in Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-char-data}.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:rule-only-witnesses},
every canonical-nonterminal witness is already one of
the rule witnesses. For each positive-rank or rank-zero
typed rule $R:X\to\rho(Y_1,\ldots,Y_q)$, its witness word
$\chi(X)[\rho(\omega(Y_1),\ldots,\omega(Y_q))]$
has length exactly $\mu(X)+b(R)$.
Summing the encoded lengths $|w|+1$ over this indexed
multiset of rule witnesses gives
$\mathsf B_{\mathrm{rule}}(G,h)$.
Passing to the set of distinct words can only lower
the total length.  The computations of $\lambda,\mu$,
the number of typed symbols and rules, and the binary lengths of
these quantities are polynomially bounded as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-char-data}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This \emph{rule-only} budget is presentation-sensitive and can be much smaller
than substituting worst-case maxima into the polynomial bounds.
It is an efficiently computable \emph{upper certificate}, not a
claim of a minimum-size characteristic sample or an improved
worst-case polynomial exponent.
\end{remark}

The singleton $\{\mathtt c^{2^n}\}$ after
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankone-polydata} shows that explicit
characteristic words can be exponentially longer than $\|G\|$.
In that case the derivation DAGs of
Proposition~\ref{prop:effective-char-data} remain of polynomial
size, so the computed sample has a polynomial-size compressed
description.


\subsection{Distributional Representatives and Abelian-Filtered Targets}
\label{subsec:short-distributional-representatives}

The computable rule-only budget above uses representatives
\emph{generated by} the typed grammar.
For an existential characteristic-sample bound there is a
different option: choose short representatives of the same
\emph{typed distribution classes}, even when the representatives
themselves have no derivation from the corresponding nonterminal.

\begin{proposition}[Relaxed distributional witnesses]
\label{prop:mcfg-relaxed-witnesses}
Let $G$ be a reduced good fan-out-$\le f$,
rank-$\le r$ presentation of
$L=L(G)\in\Cmcf{f}{h}$, and let
$\widetilde G_0$ be its trimmed yield-type refinement.
For every non-start typed nonterminal $X$, let $[X]$
be the $\equiv_{L,h}$-class containing $L_X$, and put
\[
 \kappa_G(X)=\min_{\tuple v\in[X]}\|\tuple v\|_1,
 \qquad \kappa_G=\max_X\kappa_G(X).
\]
There exists a characteristic positive sample $K^\dagger$
for both the capped operator $\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,h}$
(with cap at least the rank of $G$) and the uncapped
$\mathcal B_{f,h}$ satisfying
\[
 \begin{split}
 |K^\dagger|&\le |V||M|^f+|P||M|^{fr},\\
 \|K^\dagger\|_+
 &\le (|V||M|^f+|P||M|^{fr})
 \bigl(1+\theta_G+\|G\|+(r+1)\kappa_G\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
The bound concerns the nonempty core; if $\eps\in L$,
add the sample word $\eps$ at encoded cost one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each $X=A_{\mathbf t}$ choose a tuple
$\omega^\dagger(X)\in[X]$ of total length
$\kappa_G(X)$.  Choose a derivational sentence context
$\chi^\dagger(X)$ for the \emph{untyped} $A$ using a
shortest successful derivation containing an occurrence
of $A$, with all sibling yields fixed.  The fixed
material of this context is at most $\theta_G$,
since any such successful derivation witnesses some
rule involving $A$; if $\widetilde S\to X$ is a
start link, use $\blank_1$ instead.
This context accepts a tuple $u\in L_X$.
As $\omega^\dagger(X)\equiv_{L,h}u$, it also
accepts $\omega^\dagger(X)$.

Form $K^\dagger$ from all words
$\chi^\dagger(X)[\omega^\dagger(X)]$,
all rule words
$\chi^\dagger(X)[\rho(\omega^\dagger(Y_1),\ldots,
\omega^\dagger(Y_q))]$, and all rank-zero
rule witnesses.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:witnessed-composition}, the rule
tuple in the second family is distributionally
equivalent to a tuple in $L_X$, so every listed
word belongs to $L$.
The proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:completeness} now works with
$\iota(X)=[\omega^\dagger(X)]$: the chosen contexts
supply the Link instances, while the rule words
expose the required composition witnesses.
Proposition~\ref{prop:reconstruction-soundness}
gives the reverse inclusion for arbitrary larger
positive samples.

There is one word per typed nonterminal and one per
typed rule before deduplication.  A nonterminal
witness has length at most $\theta_G+\kappa_G$,
and a rule witness at most
$\theta_G+\|G\|+r\kappa_G$.
Summing the encoded costs gives the bound.
Unlike Proposition~\ref{prop:rule-only-witnesses},
these representatives need not be generated by
their nonterminals, so the nonterminal words
cannot in general be omitted.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For fixed $f,r,h$, the preceding bound implies that
any family $G_n$ with
$\|G_n\|\max\{1,\theta_{G_n}\}=n^{O(1)}$
and superpolynomial characteristic-data requirements
must satisfy $\kappa_{G_n}=n^{\omega(1)}$.
This is a necessary condition, not a proof that such a
family exists.  The relaxed construction is existential;
the rule-only algorithm of
Proposition~\ref{prop:computed-witness-budget} is a
distinct, effective construction using grammar-generated
representatives.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial data for abelian-filtered targets]
\label{thm:mcfg-abelian-filter-data}
Fix a finite nonempty alphabet $\Sigma$, a finite
semigroup typing $s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$ and bounds $f,r\ge1$.
Put $h=\widehat s$ and $M=\mathsf S^\sharp$.
There are constants $C,e$ depending only on
$\Sigma,s,f,r$ with the following property.
Let $\mathbb A$ be any abelian group,
$\varphi:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb A$ a morphism,
$\mathcal H\le\mathbb A$, $\gamma\in\mathbb A$,
and $\mathsf{Acc}\subseteq\mathsf S^\sharp$.
If
\[
 L=h^{-1}(\mathsf{Acc})\cap
   \varphi^{-1}(\gamma+\mathcal H)
\]
has a reduced good fan-out-$\le f$, rank-$\le r$
MCFG presentation $G$ of $L\cap\Sigma^+$, then
$L\in\Cmcf{f}{s}$ and admits a characteristic sample
for $\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,h}$ with
\[
 \|K^\dagger\|_+\le
 C\bigl(\|G\|\max\{1,\theta_G\}\bigr)^e.
\]
The constants do not depend on
$\mathbb A,\varphi,\mathcal H,\gamma$.
The datum $\eps$ is included when $\eps\in L$;
the degenerate cases $L\subseteq\{\eps\}$ are handled directly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership in $\Cmcf{f}{s}$ follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:abelian-coset-filter}; the
positive part has the assumed MCFG presentation,
and $\eps$ is admitted separately by the Start rule.

Let $\psi:\Sigma^*\to\mathbb N^\Sigma$ be the
Parikh map and write
$\Psi(\tuple u)=\sum_i\psi(u_i)$.
The morphism $\varphi$ factors through a group
homomorphism $\overline\varphi:\mathbb Z^\Sigma\to\mathbb A$.
Put
\[
 \Lambda=\{z\in\mathbb Z^\Sigma:
                 \overline\varphi(z)\in\mathcal H\}.
\]
Fix for each untyped nonterminal $A$ a productive
tuple $\omega_A$ of length at most the
untyped nonterminal thickness $t_G\le\theta_G$.
Since any two $A$-tuples can be inserted into the
same successful derivational context and their
resulting words belong to $L$,
\[
 \Psi(u)-\Psi(\omega_A)\in\Lambda
 \qquad(u\in L_A(G)).                                      \tag{*}
\]
For each rule $R:A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_q)$,
let $c_R\in\mathbb N^\Sigma$ count its explicit
terminal letters and define
\[
 \delta_R=\Psi(\omega_A)-c_R
                 -\sum_{j=1}^q\Psi(\omega_{B_j}).
\]
By (*) this vector lies in $\Lambda$, and its
$\ell_1$-norm is bounded by
$T=(r+1)t_G+\|G\|$.
Let
$\Gamma_T=\{z\in\Lambda:\|z\|_1\le T\}$ and
$\Lambda_T=\langle\Gamma_T\rangle$.
Induction on a derivation, using
\[
 \Psi(u)-\Psi(\omega_A)=
   \sum_j(\Psi(u_j)-\Psi(\omega_{B_j}))-\delta_R,
\]
shows that every $u\in L_A(G)$ satisfies
$\Psi(u)-\Psi(\omega_A)\in\Lambda_T$.

Consider now any typed nonterminal
$X=A_{\mathbf t}$ with $d\le f$, and fix
$u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\in L_X$.
For each component choose one linear set from
a fixed semilinear representation of the Parikh
image of the regular language
$h^{-1}(t_i)\cap\Sigma^+$ containing $\psi(u_i)$.
Write that linear set as
$c_i+\sum_{p\in P_i}\mathbb N p$.
The number and coefficients of all these
generators are bounded by constants depending
only on $\Sigma$ and $h$.
The nonnegative integer system
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^d
    \left(c_i+\sum_{p\in P_i}\nu_{i,p}p\right)
 -
 \sum_{z\in\Gamma_T}(\mu_z^+-\mu_z^-)z
 =\Psi(\omega_A)
\]
is feasible: use the Parikh vectors of the
original $u_i$ and the preceding
$\Lambda_T$-membership.
It has exactly $|\Sigma|$ equations,
at most $O_{f,h,\Sigma}((T+1)^{|\Sigma|})$
nonnegative variables, and coefficients
of absolute value $O_{f,h,\Sigma}(T+1)$.
Papadimitriou's small nonnegative integral
solution bound~\cite{Papadimitriou1981} therefore
gives a solution with every coefficient
bounded by a fixed polynomial in $T+1$.
For each resulting Parikh vector select a
nonempty word $v_i\in h^{-1}(t_i)$ realizing it,
and let $\tuple v=(v_1,\ldots,v_d)$.
Then $\|\tuple v\|_1$ is polynomial in $T+1$,
the componentwise $h$-types agree with $u$,
and
$\Psi(\tuple v)-\Psi(u)\in\Lambda_T\subseteq\Lambda$.

Finally, for any sentence context $E$, the total
$h$-type of $E[\tuple v]$ agrees with that of
$E[u]$, and
\[
 \varphi(E[\tuple v])-\varphi(E[u])
 =\overline\varphi(\Psi(\tuple v)-\Psi(u))
 \in\mathcal H.
\]
Hence $\tuple v\equiv_{L,h}\tuple u$.
This constructs a representative of every
typed distribution class with length polynomial
in $T+1$.  Applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:mcfg-relaxed-witnesses}
and $t_G\le\theta_G$ yields the claimed
total-length bound, with parameters independent
of the auxiliary abelian group.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mcfg-abelian-filter-data} applies
even when the positive image of $h$ is
\emph{outside} $\mathbf{LG}$.
It is a sufficient condition on the target
language, rather than on the finite control
monoid.  It does not claim polynomial-time
construction for arbitrary, possibly
ineffectively presented auxiliary abelian groups.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparison with Multidimensional Substitutability}
\label{sec:comparison}

Yoshinaka's multidimensional substitutability is the direct positive-data
baseline~\cite{Yoshinaka2009,Yoshinaka2011}.  Under trivial typing the present
framework reproduces that semantic class; finite typing refines which observed
tuples may be identified while preserving compositional rule witnesses.
The MCFG proofs additionally require componentwise type propagation and, for
the quantitative boundary theorem, simultaneous control of several tuple
components.  Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$ hierarchy is a separate CFG result
\cite{Yoshinaka2008}; $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ lifts the same boundary
information componentwise.  Since every $(f,h)$ condition includes arity one,
\(\Cmcf{f}{h}\cap\mathrm{CFL}\subseteq\Cmcf{1}{h}.\)
The boundary-typed example below exhibits intrinsic rule rank two.

\subsection{Yoshinaka's Baseline and Prefix--Suffix Specialization}

\begin{definition}[Yoshinaka multidimensional substitutability]
\label{def:yoshinaka-pd-substitutability}
Using the sentence-context sets $\mathcal E_d$ defined in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, a language $L$ is \emph{$f$D-substitutable} if,
for every $1\le d\le f$, all $E,F\in\mathcal E_d$, and all
$\tuple x,\tuple y\in(\Sigma^+)^d$,
\(E[\tuple x],\quad E[\tuple y],\quad F[\tuple x]\in L \Longrightarrow F[\tuple y]\in L.\)
Write $\YSub{f}$ for this semantic class and
$\YSL{f}{r}:=\YSub{f}\cap\mathsf{MCFL}(f,r)$, where $r$ bounds rule rank.
We refer to the displayed four-instance implication as the
\emph{substitution implication}.
Yoshinaka's original definition quantifies over every arity $d\le f$, uses
nonempty tuple components, and requires nonempty internal separators in
$d$-contexts; footnote~3 separately discusses the stronger alternative that
permits empty components and separators
\cite[Section~3.1 and footnote~3]{Yoshinaka2011}.
\end{definition}

Recall the finite monoid morphism $h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$ from
Definition~\ref{def:prefix-suffix-typing}.  In Yoshinaka's word-level
definition, $w_1,y_1,w_1',w_2,y_2,w_2'\in\Sigma^*$,
$u\in\Sigma^k$, and $v\in\Sigma^\ell$, with
$uy_1v,uy_2v\ne\eps$; the condition is
\[
 w_1uy_1vw_1',\ w_1uy_2vw_1'\in L
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(w_2uy_1vw_2'\in L\iff w_2uy_2vw_2'\in L\bigr)
\]
\cite{Yoshinaka2008}.  Thus the compared fragments $uy_iv$ are nonempty even
though the middle words $y_i$ themselves may be empty.  Equality of their
$h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}$-values is exactly equality of the boundary information
used in this condition, including the exact identity of words shorter than
$m_0=\max\{1,k+\ell\}$.

\paragraph{Specializations of the semantic condition.}
The trivial typing removes the componentwise
type premise, so
\(\Cmcf{f}{h_{\mathrm{triv}}} =\YSub{f}\cap f\text{-}\mathrm{MCFL} \subseteq\Cmcf{f}{h}.\)
At fan-out one,
$(1,h_\Sigma^{k,\ell})$-substitutability
is precisely Yoshinaka's word-level
$(k,\ell)$ condition~\cite{Yoshinaka2008}:
equal window types mean identical short words
or common prefixes and suffixes of lengths
$k$ and $\ell$, respectively.
The same correspondence applies
componentwise at larger fan-out.

\paragraph{Compatibility with Yoshinaka's efficiency scale.}
For trivial typing no productive nonterminal
has more than one yield type. Yoshinaka's
untyped grammar witnesses already satisfy
$|K_G|\le|P|$ and
$\|K_G\|\le|P|\tau_G$
\cite[Lemma~7]{Yoshinaka2011}, with polynomial
time and data for fixed dimension and rank
\cite[Theorem~1]{Yoshinaka2011}.
Thus the present quantitative claims concern
new typed, multi-component settings rather
than re-proving the trivial-typing result.

\paragraph{A boundary-typing fact used in the separation.}
A language is \emph{strictly 2-local} if membership is determined by
permitted adjacent pairs in $\vdash w\dashv$, for fixed endmarkers.
The following elementary fact is needed in the intrinsic-rank example.

\begin{lemma}[Strictly 2-local constraints are boundary typed]
\label{lem:sl2-boundary-control}
If $L_{\mathrm{sl}}\subseteq\Sigma^*$ is strictly 2-local, then for every $f\ge1$,
\(L_{\mathrm{sl}}\in\Cmcf{f}{h_\Sigma^{1,1}}.\)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let equal-$h_\Sigma^{1,1}$-type nonempty tuples $\tuple x,\tuple y$ share an
accepting context $E_0$.  Internal adjacent pairs of each $y_i$ are permitted
because they occur in $E_0[\tuple y]$.  If $E[\tuple x]\in L_{\mathrm{sl}}$,
every adjacent pair of $E[\tuple y]$ is either in fixed material, internal to
a $y_i$, or crosses a component boundary.  These are permitted respectively
by $E[\tuple x]$, by $E_0[\tuple y]$, or by equality of the first/last letters
encoded by the boundary type; end-marker pairs are unchanged.  Thus
$E[\tuple y]\in L_{\mathrm{sl}}$, and symmetry finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An Intrinsically Rank-Two Boundary-Typed Target}
\label{subsec:intrinsic-ranktwo}

The next example makes the distinction between the rank-one and higher-rank
quantitative regimes intrinsic to the language.  Its rule-rank obstruction is
not created by finite typing: the balanced-count factor used below already
belongs to Yoshinaka's trivial-typing class.  The construction instead combines
that obstruction with minimum fan-out two and a separation from the
trivial-typing class inside one boundary-typed target.

Let
\[
 \Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}
 :=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b,\mathtt l,\mathtt r,\mathtt p,\mathtt q,
       \mathtt c,\mathtt d,\mathtt{\#},\mathtt{\$},
       \mathtt u,\mathtt v,\mathtt w\}
\]
and put
\[
 P_{\mathrm{intr}}
 :=\{\mathtt{lpc},\mathtt{lpd},\mathtt{lqc},
       \mathtt{rpc},\mathtt{rpd}\}.
\]
Define
\[
 \mathrm{MIX}_2^+
 :=\{x\in\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^+:
       |x|_{\mathtt a}=|x|_{\mathtt b}\},
 \qquad
 T_3^+
 :=\{\mathtt u^n\mathtt v^n\mathtt w^n:n\ge1\},
\]
and
\[
 L_{\mathrm{intr}}
 :=P_{\mathrm{intr}}\,\mathtt{\#}\,\mathrm{MIX}_2^+\,
   \mathtt{\$}\,T_3^+.
\]

To make the role of the witness explicit, let
\[
 \varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}:\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^*\to\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 \varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathtt a)=1,\quad
 \varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathtt b)=-1.
\]
Then
\(\mathrm{MIX}_2^+ =\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^+\cap\varphi_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}(0).\)
The language $\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^+$ is trivially
$f$-tuple-substitutable, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:abelian-coset-filter}(ii) gives
$\mathrm{MIX}_2^+\in\YSub{f}$ for every $f\ge1$; the rank-two CFG for $B$
displayed below also shows that this ingredient is context-free.  Thus the
intrinsic rank-two phenomenon used here already occurs inside the
trivial-typing baseline.  The role of $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ is to combine that
known rule-rank obstruction with non-context-freeness, minimum fan-out two,
and a separation from $\YSub{f}$ in a single finitely typed target.

\begin{theorem}[A non-context-free intrinsic-rank-two typed witness]
\label{thm:intrinsic-ranktwo-witness}
The language $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ satisfies
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}}\in\mathsf{MCFL}(2,2)\setminus\mathrm{CFL},\)
and, for every $f\ge2$,
\[
 L_{\mathrm{intr}}
 \in\Cmcf{f}{h_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}}^{1,1}}
 \setminus\YSub{f}.
\]
Moreover, for every finite $f'\ge1$,
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}}\notin\mathsf{MCFL}(f',1).\)
Consequently the minimum fan-out of $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ is exactly two, and
its minimum rule rank among all finite-fan-out MCFG presentations is exactly
two.  In particular, Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary-polydata} applies to a
non-context-free language that cannot be covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankone-polydata} at any finite fan-out.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First we give a reduced good fan-out-two grammar of maximum rule rank two.
Let $B$ generate the nonempty balanced-count language by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B\to{}&
 \mathtt a B\mathtt b B
 \mid \mathtt b B\mathtt a B
 \mid \mathtt a B\mathtt b
 \mid \mathtt a\mathtt b B
 \mid \mathtt a\mathtt b\\
 &{}\mid
 \mathtt b B\mathtt a
 \mid \mathtt b\mathtt a B
 \mid \mathtt b\mathtt a .
\end{aligned}
\]
The usual first-return induction on
$|x|_{\mathtt a}-|x|_{\mathtt b}$ gives
$L_B=\mathrm{MIX}_2^+$.

Let the fan-out-two nonterminal $A$ have rules
\(A\to(\mathtt u,\mathtt w) \mid (\mathtt u\xi_1^1\mathtt v,\mathtt w\xi_1^2)(A).\)
It derives exactly the tuples
\(\{(\mathtt u^n\mathtt v^{n-1},\mathtt w^n):n\ge1\}.\)
For every $\alpha\in P_{\mathrm{intr}}$, add the start rule
\(S\to \alpha\mathtt{\#}\xi_1^1\mathtt{\$} \xi_2^1\mathtt v\xi_2^2(B,A).\)
The resulting grammar generates exactly $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$.  It is reduced,
$\lambda$-free, linear, nondeleting, and nonpermuting.  It is also nonmerging:
the two components of $A$ are separated by the literal terminal $\mathtt v$
in every start rule.  Hence it is a good fan-out-two grammar of maximum rule
rank two.

The language is not context-free.  Intersect it with the regular language
\(R:= \mathtt{lpc\#ab\$}\, \mathtt u^+\mathtt v^+\mathtt w^+ .\)
Then
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}}\cap R = \{\mathtt{lpc\#ab\$}\mathtt u^n\mathtt v^n\mathtt w^n:n\ge1\}.\)
A homomorphism erasing the fixed prefix and fixing
$\mathtt u,\mathtt v,\mathtt w$ maps this language onto
$\{\mathtt u^n\mathtt v^n\mathtt w^n:n\ge1\}$, which is not context-free.
Closure of context-free languages under intersection with regular languages
and homomorphism therefore shows that $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ is not
context-free.  Since fan-out-one MCFGs are exactly context-free grammars in
the present syntax, while the displayed grammar has fan-out two, the minimum
fan-out is exactly two.

For typed membership, let
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{intr}}
 :=P_{\mathrm{intr}}\,\mathtt{\#}\,\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^+\,
   \mathtt{\$}\,\mathtt u^+\mathtt v^+\mathtt w^+ .
\]
This language is strictly $2$-local.  One complete set of allowed adjacent
pairs, including end markers, is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \vdash\mathtt l,\ \vdash\mathtt r,\quad
 \mathtt{lp},\ \mathtt{lq},\ \mathtt{rp},\quad
 \mathtt{pc},\ \mathtt{pd},\ \mathtt{qc},\\
 \mathtt{c\#},\ \mathtt{d\#},\quad
 \mathtt{\#a},\ \mathtt{\#b},\quad
 \mathtt{aa},\ \mathtt{ab},\ \mathtt{ba},\ \mathtt{bb},\\
 \mathtt{a\$},\ \mathtt{b\$},\quad
 \mathtt{\$u},\quad
 \mathtt{uu},\ \mathtt{uv},\ \mathtt{vv},\ \mathtt{vw},\
 \mathtt{ww},\quad
 \mathtt w\dashv .
\end{gathered}
\]
Define the homomorphism
\(\Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}:\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}^*\to\mathbb Z^3\)
by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathtt a)&=(1,0,0),&
 \Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathtt b)&=(-1,0,0),\\
 \Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathtt u)&=(0,1,0),&
 \Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathtt v)&=(0,-1,1),&
 \Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}(\mathtt w)&=(0,0,-1),
\end{aligned}
\]
and let every other terminal have image $(0,0,0)$.  Then
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}} =Q_{\mathrm{intr}}\cap\Phi_{\mathrm{intr}}^{-1}(0).\)
Indeed, the three coordinates impose respectively
\[
 |x|_{\mathtt a}=|x|_{\mathtt b},\qquad
 |x|_{\mathtt u}=|x|_{\mathtt v},\qquad
 |x|_{\mathtt v}=|x|_{\mathtt w}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sl2-boundary-control} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:abelian-coset-filter}(ii) show that the displayed
intersection is $(f,h_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}}^{1,1})$-tuple-substitutable
for every $f\ge1$.  Together with the fan-out-two grammar above, this gives
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}} \in\Cmcf{f}{h_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}}^{1,1}} \qquad\text{for every }f\ge2.\)

The language is outside Yoshinaka's trivial-typing class already at arity one.
Take
\(x:=\mathtt p,\qquad y:=\mathtt q,\)
and one-hole contexts
\(E:=\mathtt l\blank_1\mathtt{c\#ab\$uvw}, \qquad F:=\mathtt r\blank_1\mathtt{d\#ab\$uvw}.\)
Then
\(E[x],\ E[y],\ F[x]\in L_{\mathrm{intr}}, \qquad F[y]\notin L_{\mathrm{intr}}.\)
Thus the arity-one substitution implication fails, so
$L_{\mathrm{intr}}\notin\YSub{f}$ for every $f\ge1$.  Notice again that the
boundary typing distinguishes the offending one-letter factors:
\(h_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}}^{1,1}(\mathtt p) \ne h_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}}^{1,1}(\mathtt q).\)

It remains to prove intrinsic rule rank.  Put
\(\mathrm{MIX}_2 :=\{x\in\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}^*: |x|_{\mathtt a}=|x|_{\mathtt b}\}.\)
Gallot calls an MCFG \emph{non-branching} when every positive-rank rule has a
single child nonterminal; in the present terminology this is exactly rule
rank at most one, with no globally fixed fan-out bound
\cite[Definition~38]{Gallot2021}.  Since every finite grammar has a finite
maximum nonterminal arity, Gallot's non-branching MCFL class is
\(\bigcup_{f'<\infty}\mathsf{MCFL}(f',1).\)
Gallot's Theorem~29 identifies this class with the finite-index EDT0L
languages, and Theorem~31 proves that
$\mathrm{MIX}_2$ is not finite-index EDT0L.
Definition~38 explicitly permits a child variable to
occur at most once, and Theorem~28 confirms that
non-permuting non-erasing presentations generate
the same non-branching class; thus the use of
erasing terminal homomorphisms below stays within
the formalism of the cited lower bound.  Equivalently,
$\mathrm{MIX}_2$ is not generated by any non-branching MCFG; Gallot's
Propositions~5--6 give the underlying bounded-$k$-derivation contradiction
\cite[Theorem~29, Theorem~31, Propositions~5--6]{Gallot2021}.

Bishop et al. later give a peer-reviewed expansion of the same obstruction in
the closely corresponding R-MCFG formalism.  Their Definition~6.1 uses
\emph{rank} for nonterminal arity (the present fan-out), while
\emph{non-branching} still means that a propagating rule has a single child
nonterminal.  Lemma~6.8 supplies the normal form; Definition~7.1 identifies
the same balanced language, and Section~7 proves the obstruction, culminating
in the stronger non-EDT0L statement of Theorem~C
\cite[Definition~6.1, Lemma~6.8, Definition~7.1, Section~7,
Theorem~C]{BishopEtAl2026EDT0L}.  This is corroborating evidence; Gallot's
Theorems~29 and~31 remain the direct implication used here.

Now let $\pi$ fix $\mathtt a,\mathtt b$ and erase every other terminal in
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{intr}}$.  Then
\(\pi(L_{\mathrm{intr}})=\mathrm{MIX}_2^+.\)
If $L_{\mathrm{intr}}$ had a fan-out-$f'$ rule-rank-one presentation,
applying $\pi$ to the terminal material in its rule templates would preserve
fan-out and rule rank and would give such a presentation of
$\mathrm{MIX}_2^+$.  Empty terminal pieces created by erasure are permitted
in the standard MCFG formalism.  Adding a fresh start symbol with one unary
identity link to the old start and one rank-zero rule generating $\eps$ would
then give a fan-out-$f'$ rule-rank-one presentation of $\mathrm{MIX}_2$,
contradicting Gallot's non-branching lower bound.  Hence
\(L_{\mathrm{intr}}\notin\mathsf{MCFL}(f',1) \qquad\text{for every finite }f'\ge1.\)
Together with the displayed rank-two grammar, this proves the claimed
intrinsic rule rank.
\end{proof}

The construction is deliberately modular: the $\mathrm{MIX}_2^+$ factor
carries the obstruction to rule rank one, while the $T_3^+$ factor forces the
target outside CFL and hence to minimum fan-out two.  Thus the theorem
establishes the coexistence of the two lower bounds in a single finitely typed
target; the two bounds arise from separate factors rather than from an
irreducible interaction between the resources.

The non-branching lower bound itself is prior work.  What is specific to the
present framework is that a strictly local wrapper and the $(1,1)$ boundary
typing place this target inside the typed class, while the displayed four-instance substitution implication keeps it outside Yoshinaka's trivial-typing baseline.
The wrapper retains the prior non-branching obstruction, so the same target
also has intrinsic rule rank two across all finite fan-outs.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We specialize Yoshinaka's positive-data signature framework
\cite{Yoshinaka2015General} to fixed finite-semigroup typing.
At bounded rule rank the qualitative reconstruction and
polynomial-time updates are consequences of that framework,
via the explicit substructure-grammar correspondence in
Lemma~\ref{lem:substp}.  The uncapped-rank case retains a
separate finite-sample argument.  These qualitative facts
do not determine the total length of characteristic data.

For total positive-data length, rank-one target rules admit a
grammar-size polynomial for every fixed typing.  At higher rank,
boundary windows give a quadratic bound in grammar size and
original thickness, while windows combined with arbitrary finite
groups give a cubic bound.  The local-group theorem
(Appendix~\ref{app:local-group}) extends the latter to every
typing with positive-image semigroup in $\mathbf{LG}$.
The corresponding pseudovariety-level obstruction uses
fan-out-one lower-bound examples; it is not a per-typing
characterization at arbitrary fan-out.

A complementary language-based result,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mcfg-abelian-filter-data},
establishes polynomial characteristic-data length for
abelian-filtered targets with bounded-rank good presentations,
including when the finite control is outside $\mathbf{LG}$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:mcfg-relaxed-witnesses} isolates short
representatives of target distribution classes as a sufficient
condition; Proposition~\ref{prop:computed-witness-budget}
instead gives a computable, presentation-sensitive rule-only
certificate using grammar-generated representatives.
These two constructions have different hypotheses and should
not be conflated.

The boundary-typed non-context-free example has intrinsic
fan-out and rule rank two (Theorem~\ref{thm:intrinsic-ranktwo-witness}).
Full yield-type refinement and empty-component elimination
can also increase thickness exponentially
(Appendix~\ref{app:normalization-details}).

\begin{openproblem}[Original-thickness bounds beyond fan-out one]
\label{op:fanout-two}
Fix $f\ge2$ and a rule-rank bound $r\ge2$.
Does there exist a fixed finite-semigroup typing $s$ and a family of
reduced good fan-out-$\le f$, rank-$\le r$ grammars $G_n$
of targets in $\Cmcf{f}{s}$ for which
$\|G_n\|\max\{1,\theta_{G_n}\}$ grows polynomially
in $n$ but every characteristic sample for
$\mathcal B^{\le r}_{f,s}$ has superpolynomial
total positive length?  The fan-out-one counterexample of the companion
CFG paper~\cite{kuriyamaCFG} does not by itself
answer this higher-arity question:
membership in $\Cmcf{1}{s}$ need not imply
membership in $\Cmcf{f}{s}$ for $f\ge2$.
\end{openproblem}

\section{Details of the Good Normalization}
\label{app:normalization-details}

The following bookkeeping records the fixed-parameter bounds used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}; semantic correctness of the
nondeleting and nonmerging conversions is standard
\cite{SekiEtAl1991,Kanazawa2019Ogden,Yoshinaka2011}.

\paragraph{Nondeleting conversion and component permutations.}
After trimming, implement the nondeleting construction (f3)
in the proof of Seki et al.'s Lemma~2.2
\cite[proof of Lemma~2.2, item~(f3)]{SekiEtAl1991}
by retaining nonempty subsets $I$ of output components.
For $R:A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_q)$ and such $I$, let $J_j$
comprise exactly the child-$j$ components used by the
$I$-indexed outputs.  Keep those outputs, reindex their
variables, use $B_j^{J_j}$ for $J_j\ne\emptyset$, and omit
a child for $J_j=\emptyset$.  The new rule is nondeleting,
and omitting children cannot increase rank.
For reduced input, every omitted child is productive,
so the projected rule can be lifted to a source rule:
$L_{A^I}=\pi_I(L_A)$.  Without prior trimming only
$\pi_I(L_A)\subseteq L_{A^I}$ is automatic.
This also establishes the quantitative bound directly:
there are $O_f(1)$ projected copies of each nonterminal
and $O_{f,r_{\max}}(1)$ copies of each rule.
Kanazawa's existence statement
\cite[Section~2.1]{Kanazawa2019Ogden} does not
by itself establish these size or thickness bounds.  For nonpermutation, decorate a fan-out-$d$
nonterminal by $\sigma\in\mathfrak S_d$.  The parent permutation determines
the child permutations, so there are at most $f!$ copies, no child is
duplicated, and rank is preserved.  Denote the result by $G_{\mathrm{nd}}$.

\paragraph{Removing empty tuple components.}
For a fan-out-$d$ nonterminal $A$, let
\[
 \mathsf{Pat}(A):=
 \{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}:\text{$I$ is the nonempty-component pattern of
 some }\tuple w\in L_A(G_{\mathrm{nd}})\}.
\]
Finite saturation computes these sets.  For realizable child patterns, replace
variables from absent components by $\eps$, delete empty parent components,
and omit all-empty children.  There are at most $2^f$ patterns per nonterminal
and $2^{fr_{\max}}$ choices per rule.  Introduce $A^I$ for each nonempty
pattern $I$.  Retained variables still occur once and in order, so
nondeletion/nonpermutation, fan-out, and rank are preserved and the result is
$\lambda$-free.

Induction on derivations gives
$L_{A^I}$ exactly as the projection of source tuples having pattern $I$;
all-empty omitted children can be restored by terminal derivations realizing their all-empty component pattern.  Since
the start symbol has fan-out one, $S^{\{1\}}$ generates precisely the nonempty
part of the original language.  Projection shortens templates, giving
$O_{f,r_{\max}}(\|G_{\mathrm{nd}}\|)$ size.

\paragraph{Nonmerging.}
Apply Yoshinaka's nonmerging conversion
\cite[Section~2.3, Lemma~1]{Yoshinaka2011}.  For the construction in the proof of the cited lemma,
a merge-decorated nonterminal generates the \emph{image}
of its preceding tuple language under consecutive-component
concatenation.  The merge need not be injective: this
image equality, not a claim about unique preimages,
is the fact used in Proposition~\ref{prop:structural-good-thickness}.
The construction preserves $\lambda$-freeness,
nonpermutation, fan-out, and rule rank.
For fixed fan-out and rank Yoshinaka's combined good-form conversion has
$O(\|G\|)$ size~\cite[Section~2.3, after Example~2]{Yoshinaka2011}; trimming
can only decrease all bounds.  This proves
Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}.

\subsection*{Thickness transfer for $\lambda$-free presentations}

\begin{proposition}[Thickness under good-form conversion]
\label{prop:structural-good-thickness}
Fix $f$ and $r_{\max}$.  Let $G_{\mathrm{in}}$ be a reduced $\lambda$-free
$f$-MCFG of maximum rule rank at most $r_{\max}$, possibly with deleting
rules, and suppose $L(G_{\mathrm{in}})\cap\Sigma^+\neq\emptyset$.  The good
normalization of Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization} yields an equivalent
reduced good grammar $G$ with the same fan-out and rule-rank bounds such that
\[
  \|G\|=O_{f,r_{\max}}(\|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|),\qquad
  t_G\le t_{G_{\mathrm{in}}},\qquad
  \theta_G=O_{f,r_{\max}}\!\bigl(
     \|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|\max\{1,t_{G_{\mathrm{in}}}\}\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We track the generated tuple languages through the normalization.
For $\tuple w=(w_1,\ldots,w_d)$ and nonempty $I\subseteq[d]$, let
$\pi_I(\tuple w)$ retain the components indexed by $I$ in increasing
order.  Write $\sigma\tuple w$ for a component permutation and
$\mu(\tuple w)$ for concatenation of consecutive blocks of components.
Then
\[
 \|\pi_I(\tuple w)\|_1\le\|\tuple w\|_1,\qquad
 \|\sigma\tuple w\|_1=\|\mu(\tuple w)\|_1=\|\tuple w\|_1.
\]
Because $G_{\mathrm{in}}$ is $\lambda$-free, every component
of every derived tuple is nonempty, by induction on derivations.

\emph{Nondeletion.}
For each nonterminal $A$ and nonempty $I\subseteq[\fan(A)]$,
introduce $A^I$.  Given
$R:A\to\rho(B_1,\ldots,B_q)$, let $J_j$ collect precisely
the indices of the components of $B_j$ occurring in the
$I$-indexed output components of $\rho$.  Keep those outputs,
rename their variables and use $B_j^{J_j}$ for $J_j\ne\emptyset$;
omit children with $J_j=\emptyset$.  This produces a nondeleting
rule with no larger fan-out or rank and with nonempty outputs.
For arbitrary derived child tuples the identity
\[
 \pi_I\bigl(\rho(\tuple v_1,\ldots,\tuple v_q)\bigr)
 =\rho^I\bigl((\pi_{J_j}\tuple v_j)_{J_j\ne\emptyset}\bigr)
\]
holds.  Induction on derivations gives
\[
 L_{A^I}=\pi_I\bigl(L_A(G_{\mathrm{in}})\bigr).
 \tag{*}
\]
For the reverse inclusion, lift all retained child tuples
using the induction hypothesis and supply a terminal derivation
of each omitted child.  This is possible because the source
grammar was reduced.  Thus deleting an entirely unused child
does not change the projected tuple language.

\emph{Nonpermutation and empty components.}
For each $\sigma\in\mathfrak S_{\fan(A)}$, decorate $A$
by $\sigma$.  The order in which a permuted parent template
uses each child's variables determines the permutation of
that child, since the grammar is linear and nondeleting.
An induction on derivations identifies the new tuple
language with $\sigma L_A$, preserving total length,
$\lambda$-freeness, fan-out and rank.  Since all derived
components are nonempty, the later empty-component-pattern
elimination is the identity on this grammar.

\emph{Nonmerging.}
Use the construction in the proof of
\cite[Section~2.3, Lemma~1]{Yoshinaka2011}.
Decorate each nonterminal with a composition $\mu$ of
its fan-out, representing concatenation of consecutive
blocks.  In the merged parent template, maximal adjacent
runs of consecutive variables of a child determine that
child's merge $\mu_j$.  Nonpermutation makes these runs
compatible with the child's component order, while
nondeletion ensures that they cover all its components.
The resulting nonmerging template satisfies
\[
 \rho^\mu\bigl(\mu_1(\tuple v_1),\ldots,
                \mu_q(\tuple v_q)\bigr)
   =\mu\bigl(\rho(\tuple v_1,\ldots,\tuple v_q)\bigr).
\]
Induction gives the \emph{image equality} for every
merge-decorated nonterminal.  No inverse-image claim
is needed, because the merge map need not be injective.
Concatenation preserves total length, $\lambda$-freeness,
fan-out and rank.

Consequently every final nonterminal $X$ is associated
with an original nonterminal $A$ such that
\[
 L_X(G)=\mu\bigl(\sigma\pi_I(L_A(G_{\mathrm{in}}))\bigr),
 \qquad
 \min_{\tuple u\in L_X(G)}\|\tuple u\|_1
 \le\min_{\tuple w\in L_A(G_{\mathrm{in}})}\|\tuple w\|_1.
\]
Trimming leaves the tuple languages of surviving
nonterminals unchanged, so $t_G\le t_{G_{\mathrm{in}}}$.
For fixed $f,r_{\max}$, the subset, permutation and merge
decorations yield only fixed-parameter linearly many
rule copies of no greater template size.  Hence
$\|G\|=O_{f,r_{\max}}(\|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|)$.
Finally Lemma~\ref{lem:thickness-equivalence} gives
\[
 \theta_G\le2\|G\|\max\{1,t_G\}
 =O_{f,r_{\max}}\!\left(
 \|G_{\mathrm{in}}\|\max\{1,t_{G_{\mathrm{in}}}\}\right).
\]
\end{proof}

Thus deleting rules do not by themselves obstruct polynomial thickness
transfer from a reduced $\lambda$-free presentation; genuine empty components
are the problematic step.

\begin{proposition}[Empty-component elimination can blow up thickness]
\label{prop:empty-component-blowup}
For every $n\ge1$ there is a reduced fan-out-two, rule-rank-two MCFG $E_n$ of
size $O(n)$ with $t_{E_n}\le2$ and $\theta_{E_n}=O(n)$ such that the
empty-component elimination stage of Proposition~\ref{prop:good-normalization}
contains a retained nonterminal and a retained rule of thickness at least
$2^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $E_n$ have fan-out-one $S$, fan-out-two $A_0,\ldots,A_n$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_0&\to(\mathtt a,\eps),\\
 A_i&\to(\eps,\mathtt c)\mid
      (\xi_1^1\xi_2^1,\xi_1^2\xi_2^2)(A_{i-1},A_{i-1})
      \quad(1\le i\le n),\\
 S&\to(\xi_1^1\mathtt d\,\xi_1^2)(A_n).
\end{aligned}
\]
It is reduced, nondeleting, nonpermuting, and nonmerging.  Short side
derivations give $t_{E_n}\le2$, and placing any chosen rule on one branch
of the binary spine while completing siblings minimally gives
$\theta_{E_n}=O(n)$.

If an $A_i$-tuple has nonempty first component and empty second component, the
rule $(\eps,\mathtt c)$ cannot occur.  Inductively the shortest such tuple is
$(\mathtt a^{2^i},\eps)$.  Thus pattern $\{1\}$ for $A_n$ survives
empty-component elimination, and the projected start rule
\(S\to\xi_1^1\mathtt d\,(A_n^{\{1\}})\)
can succeed only with a child of length at least $2^n$.  Hence the retained
nonterminal and rule have thickness at least $2^n$.
\end{proof}

This is the same obstruction exhibited by
Proposition~\ref{prop:typed-thickness-exponential}: finite state refinement can
force exponentially long shortest witnesses inside one refined state.

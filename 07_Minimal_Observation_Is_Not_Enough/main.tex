\author{Takayuki Kuriyama\\Independent Researcher, Tokyo, Japan\\\texttt{growup.kuriyama@gmail.com}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Finite-monoid typing provides a finite comparison bias for positive-data
grammar reconstruction: fragments or tuple components may be substituted only
when their observer values agree.  The companion CFG and MCFG results establish
exact reconstruction and identification in the limit once such an observer is
fixed.  The present paper studies the observer itself as a reconstruction
resource.

We first recall a background compilation principle: for a fixed alphabet and a
fixed bound on observer image size, all admissible observers factor through one
finite universal product observer.  Thus bounded unknown observation preserves
identifiability, whereas the unrestricted target-dependent union contains all
regular languages and is blocked by Gold's theorem.  The new question is what
this finite information costs for reconstruction.

For an explicit CFG family \(R_{m,q}\), the minimum safe observer image has
\(qm\) elements.  Nevertheless, minimum image size and minimum characteristic
data have disjoint optimizers.  More strongly, two incomparable observers
\(H^-\) and \(H^+\) are both maximally coarse among safe observers, yet they have
different exact reconstruction costs.  The penalty paid by minimum image size
is \(4(m-1)(q-1)\); for \(q=m^2\), the relative image-size overhead of \(H^+\)
tends to one while the reconstruction-cost ratio tends to two.  Combining the
same exact formula with universal-product compilation shows that a sufficiently
fine universal observer can eliminate all positive generalization on this
family and require the whole finite target as characteristic data.

For a finite family \(X_{k,r}\) analyzed with the fan-out-two MCFG
reconstruction, the trivial one-state observer is already safe and
reconstruction-optimal.  Here the exact minimum characteristic-sample
cardinality is \(1+r(k-1)\), obtained from an affine-span invariant, and the
standard encoded cost is \((2r+1)[1+r(k-1)]\).  The same target family and
the same trivial observer yield a sharper architecture separation: the
companion CFG constructor has no positive generalization at all on
\(X_{k,r}\), so its unique characteristic sample is the whole language of
size \(k^r\).  Thus fan-out-two reconstruction reduces exact positive data
from exponential to linear cardinality while the observer is held fixed.

The resulting picture is order-theoretic as well as quantitative: refinement
of a safe observer can only restrict the language generated from a fixed
positive sample, but observer image cardinality does not determine which
maximally coarse safe observer is best for reconstruction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Identification in the limit from positive data is possible only after a
language class is restricted strongly enough to avoid Gold's superfinite
obstruction \cite{Gold1967}.  In distributional grammar learning, one standard
restriction is substitutability: observed shared contexts license replacement
only in language classes where this evidence is semantically sound
\cite{ClarkEyraud2007,Yoshinaka2011}.  The companion CFG and MCFG papers
\cite{KuriyamaCFG,KuriyamaMCFG} refine this idea by a finite-monoid observer
\[
 h:\Sigma^*\to M.
\]
Only fragments or tuple components with equal \(h\)-values may be compared.
For each fixed \(h\), this finite algebraic bias supports exact positive
reconstruction and explanatory identification in the limit.

The choice of observer can be viewed as a finite typing or domain bias in the
sense familiar from automata inference \cite{CosteEtAl2004}.  Once an observer
is fixed, characteristic positive data are algorithm-relative witnesses for
exact reconstruction, in the characteristic-set tradition of grammatical
inference \cite{Higuera1997}.  The present paper does not claim a
learner-independent teaching dimension: throughout, reconstruction cost is
measured for the fixed constructors of the companion CFG and MCFG papers.

Teaching dimension and learner-relative teaching provide a broader neighboring
view of example complexity \cite{GoldmanKearns1995,GoldmanMathias1996}.
Recent symbolic-automata work provides passive identification with
characteristic samples over monotonic algebras
\cite{HabermehlLoulergue2026}, while granularity-aware abstraction refinement
has been made explicit in active symbolic-automata learning
\cite{KimChoi2026}.  These settings use different hypothesis classes and
information protocols.  To the best of our knowledge,
they do not isolate the exact joint optimization considered here over one
space of safe finite-monoid congruences and one fixed positive-data
reconstruction architecture.

Two orders on observers must be distinguished.  One is the numerical resource
\[
 |\im h|,
\]
the number of values in the image monoid.  The other is the refinement order
of the induced finite-index congruences.  A smaller image need not be a
coarser safe congruence, and two maximally coarse safe congruences can be incomparable.
This distinction is the source of the main separation below.

A bounded unknown observer can be compiled into one universal product observer.
The MCFG form of this observation already appears in the companion manuscript
\cite{KuriyamaMCFG}; we record the common CFG/MCFG statement only as
background.  Its new role here is to expose a tension: the universal product is
finer than every observer it compiles, while reconstruction monotonicity shows
that moving to a finer observer can only remove substitution links.  Universal
compilation may therefore preserve learnability while degrading positive
generalization.

The main construction is the finite CFG family \(R_{m,q}\).  It yields exact
optimizer separation and, more strongly, two incomparable maximally coarse
safe observers with different reconstruction costs.  The family \(X_{k,r}\)
then separates observer choice from reconstruction architecture itself.  Under
the trivial observer, fan-out-two MCFG reconstruction has an exact affine
lower bound and a linear-cardinality characteristic sample, whereas the CFG
constructor cannot generalize beyond the positive sample.  This provides a
same-target, same-observer comparison rather than attributing the effect to
typing granularity.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
The focused theorem package is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item a refinement-monotonicity theorem for the fixed-observer CFG and MCFG
      reconstruction systems, together with kernel invariance of the
      reconstruction cost;
\item a sharpening of the \(R_{m,q}\) resource analysis to two incomparable
      maximally coarse safe observers with different exact reconstruction
      costs, making the image-size/reconstruction optimizer separation
      order-theoretic as well as numerical;
\item an exact universal-observer penalty on the same family: sufficiently rich
      universal compilation can force the characteristic sample to equal the
      whole target language; and
\item a strengthening of the \(X_{k,r}\) affine characteristic-data formula
      from the slot observer used in the broader preprint to the trivial
      one-state observer under the current fan-out-two reconstruction
      architecture, with exact minimum cardinality \(1+r(k-1)\); and
\item a same-target, same-observer architecture separation on \(X_{k,r}\):
      the CFG constructor requires all \(k^r\) positive examples, while the
      fan-out-two MCFG constructor requires exactly \(1+r(k-1)\).
\end{enumerate}

The bounded-observer compilation principle is included for context rather than
counted as a new contribution.

\paragraph{Relation to the broader OFET preprint.}
A broader public preprint \cite{KuriyamaOFET} predates this focused manuscript
and contains predecessor versions of the exact \(R_{m,q}\)
observer/fragmentation/characteristic-data formulas and an affine-span

\section{Finite observation and positive reconstruction}
\label{sec:setup}

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite nonempty alphabet and let
\(h:\Sigma^*\to M\) be a morphism into a finite monoid.  We always restrict
the codomain to \(\im h\) when counting observer values.

For \(x\in\Sigma^+\), write
\[
 \D_L(x)=\{(u,v)\in\Sigma^*\times\Sigma^*:uxv\in L\}.
\]
The observer \(h\) is \emph{unary safe} for \(L\) if
\[
 h(x)=h(y),\qquad
 \D_L(x)\cap\D_L(y)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \D_L(x)=\D_L(y)
\]
for all nonempty \(x,y\).  This is the interface used by the companion CFG
reconstruction \cite{KuriyamaCFG}.

For MCFG reconstruction we use the positive sentence-context interface of
\cite{KuriyamaMCFG}.  A \(d\)-tuple
\(\mathbf x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\) has nonempty components, and an admissible
sentence context is
\[
 E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots v_{d-1}\blank_dv_d,
\]
where the internal separators \(v_1,\ldots,v_{d-1}\) are nonempty.  As in the
companion MCFG paper, write \(\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)\) for the accepting
admissible contexts.

\begin{definition}[Positive-interface safety and observation number]
For \(f\ge1\), write \(\Safe_f(h;L)\) if for every \(1\le d\le f\),
componentwise equality
\[
 h^{(d)}(\mathbf x)=h^{(d)}(\mathbf y)
\]
together with
\[
 \D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)\cap
 \D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf y)\ne\varnothing
\]
implies equality of the two positive-interface tuple distributions.
Define
\[
 \obs_f(L):=
 \min\{|\im h|:\Safe_f(h;L)\}
\]
when at least one finite safe observer exists, and put
\(\obs_f(L)=\infty\) otherwise.  At \(f=1\) this is exactly the unary
safety notion above.
\end{definition}

The image of a monoid morphism is itself a monoid and contains
\(h(\eps)=1\); thus the identity value is included in \(|\im h|\).

We measure characteristic data relative to the fixed reconstruction
algorithms, not as a learner-independent teaching dimension.  Let
\(\mathcal B_h\) denote the set-driven CFG constructor of
\cite{KuriyamaCFG}, and let \(\mathcal B_{f,h}\) denote the uncapped
fan-out-\(f\) MCFG constructor of \cite{KuriyamaMCFG}; when a rank cap is used,
it will be displayed explicitly.

\begin{definition}[Characteristic sample and reconstruction cost]
For an \(h\)-safe CFG target \(L\), a finite \(C\subseteq L\) is a
\emph{characteristic sample} for \(\mathcal B_h\) if
\[
 C\subseteq K\subseteq L
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 L(\mathcal B_h(K))=L
\]
for every finite \(K\).  Write \(\CS_h(L)\) for the family of such samples.
For the common positive-sample encoding
\[
 \|K\|_+:=\sum_{w\in K}(|w|+1),
\]
put
\[
 \cd_h(L):=\min_{C\in\CS_h(L)}\|C\|_+
\]
when \(\CS_h(L)\ne\varnothing\), and put \(\cd_h(L)=\infty\) otherwise.
In the MCFG setting the same definition is made relative to
\(\mathcal B_{f,h}\), with the value \(\infty\) when no characteristic
sample exists; we write \(\mcd_h\) when the fan-out is clear from context.
\end{definition}

The cost is therefore explicitly reconstruction-system-relative.  The same
observer may be used with other learners, but no learner-independent
characteristic-sample lower bound is claimed here.

\begin{lemma}[Sample monotonicity]
\label{lem:sample-monotonicity}
Fix the observer and the reconstruction architecture.  If
\(K\subseteq K'\) are finite positive samples, then
\[
 L(\mathcal B_h(K))\subseteq L(\mathcal B_h(K'))
\]
in the CFG case.  The analogous inclusion holds for the MCFG constructors,
both under a fixed common rank cap and in the uncapped construction.
Consequently, on a safe target \(L\), any finite exact sample
\(C\subseteq L\) with \(L(\mathcal B(C))=L\) is characteristic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the CFG constructor, every factor/context triple observed in \(K\) is
still observed in \(K'\), and every instance of Rules~(R1)--(R5) present
over \(K\) remains present over \(K'\).  Thus the former grammar is a
subgrammar of the latter.

For the MCFG constructor, observed tuples, Link witnesses, and composition
witnesses are likewise preserved by sample inclusion.  Under a fixed rank cap
the old rules remain verbatim.  In the uncapped construction the effective cap
is \(\|K\|_+\), which is nondecreasing under sample inclusion, so every old
Comp rule also remains.  Hence the generated language is monotone in the
sample.  If \(C\subseteq K\subseteq L\), exactness at \(C\) gives
\(L\subseteq L(\mathcal B(K))\), while safety gives soundness
\(L(\mathcal B(K))\subseteq L\), proving characteristicity.
\end{proof}
\section{Bounded unknown observation: background}
\label{sec:threshold}

We first record the common compilation principle behind bounded unknown
observation.  The MCFG statement is already Proposition ``Unknown typing with
a bounded codomain'' in the companion manuscript \cite{KuriyamaMCFG}; the
formulation below places the CFG and MCFG cases side by side for later use.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-observer compilation]
\label{thm:threshold}
Fix a finite nonempty alphabet \(\Sigma\) and an integer \(m\ge1\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item There is a finite monoid morphism
\[
 U_{\Sigma,m}:\Sigma^*\to M_{\Sigma,m}
\]
such that every morphism \(h:\Sigma^*\to M\) with \(|\im h|\le m\)
factors through \(U_{\Sigma,m}\).

Consequently, the union of the companion CFG classes learnable under some
such \(h\) is contained in one fixed-\(U_{\Sigma,m}\) CFG class and is
identifiable in the limit from positive data.  For every fixed MCFG fan-out
bound \(f\), the analogous bounded-observer union is likewise contained in one
fixed-\(U_{\Sigma,m}\) MCFG class and is identifiable in the limit.

\item If the observer-size bound is removed and arbitrary finite observers are
allowed, the corresponding union contains every regular language.  Hence it
is not identifiable in the limit from positive data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(\Sigma\) and \(m\), there are only finitely many multiplication
tables on sets of size at most \(m\), and only finitely many maps from
\(\Sigma\) into those finite monoids.  Each letter map extends uniquely to a
free-monoid morphism.  Take the finite direct product of all resulting
morphisms and restrict the codomain to its image; call the result
\(U_{\Sigma,m}\).

Every \(h\) with \(|\im h|\le m\) occurs, up to isomorphic relabeling of its
image, as one of the product coordinates.  Therefore
\[
 h=\pi_h\circ U_{\Sigma,m}
\]
for a monoid morphism \(\pi_h\).  Equality of \(U_{\Sigma,m}\)-type is thus
finer than equality of \(h\)-type.  Safety under \(h\) implies safety under
\(U_{\Sigma,m}\), so every bounded-observer target belongs to the corresponding
fixed-\(U_{\Sigma,m}\) class.  The fixed-observer identification theorems of
\cite{KuriyamaCFG,KuriyamaMCFG} then give the positive-data learners.

For the negative statement, let \(L\) be regular and let
\(\eta_L:\Sigma^*\to T_L\) be its finite syntactic morphism.  If two tuple
fillings have equal componentwise \(\eta_L\)-values, then inserting either
filling into the same surrounding context gives the same total syntactic
product, hence the same membership in \(L\).  Thus \(\eta_L\) is safe at every
finite arity, and every regular language lies in the unrestricted union.
That union therefore contains all finite languages and an infinite language.
Gold's superfinite obstruction applies \cite{Gold1967}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proposition is qualitative.  The universal product \(U_{\Sigma,m}\) can
be enormous; no polynomial dependence on \(m\) is claimed.  More importantly
for the present paper, \(U_{\Sigma,m}\) refines every observer that it
compiles.  Section~\ref{sec:universal-penalty} shows that this universality can
have a sharp reconstruction cost.
\end{remark}

\section{Observer refinement and reconstruction monotonicity}
\label{sec:refinement}

\begin{definition}[Observer refinement]
For finite monoid morphisms \(h:\Sigma^*\to M\) and \(g:\Sigma^*\to N\),
write
\[
 h\preceq g
\]
when, after restricting codomains to their images, there is a monoid morphism
\(\pi:\im g\to\im h\) such that \(h=\pi\circ g\).  Thus \(g\) is finer.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction monotonicity]
\label{thm:monotonicity}
Suppose \(h\preceq g\).  In the CFG case use the two constructors
\(\mathcal B_h,\mathcal B_g\).  In the MCFG case fix the same fan-out and use
either the same rank cap for both observers or the uncapped constructors.
Then for every finite positive sample \(K\),
\[
 L(\mathcal B_g(K))\subseteq L(\mathcal B_h(K)).
\]
If \(L\) is safe under \(h\), then
\[
 \CS_g(L)\subseteq\CS_h(L)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \cd_h(L)\le\cd_g(L),
\]
with the analogous statement for \(\mcd\) in the MCFG setting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of \(g\)-type implies equality of \(h\)-type.  In the CFG constructor
of \cite{KuriyamaCFG}, the only observer-dependent rule is Rule~(R3), which
changes the represented factor only under equality of observer type.  Thus
every Rule~(R3) instance licensed by \(g\) is also licensed by \(h\), while
Rules~(R1), (R2), (R4), and (R5) are unchanged.

In the MCFG constructor of \cite{KuriyamaMCFG}, the only observer-dependent
rule is Rule~(Link).  Rules~(Start), (Const), and (Comp) are unchanged, and
every Link licensed by \(g\) is also licensed by \(h\).  Hence in either
architecture the \(g\)-hypothesis is a subgrammar of the \(h\)-hypothesis.

Let \(C\in\CS_g(L)\) and \(C\subseteq K\subseteq L\).  Safety of \(h\)
gives soundness of the \(h\)-reconstruction on positive subsets of \(L\).
Therefore
\[
 L=L(\mathcal B_g(K))
 \subseteq L(\mathcal B_h(K))
 \subseteq L,
\]
so \(C\in\CS_h(L)\).  Minimizing the same sample cost over the nested
characteristic-sample families gives the cost inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Kernel invariance]
\label{prop:kernel-invariance}
If two observers \(h\) and \(g\) have the same kernel on \(\Sigma^*\), then
the corresponding fixed-observer reconstruction grammars are identical for
every finite sample, up to irrelevant renaming of observer values.  In
particular their characteristic-sample families and reconstruction costs are
equal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both companion constructors inspect an observer only through equality tests:
Rule~(R3) in the CFG case and Rule~(Link) in the MCFG case.  The same kernel
therefore licenses exactly the same observer-dependent rules; all remaining
rules are independent of the observer.
\end{proof}

Since every observer is restricted to its image, \(|\im h|\) is exactly the
index of the congruence \(\ker h\).  Thus both the observer-size objective
and the reconstruction-cost objective may be regarded as functions on
finite-index monoid congruences, although the latter is only partially ordered
by refinement rather than by cardinality alone.

\begin{definition}[Maximally coarse safe observer]
Two observers are called \emph{equivalent} when they have the same kernel.
A safe observer \(h\) is \emph{maximally coarse safe} if there is no safe observer
\(g\) with \(g\preceq h\) and \(\ker h\subsetneq\ker g\).  Equivalently,
\(h\) has no strict safe coarsening.
\end{definition}

Because codomains are restricted to images, every strict coarsening is a
proper quotient and has strictly smaller image cardinality.  The converse is
false: a smaller image need not be comparable in the refinement order.

\begin{corollary}[Incomparability of a strict tradeoff]
\label{cor:incomp}
If safe observers \(h,g\) satisfy
\[
 |\im h|<|\im g|
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \cd_h(L)>\cd_g(L),
\]
then neither observer refines the other.
\end{corollary}

Thus a larger-image/cheaper-reconstruction tradeoff cannot arise merely by
moving along one refinement chain.  It requires incomparable finite
congruences rather than a simple increase of information.

\section{Exact optimizer separation for CFGs}
\label{sec:R}

Fix \(m,q\ge2\).  Let
\[
 P_q=\{p_0,\ldots,p_{q-1}\},\qquad
 A_m=\{a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}\},
\]
and use fresh terminals \(z_{s,i}\).  Define
\[
 R_{m,q}
 =
 \{p_sa_i a_j:s<q,\ i,j<m\}
 \cup
 \{p_sa_i z_{s,i}:s<q,\ i<m\}.
\]
Every target word has length three.  Call the first block the \emph{main}
block and the second the \emph{rigidity} block.

All main suffixes \(a_i a_j\) have the same unary distribution.  For a safe
observer \(h\), put
\[
 \kappa_h:=|\{h(a_i a_j):i,j<m\}|.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Complete conflict classification]
\label{lem:R-conflicts}
Let \(x\ne y\) be nonempty factors of words in \(R_{m,q}\).  If
\[
 \D_{R_{m,q}}(x)\cap\D_{R_{m,q}}(y)\ne\varnothing
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \D_{R_{m,q}}(x)\ne\D_{R_{m,q}}(y),
\]
then, up to exchanging \(x\) and \(y\), the pair belongs to exactly one of
the following five forms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item \(x=p_s\), \(y=p_t\) with \(s\ne t\);
\item \(x=a_i\), \(y=a_j\) with \(i\ne j\);
\item \(x=a_j\), \(y=z_{s,i}\);
\item \(x=p_sa_i\), \(y=p_ta_k\) with \((s,i)\ne(t,k)\);
\item \(x=a_i a_j\), \(y=a_k z_{s,k}\).
\end{enumerate}
Conversely, every pair in these five families has overlapping but unequal
distributions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All target words have length three.  Hence factors of different lengths cannot
share a terminal context: if the same \((u,v)\) completed both factors to
target words, the two completed words would have different lengths.

For length one, the possible factors are \(p_s\), \(a_i\), and \(z_{s,i}\).
The row symbols \(p_s\) share the contexts \((\eps,a_i a_j)\) supplied by
the main block, while the private rigidity continuations distinguish their
distributions.  The symbols \(a_i\) share main-block contexts and are
distinguished, for example, by \((p_s,z_{s,i})\).  Finally
\[
 \D_{R_{m,q}}(z_{s,i})=\{(p_sa_i,\eps)\}.
\]
Thus an \(a_j\) overlaps every \(z_{s,i}\) through
\((p_sa_i,\eps)\), whereas two distinct rigidity symbols have disjoint
singleton distributions.  Row symbols have empty left context in every
occurrence, while \(a_i\) and \(z_{s,i}\) do not, so there are no further
length-one conflicts.

For length two, the only factor forms are
\[
 p_sa_i,\qquad a_i a_j,\qquad a_i z_{s,i}.
\]
Every prefix \(p_sa_i\) has all contexts \((\eps,a_j)\) and additionally
the private context \((\eps,z_{s,i})\), giving family~(d).  Every main
suffix has the common distribution
\[
 \D_{R_{m,q}}(a_i a_j)=\{(p_t,\eps):t<q\},
\]
whereas
\[
 \D_{R_{m,q}}(a_i z_{s,i})=\{(p_s,\eps)\}.
\]
Since \(q\ge2\), these overlap but are unequal, giving family~(e).  Two
main suffixes have equal distributions; two rigidity suffixes in the same row
have equal singleton distributions and those in different rows are disjoint.
Prefix contexts have empty left component while suffix contexts do not, so no
cross-family conflict remains.

Every length-three factor is a complete target word and has distribution
\(\{(\eps,\eps)\}\).  This exhausts all nonempty factors and proves both
directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact minimum observation and fragmentation gap]
\label{thm:R-observation}
For every \(m,q\ge2\),
\[
 \obs_1(R_{m,q})=qm.
\]
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item every safe \(h\) with \(|\im h|=qm\) satisfies \(\kappa_h\ge m\);
\item if \(\kappa_h<m\), then \(|\im h|\ge q(m+1)\);
\item both bounds are attained: there are safe observers \(H^-\) and \(H^+\)
with
\[
 (|\im H^-|,\kappa_{H^-})=(qm,m),
 \qquad
 (|\im H^+|,\kappa_{H^+})=(q(m+1),1).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conflict clique in Lemma~\ref{lem:R-conflicts} gives
\(|\im h|\ge qm\).

For the matching upper bound, let \(C_q\) be the cyclic group of order \(q\)
and let \(M_m=(\{0,\ldots,m-1\},\max)\), with identity \(0\).  Define
\[
 H^-(p_s)=(s,0),\qquad
 H^-(a_i)=(0,i),\qquad
 H^-(z_{s,i})=(1,m-1).
\]
The \(qm\) values \(H^-(p_sa_i)=(s,i)\) exhaust the image.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:R-conflicts}, safety reduces to the five displayed conflict
families.  Families (a), (b), and (d) are separated respectively by the first
coordinate, the second coordinate, and the pair \((s,i)\); families (c) and
(e) are separated by first coordinate \(0\) versus \(1\).  Hence \(H^-\) is
safe.  Also
\[
 H^-(a_i a_j)=(0,\max(i,j)),
\]
hence \(\kappa_{H^-}=m\).

Now let \(h\) be safe with \(|\im h|=qm\), and put
\(y_s=h(p_s)\), \(x_i=h(a_i)\).  The \(qm\) values
\[
 B=\{y_sx_i:s<q,\ i<m\}
\]
are pairwise distinct by safety and therefore equal all of \(\im h\).
The identity belongs to \(B\), say \(1=y_{s_0}x_{i_0}\).
The \(x_i\) are pairwise distinct.  If
\(x_{i_0}x_j=x_{i_0}x_k\), left multiplication by \(y_{s_0}\) gives
\(x_j=x_k\), impossible.  Thus the \(m\) values
\(x_{i_0}x_j\) are distinct members of
\(\{h(a_i a_j)\}\), proving \(\kappa_h\ge m\).

Finally suppose \(\kappa_h<m\).  We claim that no \(y_s\) lies in \(B\).
If \(y_s=y_tx_i\), then
\[
 y_sx_j=y_t(x_ix_j)
\]
takes at most \(\kappa_h<m\) values as \(j\) varies, while the left-hand
values are pairwise distinct because the prefixes \(p_sa_j\) conflict.
Hence \(B\) is disjoint from \(\{y_s:s<q\}\).  The row values \(y_s\) are
pairwise distinct, so
\[
 |\im h|\ge qm+q=q(m+1).
\]

For attainment let
\[
 N_m=\{1,c_0,\ldots,c_{m-1}\}
\]
have identity \(1\) and multiplication \(c_ic_j=c_0\).  Define
\[
 H^+(p_s)=(s,1),\qquad
 H^+(a_i)=(0,c_i),\qquad
 H^+(z_{s,i})=(1,c_0)
\]
into \(C_q\times N_m\).  Again Lemma~\ref{lem:R-conflicts} gives a complete check: families (a), (b),
and (d) are separated by the displayed row and \(c_i\) coordinates, while
families (c) and (e) have first coordinate \(0\) versus \(1\).  Hence
\(H^+\) is safe.  Its image has \(q(m+1)\) elements, and
\[
 H^+(a_i a_j)=(0,c_0)
\]
for all \(i,j\).  Hence \(\kappa_{H^+}=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two incomparable maximally coarse safe observers]
\label{prop:R-coarsest}
The observers \(H^-\) and \(H^+\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:R-observation} are both maximally coarse safe for \(R_{m,q}\).
They are not equivalent and neither refines the other.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(|\im H^-|=qm=\obs_1(R_{m,q})\), a strict safe coarsening of
\(H^-\) would have image size smaller than \(qm\), contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:R-observation}.  Thus \(H^-\) is maximally coarse safe.

Now suppose that a safe observer \(g\) strictly coarsens \(H^+\).  Because
all main suffixes already have one \(H^+\)-value, coarsening preserves
\(\kappa_g=1<m\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:R-observation}(ii) therefore gives
\(|\im g|\ge q(m+1)\).  But a strict coarsening of \(H^+\) is a proper
quotient and must have image size strictly smaller than
\(|\im H^+|=q(m+1)\), a contradiction.  Hence \(H^+\) is also coarsest
safe.

The two observers cannot be equivalent because their image cardinalities
differ.  If \(H^-\preceq H^+\), then equality of \(H^+\)-types would imply
equality of \(H^-\)-types; but \(H^+\) identifies all main suffixes while
\(\kappa_{H^-}=m>1\), a contradiction.  Conversely, if
\(H^+\preceq H^-\), then \(H^+\) would be a quotient of \(H^-\) and hence
\(|\im H^+|\le|\im H^-|\), contradicting \(q(m+1)>qm\).
\end{proof}
We now compute the positive evidence required by the fixed-observer CFG
constructor of \cite{KuriyamaCFG}.  Partition the \(m^2\) main suffixes into
their \(h\)-fibers
\[
 \mathcal C_h=\{C_1,\ldots,C_{\kappa_h}\}.
\]
For a finite sample \(K\subseteq R_{m,q}\), let \(G_t(K)\) be the bipartite
graph with left vertices \(P_q\), right vertices \(C_t\), and an edge
\((p_s,c)\) when \(p_sc\in K\).

Recall the relevant actions of the CFG constructor \cite{KuriyamaCFG}.
Rule~(R2) transports one observed factor between two observed terminal
contexts, Rule~(R3) changes the represented factor inside a fixed observed
context when the two factors have equal observer type, Rule~(R1) decomposes an
observed factor, and Rules~(R4)--(R5) are lexical and start rules.

\begin{lemma}[Exact sample-graph criterion]
\label{lem:R-graph}
For safe \(h\) and \(K\subseteq R_{m,q}\),
\[
 L(\mathcal B_h(K))=R_{m,q}
\]
if and only if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item every rigidity word \(p_sa_i z_{s,i}\) belongs to \(K\); and
\item every \(G_t(K)\) is connected and spanning.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First consider the rigidity words.  The terminal \(z_{s,i}\) occurs in no
other target word.  Rule~(R4) can emit \(z_{s,i}\) only from an observed
one-letter factor, so if \(p_sa_i z_{s,i}\notin K\), the reconstruction
cannot generate that target word.  Hence condition~(i) is necessary.

Fix a type fiber \(C_t\) and a sampled incidence \((p_s,c)\),
\(c\in C_t\).  The sample contains the occurrence nonterminal
\([c:p_s,\eps]\).  Among such length-two suffix occurrences, Rule~(R2)
moves
\[
 [c:p_s,\eps]\longrightarrow[c:p_u,\eps]
\]
exactly when both incidences \((p_s,c)\) and \((p_u,c)\) are sampled.
Rule~(R3) moves
\[
 [c:p_s,\eps]\longrightarrow[c':p_s,\eps]
\]
exactly when both incidences \((p_s,c)\), \((p_s,c')\) are sampled and
\(c,c'\in C_t\).  Thus alternating R2/R3 moves among these occurrence
nonterminals are exactly edge-walks in \(G_t(K)\), equivalently walks in the
line graph of its sampled incidences.

Once Rule~(R1) decomposes a main suffix \(a_i a_j\) into one-letter
children, no new main suffix can be assembled.  More concretely,
Lemma~\ref{lem:R-conflicts}(b) implies that distinct \(a_i\) have distinct
observer types under every safe observer, so Rule~(R3) cannot change either
letter; Rule~(R2) preserves the represented factor.  The alternative split
\(p_sa_i\mid a_j\) cannot create a missing combination either, because
families (b) and (d) of Lemma~\ref{lem:R-conflicts} block R3 changes of the
last letter and the length-two prefix.  Finally a rigidity root cannot be
converted into a main root: families (c) and (e) block the only possible
one-letter or length-two R3 changes from \(z_{s,i}\) or
\(a_i z_{s,i}\) to main material.  Hence binary decomposition cannot jump
between distinct connected components of \(G_t(K)\).

Suppose condition~(ii) holds.  To generate an arbitrary main word \(p_sc\)
with \(c\in C_t\), spanning gives a sampled edge \((p_s,c_0)\).  Start
from the sampled word \(p_sc_0\) and apply Rule~(R1) at the split
\(p_s\mid c_0\).  Since \(G_t(K)\) is connected, its line graph on sampled incidences is
connected.  A line-graph path from the sampled incidence \((p_s,c_0)\) to
any sampled incidence containing \(c\) translates into an alternating
sequence of R2 and R3 moves as above.  The resulting suffix
nonterminal then decomposes lexically to \(c\), while the outer first child
still emits \(p_s\).  Thus every main word is generated.  Condition~(i)
supplies every rigidity word.  Soundness for safe observers gives the reverse
inclusion, so the reconstruction is exact.

Conversely, if some \(G_t(K)\) is not spanning, either a row vertex has no
sampled main incidence or a suffix vertex is unobserved; the preceding
decomposition analysis shows that a corresponding main word cannot be
generated.  If \(G_t(K)\) is spanning but disconnected, choose a row vertex
and a suffix vertex in different connected components.  Any derivation of
their main word would have to cross components before the first irreversible
R1 decomposition, contradicting the R2/R3 correspondence above.  Hence exact
reconstruction implies that every \(G_t(K)\) is connected and spanning.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact CFG reconstruction cost]
\label{thm:R-cost}
For every safe observer \(h\),
\[
 \min_{C\in\CS_h(R_{m,q})}|C|
 =
 qm+m^2+\kappa_h(q-1),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \cd_h(R_{m,q})
 =
 4\bigl(qm+m^2+\kappa_h(q-1)\bigr).
 }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \(qm\) rigidity words are compulsory.  A connected spanning bipartite
graph on \(q+|C_t|\) vertices has at least
\(q+|C_t|-1\) edges, and a spanning tree attains the bound.  Hence the
minimum number of main words is
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{\kappa_h}(q+|C_t|-1)
 =
 \kappa_h(q-1)+m^2.
\]
Add the rigidity words.  Every target word has length three, so the common
encoding \(\|K\|_+\) is four times cardinality.  The constructor is monotone
under sample inclusion, while safety gives soundness on every
\(K\subseteq R_{m,q}\); hence any exact sample obtained above is characteristic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Image-size/reconstruction optimizer separation]
\label{thm:R-separation}
For every \(m,q\ge2\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \min_{\substack{h\ {\rm safe}\\|\im h|=\obs_1(R_{m,q})}}
 \cd_h(R_{m,q})
 =
 4(m^2+2qm-m).
 }
\]
In contrast,
\[
 \boxed{
 \min_{h\ {\rm safe}}\cd_h(R_{m,q})
 =
 4(m^2+qm+q-1),
 }
\]
and every reconstruction-optimal observer has at least \(q(m+1)>qm\)
image values.  Thus
\[
 \boxed{
 \arg\min_{h:\Safe_1(h;R_{m,q})}|\im h|
 \cap
 \arg\min_{h:\Safe_1(h;R_{m,q})}\cd_h(R_{m,q})
 =\varnothing.
 }
\]
The exact reconstruction penalty paid by minimum image size is
\[
 \boxed{4(m-1)(q-1).}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A minimum-size observer has \(qm\) values and, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:R-observation}, \(\kappa_h\ge m\).  Insert this into
Theorem~\ref{thm:R-cost}; \(H^-\) attains equality.  For arbitrary safe
observers, \(\kappa_h\ge1\), and \(H^+\) attains \(\kappa=1\), giving the
global reconstruction minimum.  The fragmentation-gap part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:R-observation} forces at least \(q(m+1)\) observer values
for every optimizer with \(\kappa_h<m\).  Subtracting the two costs gives the
displayed gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Vanishing observation overhead, factor-two reconstruction gain]
\label{cor:R-asymptotic}
For \(q=m^2\),
\[
 \frac{|\im H^+|}{\obs_1(R_{m,m^2})}
 =1+\frac1m\longrightarrow1,
\]
while
\[
 \frac{
 \min_{|\im h|=\obs_1(R_{m,m^2})}\cd_h(R_{m,m^2})
 }{
 \min_h\cd_h(R_{m,m^2})
 }
 \longrightarrow2.
\]
\end{corollary}

Thus an asymptotically negligible relative increase in observation size can
halve the leading reconstruction cost.

\section{The cost of universal observation}
\label{sec:universal-penalty}

The bounded-observer compilation of Section~\ref{sec:threshold} preserves
identifiability by replacing all observers up to a size bound with one common
refinement.  On \(R_{m,q}\), the exact cost formula shows that this can
destroy all positive generalization.

Let \(\Sigma_{m,q}\) be the alphabet of \(R_{m,q}\), and let
\(T_2(\Sigma_{m,q})\) be the Rees quotient of the free monoid that remembers
words of length at most two and sends every word of length at least three to
one absorbing element \(\bot\):
\[
 T_2(\Sigma_{m,q})=\{\eps\}\cup\Sigma_{m,q}\cup\Sigma_{m,q}^2
 \cup\{\bot\}.
\]
Let \(t_2:\Sigma_{m,q}^*\to T_2(\Sigma_{m,q})\) be the quotient morphism.
It separates every two distinct words of length two.

\begin{corollary}[Universal compilation can eliminate generalization]
\label{cor:universal-penalty}
Let
\[
 B\ge |T_2(\Sigma_{m,q})|
 =2+|\Sigma_{m,q}|+|\Sigma_{m,q}|^2,
\]
and let \(U_{\Sigma_{m,q},B}\) be the universal product observer from
Section~\ref{sec:threshold}.  Then \(U_{\Sigma_{m,q},B}\) is safe for
\(R_{m,q}\),
\[
 \kappa_{U_{\Sigma_{m,q},B}}=m^2,
\]
and every characteristic sample for the CFG reconstruction has cardinality at
least \(|R_{m,q}|\).  Hence the unique minimum-cardinality characteristic
sample is the whole target language.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The bound on \(B\) ensures that the truncated morphism \(t_2\) occurs, up
to relabeling, as a coordinate of the universal product.  Therefore
\(U_{\Sigma_{m,q},B}\) refines \(t_2\) and separates all \(m^2\) main
suffixes \(a_i a_j\), so \(\kappa_U=m^2\).  The same universal product
also refines the safe observer \(H^-\) because
\(|\im H^-|=qm<|T_2(\Sigma_{m,q})|\); refinement preserves safety.

Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:R-cost} gives
\[
 \min_{C\in\CS_U(R_{m,q})}|C|
 =qm+m^2+m^2(q-1)
 =qm+qm^2
 =|R_{m,q}|.
\]
Since a characteristic sample is a subset of the target, equality forces it
to be the whole target language.
\end{proof}

This makes the role of Section~\ref{sec:threshold} precise: universal
observation can be useful for class-level learnability while being maximally
conservative for positive reconstruction.
\section{Exact affine reconstruction for fan-out two}
\label{sec:X}

Fix \(k,r\ge2\).  For \(1\le j\le r\) and \(1\le i\le k\), let
\(a_{j,i}\) and \(b_{j,i}\) be distinct terminals and put
\[
 w_{\mathbf i}
 =
 a_{1,i_1}\cdots a_{r,i_r}
 b_{1,i_1}\cdots b_{r,i_r},
 \qquad
 \mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_r)\in[k]^r,
\]
\[
 X_{k,r}:=\{w_{\mathbf i}:\mathbf i\in[k]^r\}.
\]
Every word has length \(2r\).  Erasing indices gives the rigid skeleton
\[
 A_1A_2\cdots A_rB_1B_2\cdots B_r,
\]
whose symbols are all distinct.

Although \(X_{k,r}\) is finite and hence regular, we analyze it through the
fan-out-two MCFG reconstruction architecture; no minimum-fan-out claim is made.
A fan-out-two MCFG presentation uses nonterminals
\(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) with rules
\[
 A_j\to(a_{j,i},b_{j,i})
\]
and one rank-\(r\) start rule that concatenates all first components and then
all second components in slot order.

\begin{lemma}[Trivial observation is safe]
\label{lem:X-safe}
For every \(k,r\ge2\) and every finite \(f\),
\[
 \obs_f(X_{k,r})=1.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the one-element observer.  Suppose two nonempty \(d\)-tuples share an
admissible positive sentence context.  Every nonempty tuple component is a
contiguous substring of the unique skeleton.  Because each skeleton symbol
occurs only once, the fixed context pieces anchor each component to the same
skeleton interval in both fillings.  For \(d\ge2\), the required nonempty
internal separators also anchor the gaps between components; for \(d=1\) the
exterior context does so, with the root case consisting simply of two complete
target words.

Consider the paired skeleton positions \(A_j,B_j\).  If one position lies
outside the tuple material, the shared accepting context fixes its indexed
terminal and forces the terminal inside the tuple to carry the same index in
both fillings.  If both positions lie inside tuple material, each filling
supplies a complete matched pair and may change the common index freely.
Exactly the same interval pattern holds in every other accepting context.
Replacing one tuple by the other therefore preserves all slot equalities and
hence target membership.  The two tuple distributions are equal.  Thus the
trivial observer is safe at every arity.
\end{proof}

The minimum observation value is therefore already constant.  We now compute
the exact positive evidence needed by the standard fan-out-two reconstruction
of \cite{KuriyamaMCFG} under this observer.  Throughout this section,
\(\mathcal B_{2,\mathrm{triv}}\) denotes the uncapped constructor.  The same
upper and lower bounds hold for the capped constructor whenever the cap is at
least \(r\), because the explicit reconstruction below uses one rank-\(r\)
composition witness and the affine invariant applies to every retained rule.

Let
\[
 u_{j,i}:=(a_{j,i},b_{j,i})
\]
be the complete tuple for slot \(j\).

\begin{proposition}[A linear-cardinality characteristic sample]
\label{prop:X-upper}
Let \(w_0=w_{(1,\ldots,1)}\).  For each \(j\) and \(i\ge2\), let
\(w_{j,i}\) differ from \(w_0\) only by changing slot \(j\) from index \(1\)
to \(i\).  Then
\[
 C_{k,r}
 =
 \{w_0\}\cup
 \{w_{j,i}:1\le j\le r,\ 2\le i\le k\}
\]
is characteristic, with
\[
 |C_{k,r}|=1+r(k-1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each \(j,i\), the tuples \(u_{j,1}\) and \(u_{j,i}\) occur in the same
admissible sentence context in \(w_0\) and \(w_{j,i}\), respectively.
Under the trivial observer, Rule~(Link) licenses the corresponding tuple
substitution.  The base word \(w_0\) supplies a rank-\(r\) Rule~(Comp)
witness whose children are the tuples \(u_{1,1},\ldots,u_{r,1}\).  After that
decomposition, the \(r\) child slots may be changed independently by Link
rules from \(u_{j,1}\) to any \(u_{j,i_j}\).  Hence every \(w_{\mathbf i}\)
is generated.  Safety from Lemma~\ref{lem:X-safe} gives soundness, and sample
monotonicity makes the sample characteristic.
\end{proof}

For the matching lower bound, encode an index vector in the real vector space
with basis \(e_{j,i}\) by
\[
 \Phi(i_1,\ldots,i_r)
 :=
 \sum_{j=1}^r e_{j,i_j}.
\]
The affine hull of all \(\Phi([k]^r)\) has dimension \(r(k-1)\):
relative to \(\Phi(1,\ldots,1)\), the vectors
\[
 e_{j,i}-e_{j,1},
 \qquad 1\le j\le r,\quad 2\le i\le k,
\]
are linearly independent and span its translation space.

\begin{lemma}[Affine-span invariant]
\label{lem:X-affine}
For any nonempty finite \(K\subseteq X_{k,r}\),
\[
 w_{\mathbf i}\in L(\mathcal B_{2,\rm triv}(K))
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Phi(\mathbf i)\in\operatorname{Aff}\{\Phi(\mathbf j):w_{\mathbf j}\in K\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Consider first one Rule~(Link) instance \([\mathbf x]\to[\mathbf y]\).
By definition of the MCFG constructor, there are sampled complete words
\(w_{\boldsymbol\alpha},w_{\boldsymbol\beta}\in K\) in one common
admissible sentence context, with \(\mathbf x\) and \(\mathbf y\) as the
two fillings.  The rigid skeleton fixes the interval occupied by each tuple
component.  If exactly one of the paired positions \(A_j,B_j\) lies in the
tuple material, the other position lies in the common context and fixes the
index, so the Link cannot change slot \(j\).  Hence a Link can change only
those slots whose two paired positions are fully contained in the linked
tuple.  On those slots its change is exactly
\[
 \Phi(\boldsymbol\beta)-\Phi(\boldsymbol\alpha),
\]
a difference of two sampled vectors.

We prove by induction on a derivation below an observed tuple nonterminal that
its contribution to the surrounding full index vector is the witnessed sample
contribution plus a linear combination of such sampled differences, supported
only on slots fully contained in that tuple occurrence.  Rule~(Const) gives
zero change.  Rule~(Link) is the preceding paragraph.  For Rule~(Comp), the
child occurrences are disjoint because the template is linear and
nondeleting, so their supported changes add.  If the two positions of one
slot are split between different children, that slot is not fully contained
in either child and therefore cannot be changed by either child; this is the
half-contained-slot case excluded by the Link analysis.

A complete derivation begins with Rule~(Start) from a sampled root word.
Consequently every generated full vector has the form
\[
 \Phi(\boldsymbol\alpha_0)
 +\sum_{t=1}^N \lambda_t
   \bigl(\Phi(\boldsymbol\beta_t)-\Phi(\boldsymbol\alpha_t)\bigr)
\]
for sampled vectors and integer coefficients arising from the derivation.
Such a vector lies in the affine hull of the sampled vectors, proving the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact affine reconstruction cost]
\label{thm:X-cost}
For the trivial observer and the uncapped fan-out-two constructor,
\[
 \min_{C\in\CS_{\rm triv}(X_{k,r})}|C|
 =1+r(k-1).
\]
With the positive-sample encoding of \cite{KuriyamaMCFG},
\[
 \mcd_{\rm triv}(X_{k,r})
 =(2r+1)[1+r(k-1)].
\]
The same equalities hold for every rank cap at least \(r\).  Because the
trivial observer is coarser than every finite observer, it is globally
reconstruction-optimal over all safe observers for each of these fixed
architectures.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(K\) is characteristic, its reconstruction generates every element of
\(X_{k,r}\).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:X-affine}, the affine hull of the sample
vectors must contain \(\Phi([k]^r)\), whose affine dimension is
\(r(k-1)\).  The affine hull of \(|K|\) points has dimension at most
\(|K|-1\), hence
\[
 |K|\ge1+r(k-1).
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:X-upper} attains equality.  Since every target word has
length \(2r\), the encoded-cost formula follows.  The explicit upper-bound
derivation has rank \(r\), while the affine invariant remains valid after
deleting higher-rank Comp rules, proving the capped statement.

Finally every finite observer refines the trivial observer.  Applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotonicity} with the same fan-out and rank regime shows
that no finer safe observer can have a smaller characteristic-sample cost.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Yoshinaka's reconstruction]
By Proposition ``Typed guard on Yoshinaka's reconstruction'' in the companion
MCFG paper \cite{KuriyamaMCFG}, the trivial-observer constructor
\(\mathcal B_{2,\mathrm{triv}}\) agrees, up to notation and the direct
\(\eps\)-start convention, with Yoshinaka's finite-sample hypothesis grammar
for \(\mathcal A(2,*)\) \cite{Yoshinaka2011}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:X-cost}
therefore gives an exact characteristic-sample cardinality for that standard
reconstruction architecture on the family \(X_{k,r}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The two-choice family]
\label{cor:X-twochoice}
For \(L_r=X_{2,r}\),
\[
 \obs_2(L_r)=1,\qquad
 \min_{C\in\CS_{\rm triv}(L_r)}|C|=r+1,
\]
and
\[
 \mcd_{\rm triv}(L_r)=(2r+1)(r+1)=\Theta(r^2).
\]
The same values are globally optimal over all safe observers for the fixed
reconstruction architecture.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the affine family]
The point of Corollary~\ref{cor:X-twochoice} is the exact affine-dimension
characterization, not merely that encoded cost can grow while
\(\obs_2=1\).  In this family the alphabet, target-word length, and the rank
needed by the displayed upper-bound reconstruction all grow with \(r\).
Accordingly, the result does not claim a lower bound with those structural
parameters held fixed.  It shows instead that observer cardinality does not
encode the independent affine degrees of freedom that the fixed constructor
must witness.
\end{remark}
\section{Discussion}

The bounded-observer compilation principle and the reconstruction results play
different roles.  Universal products solve a class-level uncertainty problem:
they replace an unknown bounded observer by one common finer observer.  The
new monotonicity theorem shows the opposite pressure at reconstruction time:
finer observers delete substitution links.  Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-penalty}
makes the tension exact by exhibiting a family on which a sufficiently rich
universal observer requires the entire target language as characteristic
data.

The principal CFG separation is order-theoretic, not merely numerical.
Proposition~\ref{prop:R-coarsest} shows that \(H^-\) and \(H^+\) are both
maximally coarse safe observers and are incomparable.  Nevertheless their exact
reconstruction costs differ, while Theorem~\ref{thm:R-separation} shows that
the observer with minimum image cardinality is not reconstruction-optimal.
Thus image cardinality does not identify the best point even on the frontier
of safe congruences.  In particular, unlike the canonical syntactic
congruence used for recognition of a regular language, the present safety
condition need not determine a unique maximally coarse finite congruence: the
\(R_{m,q}\) family has at least the two incomparable maximally coarse safe
kernels exhibited above.

The two explicit families expose different finite reconstruction geometries.
For \(R_{m,q}\), characteristic data are controlled by connectivity of
typed row--suffix incidence graphs, and minimum samples are spanning trees.
For \(X_{k,r}\), characteristic data are controlled by an affine hull of a
product of simplices, and the exact minimum is an affine-basis size
\(1+r(k-1)\).  These are properties of the fixed reconstruction systems, not
claims about learner-independent teaching dimension.

Both families used for the exact formulas are finite.  The present paper uses
them because they isolate observer choice from unrelated grammar-growth
phenomena and permit exact optimization.  Whether analogous disjoint
maximally-coarse-safe/reconstruction optima can be forced for natural infinite target
families is left open.

\section{Conclusion}

Finite observation serves two distinct purposes in positive-data grammar
learning.  Bounded observation can delimit a learnable class, but the
cardinality of a safe observer does not determine the positive evidence needed
by a fixed reconstruction procedure.

The CFG family \(R_{m,q}\) separates minimum image size from minimum
reconstruction cost even among maximally coarse safe observers, and shows that
universal-product compilation can eliminate all positive generalization.  The
MCFG family \(X_{k,r}\) gives an independent affine geometry with exact
characteristic-sample cardinality.  Together these results motivate treating
observer image size, congruence refinement, and reconstruction evidence as
separate resources rather than as one scalar notion of observation.

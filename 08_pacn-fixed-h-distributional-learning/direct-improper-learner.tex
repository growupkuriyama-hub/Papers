
\subsection{Direct, unconditional improper \(PAC_{d+1}\) learning}
\label{subsec:direct-improper-pac}

The typing condition gives more than a VC bound: within any fixed
tuple type, the context-versus-tuple relation is a disjoint union of
complete bipartite rectangles.  The following proof builds a learner
using only observed slices.  It does not invoke the disputed
Chernikov--Towsner sufficiency theorem.

\begin{lemma}[Typed rectangular decomposition]
\label{lem:typed-rectangles}
Let \(R\subseteq Y\times X_1\times\cdots\times X_d\),
where all coordinate sets are countable, and let
\(g_i:X_i\to T_i\) map to finite sets.  Suppose that
\[
 g^{(d)}(\vec x)=g^{(d)}(\vec y),\quad
 R_{\vec x}\cap R_{\vec y}\ne\emptyset
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_{\vec x}=R_{\vec y},
\]
where \(R_{\vec x}=\{E\in Y:R(E,\vec x)\}\) and
\(g^{(d)}(\vec x)=(g_i(x_i))_i\).
For each \(\tau\in\prod_iT_i\), its nonempty incidence fiber
decomposes as
\[
 R|_{\tau}=\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j\in J_\tau}
 C_{\tau,j}\times B_{\tau,j},
\]
where the context sets \(C_{\tau,j}\) are pairwise disjoint
and nonempty and the tuple sets \(B_{\tau,j}\) are pairwise
disjoint and nonempty, with every tuple in \(B_{\tau,j}\)
having type \(\tau\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Group tuples having the same nonempty column support
\(C=R_{\vec x}\).  Different groups have disjoint tuple
sets by construction.  If distinct same-type column supports
intersected, the assumed substitutability condition would
make them equal, a contradiction.  Each resulting block
is complete by definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Direct improper PAC from typed substitution]
\label{thm:direct-improper-pac}
In the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-rectangles}, set
\(Q=\prod_{i=1}^{d}|g_i(X_i)|\).
A single distribution-independent, one-sided learning function,
given all labels on the full coordinate slices through \(m\)
i.i.d.\ anchors from any product probability measure, produces
an arbitrary relation \(\widehat R\subseteq R\) satisfying
\[
 \Pr\bigl[\mu(R\triangle\widehat R)>\eps\bigr]\le\delta
\]
provided
\[
 m\ge
 \left\lceil\frac{4Q}{e\eps}\right\rceil
 \left\lceil\log_2\frac1\delta\right\rceil,
 \qquad 0<\eps,\delta<1.
\]
The same conclusion holds with support-sensitive
coordinate slices on finite or countable domains.
The output need not belong to a prescribed grammar-induced
incidence class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work first on the product \(S\) of atomic coordinate supports,
which has full measure.  The domain of any nonempty
support-sensitive slice reveals all supports by coordinate
projection, so this restriction is observable and does not
require knowledge of the probabilities.
Put
\[
 \mu=\mu_0\otimes\mu_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_d,
 \qquad \nu=\mu_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_d.
\]
Take the decomposition from
Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-rectangles} restricted to \(S\) and write
\[
 a_{\tau,j}=\mu_0(C_{\tau,j}),\qquad
 b_{\tau,j}=\nu(B_{\tau,j}).
\]
For every \(\tau\), disjointness implies
\[
 \sum_j a_{\tau,j}\le 1,\qquad
 \sum_j b_{\tau,j}\le 1.
\]

For one batch of \(n\) anchors
\((E_s,x_{1,s},\ldots,x_{d,s})\), declare a block
\(C_{\tau,j}\times B_{\tau,j}\) captured when
a sampled context \(E_s\) lies in \(C_{\tau,j}\)
and some sampled first-factor coordinate \(x_{1,r}\)
lies in \(\pi_1(B_{\tau,j})\).  The observed
context slice at \(E_s\), restricted to type \(\tau\),
reveals \(B_{\tau,j}\) exactly.  Select
\(\vec x\in B_{\tau,j}\) with \(x_1=x_{1,r}\).
The observed first-factor slice at \(x_{1,r}\)
reveals the entire column
\[
 C_{\tau,j}=\{E:R(E,\vec x)\}.
\]
The learner returns the union \(H_n\) of these
captured rectangles, zero outside \(S\).
It is determined by the observed labelled slices,
and \(H_n\subseteq R\).

Since \(B_{\tau,j}\) lies inside the cylinder over
its first-coordinate projection,
\[
 \mu_1(\pi_1(B_{\tau,j}))\ge b_{\tau,j}.
\]
A block is missed only if either its context set
or the first projection of its tuple set is
missed by the corresponding samples.  Hence
\[
 \Pr[\text{block }(\tau,j)\text{ missed}]
 \le e^{-n a_{\tau,j}}+e^{-n b_{\tau,j}}.
\]
With \(u e^{-nu}\le 1/(en)\), monotone convergence,
and the disjointness inequalities,
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E[\mu(R\setminus H_n)]
 &\le \sum_{\tau,j}a_{\tau,j}b_{\tau,j}
       (e^{-na_{\tau,j}}+e^{-nb_{\tau,j}})\\
 &\le \sum_\tau\frac{2}{en}
 \le\frac{2Q}{en}.
\end{align*}
Take \(n=\lceil4Q/(e\eps)\rceil\).
Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr[\mu(R\setminus H_n)>\eps]\le\tfrac12.
\]

Divide the overall sample into
\(b=\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil\)
independent batches of size \(n\).
Return the union of their one-sided hypotheses.
Its error cannot exceed that of any individual
batch, so it fails only if all independent
batches fail, with probability at most
\(2^{-b}\le\delta\).  The learning function
uses no target-specific or measure-specific
parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional fixed-\(h\) incidence PAC upper bound]
\label{cor:fixed-h-improper-pac}
Let \(h:\Sigma^*\to M\) be fixed and write
\(q=|h(\Sigma^+)|\).  For every \(1\le d\le f\),
the class of arity-\(d\) incidence relations of
\((f,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages is
improperly support-\(PAC_{d+1}\)-learnable,
with
\[
 N_{\eps,\delta}\le
 \left\lceil\frac{4q^d}{e\eps}\right\rceil
 \left\lceil\log_2\frac1\delta\right\rceil.
\]
This includes the fixed-\(h\) CFG/MCFG incidence
classes and also applies to original full-slice
observations.  It is independent of any proof
of Chernikov--Towsner Theorem~6.5.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-rectangles}, take
\(Y=\mathcal E_d\), \(X_i=\Sigma^+\),
and \(g_i=h|_{\Sigma^+}\).  The hypothesis is
exactly the arity-\(d\) instance of
tuple substitutability.  Then \(Q=q^d\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The precise properness gap]
\label{rem:improper-not-incidence}
The output union of captured rectangles satisfies
the typed column-overlap rule, but need not
be the incidence relation of any word-language:
incidence labels must agree across all decompositions
of the same word.  For instance, with the
trivial typing, \(L=\{ab\}\) is substitutable.
Its positive arity-one incidence entries include
\[
 ((\epsilon,b),a),\quad
 ((a,\epsilon),b),\quad
 ((\epsilon,\epsilon),ab).
\]
If just the first rectangle is captured, the
learner's relation labels the first entry one
and the others zero, although all three
concatenate to \(ab\).  No string language has
this incidence relation.  Hence this theorem
does not establish proper grammar-valued
PAC learning or resolve Takeuchi's general
Problem~11.  Making the relation output
concatenation-consistent and grammar-presentable
without introducing substantial false-positive
mass is the remaining task.
\end{remark}


\subsection{CFG-valued output from full slices at fan-out one}

For context-free targets, the direct incidence learner can be
converted into a genuine context-free \emph{word-language} hypothesis.
It remains improper relative to the fixed-\(h\) substitutable
subclass, but it no longer outputs an arbitrary non-language
relation.

\begin{theorem}[One-sided CFG-valued improper \(PAC_2\)]
\label{thm:cfg-valued-improper-pac}
Fix \(h:\Sigma^*\to M\), and put \(q=|h(\Sigma^+)|\).
For every target \(L\in\mathcal C_h^{\mathrm{cf}}\),
there is one (not necessarily effective) distribution-independent
learning function receiving the original \emph{full-slice}
\(PAC_2\) observations and outputting a context-free language
\(\widehat L\subseteq L\).  Under any product measure on
the context--factor incidence domain,
\[
 \Pr\left[
  \mu\bigl(R_L^{(1)}\triangle R_{\widehat L}^{(1)}\bigr)
       >\eps\right]\le\delta
\]
whenever
\[
 m\ge
 \left\lceil\frac{4q}{e\eps}\right\rceil
 \left\lceil\log_2\frac1\delta\right\rceil.
\]
The output is a CFG language, but is not asserted to satisfy
the fixed-\(h\) substitutability condition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the captured rectangles \(C_j\times B_j\) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-improper-pac} with \(d=1\),
now recovered over the \emph{entire} context and factor
domains rather than only over atomic supports.
For a captured block of type \(t\), pick
\[
 (u_0,v_0)\in C_j,\qquad x_0\in B_j.
\]
Its factor language can be written
\[
 B_j=\{x\in\Sigma^+:h(x)=t,\;u_0xv_0\in L\}.
\]
This is context-free: inverse fixed-word context
insertion (equivalently, quotients by singleton regular
languages), intersection with the regular type fiber
\(h^{-1}(t)\), and restriction to \(\Sigma^+\)
preserve context-freeness.

Introduce a fresh separator \(\#\).  The encoded
context language
\[
 C_j^\#:=\{u\#v:(u,v)\in C_j\}
       =\{u\#v:ux_0v\in L\}
\]
is also context-free, being the inverse image of
\(L\) under the homomorphism sending
\(\#\) to \(x_0\), intersected with
\(\Sigma^*\#\Sigma^*\).
Substituting the context-free language \(B_j\)
for the single terminal \(\#\) gives
\[
 K_j=\{uxv:u\#v\in C_j^\#,\ x\in B_j\},
\]
which is context-free by closure under
context-free substitution.  By the complete-rectangle
property, \(K_j\subseteq L\).

Only finitely many typed blocks are captured
by finitely many anchor rows.  Return
\[
 \widehat L:=\bigcup_{\text{captured }j}K_j.
\]
It is a context-free sublanguage of \(L\).
Moreover, every positive incidence entry in
a captured rectangle concatenates to a word
in \(\widehat L\).  Consequently,
\[
 H_m\subseteq R_{\widehat L}^{(1)}
       \subseteq R_L^{(1)},
\]
where \(H_m\) denotes the one-sided rectangular
hypothesis constructed in the preceding theorem.
Thus the error of \(R_{\widehat L}^{(1)}\) cannot
exceed the error of \(H_m\), and the same
distribution-free bound applies.

The output function is well defined at the
conceptual level: every observed row/factor
language is guaranteed to be context-free,
so one can choose the first CFG for it in a
fixed enumeration of all grammars and carry
out finite CFG substitutions and unions.
Recognizing that CFG from an arbitrary
\emph{infinite} labelled slice is not claimed
to be an effective finite-input procedure.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Capturing complete blocks need not preserve fixed typing]
\label{prop:captured-not-substitutable}
Let \(\Sigma=\{a,b\}\) and use the fixed finite monoid
\[
 M=\{0,1,2,3,4\},\qquad
 i\cdot j:=\min(4,i+j),\qquad
 h(w):=\min(4,|w|).
\]
The finite language
\[
 L=\{aa,bb,aaa,bba,abb\}
\]
is \(h\)-substitutable, but the word-language image
of its complete factor-\(a\) incidence rectangle is
\[
 K=\{aa,aaa,bba,abb\},
\]
which is not \(h\)-substitutable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(L\), factors of differing length below four
have disjoint context sets.  Among one-letter
factors, the distributions of \(a\) and \(b\)
are disjoint.  At length two, \(aa\) and \(bb\)
have the same distribution
\[
 \{(\epsilon,\epsilon),(\epsilon,a),(a,\epsilon)\};
\]
the distributions of \(ab\) and \(ba\) are
disjoint from each other and from that set.
At length three, each whole-word factor has
the singleton context distribution
\(\{(\epsilon,\epsilon)\}\).  Hence \(L\) is
\(h\)-substitutable.

No letter \(b\) shares a context with the
factor \(a\), so its typed incidence block
has factor side \(\{a\}\).  The corresponding
context side represents precisely the words
of \(L\) containing \(a\), yielding the stated
\(K=L\setminus\{bb\}\).

In \(K\), however, the length-two factors
\(aa\) and \(bb\) share the contexts
\((\epsilon,a)\) and \((a,\epsilon)\), whereas
\((\epsilon,\epsilon)\) is a context of
\(aa\) but not \(bb\).  They have identical
\(h\)-type and intersecting, unequal
distributions.  Thus \(K\) fails
\(h\)-substitutability.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This strengthens the distinction between
the three goals: one-sided relation-valued
PAC, one-sided CFG-valued PAC, and
\emph{proper} fixed-\(h\) CFG-valued PAC.
The first two now have direct uniform
full-slice upper bounds; the third remains
open.  The CFG construction above is
not automatically valid for arbitrary
support-sensitive observations: intersection
of a context-free slice language with an
arbitrary atomic support need not be
context-free.
\end{remark}

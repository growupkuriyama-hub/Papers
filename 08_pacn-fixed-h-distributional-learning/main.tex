\title{\(PAC_n\) and Higher-Arity VC Geometry under Fixed Finite-Monoid Distributional Typing\\
\large Typed box obstructions for CFG and MCFG incidence relations}
\author{Takayuki Kuriyama}
\date{Working draft, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We connect fixed finite-monoid substitutability in grammatical inference with higher-arity VC dimension and \(PAC_n\) learning. For each arity \(d\), a language induces a \((d+1)\)-ary incidence relation between a sentence context and \(d\) nonempty factors. The fixed-typing substitutability axiom forbids a labelled box pattern, and the resulting bound is sharpened by a rectangular product capacity of the observer. Consequently, if \(h:\Sigma^*\to M\) is fixed and \(t_h=|h(\Sigma^+)|\), then the induced relation class has \(VC_2\)-dimension at most \(t_h\), while for arity \(d\ge2\) the stronger bound \(VC_{d+1}^{d-1}\le t_h\) holds. At fan-out one this bound is sharp, already for finite regular languages. The relation to \(PAC_n\) learning requires a model audit. Takeuchi's 2020 counterexample invalidates the original full-slice necessity implication, while Malliaris's 2025 note gives a counterexample to Chernikov--Towsner version~1 and identifies an error in the proposed proof of the revised version~2 sufficiency theorem. We consequently quarantine the disputed general proper PAC sufficiency direction. For fixed typing we construct a direct one-sided PAC learner with \(O(q^d\eps^{-1}\log(1/\delta))\) anchors, independent of the disputed proof. The positive Horn closure makes the output a typed-substitutable word-language, proper for the unrestricted semantic class, without guaranteeing a CFG or MCFG presentation. At fan-out one, full-slice observations even admit CFG-valued hypotheses, though not necessarily fixed-\(h\) substitutable hypotheses. A separate finite-support averaging argument gives unconditional sample lower bounds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Let \(\Sigma\) be a nonempty finite alphabet, \(M\) a finite monoid, and
\[
h:\Sigma^*\to M
\]
a fixed homomorphism. Put
\[
T_h^+:=h(\Sigma^+),\qquad t_h:=|T_h^+|.
\]
Only nonempty factors are compared, as in the companion fixed-\(h\) CFG and MCFG manuscripts.

For \(d\ge1\), a sentence context is
\[
E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots v_{d-1}\blank_dv_d,
\]
where \(v_0,v_d\in\Sigma^*\) and \(v_1,\ldots,v_{d-1}\in\Sigma^+\).
Let \(\E_d\) be the set of these contexts. For
\(\mathbf x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in(\Sigma^+)^d\), write \(E[\mathbf x]\) for
hole filling and
\[
\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x):=\{E\in\E_d:E[\mathbf x]\in L\},\qquad
h^{(d)}(\mathbf x):=(h(x_1),\ldots,h(x_d)).
\]

\begin{definition}[Typed tuple substitutability]
Fix \(f\ge1\). A language \(L\subseteq\Sigma^*\) is
\emph{\((f,h)\)-tuple-substitutable} if, for every \(1\le d\le f\) and
\(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in(\Sigma^+)^d\),
\[
h^{(d)}(\mathbf x)=h^{(d)}(\mathbf y),\qquad
\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)\cap\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf y)\ne\varnothing
\]
imply
\[
\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)=\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf y).
\]
At \(d=1\) this is the ordinary two-sided fixed-\(h\) substitutability condition.
\end{definition}

Each language induces the \((d+1)\)-ary incidence relation
\[
R_L^{(d)}
:=
\{(E,x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in\E_d\times(\Sigma^+)^d:
E[x_1,\ldots,x_d]\in L\}.
\]
Let \(\Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}\) be the family of all such relations induced by
\((f,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages. CFG or MCFG restrictions only pass
to subclasses.

\section{The typed-box obstruction}

\begin{theorem}[Typed-box obstruction]\label{thm:typed-box}
For \(1\le d\le f\),
\[
\VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}\right)
\le t_h\le |M|.
\]
The same bound holds for the subfamilies induced by fixed-\(h\) CFGs and
fixed-\(h\) fan-out-\(f\) MCFGs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a box
\[
A_0\times A_1\times\cdots\times A_d
\subseteq \E_d\times(\Sigma^+)^d
\]
of side size \(m>t_h\) were shattered. Since \(|A_1|>t_h\), choose distinct
\(a,b\in A_1\) with \(h(a)=h(b)\). Fix arbitrary \(x_j\in A_j\) for
\(2\le j\le d\), and put
\[
\mathbf x=(a,x_2,\ldots,x_d),\qquad
\mathbf y=(b,x_2,\ldots,x_d).
\]
Then \(h^{(d)}(\mathbf x)=h^{(d)}(\mathbf y)\).

Because \(m>t_h\ge1\), choose distinct \(E_0,E_1\in A_0\). Shattering
permits a target whose trace contains
\[
(E_0,\mathbf x),\quad(E_0,\mathbf y),\quad(E_1,\mathbf x)
\]
but omits \((E_1,\mathbf y)\). Thus
\[
E_0\in\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)\cap\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf y).
\]
Typed tuple substitutability gives
\[
\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf x)=\D_L^{(d)}(\mathbf y),
\]
contradicting the different labels at \(E_1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses neither context-freeness nor multiple context-freeness.
The dimension bound follows from the abstract closure axiom:
equal finite type plus one common context implies equal distributions.
Grammar formalisms matter when one asks representability and effective
grammar-valued learning.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Contiguous-block strengthening]\label{thm:block-bound}
Let \(2\le d\le f\). Then
\[
  \VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}\right)
  \le \left\lfloor t_h^{1/(d-1)}\right\rfloor.
\]
Equivalently, every shattered box of side size \(m\) satisfies
\[
  m^{d-1}\le t_h.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
A_0\times A_1\times\cdots\times A_d
\]
be a shattered box of side size \(m\), and fix one context
\[
E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots v_{d-1}\blank_dv_d\in A_0.
\]
For \((x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})\in A_1\times\cdots\times A_{d-1}\) put
\[
z(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})
:=x_1v_1x_2\cdots v_{d-2}x_{d-1}\in\Sigma^+.
\]
We claim that the map
\[
(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})\longmapsto h(z(x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1}))
\]
is injective. Otherwise choose distinct tuples \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\) with the
same image. Since \(m\ge2\) for any nontrivial violation of the claimed
bound, choose distinct \(a,b\in A_d\). Shattering gives a target \(L\) for which
\[
E[\mathbf x,a],\quad E[\mathbf y,a],\quad E[\mathbf x,b]\in L,
\qquad E[\mathbf y,b]\notin L.
\]
As ordinary one-factor contexts for the two block strings, use
\[
C_a=(v_0,v_{d-1}av_d),\qquad
C_b=(v_0,v_{d-1}bv_d).
\]
Then \(C_a\) belongs to both one-factor distributions of
\(z(\mathbf x)\) and \(z(\mathbf y)\), while their \(h\)-types agree. The arity-one
instance of \((f,h)\)-tuple substitutability therefore makes those distributions
equal, contradicting the two different labels at \(C_b\). Hence the map is
injective. Its domain has \(m^{d-1}\) elements and its image lies in
\(h(\Sigma^+)\), which has \(t_h\) elements.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The stronger exponent is forced already by the arity-one substitutability
condition inside the higher-fan-out model. A \((d+1)\)-ary box contains, after
fixing one sentence context and one final factor coordinate, an ordinary
one-factor substitution test on a contiguous block carrying the other
\(d-1\) coordinates.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Rectangular product capacity]
\label{def:rpc}
For \(r\ge1\), define \(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\) to be the largest \(m\)
(possibly infinite) for which there are sets
\[
 A_1,\ldots,A_r\subseteq\Sigma^+,\qquad |A_i|=m,
\]
and, when \(r\ge2\), fixed nonempty separators
\(s_1,\ldots,s_{r-1}\in\Sigma^+\), such that
\[
 (x_1,\ldots,x_r)\longmapsto
 h(x_1s_1x_2\cdots s_{r-1}x_r)
\]
is injective on \(A_1\times\cdots\times A_r\).
For \(r=1\), this is simply the maximum size of a set on which \(h\) is
injective, hence
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_1(h)=t_h.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Type-level form of rectangular capacity]
\label{prop:rpc-type-level}
Let \(P_h:=h(\Sigma^+)\subseteq\operatorname{im}h\). Then
\(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\) is the largest \(m\) for which there are subsets
\[
 B_1,\ldots,B_r\subseteq P_h,\qquad |B_i|=m,
\]
and separator types \(c_1,\ldots,c_{r-1}\in P_h\) such that
\[
 (b_1,\ldots,b_r)\longmapsto
 b_1c_1b_2\cdots c_{r-1}b_r
\]
is injective. Consequently \(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\) depends only on the
finite marked monoid
\[
 (\operatorname{im}h,P_h),
\]
and is effectively computable from that finite data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An injective word rectangle cannot contain two words of the same \(h\)-type
in any coordinate, so applying \(h\) to its coordinate sets and separators
gives the displayed type-level rectangle. Conversely, choose nonempty word
representatives of all selected elements of \(P_h\); a type-level injective
rectangle then lifts to a word-level one. The finite optimization is
therefore exact.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Full direct products are supermultiplicative]
\label{prop:rpc-product}
Suppose two marked image monoids
\((M_1,P_1)\) and \((M_2,P_2)\) occur independently in an observer whose
marked image is the full direct product
\[
 (M_1\times M_2,\;P_1\times P_2).
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(M_1\times M_2)
 \ge
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(M_1)\operatorname{rpc}_r(M_2).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take optimal rectangular products
\(B_{i,1}\subseteq P_1\) and \(B_{i,2}\subseteq P_2\), with their separator
types. In coordinate \(i\), use
\[
 B_i:=B_{i,1}\times B_{i,2}.
\]
Multiplication is coordinatewise, so equality of two product values forces
equality in both factor rectangles and hence equality of every selected
coordinate pair. The side size is the product of the two side sizes.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Refinement monotonicity of higher-arity statistical geometry]
\label{thm:vc-refinement}
Let \(h\preceq g\), so \(g\) is a refinement of \(h\). Then for every
\(f\) and \(1\le d\le f\),
\[
 \Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}
 \subseteq
 \Rclass_{f,g}^{(d)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}\right)
 \le
 \VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{f,g}^{(d)}\right),
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h)
 \le
 \operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(g)
 \qquad(d\ge2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of \(g\)-types implies equality of \(h\)-types.  Therefore every
substitutability obligation imposed by the finer observer \(g\) is already an
obligation imposed by the coarser observer \(h\).  Any
\((f,h)\)-tuple-substitutable language is thus
\((f,g)\)-tuple-substitutable, proving class inclusion.  VC dimension is
monotone under inclusion.  The rectangular-capacity inequality is
Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One refinement order, two complexity monotonicities]
\label{cor:joint-refinement}
On targets safe under the coarser observer \(h\), refinement
\(h\preceq g\) simultaneously has the following effects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the admissible fixed-observer language class, and hence its potential
higher-arity VC complexity, can only increase;
\item for the companion positive-data reconstruction architectures, the
language generated from any fixed sample can only decrease, so
characteristic-data cost can only increase.
\end{enumerate}
Thus observer refinement is a common monotone resource order for both
statistical expressiveness and exact reconstruction difficulty, although
incomparable observers can optimize the two resources differently.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first item is Theorem~\ref{thm:vc-refinement}.  The second is the
reconstruction-monotonicity theorem of the companion observer-tradeoff
manuscript.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Kernel invariance and product refinement]
\label{prop:rpc-kernel}
If two typings \(h,g:\Sigma^*\to\) finite monoids have the same kernel, then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)=\operatorname{rpc}_r(g)
\]
for every \(r\ge1\).  More generally, the product typing \((h,g)\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h,g)\ge
 \max\{\operatorname{rpc}_r(h),\operatorname{rpc}_r(g)\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(\ker h=\ker g\), the rule
\[
 h(w)\longmapsto g(w)
\]
defines a monoid isomorphism
\(\operatorname{im}h\cong\operatorname{im}g\), and it maps
\(h(\Sigma^+)\) bijectively onto \(g(\Sigma^+)\).  The type-level
description in Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-type-level} is therefore preserved
exactly.

The product typing refines each factor typing.  The second inequality follows
from the refinement monotonicity in Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Group observers and unique product factorizations]
\label{prop:rpc-group}
Assume that the marked image is a finite group \(G\) and that every element
of \(G\) is realized by a nonempty word. Then \(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\)
is the largest integer \(m\) for which there exist subsets
\[
 A_1,\ldots,A_r\subseteq G,\qquad |A_i|=m,
\]
such that the multiplication map
\[
 A_1\times\cdots\times A_r\longrightarrow G,\qquad
 (a_1,\ldots,a_r)\longmapsto a_1\cdots a_r
\]
is injective. In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\le\lfloor |G|^{1/r}\rfloor,
\]
with equality at \(|G|=m^r\) exactly when \(G\) admits an exact
equal-factor-size \(r\)-fold factorization of this form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the type-level definition, a separator-decorated product is
\[
 a_1c_1a_2c_2\cdots c_{r-1}a_r.
\]
Because \(G\) is a group and all elements are allowed as positive types, put
\[
 A'_1=A_1c_1,\quad A'_2=A_2c_2,\quad\ldots,\quad
 A'_{r-1}=A_{r-1}c_{r-1},\quad A'_r=A_r.
\]
Right translation preserves cardinality, and the decorated product is
exactly the undecorated product of the \(A'_i\). Conversely, any undecorated
injective product is obtained by choosing identity separators. The
cardinality statement is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Semilattice extremes at fixed observer size]
\label{thm:rpc-semilattice-extremes}
Fix \(r\ge2\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item Let \(C_T\) be a chain of \(T\) elements under the join operation
\(\max\), with every element nonempty-realizable. Then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(C_T)=1.
\]

\item Let \(B_{r,s}\) be the Boolean semilattice
\[
 \bigl(\mathcal P([r]\times[s]),\cup\bigr),
\]
again with every element nonempty-realizable. Then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(B_{r,s})=2^s
 =|B_{r,s}|^{1/r}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, for every \(T=2^{rs}\) there are two commutative idempotent
monoids of the same size \(T\) with rectangular capacities \(1\) and
\(T^{1/r}\), respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the chain, suppose a rectangular product had side at least two.  Let
\(z\) be the largest element appearing in any coordinate set or among the
fixed separators.  If \(z\) is a separator, every product value is \(z\).
If \(z\) occurs in a coordinate set, fix that coordinate equal to \(z\) and
vary a different coordinate; every resulting product is again \(z\).
Either way injectivity fails.

For the Boolean semilattice, partition the ground set into the \(r\) blocks
\[
 U_i=\{i\}\times[s].
\]
In coordinate \(i\), take the \(2^s\)-element family
\[
 A_i=\mathcal P(U_i),
\]
and choose the empty set as every separator type.  The union map
\[
 A_1\times\cdots\times A_r\to\mathcal P([r]\times[s])
\]
is injective because the blocks are disjoint.  Hence
\(\operatorname{rpc}_r\ge2^s\), while the universal cardinality bound gives
the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Thus neither observer cardinality nor membership in the pseudovariety of
finite semilattices determines rectangular product capacity.  Even among
commutative idempotent observers of equal size, the invariant ranges from
its minimum value to the cardinality upper bound.  The relevant resource is
the internal factorization geometry of the marked image monoid.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Observer-sensitive box bound]
\label{prop:rpc-bound}
For every \(2\le d\le f\),
\[
 \VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{f,h}^{(d)}\right)
 \le \operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h)
 \le \left\lfloor t_h^{1/(d-1)}\right\rfloor.
\]
Moreover, rectangular product capacity is monotone under typing refinement:
if \(\ker h'\subseteq\ker h\), then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h')\ge\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)
\]
for every \(r\ge1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If a \((d+1)\)-box of side \(m\) is shattered, fix one sentence context
\[
 E=v_0\blank_1v_1\cdots v_{d-1}\blank_dv_d
\]
from its context coordinate. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} shows that
\[
 (x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1})\longmapsto
 h(x_1v_1x_2\cdots v_{d-2}x_{d-1})
\]
must be injective on the first \(d-1\) factor-coordinate sets. Therefore
\(m\le\operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h)\). Any injective rectangular product of
side \(m\) has \(m^{d-1}\) distinct values in \(h(\Sigma^+)\), proving the
second inequality.

For refinement, \(\ker h'\subseteq\ker h\) induces a well-defined
homomorphism from \(\operatorname{im}h'\) onto \(\operatorname{im}h\) by
\(h'(w)\mapsto h(w)\). Hence equality of two products under \(h'\) implies
equality under \(h\). Any rectangle on which the \(h\)-product map is
injective is therefore also injective for \(h'\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Observer cardinality does not determine higher-arity VC capacity]
\label{thm:same-size-observers}
Fix \(d\ge3\) and any integer \(q\ge2\). There are two finite observers
with the same number
\[
 T=q^{d-1}
\]
of realized nonempty types but radically different arity-\(d\) incidence
geometry:
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q
\]
for one observer, whereas
\[
 \VC_{d+1}\le1
\]
for the other.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the first observer, take the cyclic-interval observer
\((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^{d-1}\) from
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-q-sharp}. Its image has \(q^{d-1}=T\) elements
and its explicit finite regular target family has \(VC_{d+1}=q\).

For the second, let
\[
 Z_T=\{1,z_1,\ldots,z_{T-1}\},
\]
where \(1\) is the identity and
\[
 z_i z_j=z_i,\qquad z_i1=z_i,\qquad 1z_j=z_j.
\]
This is a monoid. Choose an alphabet with a nonempty representative of every
element of \(Z_T\), so \(t_h=T\). We claim
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)=1\qquad(r\ge2).
\]
Indeed, suppose all \(r\) coordinate sets had at least two elements. If the
first coordinate contains two words of the same type, injectivity already
fails. Otherwise it contains a word \(x\) of nonidentity type \(z_i\).
Once \(x\) occurs first, multiplication by every separator and every later
coordinate leaves the total type equal to \(z_i\), so varying the second
coordinate gives a collision. Thus \(\operatorname{rpc}_r=1\) for every
\(r\ge2\).

Since \(d\ge3\), Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-bound} with \(r=d-1\) gives
\(\VC_{d+1}\le1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:same-size-observers} is a statistical-geometry analogue of
the project's broader \emph{observer size is not enough} theme. At higher
arity, the relevant finite resource is not only how many types the observer
has, but how its multiplication supports uniquely decodable rectangular
products.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Two explicit observer calculations}

The invariant \(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\) is finite and purely algebraic once
the realized nonempty image \(h(\Sigma^+)\) is fixed.  Two families illustrate
opposite behaviors.

\begin{proposition}[Cyclic groups attain the cardinality bound]
\label{prop:rpc-cyclic}
Suppose \(\operatorname{im}h\cong C_N\) is a cyclic group of order \(N\) and
every group element has a nonempty representative, so
\(h(\Sigma^+)=C_N\). Then, for every \(r\ge1\),
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)=\left\lfloor N^{1/r}\right\rfloor.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-bound}.  For the converse put
\(m:=\lfloor N^{1/r}\rfloor\) and write \(C_N\) additively.  For
\(1\le j\le r\), let
\[
 B_j:=\{a\,m^{j-1}:0\le a<m\}\subseteq C_N.
\]
Choose nonempty word representatives of the elements of \(B_j\) and any
fixed nonempty separators of type \(0\).  The product type is the sum
\[
 a_1+a_2m+\cdots+a_rm^{r-1}\pmod N.
\]
All such integer sums lie between \(0\) and \(m^r-1<N\), and uniqueness of
base-\(m\) expansion makes the map injective. Hence
\(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\ge m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
More generally, if a group observer has image
\(G_1\times\cdots\times G_r\), with all group elements nonempty-realizable,
then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\ge \min_i |G_i|
\]
by varying the \(i\)-th coordinate subgroup in the \(i\)-th slot.  In
particular, for \(G=H^r\),
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h)=|H|,
\]
so the universal cardinality bound is exact.
\end{remark}

We next evaluate when a prefix--suffix observer can carry any nontrivial
\(r\)-dimensional rectangular product at all.  Let
\(h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}\) be the prefix--suffix morphism from the companion
fixed-\(h\) CFG/MCFG manuscripts.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-window collapse threshold]
\label{prop:boundary-collapse}
Let \(r\ge2\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h_\Sigma^{k,\ell})=1
\]
whenever
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+
 \left\lceil\frac{\ell}{2}\right\rceil<r.
\]
If \(|\Sigma|\ge2\), the converse holds at the level of nontriviality:
when
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+
 \left\lceil\frac{\ell}{2}\right\rceil\ge r,
\]
one has
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_r(h_\Sigma^{k,\ell})\ge2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix nonempty separators \(s_1,\ldots,s_{r-1}\).  In any concatenation
\[
 x_1s_1x_2\cdots s_{r-1}x_r
\]
with all \(x_i\ne\epsilon\), the \(j\)-th variable block starts no earlier
than position \(2j-1\).  Hence the length-\(k\) prefix can meet only the
first \(\lceil k/2\rceil\) variable blocks.  Symmetrically, the
length-\(\ell\) suffix can meet only the last
\(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\) variable blocks.

If the displayed sum is smaller than \(r\), choose an index \(j\) touched by
neither window.  The total word length is at least \(2r-1\), while the same
inequality forces \(k+\ell\le2r-2\); therefore the product is in the
long-word part of the prefix--suffix coding.  Its type is completely
independent of \(x_j\).  No rectangular product with two choices in every
coordinate can therefore be injective, proving
\(\operatorname{rpc}_r=1\).

Conversely suppose the displayed sum is at least \(r\) and choose two
distinct letters \(a,b\in\Sigma\).  Take
\[
 A_i=\{a,b\}\qquad(1\le i\le r)
\]
and use a fixed one-letter separator between successive variables.  If the
resulting word lies in the short-word part, its entire word is stored and the
map is injective.  Otherwise its prefix--suffix type records every odd
(variable) position: the prefix records the first
\(\lceil k/2\rceil\) such positions and the suffix records the last
\(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\), whose union covers all \(r\) variable positions.
Thus the \(2^r\) choices give distinct types.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Small boundary windows force trivial high-arity VC]
\label{cor:boundary-vc-one}
Let \(d\ge3\) and let \(h\) be \((k,\ell)\)-boundary-determined, i.e.
\(h\preceq h_\Sigma^{k,\ell}\). If
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+
 \left\lceil\frac{\ell}{2}\right\rceil<d-1,
\]
then every arity-\(d\) incidence class induced by
\((f,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages satisfies
\[
 \VC_{d+1}\le1.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The prefix--suffix observer refines \(h\), so monotonicity under refinement
gives
\[
 \operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h)
 \le \operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h_\Sigma^{k,\ell})=1.
\]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:rpc-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Thus fixed boundary windows can be much more restrictive statistically than
their raw number of observer states suggests.  For example, prefix-only
typing with window \(k\) forces \(\VC_{d+1}\le1\) whenever
\(\lceil k/2\rceil<d-1\), regardless of the alphabet size and regardless of
the total number of prefix types.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Fan-out one]\label{cor:cfg-upper}
For fixed-\(h\) substitutable incidence relations
\[
R_L(E,x)\Longleftrightarrow E[x]\in L,
\]
one has
\[
\VC_2\le t_h.
\]
In particular, trivial typing gives \(\VC_2\le1\).
\end{corollary}

\section{Sharpness for CFG incidence relations}

\begin{proposition}[The fan-out-one bound is sharp]\label{prop:sharp-cfg}
For every \(t\ge1\), there are a finite alphabet \(\Sigma_t\), a homomorphism
\(h_t:\Sigma_t^*\to C_t\), and a family of finite regular fixed-\(h_t\)
substitutable languages whose incidence relations shatter a box of side
size \(t\). Consequently
\[
\VC_2=t=|h_t(\Sigma_t^+)|
\]
for the corresponding full fixed-\(h_t\) relation class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(C_t=\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z\) and
\[
\Sigma_t=\{c_0,\ldots,c_{t-1},x_0,\ldots,x_{t-1}\}.
\]
Set \(h_t(c_i)=h_t(x_i)=i\bmod t\). Then
\(h_t(\Sigma_t^+)=C_t\).

For \(S\subseteq[t]\times[t]\), put
\[
L_S:=\{c_i x_j:(i,j)\in S\}.
\]
Each \(L_S\) is finite and regular. The only factors having nonempty
distribution are \(c_i\), \(x_j\), and occurring \(c_i x_j\), with
\[
\D_{L_S}(c_i)=\{(\epsilon,x_j):(i,j)\in S\},
\]
\[
\D_{L_S}(x_j)=\{(c_i,\epsilon):(i,j)\in S\},
\]
and
\[
\D_{L_S}(c_i x_j)=\{(\epsilon,\epsilon)\}.
\]
Distinct one-letter factors of the same kind have distinct types; \(c_i\)
and \(x_i\) have equal type but disjoint distributions; a one-letter factor
and a two-letter factor have disjoint distributions; and occurring
two-letter factors have identical singleton distribution. Thus the
fixed-\(h_t\) substitutability implication holds.

Let \(E_i=c_i\blank\). Then on
\[
\{E_0,\ldots,E_{t-1}\}\times\{x_0,\ldots,x_{t-1}\}
\]
we have
\[
R_{L_S}(E_i,x_j)=1\Longleftrightarrow(i,j)\in S.
\]
Arbitrary \(S\) therefore realizes every trace on the box.
\end{proof}

\section{Higher-arity sharpness at the optimal exponent}

The exponent in Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} is optimal for every integer
observer side size.  The exact algebraic object forced by contiguous grammar
factors is a cyclic interval factor system.  MDS-type systems form a stronger
and classically studied special case.

\begin{definition}[Cyclic interval direct-factor system]
\label{def:interval-factor}
Let \(d\ge2\), put \(m=d-1\), and let \(G\) be an abelian group of
order \(q^m\). A family
\[
 B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_d\subseteq G,\qquad |B_j|=q,
\]
is a \emph{cyclic interval direct-factor system} if, for every cyclic
interval \(J\) of the role cycle
\[
 0,1,\ldots,d,0
\]
with \(1\le |J|\le m\), the sum map
\[
 \prod_{j\in J}B_j\longrightarrow G,\qquad
 (b_j)_{j\in J}\longmapsto\sum_{j\in J}b_j
\]
is injective. For \(|J|=m\), it is therefore bijective.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Interval-factor realization theorem]
\label{thm:interval-realization}
If \(G\) admits a cyclic interval direct-factor system, then there are a
finite alphabet, a homomorphism \(h:\Sigma^*\to G\) with
\[
 t_h=q^{d-1},
\]
and a family of finite regular \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages
whose arity-\(d\) incidence relations satisfy
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Index \(B_r=\{\beta_r(i):i\in[q]\}\). Use pairwise disjoint role
alphabets
\[
 c_i,r_i,s_{i,1},\ldots,s_{i,d-1}\quad(i\in[q]),
 \qquad
 x_{r,j}\quad(1\le r\le d,\ j\in[q]),
\]
and define
\[
 h(c_i)=h(r_i)=\beta_0(i),\qquad
 h(s_{i,t})=0,\qquad
 h(x_{r,j})=\beta_r(j).
\]
A length-\(d-1\) interval among the variable roles has a bijective sum
map onto \(G\), so every element of \(G\) has a nonempty word
representative and hence \(t_h=|G|=q^{d-1}\).

For \(\mathbf i=(i_0,\ldots,i_d)\in[q]^{d+1}\), put
\[
 w_{\mathbf i}
 =c_{i_0}x_{1,i_1}s_{i_0,1}x_{2,i_2}\cdots
 s_{i_0,d-1}x_{d,i_d}r_{i_0},
\]
and for arbitrary \(S\subseteq[q]^{d+1}\) let
\[
 L_S=\{w_{\mathbf i}:\mathbf i\in S\}.
\]

We verify tuple substitutability. The role alphabets are disjoint, so two
tuple occurrences sharing one sentence context use the same hole intervals
in the rigid role skeleton. If some context-specific marker
\(c_{i_0},s_{i_0,t},r_{i_0}\) lies outside the holes, then the common
context fixes \(i_0\). In each hole, the variable roles form one
contiguous interval. Except for the one-hole case containing all \(d\)
variable roles, there are at most \(d-1=m\) such roles. After subtracting
the now-known contribution of \(B_0\), equality of component type and the
interval-factor property recover all variable indices in that hole.

Suppose instead that no context-specific marker lies outside the holes. If
there is one hole, it contains both end markers and hence the entire word;
its accepting distribution is the singleton \(\{(\epsilon,\epsilon)\}\).
If there are at least two holes, every nonempty gap between successive holes
contains a variable-role letter, because all context-specific markers are
inside holes. Thus at least one variable position is outside the holes.
The two end holes contain the two copies of the \(B_0\)-role. At least one
of them contains at most \(d-2\) variable roles; otherwise the number of
variable roles inside the two end holes alone would exceed what is
available. Its component type is therefore a sum over a cyclic interval of
at most \(d-1=m\) roles, so it recovers \(i_0\). Once \(i_0\) is
known, the preceding interval argument recovers all remaining selected
variable indices.

The only remaining one-hole possibility is a factor containing all \(d\)
variable roles but not both end markers. It contains every internal
context-specific separator and therefore fixes \(i_0\) literally. No
variable role remains outside the factor, so every accepted factor with the
same boundary pattern has the same singleton two-sided distribution.
Therefore equal tuple type plus one common context always implies equal
tuple distributions.

Finally set
\[
 E_i=c_i\blank_1s_{i,1}\blank_2\cdots
 s_{i,d-1}\blank_dr_i,
 \qquad
 A_r=\{x_{r,j}:j\in[q]\}.
\]
Then
\[
 R_{L_S}^{(d)}(E_{i_0},x_{1,i_1},\ldots,x_{d,i_d})=1
 \Longleftrightarrow
 (i_0,\ldots,i_d)\in S.
\]
Hence the displayed \((d+1)\)-box of side \(q\) is shattered, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} gives the matching upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A universal interval frame over \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\)]
\label{prop:universal-interval-frame}
For every integer \(q\ge2\) and every \(d\ge2\), with \(m=d-1\),
the group
\[
 G=(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^m
\]
admits a cyclic interval direct-factor system of side \(q\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\) be the standard basis. Put
\[
 v_0=e_1,\ldots,v_{m-1}=e_m,\qquad
 v_m=a=(1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T},
\]
and let \(v_{m+1}=b\), where
\[
 b_r=\begin{cases}
 1,& r\equiv m\pmod2,\\
 0,& r\not\equiv m\pmod2
 \end{cases}
 \qquad(1\le r\le m).
\]
Thus \(b_m=1\) and successive coordinates of \(b\) alternate.
Every \(m\)-term cyclic window in
\[
 v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{m+1},v_0
\]
has determinant \(\pm1\). Indeed, the first two window determinants are
\(1\) and \(\pm a_1=\pm1\); the intermediate determinants are, up to
sign,
\[
 a_r b_{r+1}-a_{r+1}b_r=b_{r+1}-b_r\in\{1,-1\},
\]
and the last is \(\pm b_m=\pm1\). Hence each window is invertible over
\(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), for arbitrary (not necessarily prime-power)
\(q\). Any shorter cyclic interval extends to such a basis and is therefore
injective.

Define
\[
 B_j=\{t v_j:t\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\}.
\]
Each \(B_j\) has \(q\) elements, and the preceding basis property is
exactly the cyclic interval direct-factor condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharpness for every integer side size]
\label{cor:all-q-sharp}
For every \(d\ge2\) and every integer \(q\ge1\), there is a finite
abelian observer with
\[
 t_h=q^{d-1}
\]
and a finite regular \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable target family satisfying
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
For \(q\ge2\) use Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-interval-frame} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:interval-realization}; \(q=1\) is the trivial
one-state case.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The MDS-type systems below are therefore sufficient but not necessary for
sharpness. They require injectivity for every small subset of role positions,
whereas grammatical substitutability only forces the cyclic-interval
patterns created by contiguous factors. This distinction is already visible
at \(d=3,q=6\): Proposition~\ref{prop:q6-computational} rules out the
strong four-way abelian direct-factor system, while
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-q-sharp} still attains \(VC_4=6\) over
\((\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z)^2\).
\end{remark}
\begin{definition}[MDS-type direct-factor system]
\label{def:mds-factor}
Let \(d\ge2\).  An abelian group \(G\) of order \(q^{d-1}\) has a
\emph{\((d+1,d-1;q)\) direct-factor system} if there are indexed subsets
\[
 B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_d\subseteq G,\qquad |B_j|=q,
\]
such that for every \(J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,d\}\) with
\(|J|\le d-1\), the sum map
\[
 \prod_{j\in J} B_j\longrightarrow G,\qquad
 (b_j)_{j\in J}\longmapsto\sum_{j\in J}b_j
\]
is injective.
For \(|J|=d-1\), it is therefore bijective.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Direct-factor systems multiply]
\label{prop:factor-product}
Fix \(d\ge2\).  If \(G_1\) has a
\((d+1,d-1;q_1)\) direct-factor system and \(G_2\) has a
\((d+1,d-1;q_2)\) direct-factor system, then \(G_1\times G_2\) has a
\[
 (d+1,d-1;q_1q_2)
\]
direct-factor system.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each role \(j\), take the Cartesian product of the two role sets:
\[
 B_j:=B_{j,1}\times B_{j,2}.
\]
For any \(J\) with \(|J|\le d-1\), equality of two sums in
\(G_1\times G_2\) gives equality in both coordinates.  Injectivity in each
factor system then recovers every selected role value.  Each \(B_j\) has
size \(q_1q_2\), while
\[
 |G_1\times G_2|
 =(q_1q_2)^{d-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to arcs over groups and orthogonal arrays}

The subgroup-valued special case of
Definition~\ref{def:mds-factor} is classical finite geometry in different
language.  Let \(m=d-1\).  If every \(B_j\) is a subgroup of an abelian group
\(A\) of order \(q^m\), and every \(m\) of the \(m+2=d+1\) subgroups sum
directly to \(A\), then their coset partitions form a regular set of two
mutually orthogonal diagonal semilattices in the terminology of
Bailey--Cameron--Kinyon--Praeger.  In the elementary-abelian case the same
configuration is an \((m+2)\)-arc in
\(\mathrm{PG}(m-1,q)\).  Their normal-rational-curve construction is exactly
the extended Vandermonde construction used below.

Our direct-factor systems are deliberately more general: the role sets
\(B_j\) need only be subsets, not subgroups.  This distinction matters for
the exact observer-size spectrum.

\begin{proposition}[The \(d=3\) sharpness problem as a \(K_4\) factorization problem]
\label{prop:k4-factorization}
Let \(G\) be an abelian group of order \(q^2\).  A
\((4,2;q)\) direct-factor system
\[
 B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3\subseteq G,\qquad |B_i|=q,
\]
is equivalently a \(K_4\) in the associated graph of simultaneous
factorizations: the four vertices are the \(B_i\), and two vertices are
adjacent exactly when
\[
 G=B_i+B_j
\]
is an exact factorization.

Thus the first unresolved sharpness cases for \(d=3\) can be attacked as
maximum-clique questions in the factorization graphs studied by Szab\'o.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because \(|B_i||B_j|=q^2=|G|\), injectivity of the sum map
\(B_i\times B_j\to G\) is equivalent to its being bijective, i.e. to
\(G=B_i+B_j\) being a factorization.  Requiring this for every pair is
precisely the complete graph \(K_4\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Szab\'o's simultaneous-factorization examples already exhibit a \(K_3\)
inside a group of type \((4,4)\) and a \(K_{2,2}\) inside a group of type
\((3,3)\).  The grammar-learning sharpness problem asks for a different
extremal object: a \(K_4\) on four equal-size factors in a group of square
order.  This makes exhaustive-search and factorization-graph methods directly
relevant to the exact \(VC_4\) spectrum.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Computer-assisted \(q=6\) obstruction for abelian direct factors]
\label{prop:q6-computational}
No abelian group of order \(36\) contains four \(6\)-element subsets
\[
 B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3
\]
such that every pair \(B_i+B_j\) is an exact factorization of the group.
Equivalently, no abelian-group \((4,2;6)\) direct-factor system exists, even
when the role sets are arbitrary subsets rather than subgroups.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Exhaustive verification]
There are four abelian groups of order \(36\), up to isomorphism:
\[
 C_{36},\qquad C_{18}\times C_2,\qquad C_{12}\times C_3,\qquad C_6\times C_6.
\]
Every candidate factor may be translated independently, so it is enough to
enumerate \(6\)-subsets containing \(0\), of which there are
\[
 \binom{35}{5}=324632
\]
in each group.

For two such subsets \(A,B\), since \(|A||B|=|G|=36\), the sum map
\(A\times B\to G\) is bijective exactly when
\[
 (A-A)\cap(B-B)=\{0\}.
\]
Thus a \(K_4\) witness is exactly four nonzero difference masks that are
pairwise disjoint. The exhaustive search collapses candidates having the
same difference mask and performs this set-packing test. The numbers of
distinct masks are respectively
\[
 15950,\quad 17402,\quad 16147,\quad 17320,
\]
and no \(K_4\) occurs in any of the four cases.

The reproducible implementation and output are stored in
\texttt{tools/q6\_k4\_factorization\_search.py} and
\texttt{computations/q6\_k4\_factorization.txt}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This proposition closes the stronger all-pairs abelian direct-factor route
for \(d=3,q=6\), but Corollary~\ref{cor:all-q-sharp} simultaneously shows
\[
 \mathfrak V_3(36)=6
\]
using the weaker cyclic-interval system over
\((\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z)^2\). Thus the computation pinpoints an algebraic
gap: MDS/all-pairs factorization is strictly stronger than what contiguous
grammar factors need for statistical sharpness.
\end{remark}
\begin{proposition}[Obstructions for subgroup sharpness witnesses]
\label{prop:subgroup-sharpness-obstruction}
Suppose a sharpness witness in Definition~\ref{def:mds-factor} can be chosen
with all \(B_j\) subgroups.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(d=3\) and \(q\equiv2\pmod4\), no such subgroup witness exists.
\item If \(d\ge4\), write a subgroup witness as a regular MODS configuration
of dimension \(m=d-1\) and order \(q\).  Then the common order-\(q\) base
group must admit three fixed-point-free automorphisms whose product is the
identity.  Equivalently, in its decomposition into cyclic prime-power
factors, every factor of \(2\)-power or \(3\)-power order must occur with
multiplicity greater than one.  In particular, no subgroup witness exists
when
\[
 q\equiv2\pmod4
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 q\equiv\pm3\pmod9.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(d=3\), four subgroup directions give two mutually orthogonal group
squares of order \(q\).  Bailey--Cameron--Kinyon--Praeger, using the
Hall--Paige theorem, show that when \(q\equiv2\pmod4\) there can be only one
group square in such a mutually orthogonal family.

Now let \(d\ge4\), so \(m=d-1\ge3\).  Any \(m\) of the \(m+2\) subgroup
roles form an internal direct product equal to the ambient group.  Choosing
one such \(m\)-tuple as coordinates identifies the configuration with a
regular set of two mutually orthogonal diagonal semilattices.  Replacing one
coordinate subgroup at a time shows that all role subgroups are isomorphic.
Propositions 2 and 3 of Bailey--Cameron--Kinyon--Praeger then give the stated
fixed-point-free automorphism condition and its finite-abelian-group
classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:subgroup-sharpness-obstruction} does \emph{not} rule
out exact sharpness for the excluded values of \(q\) in the present paper,
because our direct-factor systems permit non-subgroup role sets.  It says
instead that any such sharpness witness must leave the classical
arc-over-groups regime.  Thus values such as \(d=3,q=6\), and more generally
the congruence obstructions above, are natural test cases for genuinely
nonlinear subset factorizations.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Direct-factor realization theorem]
\label{thm:factor-realization}
If \(G\) admits a \((d+1,d-1;q)\) direct-factor system, then there are a
finite alphabet, a homomorphism
\[
 h:\Sigma^*\to G
\]
with \(t_h=|G|=q^{d-1}\), and a family of finite regular
\((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages whose arity-\(d\) incidence relations
satisfy
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Index
\[
 B_r=\{\beta_r(i):i\in[q]\}\qquad(0\le r\le d).
\]
Use pairwise disjoint role alphabets
\[
 c_i,r_i,s_{i,1},\ldots,s_{i,d-1}\quad(i\in[q])
\]
and
\[
 x_{r,j}\quad(1\le r\le d,\ j\in[q]).
\]
Set
\[
 h(c_i)=h(r_i)=\beta_0(i),\qquad
 h(s_{i,t})=0,\qquad
 h(x_{r,j})=\beta_r(j).
\]
Because the sum map for any \(d-1\) of the \(B_r\) is onto \(G\), every
group element is represented by a nonempty word, so \(t_h=|G|\).

For \(\mathbf i=(i_0,\ldots,i_d)\in[q]^{d+1}\), define
\[
 w_{\mathbf i}
 =
 c_{i_0}x_{1,i_1}s_{i_0,1}x_{2,i_2}\cdots
 s_{i_0,d-1}x_{d,i_d}r_{i_0},
\]
and, for arbitrary \(S\subseteq[q]^{d+1}\), let
\[
 L_S:=\{w_{\mathbf i}:\mathbf i\in S\}.
\]
These languages are finite.

The verification of tuple substitutability follows the rigid role skeleton.
A common sentence context fixes all role positions outside the tuple holes.
If \(i_0\) is known, the type of every tuple component containing at most
\(d-1\) variable roles determines all variable indices in that component by
the direct-factor property.  If every context-specific marker lies inside
the holes, an end component contains \(\beta_0(i_0)\) together with at most
\(d-2\) variable-role values; injectivity for at most \(d-1\) role sets then
recovers \(i_0\), after which the preceding argument applies.  The only
remaining one-hole case is a factor spanning the entire skeleton; two
accepted such factors have the same singleton distribution
\(\{(\epsilon,\epsilon)\}\), so substitutability again holds.

Finally define
\[
 E_i=c_i\blank_1s_{i,1}\blank_2\cdots
 s_{i,d-1}\blank_dr_i
\]
and
\[
 A_r=\{x_{r,j}:j\in[q]\}.
\]
Then
\[
 R_{L_S}^{(d)}(E_{i_0},x_{1,i_1},\ldots,x_{d,i_d})=1
 \Longleftrightarrow
 (i_0,\ldots,i_d)\in S.
\]
Thus a \((d+1)\)-box of side \(q\) is shattered.  Since
\(t_h=q^{d-1}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} gives the matching upper
bound.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Finite-field sharpness]\label{thm:higher-sharp}
Fix \(d\ge2\).  Let \(q\) be a prime power, with \(q\ge d\) when \(d\ge3\).
There are a finite
alphabet \(\Sigma\), a homomorphism
\[
h:\Sigma^*\to (\mathbb F_q^{\,d-1},+)
\]
with
\[
t_h=q^{d-1},
\]
and a family of finite regular, \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages whose
arity-\(d\) incidence relations shatter a box of side size \(q\). Consequently
\[
\VC_{d+1}=q=t_h^{1/(d-1)}
\]
for the corresponding full relation class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose vectors
\[
a_1,\ldots,a_d,b\in\mathbb F_q^{d-1}
\]
such that every \(d-1\) distinct roles are linearly independent.  For
\(d\ge3\), take \(d+1\) columns from the extended Vandermonde family
\[
 (1,t,t^2,\ldots,t^{d-2})^{\mathsf T}\quad(t\in\mathbb F_q),
 \qquad
 (0,\ldots,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Any \(d-1\) selected columns are independent, and \(q+1\ge d+1\) by
\(q\ge d\).  For \(d=2\), any three nonzero one-dimensional role vectors
suffice because the required independent subsets have size one.
In particular, \(a_1,\ldots,a_{d-1}\) form a basis.

Use pairwise disjoint role alphabets consisting of symbols
\[
c_i,r_i,s_{i,1},\ldots,s_{i,d-1}\quad(i\in\mathbb F_q)
\]
and
\[
x_{r,j}\quad(1\le r\le d,\ j\in\mathbb F_q).
\]
Define the letter images by
\[
h(x_{r,j})=j a_r,\qquad
h(c_i)=h(r_i)=i b,\qquad
h(s_{i,r})=0.
\]
Because \(a_1,\ldots,a_{d-1}\) span the group and a nonempty separator maps to
zero, every group element is realized by a nonempty word; hence
\(t_h=q^{d-1}\).

For
\(\mathbf i=(i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_d)\in\mathbb F_q^{d+1}\) define the rigid codeword
\[
w_{\mathbf i}
=c_{i_0}x_{1,i_1}s_{i_0,1}x_{2,i_2}\cdots
s_{i_0,d-1}x_{d,i_d}r_{i_0}.
\]
For arbitrary \(S\subseteq\mathbb F_q^{d+1}\) let
\[
L_S:=\{w_{\mathbf i}:\mathbf i\in S\}.
\]
These languages are finite and therefore regular.

We verify \((d,h)\)-tuple substitutability. Project every codeword to its
role skeleton
\[
C,X_1,S_1,X_2,\ldots,S_{d-1},X_d,R.
\]
All role alphabets are disjoint, so two tuple occurrences with a common
sentence context have the same interval positions in this skeleton. Their
common context fixes every variable \(i_r\) whose \(X_r\) position lies outside
the holes. It also fixes \(i_0\) whenever at least one of the context-specific
marker positions \(C,S_1,\ldots,S_{d-1},R\) lies outside the holes.

Consider two such tuples with equal componentwise \(h\)-types. Once \(i_0\)
is known, every component containing at most \(d-1\) variable positions is
determined by its \(h\)-type, because the corresponding subset of the
\(a_r\) is linearly independent. Thus all selected variable values are
recovered component by component unless one single interval contains all
\(d\) variable positions.

It remains only to justify recovery of \(i_0\) when all context-specific markers
lie inside the holes. With at least two holes, a nonempty gap must then
contain a variable position. The two end components contain respectively
\(c_{i_0}\) and \(r_{i_0}\). If one end component contains no variable position,
its type \(i_0b\) determines \(i_0\). Otherwise each end component contains at
most \(d-2\) variable positions; since \(b\) together with any at most \(d-2\) of
the \(a_r\) is linearly independent, equality of the end-component type again
determines \(i_0\). Once \(i_0\) is known, the preceding componentwise argument
recovers all selected variable values.

The only remaining possibility is a single interval containing all \(d\)
variable positions. It necessarily contains every internal separator
\(s_{i_0,r}\), so the factor itself fixes \(i_0\) literally; there is no variable
coordinate left outside the factor. Any two such same-type factors that
share a context therefore have the same singleton distribution. Thus in all
cases, equal tuple type plus one common context implies equal tuple
distributions, proving \((d,h)\)-tuple substitutability.

Finally, for each \(i\in\mathbb F_q\) put
\[
E_i=c_i\blank_1s_{i,1}\blank_2\cdots
s_{i,d-1}\blank_dr_i,
\]
and for coordinate \(r\) put
\[
A_r=\{x_{r,j}:j\in\mathbb F_q\}.
\]
Then
\[
R_{L_S}^{(d)}(E_{i_0},x_{1,i_1},\ldots,x_{d,i_d})=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_d)\in S.
\]
As \(S\) is arbitrary, the \((d+1)\)-box
\(\{E_i:i\in\mathbb F_q\}\times A_1\times\cdots\times A_d\) is shattered.
The lower bound \(\VC_{d+1}\ge q\) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} give equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For \(d=2\) the construction can be read over the cyclic group \(C_t\) for every
\(t\ge1\): use codewords \(c_i x_j s_i y_k r_i\) with
\(h(c_i)=h(r_i)=h(x_i)=h(y_i)=i\) and \(h(s_i)=0\). Hence the \(VC_3\le t_h\)
bound is sharp for all observer sizes in arity two. The finite-field theorem
is used only to obtain a clean uniform construction in arbitrary arity.
\end{remark}


\begin{corollary}[Composite alphabet sizes from prime-power factors]
\label{cor:composite-sharp}
Fix \(d\ge3\), and factor
\[
 q=\prod_{j=1}^m q_j
\]
into pairwise coprime prime powers.  If every \(q_j\ge d\), then there is an
abelian group observer of size
\[
 q^{d-1}
\]
and a finite regular \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable target family with
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
In particular, for \(d=3\) the cardinality upper bound is attained for every
\(q\ge3\) that is either odd or divisible by \(4\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each prime-power factor \(q_j\) has the finite-field direct-factor system
from Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-sharp}.  Repeatedly apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:factor-product}, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:factor-realization}.  For \(d=3\), every odd prime power is
at least \(3\), and every \(2\)-power factor is admissible exactly when its
order is at least \(4\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The all-pairs MDS/direct-factor spectrum remains a finite-factorization and
design problem, but it is no longer the sharpness spectrum for the grammar
class itself.  The cyclic-interval realization above shows that contiguous
grammar factors require strictly less algebraic independence.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact worst-case observer-budget law]
\label{thm:observer-budget-law}
For \(d\ge1\) and \(T\ge1\), define
\[
 \mathfrak V_d(T):=
 \sup\left\{
 \VC_{d+1}\!\left(\Rclass_{d,h}^{(d)}\right):
 |h(\Sigma^+)|\le T
 \right\},
\]
where the supremum ranges over finite alphabets and finite-monoid typings.
Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak V_1(T)=T},
\]
and for every \(d\ge2\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak V_d(T)
 =\left\lfloor T^{1/(d-1)}\right\rfloor.
 }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(d=1\), Corollary~\ref{cor:cfg-upper} gives
\(\mathfrak V_1(T)\le T\), and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-cfg} attains equality.

Now let \(d\ge2\) and put
\[
 q=\left\lfloor T^{1/(d-1)}\right\rfloor.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} gives the upper bound
\(\mathfrak V_d(T)\le q\). On the other hand,
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-q-sharp} supplies an observer with
\[
 t_h=q^{d-1}\le T
\]
whose induced arity-\(d\) class has \(VC_{d+1}=q\).
Thus the upper bound is attained.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The formula exhibits a sharp arity transition. Unary factors and
two-component tuples use the observer budget linearly:
\[
 \mathfrak V_1(T)=\mathfrak V_2(T)=T.
\]
Starting with three-component tuples, the simultaneous lower-arity
substitutability constraints force the exact root law
\[
 \mathfrak V_d(T)=\lfloor T^{1/(d-1)}\rfloor.
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact padding to an arbitrary observer size]
\label{prop:arbitrary-budget}
Fix \(d\ge2\), \(T\ge1\), and put
\[
 q=\left\lfloor T^{1/(d-1)}\right\rfloor.
\]
There is a finite monoid observer with exactly
\[
 t_h=T
\]
and a family of finite regular \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable languages
whose arity-\(d\) incidence relations satisfy
\[
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Start with the interval-frame construction from
Corollary~\ref{cor:all-q-sharp}, whose image group \(G\) has
\(|G|=q^{d-1}\). If \(|G|=T\), there is nothing to add.
Otherwise adjoin elements
\[
 z_1,\ldots,z_{T-|G|}
\]
and define
\[
 gz_i=z_i g=z_i\quad(g\in G),\qquad
 z_i z_j=z_{\max\{i,j\}}.
\]
Together with the identity of \(G\), this is a monoid of size \(T\).
Add fresh letters \(e_i\) with \(h(e_i)=z_i\), while retaining the old
typing on the original alphabet. Thus every monoid element is realized by a
nonempty word and \(t_h=T\).

The target languages use none of the fresh letters. Every factor containing
a fresh letter therefore has empty target distribution and cannot create a
new substitutability premise with an occurring factor. The old target family
remains \((d,h)\)-tuple-substitutable and retains its shattered box of
side \(q\). Theorem~\ref{thm:block-bound} gives the matching upper bound.
\end{proof}
\section{\(PAC_n\) model audit: disputed sufficiency and safe consequences}
\label{sec:pac-audit}

The combinatorial results above are independent of any PAC definition.  The
statistical interpretation is more delicate and is kept logically separate.

Kuriyama--Takeuchi's 2015 definition uses full coordinate slices through
sampled anchors.  Their paper claimed the necessity implication
\[
 PAC_n\text{-learnable}\Longrightarrow VC_n<\infty.
\]
Takeuchi's 2020 note shows that this implication fails for the original
\(PAC_2\) definition as stated: the circle class has infinite \(VC_2\) but is
\(PAC_2\)-learnable under the old full-slice Definition~3.  The 2020 note
therefore replaces the full slice by a support-sensitive observation on
finite or countable domains.

Chernikov--Towsner (2025) initially used a different high-arity learning
definition.  Malliaris (2025) gives a counterexample to the main theorem of
that first version.  In version~2, Chernikov--Towsner change the learning
definition back to the Kobayashi--Kuriyama--Takeuchi full-slice model and
state in Theorem~6.5 that
\[
 VC_k(F)<\infty
 \Longrightarrow
 F\text{ is properly }PAC_k\text{-learnable}.
\]
Malliaris explicitly says that she has not verified whether this revised
statement is correct, and points out an error in the proposed proof: the
packing centers used by the argument depend on the unknown underlying
measure, so the learner cannot in general be assumed to know them.

Accordingly, the present paper does \emph{not} use Chernikov--Towsner
Theorem~6.5 as an established theorem.  Unconditional results below include the combinatorial \(VC_k\) bounds,
an explicit proper semantic one-sided \(PAC_k\) learner, and direct finite-support
lower bounds.  Any PAC sufficiency statement that depends on Theorem~6.5 is marked
conditional.

\subsection{Support-sensitive observation on countable products}

Let \(k\ge2\) and
\[
 X=X_1\times\cdots\times X_k
\]
with each \(X_i\) finite or countable.  For a product probability measure
\(\mu=\mu_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_k\), put
\[
 S_i(\mu):=\{a\in X_i:\mu_i(\{a\})>0\},
 \qquad
 S_\mu:=\prod_{i=1}^k S_i(\mu).
\]
For an anchor \(\bar a\in X\), define
\[
 D_{k,\mu}(\bar a):=D_k(\bar a)\cap S_\mu.
\]
For \(k=2\), this is Takeuchi's support-sensitive repair.

\begin{definition}[Support-sensitive \(PAC_k\)]
\label{def:support-pac}
A concept class \(F\subseteq\mathcal P(X)\) is
\emph{properly support-\(PAC_k\)-learnable} if there is one learning function
with image in \(F\) which, for every product probability measure \(\mu\),
learns from restrictions to unions of the sets \(D_{k,\mu}(\bar a)\), with
the usual \((\eps,\delta)\) guarantee and a sample bound independent of
\(\mu\) and of the target.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Independent finite-support lower bounds}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-support information lower bound]
\label{prop:finite-support-lower}
Let \(F\) shatter a \(k\)-box
\[
 A_1\times\cdots\times A_k,\qquad |A_i|=D,
\]
and put the uniform product measure on this finite box.  Consider any learner
whose observations from \(m\) sampled anchors are restricted to the union of
the support-sensitive coordinate slices through those anchors.  If, for every
target in \(F\), the learner has error at most \(\eps\) with probability at
least \(1-\delta\), then
\[
 m\ge
 D\left(1-\sqrt[k]{2(\eps+\delta)}\right)
\]
whenever the right-hand side is positive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to consider \(m<D\).  Choose one target concept for each of the
\(2^{D^k}\) traces on the shattered box, and then choose the trace uniformly
at random.  Also draw the \(m\) anchors independently from the uniform
product measure.

For coordinate \(i\), let \(U_i\subseteq A_i\) be the set of values that
appear in the sampled anchors.  Then \(|U_i|\le m\).  The rectangle
\[
 W:=(A_1\setminus U_1)\times\cdots\times(A_k\setminus U_k)
\]
is completely unobserved and satisfies
\[
 |W|\ge(D-m)^k.
\]
Conditional on the anchors and on all observed labels, the random target
labels on \(W\) remain independent unbiased bits.  Whatever hypothesis the
learner outputs, its conditional expected number of mistakes on \(W\) is
therefore \(|W|/2\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{err}]
 \ge
 \frac12\left(\frac{D-m}{D}\right)^k.
\]
The assumed PAC guarantee gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\operatorname{err}]\le\eps+\delta.
\]
Combining the two inequalities yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional support-sensitive sufficiency]
\label{prop:conditional-support-pac}
Assume the following full-slice sufficiency statement for countable product
domains:
\[
 VC_k(F)<\infty
 \Longrightarrow
 F\text{ is properly }PAC_k\text{-learnable},
\]
with a sample bound depending only on \(k,VC_k(F),\eps,\delta\).
Then every class of finite \(VC_k\)-dimension on a finite or countable
product domain is properly support-\(PAC_k\)-learnable with the same form of
uniform bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mu\) and restrict the concept class to the support product
\(S_\mu\).  Restriction does not increase \(VC_k\), and on \(S_\mu\) the
support-sensitive slices are exactly the ordinary full coordinate slices.
Apply the assumed full-slice learner to the restricted class and choose a
global extension of its proper restricted hypothesis.  Since
\(\mu(S_\mu)=1\), the extension does not change the error.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of Takeuchi's Problem 11]
Under the disputed sufficiency statement above, Proposition
\ref{prop:conditional-support-pac} would answer Takeuchi's 2020 Problem~11
affirmatively.  Because the only general sufficiency proof currently being
invoked is the proof criticized by Malliaris, this paper does not claim that
Problem~11 is solved.  A direct proof for the fixed-\(h\) incidence classes
is an open task.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional lower bound for the sharp CFG family]
\label{cor:sharp-support-pac-lower}
For the sharp family in Proposition~\ref{prop:sharp-cfg}, where
\(\VC_2=t\), every support-sensitive \(PAC_2\) learner, if one exists,
satisfies
\[
 N_{\eps,\delta}
 \ge
 t\left(1-\sqrt{2(\eps+\delta)}\right)
\]
whenever the right-hand side is positive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional higher-arity lower bound]
\label{cor:higher-support-pac-lower}
For the sharp finite-field family of Theorem~\ref{thm:higher-sharp},
\[
 t_h=q^{d-1},
 \qquad
 \VC_{d+1}=q.
\]
Hence every support-sensitive \(PAC_{d+1}\) learner, if one exists, satisfies
\[
 N_{\eps,\delta}
 \ge
 q\left(1-\sqrt[d+1]{2(\eps+\delta)}\right)
 =
 t_h^{1/(d-1)}
 \left(1-\sqrt[d+1]{2(\eps+\delta)}\right)
\]
whenever the right-hand side is positive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Anchor complexity is not label-volume complexity]
\label{rem:anchor-volume}
Higher-arity PAC sample complexity counts sampled anchors, not the total
number of revealed labels.  On a countably infinite product domain, one
full coordinate slice---and likewise one support-sensitive slice when the
atomic support is infinite---may contain infinitely many labelled points.
Thus the PAC comparison concerns observation geometry, not finite-bit sample
volume.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact reconstruction versus statistical complexity}

The companion fixed-\(h\) CFG manuscript contains an operator-independent
lower bound for one fixed two-element zero-monoid observer \(h_\circ\).
Its nested finite targets \(\mathcal T_n^-\subsetneq\mathcal T_n\) have reduced
CFG representations of polynomial size and ordinary thickness \(O(n)\), while
every characteristic sample for \(\mathcal T_n\), for any set-driven operator
having characteristic samples for both targets, has encoded size at least
\[
  5\cdot 2^n-1.
\]
The typed-box theorem gives a qualitatively different complexity statement
for the incidence-relation view of the very same fixed observer.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-observer exact-vs-\(VC_2\) separation]
\label{thm:observation-separation}
There is a fixed two-element monoid homomorphism \(h_\circ\) such that:

\begin{enumerate}
\item the incidence-relation class induced by
\(\mathcal C_{h_\circ}^{\mathrm{cf}}\) satisfies
\[
  \VC_2\le2;
\]

\item no set-driven exact positive-data reconstruction operator having
characteristic samples throughout
\(\mathcal C_{h_\circ}^{\mathrm{cf}}\) admits a characteristic-data bound
polynomial in reduced CFG size and ordinary grammar thickness.  More
concretely, the companion lower-bound family forces characteristic-sample
size at least \(5\cdot2^n-1\) while both grammar size and ordinary thickness
are polynomial in \(n\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (1) is Corollary~\ref{cor:cfg-upper} with
\(|h_\circ(\Sigma^+)|\le2\).  Part (2) is exactly the set-driven
ordinary-thickness lower bound of the companion fixed-\(h\) CFG manuscript.
\end{proof}

Thus under one fixed finite observer, exact positive-data reconstruction can
require exponentially large characteristic exposure while the induced
context--factor incidence class has uniformly constant \(VC_2\).  There is no contradiction: exact
identification receives positive target strings, whereas \(PAC_2\) receives
labelled coordinate slices of the incidence relation.


The architecture separation from the companion observer-tradeoff manuscript
gives a second, orthogonal comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Architecture is invisible to the trivial-observer VC bound]
\label{thm:architecture-vc}
Let \(h_{\mathrm{triv}}\) be the one-state observer. Then
\[
 \VC_2\!\left(\Rclass_{1,h_{\mathrm{triv}}}^{(1)}\right)\le1,
 \qquad
 \VC_3\!\left(\Rclass_{2,h_{\mathrm{triv}}}^{(2)}\right)\le1.
\]
Nevertheless, on the same finite target family \(X_{k,r}\) and under this
same observer, the companion exact-reconstruction architectures have
characteristic-sample cardinalities
\[
 k^r
 \qquad\text{(CFG constructor)}
\]
and
\[
 1+r(k-1)
 \qquad\text{(fan-out-two MCFG constructor)}.
\]
Hence higher-arity VC complexity at the coarsest possible observer does not
determine exact positive-data reconstruction cost, and it does not detect the
exponential-vs-linear architecture separation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the trivial observer, \(t_h=1\). Corollary~\ref{cor:cfg-upper} gives
the \(VC_2\) bound, and Theorem~\ref{thm:typed-box} with \(d=2\) gives the
\(VC_3\) bound. The two exact characteristic-sample formulas are the
same-target, same-observer architecture theorem for \(X_{k,r}\) in the
companion manuscript on minimum observer size.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This separates three resources that are easy to conflate:
observer granularity, statistical box complexity, and reconstruction
architecture.  Holding the observer fixed at one state minimizes the first
resource and forces the ambient higher-arity VC bounds to one, yet changing
only the grammar architecture still changes exact positive-data requirements
from \(k^r\) to \(1+r(k-1)\).
\end{remark}

\section{Next problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fixed-observer sharpness.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:observer-budget-law} solves the worst-case spectrum over
all observers of a given size. Determine, for a \emph{fixed} marked monoid
\((\operatorname{im}h,h(\Sigma^+))\), when the upper bound
\[
 \VC_{d+1}\le\operatorname{rpc}_{d-1}(h)
\]
is itself attainable by a tuple-substitutable grammar family. The
\(q=6\) comparison shows that full MDS factorization is not the right
criterion.

\item \textbf{Effective grammar-proper PAC sufficiency.} The direct
semantic learner of Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-proper-semantic-pac}
has explicit sample bounds and avoids the disputed
Chernikov--Towsner proof. Determine when its positive
Horn closure admits an effective finite CFG/MCFG representation,
and whether hypotheses can be chosen inside the
fixed-\(h\) grammar-representable subclasses.

\item \textbf{Necessity-direction audit.} Reconcile Takeuchi's 2020
counterexample with the \(PAC_n\Rightarrow VC_n<\infty\) claim reused in
Chernikov--Towsner's Corollary~6.6.  The sufficiency direction used in this
paper is independent of that issue.

\item \textbf{Observer refinement.} Compare the monotonic behavior of
\(VC_{d+1}\) under typing refinement with exact reconstruction cost.

\item \textbf{Algebraic classification of rectangular capacity.}
Determine \(\operatorname{rpc}_r(h)\) for natural finite groups, aperiodic
monoids, and product observers, and identify which pseudovariety properties
control its asymptotic growth.

\item \textbf{Sharpen the independent PAC bound.} Determine
whether the direct \(O(t_h^d\eps^{-1}\log(1/\delta))\)
anchor bound can be reduced using lower-arity substitutability,
the observer algebra, or rectangular product capacity,
and compare it to the independent finite-box lower bound.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to earlier grammatical-inference work}

Earlier work already gives probabilistic or stochastic learning results for
restricted CFG/MCFG families under distributional assumptions, including
Clark, Shibata--Yoshinaka, and Case--Yoshinaka--Zeugmann.  On the algebraic
side, subgroup-valued sharpness witnesses meet the established theory of
arcs over groups, mutually orthogonal diagonal semilattices, and orthogonal
arrays developed by Bailey--Cameron--Kinyon--Praeger. The intended
contribution here is not merely probabilistic grammar learnability, but the
structural chain
\[
\text{finite-monoid typed substitutability}
\Longrightarrow
\text{forbidden higher-arity boxes}
\Longrightarrow
\text{bounded }VC_n.
\]
together with its comparison to exact reconstruction under the same observer.

Exhaustive cyclic-interval sharpness audit
Date: 2026-10-05

Purpose
-------
Search for a counterexample to the claim that every arbitrary sublanguage
L_S of the rigid cyclic-interval codebook is (d,h)-tuple-substitutable.

The test enumerates every legal e-hole occurrence for every 1 <= e <= d.
For every pair of distinct tuples of the same componentwise h-type sharing
a common full-codebook context, it checks the exact finite condition required
for substitutability to survive every arbitrary choice of S.

This is a computational audit, not a proof.

Construction
------------
Observer group: (Z/qZ)^(d-1).

Universal interval-frame role vectors:
  v_0,...,v_{d-2} = standard basis,
  v_{d-1}         = (1,...,1),
  v_d             = alternating 0/1 vector with final coordinate 1.

Rigid codeword:
  c_i0 x_1,i1 s_i0,1 x_2,i2 ... s_i0,d-1 x_d,id r_i0.

  e = tuple arity audited
  words = number of full-codebook words q^(d+1)
  tuples = distinct tuple objects
  tcells = distinct (type,context) cells
  occ = legal tuple occurrences
  shared = type/context cells containing >1 distinct tuple
  pairs = distinct same-type tuple pairs actually requiring the universal-S check

Interpretation
--------------
No counterexample was found in any audited case.

A useful structural pattern is visible in the counts: for every audited
case, all nontrivial same-type/common-context collisions at arity e>=2
disappear entirely. The only collisions occur for e=1, and the exact
arbitrary-sublanguage condition still passes in every case. Inspection of
representative e=1 collisions shows the exceptional pairs have singleton
full-codebook distributions, exactly as the manuscript proof intends.

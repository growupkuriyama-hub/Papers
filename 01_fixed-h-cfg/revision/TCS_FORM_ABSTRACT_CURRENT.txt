We study positive-data learning of context-free languages under a fixed homomorphism h into a finite monoid. Nonempty factors of the same h-type sharing a context must have identical context sets. For each fixed h, a set-driven operator reconstructs every context-free h-substitutable target from finitely many positive witnesses. A separate conservative sequential learner identifies every nonempty target in the limit, with polynomial-time reconstruction and updates.

Characteristic-data bounds depend on shortest yields in a type-refined target grammar, which may be exponentially larger than ordinary grammar thickness. The classical characterization of locally trivial finite semigroups shows that positive h-types are determined by a fixed prefix-suffix window exactly when h(Sigma+) is locally trivial. Ordinary grammar thickness suffices in this case and, more generally, when the positive image is a homomorphic image of a subsemigroup of a product of a locally trivial finite semigroup and a finite group. The union of locally trivial slices coincides with Yoshinaka's fixed-window hierarchy.

For linear targets, characteristic data are polynomial in linear grammar size for every fixed h. We also exhibit nonregular linear and nonlinear context-free targets outside every fixed-window class, while the one-bracket Dyck language lies outside every fixed-typing class.

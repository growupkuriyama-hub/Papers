\section{Response to Reviewers ---
TCS-D-26-00494}\label{response-to-reviewers-tcs-d-26-00494}

Manuscript: ``Distributional Learning of Context-Free Languages under
Fixed Finite-Monoid Typing''

Author: Takayuki Kuriyama

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their detailed and constructive reports. Their comments led us to correct the learning algorithm, remove an unused typing component, repair the terminal-rule completeness argument, and substantially reorganize the manuscript.

Throughout this response, unqualified section, theorem, proposition, lemma,
and corollary numbers refer to the revised manuscript. References to the
submitted manuscript are explicitly marked ``submitted'' or ``former'';
reviewer quotations retain their original numbering. Quoted excerpts reproduce
the reviewers' wording; list formatting and LaTeX notation may be normalized
typographically, and omissions are indicated by \ldots{}.  Each reviewer
comment is numbered below for traceability.  Each response gives a structural
locator (section/theorem/lemma, which identifies the relevant paragraph) and
the page and numbered-line range in the current 24-page revised PDF, as
requested by the Editor.

\subsection{Overview of changes}\label{overview-of-changes}

The revision makes four structural corrections.  First, it separates the
set-driven reconstruction operator $\mathcal B_h$ from the conservative
sequential learner $\mathcal A_h$; the submitted learner could fail to
stabilize syntactically.  Second, the unused outer-context type annotations
have been removed, leaving only the yield type used by completeness.  Third,
the terminal-rule gap is repaired by adding a witness for every typed terminal
rule and using Rule (R3) before Rule (R4) when necessary.  Fourth,
fixed-$h$ substitutability is stated for nonempty internal fragments, with
$\lambda$ handled separately at the start symbol.

The quantitative claims have also been separated more carefully.  The general
fixed-$h$ analysis uses typed thickness and includes a set-driven lower bound
showing that ordinary source thickness does not suffice in general.  The
fixed-window case retains a characteristic-data bound polynomial in grammar
size and ordinary thickness, and the linear case retains a grammar-size bound.
These are finite-sample statements for $\mathcal B_h$; the corrected
sequential learner is stated separately as a Gold learner with polynomial-time
updates.

Finally, the presentation has been compressed and reordered.  Basic grammar
and learning terminology is consolidated in Section~2; reconstruction is
defined before target-side typing; the long fixed-window and linear
normalization arguments are moved to the appendices; the former boundary
sections are consolidated; and Section~9 now includes a nonregular linear
fixed-$h$ example outside every fixed-window class.  Repeated positioning and
explanatory prose were removed while theorem statements and the arguments
needed for the corrected learning and quantitative results were retained.
The submitted claims of order-independence, necessity of outer-context
typing, and polynomial time-and-data for the sequential linear learner have
been withdrawn.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{New material requiring review.}
Section~7 is new.  It isolates the quantitative limitation that became
necessary once the batch finite-sample result was separated from Gold
identification: for arbitrary fixed typings, ordinary source-grammar
thickness alone does not control characteristic-data size.  The section first
records the target-side typed-thickness bound and then gives a fixed
two-element typing with a set-driven lower bound in reduced CFG size and
ordinary thickness.  This material is not needed for correctness of the
learning theorem itself; it states the quantitative boundary of the corrected
claims.  The proof is self-contained; the detailed tree-parsing argument has been
moved to Appendix~D so that the main lower-bound section remains focused on
the construction and theorem.  The immutable Zenodo archive predates this
new lower-bound section.  A separate current Lean re-verification covers the
revised level-coded Appendix argument and lower-bound package; the paper proof
remains complete independently of the formalization.

\textbf{R1.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The main result of this submission
is an elegant generalization of the results of Clark and Eyraud (2007)
on the learning of substitutable context-free languages (SCFLs) and of
Yoshinaka (2008) on the learning of k,l-SCFLs. \ldots{} The only serious

\textbf{Response:} We thank the reviewer for identifying the central correctness problem.
Recomputing the batch grammar after every datum need not stabilize
syntactically.  The revision therefore separates a set-driven operator
$\mathcal B_h$ (Section~4.1) from a conservative sequential learner
$\mathcal A_h$ (Section~4.2), using the same rebuild-on-counterexample
pattern as Clark--Eyraud's SGL and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$-SGL.
Corollary~5.5 proves eventual syntactic stabilization.  We also now cite
Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka (2016, Appendix) at the point where their
delayed-rebuild phenomenon is relevant: a characteristic sample may
already have been seen while an earlier hypothesis persists, but the first
later positive example that it misses forces a reconstruction from the
accumulated data.  Polynomial characteristic-data claims are made for the
batch operator; the sequential learner is claimed only to have
polynomial-time updates together with Gold convergence.

target. This generic argument covers the reviewer's motivating example.

\section{Response to Reviewers ---
TCS-D-26-00494}\label{response-to-reviewers-tcs-d-26-00494}

Manuscript: ``Distributional Learning of Context-Free Languages under
Fixed Finite-Monoid Typing''

Author: Takayuki Kuriyama

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their detailed and constructive reports. Their comments led us to correct the learning algorithm, remove an unused typing component, repair the terminal-rule completeness argument, and substantially reorganize the manuscript.

Throughout this response, unqualified section, theorem, proposition, lemma,
and corollary numbers refer to the revised manuscript; references to the
submitted version are marked ``submitted'' or ``former''. Subheadings that
reproduce a reviewer's original section grouping (for example, ``Section 7''
or ``Sections 8--10'') refer to the submitted manuscript. For ease of reference, we number the Editor's comment as E.1 and the
reviewers' comments as R1.1--R1.37 and R2.1--R2.11. Reviewer quotations retain
the original section grouping, with only typographical normalization and
indicated omissions. Each response gives a structural locator in the revised
manuscript; exact page, paragraph, and numbered-line locators will be inserted
from the final typeset version before resubmission.

\subsection{Overview of changes}\label{overview-of-changes}

The revision makes four central corrections.  First, it separates the
set-driven reconstruction operator $\mathcal B_h$ from the conservative
sequential learner $\mathcal A_h$, so that the Gold learner now stabilizes
syntactically.  Second, the unused outer-context type annotations are removed;
only yield typing remains.  Third, the terminal-rule completeness gap is
repaired by adding witnesses for typed terminal rules and using Rule~(R3)
before Rule~(R4) when necessary.  Fourth, fixed-$h$ substitutability is stated
for nonempty internal fragments, with $\lambda$ handled separately at the
start symbol.

Several claims have been narrowed accordingly.  Presentation-order
independence is claimed only for the batch operator, not for the sequential
learner.  Polynomial characteristic-data bounds are stated for the batch
operator, while the sequential learner is stated separately to have Gold
convergence and polynomial-time updates.  The former claim that outer-context
types are necessary has been withdrawn.

The manuscript has also been substantially shortened and reorganized.  Basic
definitions are consolidated in Section~2, reconstruction now precedes the
target-side refinement used in completeness, long normalization proofs are
moved to the appendices, and the former three boundary sections are combined
as Section~9.  The main additions prompted by Reviewer~2 are the short
product-typing closure result in Proposition~3.2, the quantitative analysis in
Sections~6--8, and the nonregular linear example in Proposition~8.4.  Section~9
has otherwise been simplified.

\subsubsection{General assessment and learning
algorithm}\label{general-assessment-and-learning-algorithm}

\textbf{R1.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The main result of this submission
is an elegant generalization of the results of Clark and Eyraud (2007)
on the learning of substitutable context-free languages (SCFLs) and of
Yoshinaka (2008) on the learning of k,l-SCFLs. \ldots{} The only serious
error is in the definition of their learning algorithm, but this is
easily repairable by following prior work.''

\textbf{Response:} We thank the reviewer for identifying the central correctness problem.
Recomputing the batch grammar after every datum need not stabilize
syntactically.  The revision therefore separates a set-driven operator
$\mathcal B_h$ (Section~4.1) from a conservative sequential learner
$\mathcal A_h$ (Section~4.2), using the same conservative rebuild-on-missed-positive-example
pattern as Clark--Eyraud's SGL and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$-SGL.
Corollary~5.5 proves eventual syntactic stabilization.  We also now cite
Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka (2016, Appendix) at the point where their
delayed-rebuild phenomenon is relevant: a characteristic sample may
already have been seen while an earlier hypothesis persists, but the first
later positive example that it misses forces a reconstruction from the
accumulated data.  Polynomial characteristic-data claims are made for the
batch operator; the sequential learner is claimed only to have
polynomial-time updates together with Gold convergence.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 2.1; Section 4.2; Corollary 5.5; Theorem 7.2; Theorem 8.3 and the following clarification.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{General positioning of
fixed-\(h\)}{General positioning of fixed-h}}\label{general-positioning-of-fixed-h}

\textbf{R2.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``At a general level, the paper
offers an abstraction of the learning of Yoshinaka's
\((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages by replacing the fixed-length
window by an arbitrary recognizable congruence, and lifts the known
results accordingly. The abstraction places the problem in a powerful
and well-understood framework, but it requires a whole morphism to be
known in advance for each language, whereas the earlier settings fixed a
single uniform parameter valid across the entire class. The paper
acknowledges that learning the morphism is outside its scope, yet the
interest of the setting -- beyond providing a more abstract framework
for the study of \((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages -- remains
unclear. A promising direction would be to deepen the links with
pushdown automata, which Sections 8 to 10 already touch on. The links
with the work of Takada \ldots{} are also worth investigating. In a more
practical direction, this work could be read as extending automaton
typing \ldots{} to context-free substitutable languages, and the
conditions a typing must satisfy could be studied.''

\textbf{Response:} We clarified this point in the revision. A single
homomorphism $h$ is fixed before learning and defines the entire slice
$\mathcal C_h^{\mathrm{cf}}$; the same learner is uniform over that slice.
This is analogous to fixing one pair $(k,\ell)$ for a Yoshinaka class, and
Proposition~3.3 shows that $h_{k,\ell}$ is one concrete instance. Choosing a
useful fixed typing (or slice), or learning such a typing from data, remains a
separate problem outside the present theorem.

To address the broader motivation, the revision also makes the abstraction
compositional rather than relying only on target-specific examples.  Proposition~3.2
shows that if $L\in\mathsf{RS}_h$ and $K\in\mathsf{RS}_g$, then
$L\cap K\in\mathsf{RS}_{h\times g}$.
Consequently, intersecting a context-free fixed-$h$ target with any regular
language recognized by $g$ keeps the result inside the context-free
fixed-$(h\times g)$ slice.  The same proposition proves closure under
\emph{arbitrary} inverse homomorphism, including erasing homomorphisms, by
augmenting $h\circ\varphi$ with one bit recording whether a factor is erased.
Yoshinaka's Proposition~1 gives counterexamples to closure of the fixed-window
hierarchy under regular filtering and arbitrary inverse homomorphism, while
the unrestricted $(k,\ell)$-substitutable languages retain ordinary
intersection and $\lambda$-free inverse homomorphism; the paragraph following
that proposition notes separately that the context-free $(k,\ell)$ subfamily
is not closed under intersection.  Theorem~9.2 also gives the regular-filter
failure directly in our notation.  Thus finite-monoid typing provides a
structural operation unavailable to a single prefix--suffix window:
independent finite information can be absorbed into one product typing.  We do not claim that $\mathsf{RS}$ is exactly the
closure of the fixed-window hierarchy under these operations.

The revision also separates two requirements on a useful typing.  For
correctness, Definition~2.1 says that a typing must separate every pair of
nonempty factors that share a context but have different distributions; this
condition is preserved by refinement, and Lemma~9.3 gives an infinite-family
obstruction to the existence of any finite such typing.  For efficiency,
correctness alone is not enough: Section~6 shows that refinement can create a
large typed-thickness parameter, while Sections~7--8 give two sufficient
regimes---fixed windows and linear targets---in which that cost is controlled.
Sections~8--9 still provide nonregular linear and nonlinear examples beyond
every fixed-window class, but these examples are now supporting evidence
rather than the sole answer to the reviewer's positioning question.

The Introduction also positions our compositional factor typing alongside
Coste et al.'s occurrence-level domain/typing bias in automata inference and
Takada's regular control languages over production labels.  We now address the
pushdown suggestion explicitly as well: Section~8 recalls the one-turn
pushdown characterization of linear languages, Section~9.3 gives the
finite-monoid obstruction behind the counter/Dyck examples, and the Conclusion
lists the relation between finite factor typing and pushdown finite control as
an open problem.  We do not claim a pushdown characterization here.  Finally,
the finite-group comparison at the end of Section~9 shows that invertible
finite typings do not suffice even for all regular targets.

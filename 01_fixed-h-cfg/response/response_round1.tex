\section{Response to Reviewers ---
TCS-D-26-00494}\label{response-to-reviewers-tcs-d-26-00494}

Manuscript: ``Distributional Learning of Context-Free Languages under
Fixed Finite-Monoid Typing''

Author: Takayuki Kuriyama

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their detailed and constructive reports. Their comments led us to correct the learning algorithm, remove an unused typing component, repair the terminal-rule completeness argument, and substantially reorganize the manuscript.

Throughout this response, unqualified section, theorem, proposition, lemma,
and corollary numbers refer to the revised manuscript; references to the
submitted version are marked ``submitted'' or ``former''. Reviewer quotations
retain their original numbering, with only typographical normalization and
indicated omissions. Each response gives both a structural locator and the
page/numbered-line range in the current revised PDF.

\subsection{Overview of changes}\label{overview-of-changes}

The revision corrects four substantive issues. First, it separates the
set-driven reconstruction operator $\mathcal B_h$ from the conservative
sequential learner $\mathcal A_h$, because recomputing the batch grammar
after every datum need not stabilize syntactically. Second, the unused
outer-context type annotations are removed; only the yield type used in the
completeness proof remains. Third, the terminal-rule gap is repaired by adding
a witness for every typed terminal rule and, when needed, using Rule (R3)
before Rule (R4). Fourth, fixed-$h$ substitutability is stated for nonempty
internal fragments, with $\lambda$ handled separately at the start symbol.

The quantitative scope is now explicit. For general fixed typings, the paper
bounds characteristic data by a target-side typed-thickness parameter and
shows that ordinary source thickness alone cannot give a polynomial bound for
all set-driven reconstruction operators on the fixed-$h$ slice. For every
fixed prefix--suffix window, ordinary thickness again suffices, and for linear
targets the characteristic data are polynomial in the size of a chosen linear
CFG representation. Polynomial characteristic-data claims are made for the
set-driven operator; the sequential learner is stated separately as a Gold
learner with polynomial-time updates.

The presentation has also been compressed and reordered. Basic grammar and
learning terminology is consolidated in Section~2; reconstruction precedes
target-side refinement; long fixed-window and linear normalization arguments
are moved to the appendices; and the former boundary sections are consolidated
into Section~10. The revision also corrects three bibliographic attributions:
Kanazawa (1996) is cited for positive-data learning of $k$-valued categorial
grammars, Pin for the standard finite-monoid characterization, and Yoshinaka
(2008) for the thickness attribution to Wakatsuki--Tomita.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{New material relative to the submitted manuscript.}
In response to Reviewer~2's question about what is gained by arbitrary fixed
finite-monoid typings, Sections~7--8 now quantify one concrete cost of the
choice of typing: yield-type refinement can greatly increase the shortest
witnessed yields, while fixed-window typings keep this cost polynomially
controlled. Appendix~A supplies the fixed-window normalization and Appendix~D
the level-coded-tree substitutability proof used by the lower bound.
Proposition~9.4 adds a nonregular linear fixed-$h$ example outside every
fixed-window class, and Proposition~10.8 adds the comparison with Clark's
congruential family. The submitted auxiliary appendices on closure/Kleene-star
phenomena and the exceptional $\mathrm{CCL}_1$ case have been removed.

\textbf{R1.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The main result of this submission
is an elegant generalization of the results of Clark and Eyraud (2007)

\textbf{Response:} We thank the reviewer for identifying the central correctness problem.
Recomputing the batch grammar after every datum need not stabilize
syntactically.  The revision therefore separates a set-driven operator
$\mathcal B_h$ (Section~4.1) from a conservative sequential learner
$\mathcal A_h$ (Section~4.2), using the same rebuild-on-counterexample

Corollary~5.5 proves eventual syntactic stabilization.  We also now cite
Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka (2016, Appendix) at the point where their
delayed-rebuild phenomenon is relevant: a characteristic sample may
already have been seen while an earlier hypothesis persists, but the first
later positive example that it misses forces a reconstruction from the
accumulated data.  Polynomial characteristic-data claims are made for the
batch operator; the sequential learner is claimed only to have
polynomial-time updates together with Gold convergence.

\textbf{Response:} We agree that choosing a useful morphism is the main limitation of the

outside the present theorem.

The revision also makes two benefits and one cost of the abstraction concrete.
Product homomorphisms combine independent finite features, and Sections~9--10
give nonregular linear and nonlinear examples captured by fixed typings but
outside every fixed $(k,\ell)$ class. Conversely, Section~7 shows that refining
yield types can increase the shortest witnessed yields and hence the
characteristic-data cost. We distinguish this factor typing from Coste et
al.'s occurrence-level automaton typing and from Takada's regular control
languages over production labels; no equivalence or subsumption is claimed.

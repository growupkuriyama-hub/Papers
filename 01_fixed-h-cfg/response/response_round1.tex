\section{Response to Reviewers ---
TCS-D-26-00494}\label{response-to-reviewers-tcs-d-26-00494}

Manuscript: ``Distributional Learning of Context-Free Languages under
Fixed Finite-Monoid Typing''

Author: Takayuki Kuriyama

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their detailed and constructive reports. Their comments led us to correct the learning algorithm, remove an unused typing component, repair the terminal-rule completeness argument, and substantially reorganize the manuscript.

Throughout this response, unqualified section, theorem, proposition, lemma,
and corollary numbers refer to the revised manuscript; references to the
submitted version are marked ``submitted'' or ``former''. Subheadings that
reproduce a reviewer's original section grouping (for example, ``Section 7''
or ``Sections 8--10'') refer to the submitted manuscript. For ease of reference, we number the Editor's comment as E.1 and the
reviewers' comments as R1.1--R1.37 and R2.1--R2.11. Reviewer quotations retain the original section grouping, with only typographical
normalization and omissions indicated by ellipses; related remarks are
grouped by topic within each section. Each response identifies the revised
section or statement directly.  A marked-up revised manuscript generated from
the submitted and revised sources is supplied together with this response.

\subsection{Overview of changes}\label{overview-of-changes}

The revision makes four central corrections.  First, it separates the
set-driven reconstruction operator $\mathcal B_h$ from the conservative
sequential learner $\mathcal A_h$, so that the Gold learner now stabilizes
syntactically.  Second, the unused outer-context type annotations are removed,
and the occurrence-indexed hypothesis symbols are quotiented to one
substring-indexed symbol $[x]$ per observed factor.  The resulting batch
construction is Clark's substring grammar with its unary rules filtered by
$h$.  Third, the terminal-rule completeness gap is repaired by adding
witnesses for typed terminal rules and using the unary Rule~\textup{(U)}
before the lexical Rule~\textup{(L)} when necessary.  Fourth, fixed-$h$ substitutability is stated
for nonempty internal fragments, with $\lambda$ handled separately at the
start symbol.

Several claims have been narrowed accordingly.  Presentation-order
independence is claimed only for the batch operator, not for the sequential
learner.  Polynomial characteristic-data bounds are stated for the batch
operator, while the sequential learner is stated separately to have Gold
convergence and polynomial-time updates.  The former claim that outer-context
types are necessary has been withdrawn, and the separate context-transport
rule is no longer needed in the quotient construction.

The manuscript has also been substantially shortened and reorganized.  Basic definitions are consolidated in Section~2, the reconstruction operator is now defined before the target-side
refinement used in the completeness proof, the former three boundary
sections are merged into Section~9, and technical normalization proofs are
in Appendices~A--C. The submitted Appendix~A already established
intersection and inverse-homomorphism closure facts; these are condensed
in Proposition~3.2, whose inverse-homomorphism argument is adapted to the
revised nonempty-fragment convention by distinguishing erased factors.
Sections~6--8 reorganize the quantitative discussion and give the
nonregular linear example addressing Reviewer~2's question on the
extent of the linear theorem. Section~9 is shorter
overall and also contains the new congruential-class Proposition~9.7,
which was suggested by Reviewer~1.
We distinguish new additions from submitted material and known results
of Yoshinaka (2008).

\subsection{Response to the Editor}\label{response-to-the-editor}

\textbf{E.1. Editor (quoted excerpt):} ``The reviewers agree that the
scientific contribution has merit. However, they both point to many
issues with the presentation, many of which leak also into the
scientific part of the paper and are not merely language revisions. This
is especially relevant in the learning part of the paper. \ldots{}
Specifically, any correctness issues should be carefully considered.''

\textbf{Response:} We agree. The submitted learner has been replaced by
a conservative Gold learner; the terminal-rule gap has been repaired in both
the witness set and the completeness proof; and the unused outer-context
annotations have been removed. Fixed-\(h\) substitutability is now stated for
nonempty internal fragments, with \(\lambda\) handled separately. Section 4
defines the batch operator and sequential learner before Section 5 introduces
target-side yield typing for completeness. Polynomial characteristic data are
attributed to the set-driven batch operator, while the sequential learner is
asserted to have Gold stabilization with polynomial-time updates.

\textbf{Revision location:} Definition 2.1; Section 4.2; Section 5.1 and Theorem 5.3; Corollary 5.5; Sections 6--8.


\subsection{Reviewer 1}\label{reviewer-1}

\subsubsection{General assessment and learning
algorithm}\label{general-assessment-and-learning-algorithm}

\textbf{R1.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The main result of this submission
is an elegant generalization of the results of Clark and Eyraud (2007)
on the learning of substitutable context-free languages (SCFLs) and of
Yoshinaka (2008) on the learning of k,l-SCFLs. \ldots{} The only serious
error is in the definition of their learning algorithm, but this is
easily repairable by following prior work.''

\textbf{Response:} We thank the reviewer for identifying the central correctness problem.
Recomputing the batch grammar after every datum need not stabilize
syntactically.  The revision therefore separates a set-driven operator
$\mathcal B_h$ (Section~4.1) from a conservative sequential learner
$\mathcal A_h$ (Section~4.2), using the same conservative rebuild-on-missed-positive-example
pattern as Clark--Eyraud's SGL and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$-SGL.
Corollary~5.5 proves eventual syntactic stabilization.  We also now cite
Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka (2016, Appendix) at the point where their
delayed-rebuild phenomenon is relevant: a characteristic sample may
already have been seen while an earlier hypothesis persists, but the first
later positive example that it misses forces a reconstruction from the
accumulated data.  Polynomial characteristic-data claims are made for the
batch operator; the sequential learner is claimed only to have
polynomial-time updates together with Gold convergence.
In particular, $\mathcal A_h$ is not claimed to satisfy the incremental
polynomial-time-and-data criterion of Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka
(2016, Definition~2.5); our polynomial characteristic-data claim for
$\mathcal B_h$ is set-based (ibid., Definition~2.8).

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 2.1; Section 4.2; Corollary 5.5; Theorem 7.2; Theorem 8.3 and the following clarification.


\textbf{R1.2. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``On the other hand, the presentation leaves
considerable room for improvement. By addressing the absence of definitions for
technical terms, unnecessarily long proofs, inconsistent use of metavariables, and
the over-fragmentation of the paper into too many sections, the authors could
substantially reduce the burden on the reader, and I recommend a major revision
along these lines. \ldots{} I suspect that this material could be presented in less
than half the current length.''

\textbf{Response:} We have substantially shortened the presentation. In local compilations using their respective source
preambles, the submitted and revised manuscripts compile to 40 and 24 pages,
respectively; these counts are typesetting-dependent.  Numbered theorem-like environments have
been reduced by more than half, main-text sections from 11 to 10, and
main-text subsections from 31 to 18. Section~2 consolidates definitions
and the positive-data model, the former complexity section has
been folded into Section~5, and the three boundary sections have
been consolidated as Section~9. Sections~6--7 organize the new
quantitative discussion rather than preserving the old layout.

The revision also contains substantive material added in direct response
to the reports.
The new Proposition~3.5 (the submitted Proposition~3.5 is now
Proposition~3.3) and Corollary~7.5 answer R2.1 by locating fixed windows
algebraically inside the fixed-$h$ framework and extending the corresponding
ordinary-thickness bound; Proposition~8.4 supplies the nonregular linear
example requested in R2.3; and Proposition~9.7 answers R1.37 by placing
$\mathsf{RS}\cap\mathrm{CFL}$ inside Clark's congruential family.
Section~6 states the quantitative scope more precisely.  The submitted
manuscript claimed exact reconstruction, Gold identification, and polynomial-time
hypothesis updates for general fixed-$h$ context-free targets, but explicitly
did not claim a polynomial characteristic-data bound for that full class.
The revision adds a parameterized characteristic-data bound for the set-driven
operator $\mathcal B_h$, polynomial in source grammar size and the target-side
typed-thickness parameter.  This does not assert a polynomial bound in
ordinary grammar thickness alone for arbitrary fixed $h$, nor does it
attribute a polynomial-data guarantee to the conservative sequential
learner.  Sections~7--8 identify restricted settings in which ordinary
thickness or grammar size suffices.

Undefined and unnecessary terminology is removed, and metavariables
are clarified: in distribution and reconstruction arguments $u,v$ denote
contexts and $x,y$ denote fragments; the fixed-window definition now follows
the same convention; the locally-trivial-semigroup proof uses $t$ for a
semigroup element and $j$ for an exponent; and the finite-state control
construction uses $\mu_L,\mu_R$ for monoid registers. The counter alphabet
uses $\uparrow,\downarrow,\#$ rather than the letter $u$ and a semicolon.
Appendix~A contains the fixed-window bound and
thickness-preserving normalization, while Appendices~B--C
contain the linear-spine normalization and short-yield proof.
The linear normalization was moved or condensed as both reviewers
suggested; Section~9 is shorter overall.

\textbf{Revision location:} Sections 1--2; Sections 4--9; Appendices A--C.

\subsubsection{Section 2}\label{section-2}

\textbf{R1.7. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``I doubt the claim that `the
learners considered in this paper produce their output independently of
the order in which positive examples are observed.' It appears that the
learning algorithm is being conflated with the grammar-construction
algorithm that it uses as a subroutine. Indeed, the `learning algorithm'
in Section 5 does not converge, and hence does not satisfy Gold's
criterion. Even if the learning algorithm were corrected to follow Clark
\& Eyraud, the output would still differ, when learning, e.g., the
language \(a^n c b^n\), between the case where the characteristic
sample itself is received first and the case where the first positive
example received is very long.''

\textbf{Response:} We agree. We withdrew the submitted
order-independence claim and distinguished the
set-driven batch operator from the conservative
sequential learner. Section~4.2 explains the
presentation-order dependence: placing a
sufficiently long positive example early in
a text may retain an extra observed
nonterminal in the stabilized grammar.
Thus two texts can yield different final grammars for the same target, as in
the reviewer's center-marked example.  The revised text also notes that
Clark (2013, Section~4) exhibits the same presentation-order phenomenon for
the substitutable center-marked language.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 2.1; Section 4.2; Corollary 5.5.


\textbf{R1.8. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``This section should thoroughly
define the necessary notation and terminology, including basic notions
such as (linear) context-free grammars - some terms appear to be defined
ad hoc in footnotes. Those terms include `type', `reconstruction',
`realize', `reachable', `observation',
`control', `local configuration', etc. If these words carry no
mathematical definition required to read the paper and were introduced
only to aid intuition, then in the present form they tend to confuse the
reader, and in some cases it would be better not to name them at all. A
flood of excessive terminology can itself burden the reader and impede
smooth understanding.''

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. Section~2 now consolidates the grammar and
learning notions needed later, including linear CFGs,
reachable/productive/reduced grammars, the positive-data model,
set-driven reconstruction, characteristic samples, distributions, and the
fixed-\(h\) classes. Proof-local labels that did not carry mathematical
content have been removed or are introduced only at their point of use; in
particular, the former \emph{finite typed reconstruction basis} terminology is
gone. We also removed several notation collisions that impeded reading.
Section~4.1 defines \emph{observed nonterminal} before that phrase is used in
proofs.  The revision also defines the letter-count notation $|w|_a$ in
Section~2, spells out Clark--Eyraud's SGL as \emph{Substitution Graph
Learner} and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$-SGL as
\emph{$(k,\ell)$-Substitutable Grammar Learner} at their point of use,
specifies
$\mathsf{Acc}\subseteq M$ at its first appearance, and removes the unused
class notation $[x]_{\equiv_L}$.  More specifically, \emph{reachable} and yield \emph{type}
are defined in the preliminaries and typing setup,
respectively; \emph{reconstruction} refers to the defined
batch operator $\mathcal B_h$ and its operation.
\emph{Observation} is not introduced as a separate notion;
the retained phrase \emph{observed nonterminal} is defined
in Section~4.1. \emph{Realize} and \emph{local configuration}
have been removed, while \emph{control} remains in standard
uses such as control sets and pushdown finite control.
These are presentational changes.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 2; Section 4.1; Section 5.1; Appendix A.2.

is used consistently as an illustration;

names the finite image on which the
multiplication is defined.  Thus we follow the suggestion to simplify the

$(k,\ell)$-substitutability imposes no comparison, remain individually typed.
The fixed-window counterexample criterion following Proposition~3.3
summarizes the separation argument used later.

\textbf{Revision location:} Proposition 3.3 and the fixed-window counterexample criterion; Proposition~3.5; Corollaries~7.4--7.5.

\textbf{R1.18. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The first paragraph states no
reason why `different occurrences must be treated as different typed
copies' at all. Expressions making clear `for what purpose and for what
reason' are needed. Example 4.3 is labeled `(why typing is needed)', yet
why it is needed is not stated. \ldots{} If the authors wish to explain

\textbf{Response:} We have adopted the reviewer's suggested order of
presentation. Section 4 first defines the batch reconstruction operator
\(\mathcal B_h\) and the conservative sequential learner
\(\mathcal A_h\), with no target refinement assumed. Section 5 then
introduces the yield-typed target grammar specifically for the
completeness proof and states explicitly that the purpose is to show
that the already-defined hypothesis grammar can mimic typed target
derivations. Outer-context typing has been removed. The remaining yield
typing is visible at the two unary Rule~\textup{(U)} steps in that
simulation.  For a typed terminal rule $X\to a$, it gives
$h(\omega(X))=h(a)$; for a typed binary rule, Rule~\textup{(U)} moves from
the canonical yield \(\omega(X)\) to \(\omega(Y)\omega(Z)\), and the typed
refinement gives
\(h(\omega(X))=h(\omega(Y))h(\omega(Z))\). No outer-context annotation is
used.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{General positioning of

\textbf{R2.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``At a general level, the paper
offers an abstraction of the learning of Yoshinaka's
\((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages by replacing the fixed-length
window by an arbitrary recognizable congruence, and lifts the known
results accordingly. The abstraction places the problem in a powerful
and well-understood framework, but it requires a whole morphism to be
known in advance for each language, whereas the earlier settings fixed a
single uniform parameter valid across the entire class. The paper
acknowledges that learning the morphism is outside its scope, yet the
interest of the setting -- beyond providing a more abstract framework
for the study of \((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages -- remains
unclear. A promising direction would be to deepen the links with
pushdown automata, which Sections 8 to 10 already touch on. The links
with the work of Takada \ldots{} are also worth investigating. In a more
practical direction, this work could be read as extending automaton
typing \ldots{} to context-free substitutable languages, and the
conditions a typing must satisfy could be studied.''

\textbf{Response:} We agree that specifying an entire finite-monoid homomorphism $h$
requires more prior information than a window pair $(k,\ell)$ and
must be done before learning. Nevertheless, $h$ is fixed
\emph{uniformly} for
the whole learning class $\mathcal C_h^{\mathrm{cf}}$, just as
$(k,\ell)$ is fixed for a window class; it is not chosen separately for
each target within that class.  A target-dependent witnessing morphism
arises only when taking the expressive union $\mathsf{RS}$, for which
we make no learnability claim.  The revised Introduction and Section~3 state the typing-adequacy
condition and separate choosing $h$ from learning with it.
Yoshinaka (2008, Section~4) likewise notes that the union over all
$(k,\ell)$-substitutable context-free classes is not identifiable
from positive data. The broader union $\mathsf{RS}$ is likewise
used for expressive comparisons, not as a fixed-parameter learning target.

The framework also yields more than a notational reformulation:
the new Proposition~3.5 (submitted Proposition~3.5 is now
Proposition~3.3) characterizes exactly when a typing on nonempty factors
is already determined by a prefix--suffix window (local triviality of
$h(\Sigma^+)$), while Proposition~8.4 exhibits a nonregular linear
fixed-$h$ target lying outside every fixed-window class.
Corollary~7.5 extends the ordinary-thickness characteristic-data bound
to the locally trivial regime.  As another structural distinction,
Proposition~3.2 consolidates the closure facts for finite product typings:
regular filtering and arbitrary, including erasing, inverse homomorphisms
can be accommodated by an appropriate fixed finite typing.  This contrasts
with the corresponding nonclosure of fixed-window substitutability
(Yoshinaka 2008, Proposition~1).  The underlying closure material was
already present in submitted Appendix~A; we do not present it as a new
discovery in the revision.

Finally, the Introduction and Section~3 interpret $h$ as a finite-state
factor feature, distinguish it from the occurrence-level typing of
Coste et al., and formulate adequacy through finite-index separation
of distributional conflicts: $h$ must not identify nonempty factors
having a common context but unequal distributions.
Lemma~9.3 gives the corresponding finite-monoid obstruction when
infinitely many pairwise-conflicting factors require distinct types.  Section~8 gives a limited but concrete
connection with Takada: for a labeled linear grammar, yield typing
induces a regular control language on successful production-label
sequences.  We claim neither equivalence nor subsumption of the learning
frameworks or a general pushdown characterization.

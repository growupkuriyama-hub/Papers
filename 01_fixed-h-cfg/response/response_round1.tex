\section{Response to Reviewers ---
TCS-D-26-00494}\label{response-to-reviewers-tcs-d-26-00494}

Manuscript: ``Distributional Learning of Context-Free Languages under
Fixed Finite-Semigroup Typing''

Author: Takayuki Kuriyama

We thank the Editor and both reviewers for their detailed and constructive reports. Their comments led us to correct the learning algorithm, remove an unused typing component, repair the terminal-rule completeness argument, and substantially reorganize the manuscript.

Unqualified section and statement numbers refer to the revised
manuscript; submitted numbers are marked ``submitted'' or ``former''.
Subheadings such as ``Section 7'' or ``Sections 8--10'' follow the
reviewers' grouping and refer to the submitted manuscript. We number the
Editor's comment E.1 and the reviewers' comments R1.1--R1.37 and
R2.1--R2.11; quotations are excerpts with typographical normalization,
omissions marked by ellipses, and related remarks grouped by topic.
The locator guide below refers to the additions-marked revised manuscript and is checked against the marked PDF. Because the revision substantially reorganizes the paper, the marked PDF displays extensive additions, including passages rewritten for presentation rather than newly claimed results. An unmarked LaTeX source file is supplied separately.

\subsection{Overview of changes}\label{overview-of-changes}

\paragraph{Framework of the revised manuscript.}
Reviewer quotations retain the reviewers' original notation.
The revised manuscript fixes a surjective finite-semigroup typing
$s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$, with the fresh-identity completion
$\widehat s$ distinguishing empty yields.
This is more than a change of terminology. In the submitted
Definition~2.3, the relation induced by $h:\Sigma^*\to M$ was tested
on all $x,y\in\Sigma^*$, including $\lambda$ and positive words of
type $h(\lambda)$. The revised definition uses the positive
restriction $s=h|_{\Sigma^+}$ and tests only nonempty factors, while
$\widehat s$ handles empty yields separately (Lemma~2.2).
Thus each submitted fixed-$h$ target class is contained in the
corresponding revised fixed-$s$ class; we do not claim the two
definitions are equivalent. The title has been changed accordingly.
Section~5 now types
arbitrary CFG productions directly, including unit, nullable,
and mixed right-hand sides, without start-symbol separation.
BUNF (Definition~6.1) is retained only for the quantitative
bounds in Sections~6--8. The revised sequential learner
$\mathsf{Learn}_s$ is conservative; no incremental polynomial-data
bound is claimed.


\paragraph{Central corrections.}
The revision makes four central corrections.  First, it separates the
set-driven reconstruction operator $\mathsf{Rec}_s$ from the conservative
sequential learner $\mathsf{Learn}_s$, so that the Gold learner now stabilizes
syntactically.  Second, the unused outer-context type annotations are removed,
and the occurrence-indexed hypothesis symbols are quotiented to one
substring-indexed symbol $\NT{x}$ per observed factor.  The resulting batch construction follows Yoshinaka's substring grammar
(as also presented by Clark), replacing the window comparison in the
unary rules by equality of $s$-types.  Third, the terminal-rule completeness gap is repaired: witnesses for
typed terminal rules, already implicit in the submitted characteristic
sample, are now listed explicitly, and the completeness proof uses
unary Rule~\textup{(U)} before lexical Rule~\textup{(L)} when necessary.  Fourth, fixed-$s$ substitutability is stated for nonempty internal
fragments, while $\lambda$ is distinguished in typed grammars
by the fresh identity of $\mathsf S^\sharp$.

\paragraph{Scope and prior work.}
The revised Introduction cites the author's 2014 preprint
(arXiv:1409.6247v3) as historical background and acknowledges
Yoshinaka's (2015) general disjoint-sort framework, which also
supplies positive-data identification, polynomial-time hypothesis updates,
and polynomial-cardinality characteristic data under the corresponding
many-sorted specialization. These general learning guarantees and
fixed-window signatures are not presented as independent novelties.
The semigroup identities and recognizable-language background of
Proposition~3.5 are attributed to Pin's published 1986 monograph;
the positive-image/kernel formulation is proved explicitly in the
revised manuscript.
Yoshinaka (2008, Theorem~1) already provided polynomial
encoded-size characteristic-data bounds for fixed-window classes.
The revised paper's quantitative contribution instead concerns
encoded-length bounds beyond fixed windows in the stated
window--group and linear regimes, together with a fixed-typing
exponential lower bound despite small ordinary grammar thickness.
We also give expressive separations and obstructions.
The elementary retyping-closure facts and classical fixed-window
criteria are recorded to delimit the framework, rather than
claimed as independent new closure or semigroup theorems.
The relevant prior work is cited in the manuscript and addressed
in the responses below.

\paragraph{Narrowed claims.}
Presentation-order independence and polynomial characteristic-data
bounds are claimed only for the set-driven operator $\mathsf{Rec}_s$, the
latter only in the stated quantitative regimes; the sequential learner
$\mathsf{Learn}_s$ is proved to have Gold convergence and
polynomial-time updates, without an incremental polynomial-data guarantee
(Section~2.1; R1.1). The submitted Section~7 title is withdrawn
accordingly (R2.3). The former claim that outer-context types are
necessary is withdrawn, and the quotient construction needs no separate
context-transport rule (R2.2).

\paragraph{New quantitative results.}
Proposition~6.4 gives finite Clark--Eyraud substitutable languages $L_n$
with grammars of size and ordinary thickness $O(n)$: under trivial typing
$\mathsf{Rec}_{s_0}$ has polynomial characteristic data, whereas under a
fixed two-element semilattice typing every characteristic sample for
$\mathsf{Rec}_{s_{\mathrm U}}$ contains a word of length $3\cdot2^n-2$.
By Proposition~6.4(iv), the same holds for any set-driven operator
admitting characteristic samples for both $L_n$ and
$L_n\setminus\{w_n^*\}$ in the fixed-typing class. Corollary~6.6 gives
$\tau_{s_{\mathrm U}}^{\mathrm{typ}}(G)\ge3\cdot2^n-2$ for every reduced
BUNF grammar $G$ of $L_n$, regardless of size, and Lemma~7.6, an
elementary semigroup fact recorded for completeness, shows
$U_1\notin\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}$; hence the algebraic condition of
Corollary~7.5 cannot simply be dropped from a uniform characteristic-data
bound. Neither necessity for every typing nor any lower bound on the
running time or convergence stage of arbitrary Gold learners is claimed
(R2.1).

\paragraph{Reorganization and clarifications.}
The manuscript has been substantially shortened (R1.2). Definitions are
consolidated in Section~2; the reconstruction operator precedes the
target-side refinement used for completeness; Sections~6--8 reorganize
the quantitative discussion and answer Reviewer~2's question on the linear
theorem (R2.3); the three former boundary sections form Section~9, which
also contains Proposition~9.7 suggested by Reviewer~1; normalization
proofs are in Appendices~A--B; and Appendix~C proves
Proposition~6.4(i) by a reversible deterministic parser. The closure facts
of the submitted Appendix~A are condensed in Proposition~3.2 (R1.36).
For the normalized linear grammar $G_0$, Theorem~8.3 is made explicit as
$\|\mathsf{Wit}(\widetilde G_0)\|=O(|G_0|^3|\mathsf S^\sharp|^4)$; the same degree is
not claimed relative to an unnormalized input grammar.
We also explicitly attribute the known fixed-window closure and
nonclosure properties to Yoshinaka (2008, Proposition~1)
(Section~3), clarify the factorization
$\mathsf{S}^n=\mathsf{S} E(\mathsf{S})\mathsf{S}$ (Proposition~3.5), and clarify the
essential-node width estimate in the proof of Lemma~7.1 (Appendix~A.1).
Changes to Proposition~9.1 and the sharpness of $B\ge2$ are described
in R1.33--R1.35.

\subsection{Response to the Editor}\label{response-to-the-editor}

\textbf{E.1. Editor (quoted excerpt):} ``The reviewers agree that the
scientific contribution has merit. However, they both point to many
issues with the presentation, many of which leak also into the
scientific part of the paper and are not merely language revisions. This
is especially relevant in the learning part of the paper. \ldots{}
Specifically, any correctness issues should be carefully considered.''

\textbf{Response:} We agree. The submitted learner has been replaced by
a conservative Gold learner; the terminal-rule gap in the completeness
proof has been repaired, and the terminal-rule witnesses are now listed
explicitly; the unused outer-context
annotations have been removed. Fixed-\(s\) substitutability is now stated for
nonempty internal fragments, with \(\lambda\) handled separately. Section 4
defines the batch operator and sequential learner before Section 5 introduces
target-side yield typing for completeness. Polynomial characteristic data are
attributed to the set-driven batch operator, while the sequential learner is
asserted to have Gold stabilization with polynomial-time updates.

\textbf{Revision location:} Definition 2.1; Section 4.2; Section 5.1 and Theorem 5.2; Corollary 5.4; Sections 6--8.


\subsection{Reviewer 1}\label{reviewer-1}

\subsubsection{General assessment and learning
algorithm}\label{general-assessment-and-learning-algorithm}

\textbf{R1.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The main result of this submission
is an elegant generalization of the results of Clark and Eyraud (2007)
on the learning of substitutable context-free languages (SCFLs) and of
Yoshinaka (2008) on the learning of k,l-SCFLs. \ldots{} The only serious
error is in the definition of their learning algorithm, but this is
easily repairable by following prior work.''

\textbf{Response:} We thank the reviewer for identifying the central correctness problem.
We agree that the qualitative identification scheme and many of
the arguments are standard or follow earlier work.
The revised Introduction therefore identifies these guarantees
as specializations of Yoshinaka's 2015 framework, while
separately crediting the 2008 fixed-window results; we do not
present the qualitative learning theorem or polynomial-time
hypothesis updates as independent contributions.
Recomputing the batch grammar after every datum need not stabilize
syntactically.  The revision therefore separates a set-driven operator
$\mathsf{Rec}_s$ (Section~4.1) from a conservative sequential learner
$\mathsf{Learn}_s$ (Section~4.2), using the same conservative rebuild-on-missed-positive-example
pattern as Clark--Eyraud's SGL and Yoshinaka's $(k,\ell)$-SGL.
Corollary~5.4 proves eventual syntactic stabilization. As noted by
Eyraud--Heinz--Yoshinaka (2016, Appendix), now cited in Section~4.2,
the correct rebuild may be delayed until the first positive example
missed after a characteristic sample has appeared. Hence $\mathsf{Learn}_s$ is claimed to
have Gold convergence with polynomial-time updates, but not the incremental
polynomial-time-and-data criterion (ibid., Definition~2.5). Our polynomial
characteristic-data guarantees are set-based (ibid., Definition~2.8): they
concern $\mathsf{Rec}_s$, only in the stated quantitative regimes, not
uniformly for all $s$. Section~2.1 now makes this distinction explicit.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 2.1; Section 4.2; Corollary 5.4; Theorem 7.2; Theorem 8.3 and the following clarification.


\textbf{R1.2. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``On the other hand, the presentation leaves
considerable room for improvement. By addressing the absence of definitions for
technical terms, unnecessarily long proofs, inconsistent use of metavariables, and
the over-fragmentation of the paper into too many sections, the authors could
substantially reduce the burden on the reader, and I recommend a major revision
along these lines. \ldots{} I suspect that this material could be presented in less
than half the current length.''

\textbf{Response:} We have reduced the paper from 40 to 26 pages
(including the appendices), while maintaining the central proofs and
the technical definitions requested by the reviewers. The revised
sections consolidate the reconstruction and convergence arguments,
the polynomial-data bounds, and the expressive examples. Binary--unit--nullable normal form (BUNF) is defined in
Definition~6.1 only as a device for the quantitative estimates.
The qualitative witness construction and completeness proof
of Section~5 work directly for arbitrary CFG right-hand sides.
The quantitative normalization and reversible
stack-parser arguments are in Appendices~A--C.
We have also addressed the reviewer's four presentation concerns:
technical terminology is defined at first use or in Section~2;
proof-local detail was shortened or moved to Appendices~A--C;
word metavariables in the Section~9 concatenation identity and
quotient argument no longer reuse the typing symbols $s,t$,
and the temporary component count in the completeness proof
of Theorem~5.2 has been renamed from $h$ to $\nu$.
The integer offset in Section~9 is denoted by $\delta$,
typed productions in Section~5 use $\rho$, and the capped-counter
bound in Section~9 uses $B$;
the former boundary sections have been consolidated into
Section~9. Sections~6--8 remain separate because they concern
distinct quantitative regimes (general typings, window--group
typings, and linear targets). We acknowledge that the revised
paper is shorter but not half the length of the submitted version;
it also incorporates the substantive corrections and new
results requested by the reviewers.

\textbf{Revision location:} Sections 1--2; Sections 4--9; Appendices A--C.


\subsubsection{Section 1}\label{section-1}

\textbf{R1.3. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``Stating the paper's place within
the field and the significance of the language families considered would
make the paper more compelling. Conversely, the current text is too
heavily weighted toward technical content and could be made more
concise.''

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. Section~1.2 now begins with a concise
account of the fixed-$s$ batch reconstruction operator and conservative
Gold learner as the learning foundation, expressly acknowledging that
the learning and polynomial-time guarantees specialize Yoshinaka's general
many-sorted signature framework. Rather than listing those existing
learning guarantees as separate novel contributions, the Introduction emphasizes two
main contributions: (1) quantitative characteristic-data results,
including their dependence on the chosen typing, the
locally trivial--group and linear bounds, and the exponential
lower bound; and (2) expressive scope and limitations,
including specific examples beyond fixed windows, counter/Dyck
obstructions, and comparison with Clark's congruential family.
Elementary retyping closure and the classical fixed-window criterion
are discussed after the list as background, not counted as new
contributions. The fixed morphism is presented as a finite
typing bias (R2.1). The historical account distinguishes the
author's 2014 preprint from Yoshinaka's subsequent general many-sorted
signature learning theorem and identifies which results are particular
to fixed finite-semigroup typing (see also R2.1).

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 1.


\textbf{R1.4. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The notations \(\mathcal D_L\) and
\(\mathcal C_h^{\mathrm{cf}}\) are not standard and require definitions.
In particular, the phrasing `the general context-free class

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. \(\mathcal D_L(x)\) is defined at its first
occurrence in the Introduction and again formally in the preliminaries. The
notation \(\mathcal C_s^{\mathrm{cf}}\) is now defined at its first use in the
Introduction as the class of context-free \(\sim_s\)-substitutable languages.
The unrestricted fixed-typing class \(\mathsf{RS}_s\)
is then introduced before \(\mathsf{RS}=\bigcup_s\mathsf{RS}_s\).
Section~3 develops these classes and
\(\mathcal C_s^{\mathrm{lin}}\) formally. The phrase ``general context-free
class'' has been removed.

\textbf{Revision location:} Introduction; Section 2.2 and the opening of Section 3.


\textbf{R1.5. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The contributions are enumerated as
(1)-(6), but it is unnecessary to count technical details as separate
contributions. Moreover, at this point the text uses many terms whose
technical meaning has not yet been made clear, and readers may not be
able to follow them.''

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. Instead of the submitted six-item list,
Section~1.2 now gives a short unnumbered paragraph on the qualitative
learning construction and its relation to Yoshinaka's general framework,
followed by only the two contributions described in R1.3; proof-local
devices are not counted. The quantitative item summarizes polynomial
upper bounds and exponential lower bounds on characteristic data
without developing proof-local machinery in the Introduction.
The typed shortest-yield parameters and binary--unit--nullable
normal form (BUNF) are formally defined in Section~6. The
second item instead focuses on expressive examples and obstructions;
the classical fixed-window criterion and elementary closure
facts are mentioned separately after the list as background.
The term \emph{locally trivial} is defined in Section~2.2;
the join $\mathbf{LI}\vee\mathbf{Grp}$ is explained in
Section~1.2 and formalized in Section~7.
The Clark--Eyraud (2007, Section~6.1) example
$L=\{a,aa\}$, recalled after the fixed-$s$ definition in
Section~1.1, shows that trivial typing fails (two factors share a
context but have different distributions); parity typing then
distinguishes them. It illustrates the definition and is not a
separation from fixed-window classes.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 1.1 (minimal example after the
definition of fixed-$s$ substitutability); Section 1.2.


\textbf{R1.6. Comment (quoted excerpt):} `` `Yoshinaka's classical language'
is often called `the Łukasiewicz language' in the literature.''

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. We use ``Łukasiewicz language,'' add the
Autebert--Berstel--Boasson (1997) handbook reference, and
state its relation to the one-bracket Dyck language concisely.  We also now
cite Yoshinaka (2008, Section~3) for the prior observation that this language
is outside every fixed $(k,\ell)$ class, and distinguish our stronger
conclusion that it lies outside the finite-typing union $\mathsf{RS}$.

\textbf{Revision location:} Section 9.3.


\subsubsection{Section 2}\label{section-2}

\textbf{R1.7. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``I doubt the claim that `the
learners considered in this paper produce their output independently of
the order in which positive examples are observed.' It appears that the
learning algorithm is being conflated with the grammar-construction
algorithm that it uses as a subroutine. Indeed, the `learning algorithm'

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{General positioning of
fixed-\(h\)}{General positioning of fixed-h}}\label{general-positioning-of-fixed-h}

\textbf{R2.1. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``At a general level, the paper
offers an abstraction of the learning of Yoshinaka's
\((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages by replacing the fixed-length
window by an arbitrary recognizable congruence, and lifts the known
results accordingly. The abstraction places the problem in a powerful
and well-understood framework, but it requires a whole morphism to be
known in advance for each language, whereas the earlier settings fixed a
single uniform parameter valid across the entire class. The paper
acknowledges that learning the morphism is outside its scope, yet the
interest of the setting -- beyond providing a more abstract framework
for the study of \((k,\ell)\)-substitutable languages -- remains
unclear. A promising direction would be to deepen the links with
pushdown automata, which Sections 8 to 10 already touch on. The links
with the work of Takada \ldots{} are also worth investigating. In a more
practical direction, this work could be read as extending automaton
typing \ldots{} to context-free substitutable languages, and the
conditions a typing must satisfy could be studied.''

\textbf{Response:} We agree that supplying a finite-semigroup
morphism is a stronger prior bias than fixing $(k,\ell)$, and
we do not infer this morphism. However, $s$ is fixed uniformly
across the entire target class $\mathcal C_s^{\mathrm{cf}}$;
it is not chosen anew for each individual target language.
For $s=s_{k,\ell}$, our reconstruction specializes to
Yoshinaka's 2008 substring-rule construction, up to the stated
representation choices.

The interest of fixing $s$ is to make the quantitative
cost of a typing bias explicit, not to learn the bias.
For every fixed $s$, nonempty targets in
$\mathcal C_s^{\mathrm{cf}}$ are identifiable from positive
data, whereas the encoded size of characteristic data
can depend sharply on the algebra of $s$.
The locally trivial--group condition supplies a sufficient
polynomial regime; a fixed two-element semilattice typing
already exhibits an exponential obstruction.
We do not claim a characterization of all polynomial regimes.

The qualitative identification guarantees and polynomial-time
hypothesis updates are specializations of Yoshinaka's 2015
general many-sorted framework, not independent novelties.
Beyond the fixed-window encoded-size bound already obtained by
Yoshinaka (2008, Theorem~1), the new content is quantitative and
expressive. First, we obtain characteristic-data encoded-length
bounds using ordinary thickness for window--finite-group
typings (Corollary~7.5), and a grammar-size-only bound for
linear targets (Theorem~8.3). Second, a fixed two-element
semilattice typing yields finite targets with grammar size and
ordinary thickness $O(n)$ but requiring a characteristic-sample
word of length $3\cdot2^n-2$ for every set-driven operator
admitting characteristic samples for the specified
distinguishing pair (Proposition~6.4); Corollary~6.6 gives
the corresponding intrinsic typed-thickness obstruction.
This contrasts with examples whose ordinary thickness is
already exponential. Third, nonregular linear and nonlinear
examples lie outside every fixed-window class
(Propositions~8.4 and~9.1), while the counter/Dyck results
limit what fixed finite typing can represent (Section~9).
These results do not provide a method to infer $s$, a uniform
bound for all fixed typings, or a convergence-stage lower
bound for arbitrary Gold learners.

We have also clarified the algebraic scope.
Yoshinaka (2008, Proposition~1) established closure of the
unrestricted fixed-$(k,\ell)$-substitutable class under
intersection of two class members and under nonerasing inverse
homomorphisms, as well as nonclosure under arbitrary regular
intersection and arbitrary inverse homomorphisms; its
context-free subclass need not be closed under intersection.
Proposition~3.2 records elementary finite-typing analogues,
including regular filtering and erasing inverse images after
changing the typing; these closure facts are not claimed as
new theorems. Section~3 also describes adequate typing as
finite-index congruence separation of conflicting distributions,
not a procedure for finding the typing. Finally, the revised
Introduction cites automata typing bias (Coste et al., 2004),
and Section~8 gives a limited connection with Takada's regular
control sets for linear grammars. We claim neither a general
pushdown characterization nor an equivalence with Takada's
learning framework.

\subsubsection{Main concern 1: outer context typing and

\textbf{R2.2. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``My main concern is the typed
refinement, and specifically transport. Rule (2) allows a nonterminal to
move between arbitrary contexts with no condition whatever on their
types. Is this intended? If the outer components $m,n$ record where a
nonterminal may appear, why does the rule that relocates it ignore them
entirely? As far as I can tell the components are then read by no proof:

$A^{m,n}_{p}$ by $A_{p}$ throughout leaves every result intact. Does the
author agree, and if not, where exactly is an outer type consulted? The

nothing to guard transport with. If that is right, then the typed transport
announced in Section 1.1 does not occur anywhere in the article, and the
claim that occurrences must be separated according to their outer types
cannot be established by any example. The yield typing, which is genuinely

\textbf{Response:} We agree, and the observation permits a
stronger simplification than merely deleting the outer type annotations.
No proof in the \emph{submitted version} consulted the outer components, so
$A^{m,n}_p$ has been replaced by the yield-typed $A_p$ on the target side.
On the hypothesis side, the context index is also inessential: submitted
Rule~(2) made all symbols $[x:u,v]$ with the same factor $x$ mutually
derivable.  Quotienting those symbols therefore gives one nonterminal $\NT{x}$
per observed factor.  Submitted Rule~(3) then becomes exactly the unary rule
$\NT{x}\to\NT{y}$ when $x$ and $y$ share an observed context and
$s(x)=s(y)$.  The separate transport rule disappears.

separated by their outer types, as well as the typed-transport claim in
submitted Section~1.1.

Apart from the start-symbol presentation, the treatment of

follows Yoshinaka's substring-indexed rule scheme (2008, Section~4),
also presented by Clark (2013, Section~4).  For window typing

for general fixed $s$ they require type equality and a shared sample
context. Soundness rests on the invariant of Theorem~4.2,
$\NT{x}\Rightarrow^* w$ implies $x\equiv_L w$ and $s(w)=s(x)$ (R1.27),
which consults no outer type.

Yield typing, which the reviewer rightly identifies as essential, is now
discussed in the main text of Section~5.1 rather than in a footnote. It is
needed only for the present witness-based completeness argument, where it
licenses the unary steps from a canonical parent yield to a typed terminal
yield or to a canonical right-hand-side yield. This is a requirement
of the witness-based completeness proof, not a claim of
algorithm-independent necessity.

\subsubsection{Main concern 2: extent of the linear

\textbf{R2.3. Comment (quoted excerpt):} ``The linear result raises a
different question: how large is the class it covers? The three closing

\[
 L\in\mathcal C_s^{\mathrm{lin}},\quad R_{\mathrm{reg}}=g^{-1}(\mathsf{Acc}_{g})

Here $t=g|_{\Sigma^+}$, with codomain $g(\Sigma^+)$, as in

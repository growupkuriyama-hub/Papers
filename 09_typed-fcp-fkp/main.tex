\title{Frames versus Exemplars: An Exponential Gap for Syntactic Categories}
\author{Takayuki Kuriyama\\
Independent Researcher, Tokyo, Japan\\
\texttt{growup.kuriyama@gmail.com}}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\linenumbers

\begin{abstract}
Distributional approaches to syntax name a category in one of two finite
ways: by a few sentence frames in which its members occur, or by a few
exemplars, the category then consisting of the expressions that fit every
frame shared by all the exemplars.  Kanazawa and Yoshinaka (2017) identified the finite
context and kernel properties under which the corresponding distributional
learners succeed.  We show that the two ways can differ exponentially.
For structures built by binary combination and unary marking in which
every branch has exactly $n$ levels, consider the licensing condition that
at least one mark occurs somewhere.  A grammar for this language names each
of its $n+1$ categories by one frame of length at most $2n$, with right-hand
sides of length at most $3n$.  By contrast, in every grammar presented by
exemplars the number of nonterminals, the length of right-hand sides and
the length of exemplars have a product exponential in $n$, and this is
optimal up to a factor $O(n)$.  The gap concerns grammars, not learning; it
yields exponential lower bounds for the author's reconstruction from
positive data and for the exemplar-based learner with membership queries.
A fixed finite observer, such as a morphological feature recording whether
an expression is marked, does not remove the gap: it cannot shorten the exemplar presentations of
this language, and it brings our frame grammar down from about $2^n$ to
$O(n)$ productions.  Under this observer the same grammar has a frame
presentation of total length $O(n^2)$, while every exemplar presentation
has exponential total length.  Both bounds are instances of general
statements, about observers given by ideals of types and about one-word
deletions.  In general, refining the observer can only help frames and
only hurt exemplars, for the strong and very weak finite context properties
and the very weak finite kernel property; the weak finite context property
is not monotone.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} distributional learning; syntactic concept
lattice; finite context property; finite kernel property; finite
semigroup; characteristic sample.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Categories from distribution.}
In the distributional tradition of \citet{harris1954}, a syntactic
category is a set of expressions that occur in common contexts; the
syntactic concept lattice of \citet{clark2009fg,clark2010icgi,clark2013}
formalises this idea.  Fix a language $L$.  A \emph{frame} or \emph{context}
is a pair $(u,v)$ of strings, and an expression $x$ \emph{fits} it if
$uxv\in L$.  For a set $C$ of frames, $C\Lc$ is the set of expressions that
fit every frame of $C$; for a set $K$ of expressions, $K\R$ is the set of
frames that all expressions of $K$ fit.  A category can thus be named by
finitely many \emph{frames}, as $C\Lc$, or by finitely many
\emph{exemplars}, as $K\RL$: the expressions that fit every frame shared by
the exemplars.  A grammar $G$ has the \emph{finite context property} (FCP)
if $L(G,A)=C_A\Lc$ for finite sets $C_A$, and the \emph{finite kernel
property} (FKP) if $L(G,A)=K_A\RL$ for finite sets $K_A$
\citep{clark2010icgi,yoshinaka2011dlt,clark-yoshinaka2016}; \citet{ky2017}
showed that the \emph{very weak} versions (Section~\ref{sec:prelim})
characterise the success of the frame-based (dual) and the exemplar-based
(primal) distributional learners with membership queries.

\begin{example}[a toy fragment with agreement]\label{ex:toy}
Let $L_{\mathrm{ag}}$, over the words \emph{the, dog, dogs, John, barks,
bark, saw}, be generated by
\begin{gather*}
S\to NP_{\mathrm{sg}}\ \textit{barks}\mid
NP_{\mathrm{pl}}\ \textit{bark}\mid NP\ \textit{saw}\ NP,\\
NP\to NP_{\mathrm{sg}}\mid NP_{\mathrm{pl}},\\
NP_{\mathrm{sg}}\to\textit{the dog}\mid\textit{John},\qquad
NP_{\mathrm{pl}}\to\textit{the dogs}.
\end{gather*}
The singular noun phrases are named by one frame and by one exemplar,
$\{(\eps,\textit{barks})\}\Lc=\{\textit{John}\}\RL=\{\textit{the dog},\textit{John}\}$.
All noun phrases are named by one frame, but a single exemplar keeps the
agreement constraint, and one singular and one plural exemplar are needed:
$\{(\textit{John saw},\eps)\}\Lc=\{\textit{the dog},\textit{the dogs}\}\RL
=\{\textit{the dog},\textit{John},\textit{the dogs}\}$.  So the numbers of
frames and of exemplars needed for the same category can differ.
\end{example}

\paragraph{The question and the answer.}
How large must a finite presentation of a context-free language be when
categories are named by frames and when they are named by exemplars?  We
measure a presentation by the number $|N|$ of nonterminals, the maximal
length $\rho$ of a right-hand side and the maximal length $\kappa$ of an
exemplar or $\ell=|u|+|v|$ of a frame, and separately by its total length
(Definition~\ref{def:presentation}).

Consider a \emph{global licensing condition}, which admits a structure as
soon as one constituent anywhere in it carries a mark.  Outside
linguistics, a Python function is a generator function if its body
contains a \texttt{yield} anywhere in its own scope, even in a branch that
is never executed \citep[Sec.~6.2.9]{python312}; the condition classifies
functions rather than deciding well-formedness
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).  Abstractly, write $\Lfl xy\Rfl$ for pairs
and $\Lce x\Rce$ for \texttt{(yield x)}, and let $M_n$ consist of the
structures in which every root-to-leaf path passes through exactly $n$
constructors and that contain \emph{at least one mark}; we claim nothing
for Python as a whole.  A marked constituent suffices for membership, but
an unmarked one occurs both in admitted structures and in the single
excluded one.  Frames handle this easily: a grammar can place the mark in
its root productions, one for each position of a highest mark, and leave
every other category unrestricted.  Exemplars cannot.

\begin{mainthm}[frames versus exemplars; Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}]
Let $n\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item There is a grammar $F_n$ for $M_n$ with $n+1$ nonterminals and
right-hand sides of length at most $3n$ in which every category is named by
one frame of length at most $2n$.
\item Every CFG $G$ for $M_n$ with the very weak FKP satisfies
$\rho_G\,|N_G|\,\max(\kappa_G,1)\ge3\cdot2^n-n-5$.
\item There is a grammar $H_n$ for $M_n$ with $2n$ nonterminals and
right-hand sides of length at most $4$ that has the very weak FKP with one
exemplar per nonterminal, of length at most $\max(3\cdot2^{n-1}-2,\,2n+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{mainthm}

In the measure $\rho|N|$ times the witness length, frames give at most
$6n^2(n+1)$, while the least value for exemplars lies between
$3\cdot2^n-n-5$ and $8n\max(3\cdot2^{n-1}-2,2n+1)$.  The bound (ii) holds
however many productions a grammar has, whereas $F_n$ pays with $2^n+2n-2$
productions; whether some untyped frame presentation of $M_n$ has
polynomial total length is open.  Without the deletion there is no gap:
$L_n$, the language of all such structures, has frame and exemplar
presentations of size $O(n)$ with witnesses of length $O(n)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}(iv)).  The reason is \emph{capture}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:capture}): an exemplar-named category that covers a large
unmarked part of a marked structure must contain that very part among its
exemplars, whereas a frame-named category can contain all structures of a
given depth, since its frame already contains a mark.

\paragraph{Finite observation.}
We also use finite information about an expression itself, modelled by a
surjective morphism $s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$ onto a finite semigroup, a
\emph{typing} or \emph{observer}; $s(w)$ is the \emph{type} of $w$, and the
type of a compound is determined by the types of its parts.  Following
\citet[Def.~2.1]{kuriyama-tcs1},\footnote{Numbers of results of
\citet{kuriyama-tcs1} refer to its arXiv version~5 (arXiv:1409.6247v5).}
$L$ is \emph{$\sim_s$-substitutable} if for $x,y\in\Sigma^+$, $s(x)=s(y)$ and
$\{x\}\R\cap\{y\}\R\ne\emptyset$ imply $\{x\}\R=\{y\}\R$, and $\Ccf s$ is the
class of context-free $\sim_s$-substitutable languages; for the trivial
typing $s_0$ this is the substitutability of \citet{clark-eyraud2007},
restricted to nonempty fragments.  In Example~\ref{ex:toy}, let
$s(w)=\zeta$ if $w$ contains \emph{dogs} or \emph{bark} and $s(w)=1$
otherwise, in $U_1=\{1,\zeta\}$ with $\zeta$ absorbing.  A typed frame can
restrict its category to one type: $\{(\textit{John saw},\eps)\}\Lc\cap
s^{-1}(1)$ is the set of singular noun phrases, named without an agreement
frame.  Typed exemplars, however, must all have the type of their
category, so \emph{the dog} and \emph{the dogs} can no longer name all noun
phrases together.

\paragraph{Positive data and membership queries.}
The learning model of \citet{kuriyama-tcs1} uses positive data only.  A
membership query is, in linguistic terms, a request for a grammaticality
judgement, and explicit negative evidence of this kind has long been
regarded as scarce in child language acquisition \citep{marcus1993}; we use
this only as motivation and make no claim about acquisition.  Without
queries the observer does real work: each class $\Ccf s$ is identifiable in
the limit from positive data \citep{kuriyama-tcs1}, but their union
contains every regular language \citep[Prop.~3.1]{kuriyama-tcs1} and so is
not \citep{gold1967}.  With membership queries one untyped primal learner
identifies the whole union (Remark~\ref{rem:uniform-known}).

\paragraph{Applications to learning.}
Theorem~A bounds learners that name categories by exemplars
(Section~\ref{sec:applications}).  From positive data, the author's
reconstruction $\mathsf{Rec}_s$ \citep{kuriyama-tcs1} needs characteristic
data of size at least $(3\cdot2^n-n-5)^{1/3}$ on $M_n$ for every typing $s$
with $M_n\in\Ccf s$; with membership queries, the primal learner of
\citet{ky2017} with fixed parameters needs $2^{\Omega(n)}$ data.  A separate
argument shows that the dual learner, too, needs a datum of length
$3\cdot2^{n-1}-2$ (Corollaries~\ref{cor:rec-data} and~\ref{cor:primal-data},
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-refutation}).

\paragraph{Refining the observer.}
A typing $h$ \emph{refines} $s$, written $s\preceq h$, if $h(x)=h(y)$ implies
$s(x)=s(y)$.  The coarsest typing is $s_0$; a \emph{two-element indicator
typing} records only whether an expression contains a letter of a given
set, as the plural observer above does.  We write $\FCP{k}{vw}$ and
$\FKP{k}{vw}$ for the classes of languages with a CFG with the very weak
$k$-FCP or $k$-FKP, and $\tFCP{k}{vw}{s}$, $\tFKP{k}{vw}{s}$ for their typed
versions, in which every category carries a type and is restricted to it;
$\mathrm{st}$ and $\mathrm{w}$ denote the strong and weak versions
(Section~\ref{sec:typed}).

\begin{mainthm}[context--kernel duality; Propositions~\ref{prop:duality}
and~\ref{prop:strict-context}--\ref{prop:weak-not-preserved}]
Let $s\preceq h$ and $k\ge1$.  Then
\[
\tFKP{k}{vw}{h}\subseteq\tFKP{k}{vw}{s}\subseteq\FKP{k}{vw},\qquad
\FCP{k}{vw}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{vw}{s}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{vw}{h},
\]
the same inclusions hold for the strong FCP, and the typed very weak and
weak FKP coincide.  For every $k\ge1$ the typed
inclusions between the trivial typing and a two-element indicator typing
are strict on both sides.  The weak FCP is not monotone: some language has
the weak $2$-FCP untyped and for the trivial typing, but no typed weak FCP
for a two-element indicator typing.
\end{mainthm}

\paragraph{What the mark observer adds.}
Let $s_U$ be the two-element observer that records whether an expression
contains a mark; in the Python reading it is the compositional bit
``contains a \texttt{yield}''.

\begin{mainthm}[the mark observer; Theorem~\ref{thm:observer}]
Let $n\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item Under $s_U$, every category of the grammar $H_n$ of Theorem~A is
named by one frame of length at most $2n$; $H_n$ has $6n-4$ productions,
against $2^n+2n-2$ for $F_n$.
\item Under every observer, every typed exemplar presentation of $M_n$
satisfies the bound of Theorem~A(ii).  Under $s_U$, every typed exemplar
presentation of $L_n$ contains an exemplar of length $3\cdot2^n-2$, whereas
without an observer exemplars of length $2n+1$ suffice.
\item In total length, the frame presentation of (i) has $O(n^2)$, while
every exemplar presentation of $M_n$, typed under any observer or untyped,
has at least $2\sqrt{3\cdot2^n-n-5}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{mainthm}

In short, under the same fixed mark observer the same grammar $H_n$ has a
strong frame presentation of total length $O(n^2)$ and a strong exemplar
presentation of exponential total length, and every exemplar presentation
of $M_n$, for any grammar and any observer, has exponential total length.
Theorem~A compares the parameters $\rho$, $|N|$ and the witness length and
needs no observer; Theorem~C(iii) compares total length, where we can prove
the gap only with the observer (Open Problem~\ref{op:Mn-context}).  The
choice of observer matters: the length-parity observer does not hurt the
exemplars of $L_n$ (Remark~\ref{rem:Ln-sU}).

Both halves are instances of general statements.  Frames carry over to the
intersection with an ideal of types whenever the frames of all non-initial
categories force the ideal (Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal}); this answers which
observers help frames.  Every exemplar presentation of a finite one-word
deletion is at least as large as a prefix of the deleted word that has a
second completion inheriting all its left contexts
(Lemma~\ref{lem:one-word}, Theorem~\ref{thm:general-lower}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-gap} combines the two, and $M_n$ is one instance.

\paragraph{What is known and what is new.}
The typed learners are instances of the sorted framework of
\citet{yoshinaka2015}; we prove only the soundness we use
(Proposition~\ref{prop:learners}).  A typed context set lies in the
extended regular closure of \citet{ky2023} (Remark~\ref{rem:exreg}), and
\citet[Sec.~8]{yoshinaka2015} already compares learnable classes for
different signatures; the inclusions of Theorem~B are short consequences
of the closure calculus (Lemmas~\ref{lem:star} and~\ref{lem:reduction}), and
what we add is the opposite behaviour of frames and exemplars, strictness
witnesses for every $k$, and the failure of monotonicity for the weak FCP.
The family $L_n$, its grammar and the exemplars $u_i$ come from
\citet[Sec.~6]{kuriyama-tcs1}, where $M_n$ is written $L_n^-$ and appears
only as the second member of a distinguishing pair; the bound for $L_n$
under the mark observer (Remark~\ref{rem:Ln-sU}) is that of
\citet[Eq.~($\spadesuit$), Cor.~6.6]{kuriyama-tcs1}, transferred from
yields to kernels.  The gap, the grammar $F_n$, the capture lemma and the
resulting lower bounds are new as far as we could check; \citet{ky2023}
treat only the frame side.  Exponential characteristic data are not new in
themselves \citep[Example~2]{clark-eyraud2007}; see also
\citet{coste-nicolas2019}.  Here, however, long words alone do not force
the bound: $L_n$ contains the same long words and has exemplar
presentations of linear size.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection*{Contexts, closures and sound pre-fixed points}
Let $\Sigma$ be a finite alphabet, $\eps$ the empty word and
$\Sigma^+=\Sigma^*\setminus\{\eps\}$.  Fix $L\subseteq\Sigma^*$.
For $C\subseteq\Sigma^*\times\Sigma^*$ and $K\subseteq\Sigma^*$ put
\[
C\Lc=\{x\in\Sigma^*: uxv\in L\ \forall(u,v)\in C\},\qquad
K\R=\{(u,v): uKv\subseteq L\},
\]
so $\emptyset\Lc=\Sigma^*$ and $\emptyset\R=\Sigma^*\times\Sigma^*$.
Write $\D_L(x)=\{x\}\R$.  The pair $(\cdot\R,\cdot\Lc)$ is a Galois
connection: $K\subseteq C\Lc\iff K\R\supseteq C$.  In particular
$K\subseteq K\RL$ and $C\subseteq C\Lc{}\R$ always hold.  Hence $K\mapsto K\RL$
is a closure operator, $K\R=K\RL{}\R$, and $Y\subseteq K\RL\iff Y\R\supseteq K\R$.
These operators depend on $L$; when several languages occur we say
``relative to $L$''.

\begin{lemma}[Clark's inclusion; cf.\ \citealp{clark2009fg}]\label{lem:clark}
For all $X,Y\subseteq\Sigma^*$, $X\RL Y\RL\subseteq(XY)\RL$.  Consequently, for
terminal words $w_0,\dots,w_n$ and sets $X_1,\dots,X_n$,
$w_0X_1\RL w_1\cdots X_n\RL w_n\subseteq(w_0X_1w_1\cdots X_nw_n)\RL$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $x\in X\RL$, $y\in Y\RL$ and $(u,v)\in(XY)\R$, then $(ux',v)\in Y\R$ for all
$x'\in X$, so $(u,yv)\in X\R$ and $uxyv\in L$.  Induct for the second claim.
\end{proof}

A \emph{context-free grammar} (CFG) is $G=(N,\Sigma,P,I)$ with a set
$I\subseteq N$ of initial nonterminals \citep{ky2017}.  We write
$L(G,A)$ for the terminal yields of $A$, $L(G)=\bigcup_{S\in I}L(G,S)$, and
$\mathcal C(G,A)=\{(u,v): S\Rightarrow^*_G uAv \text{ for some } S\in I\}$.

A family $(X_A)_{A\in N}$ is a \emph{pre-fixed point} of $G$ if
$X_A\supseteq w_0X_{A_1}w_1\cdots X_{A_n}w_n$ for every production
$A\to w_0A_1w_1\cdots A_nw_n$.  The family $(L(G,A))_A$ is the least
pre-fixed point, so $L(G,A)\subseteq X_A$ for every pre-fixed point.  It is a
\emph{sound pre-fixed point} (SPP) if moreover $\bigcup_{S\in I}X_S=L(G)$.
Thus a sound pre-fixed point may overestimate the language of each
nonterminal, as long as it stays consistent with the productions and does
not change the language of the grammar.

For example, for $S\to bAc\mid baaac$ and $A\to a\mid aa$, the family
$X_A=\{a,aa,aaa\}$, $X_S=L(G,S)=\{bac,baac,baaac\}$ is an SPP with
$L(G,A)\subsetneq X_A$: the extra word $aaa$ yields only $baaac$, which is
generated anyway.  Without $S\to baaac$ the family is still a pre-fixed
point but no longer sound.

\begin{lemma}[reaching contexts]\label{lem:reach}
If $(X_A)_A$ is an SPP of $G$ and $(u,v)\in\mathcal C(G,A)$, then
$uX_Av\subseteq L(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replacing each nonterminal $B$ of a sentential form by $X_B$ can only shrink
along a derivation step, since $(X_B)$ is pre-fixed; so $S\Rightarrow^*uAv$
gives $uX_Av\subseteq X_S\subseteq L(G)$.
\end{proof}

Following \citet[Sec.~3]{ky2017}, relative to $L=L(G)$, a set $X$ is
\emph{$k$-context-generated} if $X=C\Lc$ with $|C|\le k$, and
\emph{$k$-kernel-generated} if $X=K\RL$ with $|K|\le k$.  The grammar $G$ has
the \emph{strong} (\emph{weak}) $k$-FCP if $(L(G,A))_A$
(resp.\ $(L(G,A)\RL)_A$) is $k$-context-generated, and the \emph{very weak}
$k$-FCP if $G$ has a $k$-context-generated SPP; likewise for the FKP with
``kernel'' in place of ``context''.  We write $\FCP{k}{st}$, $\FCP{k}{w}$
and $\FCP{k}{vw}$ (likewise $\FKP{k}{st}$, $\FKP{k}{w}$ and $\FKP{k}{vw}$)
for the classes of languages having some CFG with the strong, weak and very
weak property, respectively; omitting $k$ means the union over all $k\ge1$.
The strong property implies the weak one, since $(C\Lc)\RL=C\Lc$ and
$(K\RL)\RL=K\RL$, and the weak one implies the very weak one, since
$(L(G,A)\RL)_A$ is always an SPP of $G$ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:clark}, and it
is sound because $L(G,A)\subseteq L$ gives $(\eps,\eps)\in L(G,A)\R$ for
initial $A$).  Hence
\[
\FCP{k}{st}\subseteq\FCP{k}{w}\subseteq\FCP{k}{vw},\qquad
\FKP{k}{st}\subseteq\FKP{k}{w}\subseteq\FKP{k}{vw};
\]
the second inclusion is an equality for kernels and, for $k\ge2$, strict for contexts
(Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(a),(c)).

\begin{fact}[\citealp{ky2017}]\label{fact:ky}
(a) (Prop.~10) $\FKP{k}{vw}=\FKP{k}{w}$; more precisely, if $(K_A\RL)_A$ is
an SPP of $G$, then adding the productions $A\to w$ $(w\in K_A)$ yields
$G'$ with $L(G')=L(G)$ and $L(G',A)\RL=K_A\RL$.
(b) (Theorems~3--6) The dual (primal) learner with parameters $r,k$, given a
positive presentation of $L_*$ and a membership oracle, converges to a
grammar for $L_*$ whenever $L_*$ has a CFG with at most $r$ nonterminals per
right-hand side and the very weak $k$-FCP ($k$-FKP); and every grammar to
which it converges has the very weak $k$-FCP ($k$-FKP), with SPP
$(C\Lc)_C$ (resp.\ $(K\RL)_K$) indexed by its own nonterminals.
(c) (Prop.~2, Thm.~14) There are languages in $\FKP1{st}\setminus\FCP{}{vw}$, in
$\FCP1{st}\setminus\FKP{}{vw}$, and in $\FCP2{vw}\setminus\FCP{}{w}$.
\end{fact}

\paragraph{The learners.}
We recall Algorithms~1 and~2 of \citet{ky2017}, the \emph{dual learner}
$\mathrm{Dual}_{r,k}$ and the \emph{primal learner} $\mathrm{Primal}_{r,k}$
($r,k\ge1$).  Both read a positive presentation $t_1,t_2,\dots$ of the target
$L_*$, ask membership queries, and output a grammar $G_i$ at each stage $i$;
$\Lc$ and $\R$ are relative to $L_*$.  Let $D_i=\{t_1,\dots,t_i\}$, let
$\Sub(D)$ be the set of substrings of words of $D$,
$\Con(D)=\{(u,v):uwv\in D\text{ for some }w\}$, and let $\Sub^{\le m}(D)$ be
the set of tuples $(w_1,\dots,w_l)$, $1\le l\le m$, with
$u_0w_1u_1\cdots w_lu_l\in D$ for some $u_0,\dots,u_l$.  Stage $i$ is a
\emph{reset} if $D_i\not\subseteq L(G_{i-1})$.  The dual learner sets
$\mathcal E_i=\Sub(D_i)$ at every stage, and $J_i=\Con(D_i)$ and
$H_i=\Sub^{\le r+1}(D_i)$ at resets (otherwise $J_i=J_{i-1}$, $H_i=H_{i-1}$).
Its nonterminals are the sets $C\subseteq J_i$ with $1\le|C|\le k$; its
productions are the $C_0\to w_0C_1w_1\cdots C_nw_n$ with
$(w_0,\dots,w_n)\in H_i$ that are \emph{valid on $\mathcal E_i$}, i.e.\
$C_0\Lc\supseteq w_0(\mathcal E_i\cap C_1\Lc)w_1\cdots(\mathcal E_i\cap
C_n\Lc)w_n$; its initial nonterminals are the $C$ with
$\mathcal E_i\cap C\Lc\subseteq L_*$.  The primal learner sets
$J_i=\Con(D_i)$ at every stage and $\mathcal E_i=\Sub(D_i)$,
$H_i=\Sub^{\le r+1}(D_i)$ at resets; its nonterminals are the sets
$K\subseteq\mathcal E_i$ with $1\le|K|\le k$, its productions those with
$(w_0,\dots,w_n)\in H_i$ and $J_i\cap K_0\R\subseteq(w_0K_1w_1\cdots
K_nw_n)\R$, and its initial nonterminals the $K\subseteq L_*$.  A learner
\emph{converges at stage $i$} if $G_j=G_i$ for all $j\ge i$.  (We write
$\mathcal E_i$ for the set called $E_i$ by \citet{ky2017}, to keep $E_i$ for
the nonterminals of Section~\ref{sec:deletion}.)

\subsection*{Fixed finite-semigroup typings}
A \emph{typing}, or \emph{observer}, is a surjective semigroup morphism
$s:\Sigma^+\to\mathsf S$ onto a finite semigroup.  Let $\mathsf S^\sharp=\mathsf S\cup\{\one\}$ with a
fresh identity and $\widehat s:\Sigma^*\to\mathsf S^\sharp$ its monoid
extension, $\widehat s(\eps)=\one$ \citep[Lemma~2.2]{kuriyama-tcs1}.  The
\emph{fibres} are $F_\mu=\widehat s^{-1}(\mu)$ for $\mu\in\mathsf S^\sharp$;
thus $F_{\one}=\{\eps\}$ and $\{F_\mu\}$ partitions $\Sigma^*$ into regular
sets.  The trivial typing is $s_0:\Sigma^+\to\{0\}$.

We say $h$ \emph{refines} $s$, written $s\preceq h$, if $h(x)=h(y)$ implies
$s(x)=s(y)$ for $x,y\in\Sigma^+$.  Then there is a unique monoid morphism
$\pi:\mathsf H^\sharp\to\mathsf S^\sharp$ with $\widehat s=\pi\circ\widehat h$
($\pi(h(w))=s(w)$, $\pi(\one)=\one$), and
$F^h_\nu\subseteq F^s_{\pi(\nu)}$.
For a set $Z\subseteq\Sigma$ of letters with $\emptyset\ne Z\ne\Sigma$, the
\emph{$Z$-indicator} $h_Z:\Sigma^+\to U_1=\{1,\zeta\}$ ($\zeta$ absorbing)
sends $w$ to $\zeta$ iff $w$ contains a letter of $Z$; it refines $s_0$.

\begin{definition}[typed grammar]\label{def:typed}
An \emph{$s$-typed CFG} is a CFG together with $\tau:N\to\mathsf S^\sharp$
such that every production $A\to w_0A_1w_1\cdots A_nw_n$ satisfies
$\tau(A)=\widehat s(w_0)\tau(A_1)\widehat s(w_1)\cdots\tau(A_n)\widehat s(w_n)$.
\end{definition}
By induction on derivations, $L(G,A)\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$ for every $A$.
If $s\preceq h$ and $(G,\tau)$ is $h$-typed, then $(G,\pi\circ\tau)$ is
$s$-typed.

\begin{lemma}[typed refinement of a grammar]\label{lem:product}
Let $G=(N,\Sigma,P,I)$ be a CFG, $h$ a typing and $\sigma:N\to\mathsf S^\sharp$
either undefined (untyped case) or an $s$-typing of $G$ with $s\preceq h$.
Let $G^h$ have nonterminals $(A,\nu)$ with $\nu\in\mathsf H^\sharp$ (and
$\pi(\nu)=\sigma(A)$ in the typed case), productions
$(A,\nu)\to w_0(A_1,\nu_1)w_1\cdots(A_n,\nu_n)w_n$ for
$A\to w_0A_1w_1\cdots A_nw_n\in P$ and
$\nu=\widehat h(w_0)\nu_1\widehat h(w_1)\cdots\nu_n\widehat h(w_n)$, initial nonterminals
$(S,\nu)$, $S\in I$, and $\tau(A,\nu)=\nu$.  Then $G^h$ is $h$-typed and
$L(G^h,(A,\nu))=L(G,A)\cap F^h_\nu$; in particular $L(G^h)=L(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Type consistency holds by construction.  The inclusion $\subseteq$ is
induction on derivations in $G^h$; for $\supseteq$, induct on derivations in
$G$, giving each child with yield $x_i$ the type $\nu_i=\widehat h(x_i)$ (in
the typed case $\pi(\nu_i)=\widehat s(x_i)=\sigma(A_i)$).
\end{proof}

\section{Typed closures and typed finite context/kernel properties}
\label{sec:typed}

Fix $L$ and a typing $s$.  For $\mu\in\mathsf S^\sharp$ and $K\subseteq F_\mu$
define the \emph{typed closure}
\[
\cl_\mu(K)=K\RL\cap F_\mu .
\]

\begin{lemma}[typed Galois connection]\label{lem:typed-galois}
For $K\subseteq F_\mu$ and any $C$: $K\subseteq C\Lc\cap F_\mu\iff
C\subseteq K\R$.  Hence $\cl_\mu$ is a closure operator on the subsets of
$F_\mu$, its closed sets are exactly the sets $C\Lc\cap F_\mu$, and for
$Y\subseteq F_\mu$ we have $Y\subseteq\cl_\mu(K)\iff K\R\subseteq Y\R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The equivalence is the untyped one restricted to $K\subseteq F_\mu$.  The
other claims follow as for $\cdot\RL$, since $\cl_\mu(K)=(K\R)\Lc\cap F_\mu$
and $\cl_\mu(K)\R=K\R$.
\end{proof}

The next identity records a direct consequence of the closure-product
identity of \citet[Sec.~3, Eq.~(1)]{ky2017}.

\begin{lemma}[closure-insensitivity of context sets]\label{lem:star}
Let $w_0,\dots,w_n$ be terminal words and $X_i\subseteq Y_i\subseteq X_i\RL$
for $1\le i\le n$.  Then
\[
(w_0Y_1w_1\cdots Y_nw_n)\R=(w_0X_1w_1\cdots X_nw_n)\R .
\]
In particular this holds for $Y_i=\cl_{\mu_i}(X_i)$ when $X_i\subseteq
F_{\mu_i}$, and for $Y_i=X_i\RL$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $P_X=w_0X_1\cdots X_nw_n$ and $P_Y=w_0Y_1\cdots Y_nw_n$.  From $P_X\subseteq
P_Y$ we get $P_Y\R\subseteq P_X\R$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:clark},
$P_Y\subseteq w_0X_1\RL\cdots X_n\RL w_n\subseteq P_X\RL$, hence
$P_Y\R\supseteq P_X\RL{}\R=P_X\R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[typed FCP and FKP]\label{def:typed-props}
Let $(G,\tau)$ be $s$-typed and work relative to $L(G)$.  A family
$(X_A)_A$ with $X_A\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$ is \emph{typed $k$-context-generated}
if $X_A=C_A\Lc\cap F_{\tau(A)}$ with $|C_A|\le k$, and \emph{typed
$k$-kernel-generated} if $X_A=\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A)$ with
$K_A\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$ and $|K_A|\le k$ (kernels are
\emph{type-homogeneous}).  $(G,\tau)$ has the typed strong / weak / very weak
$k$-FCP (resp.\ $k$-FKP) if $(L(G,A))_A$ / $(\cl_{\tau(A)}(L(G,A)))_A$ / some
SPP is typed $k$-context-generated (resp.\ $k$-kernel-generated).  The
corresponding language classes, ``$L$ has an $s$-typed CFG with the
property'', are denoted $\tFCP{k}{st}{s}$, $\tFCP{k}{w}{s}$, $\tFCP{k}{vw}{s}$,
and $\tFKP{k}{\cdot}{s}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[presentations and their size]\label{def:presentation}
A \emph{$k$-context presentation} (\emph{frame presentation}) of $L$ is a CFG
$G$ with $L(G)=L$ together with finite sets $C_A$, $|C_A|\le k$, such that
$(C_A\Lc)_A$ is an SPP of $G$; a \emph{$k$-kernel presentation}
(\emph{exemplar presentation}) is the same with finite sets $K_A$,
$|K_A|\le k$, and the SPP $(K_A\RL)_A$.  A \emph{typed} presentation uses an
$s$-typed $(G,\tau)$ and the SPP $(C_A\Lc\cap F_{\tau(A)})_A$, resp.\
$(\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$ with $K_A\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$.  A presentation is
\emph{strong} if the SPP is $(L(G,A))_A$.  Its size is measured by the number
$|N|$ of nonterminals, the maximal number $\rho$ of symbols on a right-hand
side, the maximal length $\kappa$ of a kernel word, and the maximal length
$|u|+|v|$ of a context $(u,v)$ in some $C_A$.  Its \emph{total length} is
$\sum_{A\to\alpha}(|\alpha|+1)$ over all productions plus $\sum(|e|+1)$ over
all kernel words $e$, resp.\ $\sum(|u|+|v|+1)$ over all contexts $(u,v)$,
the sums running over all nonterminals $A$ and all members of $K_A$ or
$C_A$; the maximum of an empty set is $0$, and the observer, the types
$\tau(A)$ and the set of initial nonterminals are not counted.
\end{definition}
Thus $L$ has an ($s$-typed) $k$-context presentation iff
$L\in\FCP{k}{vw}$ (resp.\ $\tFCP{k}{vw}{s}$), and likewise for kernels.

Restricting an SPP to the fibres keeps it an SPP, so typed SPPs lose
nothing.  An $s_0$-typed grammar is not quite untyped, since it separates
$\eps$ from nonempty yields; comparisons with the untyped classes are stated
explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[typed learners: soundness]\label{prop:learners}
Define the \emph{typed dual learner} $\mathrm{Dual}^s_{r,k}$ as the dual
learner of Section~\ref{sec:prelim} with nonterminals $(C,\mu)$,
$C\subseteq J_i$, $1\le|C|\le k$, $\mu\in\mathsf S^\sharp$, only
type-consistent productions, the validity test
$C_0\Lc\cap F_{\mu_0}\supseteq w_0(\mathcal E_i\cap C_1\Lc\cap F_{\mu_1})w_1
\cdots(\mathcal E_i\cap C_n\Lc\cap F_{\mu_n})w_n$, and initial nonterminals the
$(C,\mu)$ with $\mathcal E_i\cap C\Lc\cap F_\mu\subseteq L_*$.  Define the
\emph{typed primal learner} $\mathrm{Primal}^s_{r,k}$ as the primal learner
with nonterminals $(K,\mu)$, $K\subseteq\mathcal E_i\cap F_\mu$,
$1\le|K|\le k$, only type-consistent productions, and the untyped validity
test.  If one of these learners converges to $G$ on a positive presentation
of $L_*$, then $L(G)=L_*$, and $(C\Lc\cap F_\mu)_{(C,\mu)}$, resp.\
$(\cl_\mu(K))_{(K,\mu)}$, is an SPP of $G$; for the primal learner
$(K\RL)_{(K,\mu)}$ is an SPP of $G$ as well.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the output be $G$ from stage $i$ on.  First, $L_*\subseteq L(G)$: if
$d\in L_*\setminus L(G)$, choose $j>i$ with $d\in D_j$ (the presentation
lists $d$, and $D_j$ grows with $j$).  Then $D_j\not\subseteq L(G)=L(G_{j-1})$,
so stage $j$ is a reset, and $G_j$ contains the initial nonterminal $(\{(\eps,\eps)\},\widehat s(d))$
with the valid production $(\{(\eps,\eps)\},\widehat s(d))\to d$ (dual), resp.\
$(\{d\},\widehat s(d))$ with $(\{d\},\widehat s(d))\to d$ (primal), so $G_j$
derives $d$, contradicting $G_j=G$.  Next, the tests hold at every stage
$j\ge i$.  For the dual learner every $C$ is a nonempty subset of
$\Con(L_*)$, so $C\Lc\subseteq\Sub(L_*)=\bigcup_j\mathcal E_j$; hence
$C_0\Lc\cap F_{\mu_0}\supseteq w_0(C_1\Lc\cap F_{\mu_1})w_1\cdots$ for every
production and $C\Lc\cap F_\mu\subseteq L_*$ for every initial $(C,\mu)$.  For
the primal learner every $K$ is nonempty, so $K\R\subseteq\Con(L_*)=
\bigcup_jJ_j$; hence $K_0\R\subseteq(w_0K_1w_1\cdots K_nw_n)\R$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:star} and the Galois connection says
$K_0\RL\supseteq w_0K_1\RL w_1\cdots K_n\RL w_n$, and $K\subseteq L_*$ for every
initial $K$.  In both cases the family is a pre-fixed point whose initial
components lie in $L_*$, so
$L(G)\subseteq\bigcup_{S\in I}X_S\subseteq L_*\subseteq L(G)$: the family is an
SPP and $L(G)=L_*$.  For the primal learner, Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} turns
$(K\RL)$ into the typed SPP $(\cl_\mu(K))$.
\end{proof}
The converse, the typed analogue of \citet[Theorems~5--6]{ky2017}, is
neither proved nor used here.


\begin{remark}[relation to extended regular closure]\label{rem:exreg}
With a regular expression $R_\mu$ for $F_\mu$ of size $b_\mu$
\citep[Sec.~3.1]{ky2023}, a typed context set with $1\le|C|=m\le k$ is the
guarded expression $((u_1,v_1)^\lhd\cap\cdots\cap(u_m,v_m)^\lhd)\cap R_\mu$
of size $2m+b_\mu$.  An empty $C_A$ denotes $F_\mu$, which after trimming
equals $\{c\}\Lc\cap F_\mu$ for a reaching context $c$ of $A$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:reach}), and several initial symbols are joined by a new one
interpreted by $(\eps,\eps)^\lhd$.  So a rank-$r$ typed very weak $k$-FCP
presentation gives membership in
$\mathrm{EXREG}_{\max(r,1)}(\max(1,2k+\max_\mu b_\mu))$
\citep[Sec.~3.2, Lemmas~2,4, Theorem~6]{ky2023}, a qualitative inclusion
for fixed $s$.
\end{remark}

The next lemma says that a typed kernel presentation is nothing but an
untyped kernel presentation whose kernels are type-homogeneous; it is used
both for the gap (Section~\ref{sec:deletion}) and for the duality
(Section~\ref{sec:duality}).

\begin{lemma}[Kernel Reduction Lemma]\label{lem:reduction}
Let $(G,\tau)$ be $s$-typed, $L=L(G)$, and $K_A\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$ for
each $A\in N$.  Then $(\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$ is an SPP of $G$ if and only if
$(K_A\RL)_A$ is an SPP of $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a production $A\to w_0A_1w_1\cdots A_nw_n$ and write $K_i=K_{A_i}$,
$P_0=w_0K_1w_1\cdots K_nw_n$, $P_1=w_0\cl(K_1)w_1\cdots\cl(K_n)w_n$ and
$P_2=w_0K_1\RL w_1\cdots K_n\RL w_n$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:star},
$P_0\R=P_1\R=P_2\R$.  Since $P_1\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$ (type consistency),
Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-galois} gives
$\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A)\supseteq P_1\iff K_A\R\subseteq P_1\R$, and the untyped
Galois connection gives $K_A\RL\supseteq P_2\iff K_A\R\subseteq P_2\R$.  So the
two pre-fixed point conditions coincide production by production.

For soundness, both families are pre-fixed points (if either is), hence
contain $(L(G,A))_A$, so $\bigcup_I X_S\supseteq L$ in both cases.  For
$S\in I$: if $K_S\subseteq L$ then $(\eps,\eps)\in K_S\R$ and
$\cl(K_S)\subseteq K_S\RL\subseteq L$; conversely $K_S\subseteq\cl(K_S)$ and
$K_S\subseteq K_S\RL$.  So each soundness condition is equivalent to
$K_S\subseteq L$ for all $S\in I$.
\end{proof}

\section{The kernel--context gap}\label{sec:deletion}

This section proves Theorem~A; the lower bound is proved for finite
one-word deletions in general (Theorem~\ref{thm:general-lower}).  The languages are finite, so every
statement is about the size of presentations, not about generative
capacity.

We use the bracketed doubling family of \citet[Sec.~6]{kuriyama-tcs1}.
Let $\Gamma=\{\ta,\Lfl,\Rfl,\Lce,\Rce\}$ and $G_n$:
$S_0\to E_n$, $E_0\to\ta$, $E_i\to\Lfl E_{i-1}E_{i-1}\Rfl\mid\Lce E_{i-1}\Rce$
$(1\le i\le n)$, $L_n=L(G_n)$.  Put $w^*_0=\ta$,
$w^*_i=\Lfl w^*_{i-1}w^*_{i-1}\Rfl$ (so $|w^*_i|=3\cdot2^i-2$),
$u_i=\Lce^i\ta\Rce^i$, and
\[
M_n=L_n\setminus\{w^*_n\}.
\]
Let $s_U:\Gamma^+\to U_1$ be the indicator of $\{\Lce,\Rce\}$.  Then
$w^*_i$ is the unique word of type $1$ in $L(G_n,E_i)$: without unary
nodes the derivation is the complete binary tree of height $i$.  Theorem~A
and its proof use only these definitions; the learnability facts about $M_n$
from \citet{kuriyama-tcs1} are needed only in Section~\ref{sec:applications}.

\begin{lemma}[node structure]\label{lem:nodes}
Let $m\in L_n$ and let $y\in L(G_n,E_j)$ occur in $m$.  Then the
occurrence is the yield of an $E_j$-node of the (unique) derivation tree of
$m$, and replacing it by any $y'\in L(G_n,E_j)$ gives a word of $L_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Words of $L_n$ are well-bracketed with two bracket kinds, every matched
pair is the pair of an $E_i$-node, and the derivation tree is determined by
the bracketing.  All root-to-leaf paths of an $E_i$-node contain exactly $i$
bracket pairs, so the yield of an $E_i$-node has nesting depth $i$.  If
$j=0$, $y=\ta$ is a leaf.  If $j\ge1$, $y$ is balanced and its first bracket
is matched by its last one inside $y$ (every proper nonempty prefix of $y$
has positive depth), hence also inside $m$; so the occurrence is the yield of
a node whose nesting depth is $j$, i.e.\ of an $E_j$-node.  Replacing the
subtree gives a derivation tree of $G_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[upper bounds]\label{prop:deletion-upper}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item Relative to $L_n$, $L(G_n,E_i)=\{u_i\}\RL=\{(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})\}\Lc$;
hence $G_n$ has the strong $1$-FKP and strong $1$-FCP with kernels and
contexts of length $\le2n+1$.
\item Relative to $M_n$, the $s_U$-typed grammar $H_n$ with nonterminals
$(E_i,1)$ $(0\le i\le n-1)$, $(E_i,\zeta)$ $(1\le i\le n)$, productions
$(E_0,1)\to\ta$, $(E_i,1)\to\Lfl(E_{i-1},1)(E_{i-1},1)\Rfl$ $(1\le i<n)$,
$(E_i,\zeta)\to\Lfl(E_{i-1},\alpha)(E_{i-1},\beta)\Rfl$ for
$(\alpha,\beta)\ne(1,1)$, $(E_i,\zeta)\to\Lce(E_{i-1},\alpha)\Rce$, and initial
$(E_n,\zeta)$, where the $\zeta$-rules have $1\le i\le n$ and only
instances whose children belong to the displayed nonterminal set are
included, generates $M_n$ and has the typed strong $1$-FCP with contexts
$(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})$ $(i<n)$ and $(\eps,\eps)$.  Its size is $O(n)$.
\item Relative to $M_n$, $H_n$ also has the typed strong $1$-FKP, with
kernels $\{w^*_i\}$ for $(E_i,1)$ and $\{u_i\}$ for $(E_i,\zeta)$; hence
$(\{w^*_i\}\RL)$, $(\{u_i\}\RL)$ is an untyped SPP of $H_n$, and $H_n$ has
the very weak $1$-FKP with $\kappa=\max(|w^*_{n-1}|,|u_n|)
=\max(3\cdot2^{n-1}-2,\,2n+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) $\Lce^{n-i}x\Rce^{n-i}\in L_n$ iff $x\in L(G_n,E_i)$: the leading $\Lce$
forces $E_n\to\Lce E_{n-1}\Rce$, and so on.  For the kernel: if
$(u,v)\in\D(u_i)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes} shows $uyv\in L_n$ for every
$y\in L(G_n,E_i)$, so $\D(y)\supseteq\D(u_i)$ and
$L(G_n,E_i)\subseteq\{u_i\}\RL\subseteq\{(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})\}\Lc=L(G_n,E_i)$.

(b) By induction $L(H_n,(E_i,1))=\{w^*_i\}$ and
$L(H_n,(E_i,\zeta))=L(G_n,E_i)\setminus\{w^*_i\}$, so $L(H_n)=M_n$.  For $i<n$,
$\Lce^{n-i}x\Rce^{n-i}$ contains $\Lce$, so it lies in $M_n$ iff it lies in
$L_n$, i.e.\ iff $x\in L(G_n,E_i)$; intersecting with $F_1$ and $F_\zeta$ gives
the two components.  For $i=n$, $\{(\eps,\eps)\}\Lc\cap F_\zeta=M_n$.

(c) Closures are relative to $M_n$.  Write $c_i=(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})$
for $i<n$ and $c_n=(\eps,\eps)$; by (b), $\{c_i\}\Lc=L(G_n,E_i)$ for $i<n$ and
$\{c_n\}\Lc=M_n=L(G_n,E_n)\setminus\{w^*_n\}$.  Let $(p_i,q_i)$ be the context
of an $E_i$-node of $w^*_n$, so that $p_iw^*_iq_i=w^*_n$ and $p_i,q_i$ carry no
mark.

For $0\le i<n$ we show $\{w^*_i\}\RL=L(G_n,E_i)$.  The context $c_i$ accepts
$w^*_i$, so $\{w^*_i\}\RL\subseteq\{c_i\}\Lc=L(G_n,E_i)$.  Conversely let
$(p,q)\in\D(w^*_i)$ and $y\in L(G_n,E_i)$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes},
$pyq\in L_n$; since $pw^*_iq\in M_n$ is marked and $w^*_i$ is not, $p$ or $q$
carries a mark, so $pyq\ne w^*_n$ and $pyq\in M_n$.

For $1\le i\le n$ we show $\{u_i\}\RL=L(G_n,E_i)\setminus\{w^*_i\}$.  If
$(p,q)\in\D(u_i)$ and $y\in L(G_n,E_i)$ is marked, then $pyq\in L_n$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes} and $pyq$ is marked, so $pyq\in M_n$; this gives
$\supseteq$.  Conversely, $c_i$ and $(p_i,q_i)$ accept $u_i$, since
$\Lce^{n-i}u_i\Rce^{n-i}=u_n$ and $p_iu_iq_i$ are marked words of $L_n$.  So
$\{u_i\}\RL\subseteq\{c_i\}\Lc\subseteq L(G_n,E_i)$, and no
$x\in\{u_i\}\RL$ equals $w^*_i$, as $p_iw^*_iq_i=w^*_n\notin M_n$.

Since $w^*_i$ is the only word of type $1$ in $L(G_n,E_i)$, intersecting with
$F_1$ and $F_\zeta$ gives $\cl_1(\{w^*_i\})=\{w^*_i\}=L(H_n,(E_i,1))$ and
$\cl_\zeta(\{u_i\})=L(G_n,E_i)\setminus\{w^*_i\}=L(H_n,(E_i,\zeta))$, by the
proof of (b).  The kernels are type-homogeneous, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} turns this typed SPP into the untyped SPP
$(\{w^*_i\}\RL)$, $(\{u_i\}\RL)$ of the same grammar.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[frames without an observer]\label{prop:untyped-frames}
Let $n\ge1$.  For $0\le d\le n-1$ and $\pi=\pi_1\cdots\pi_d\in\{l,r\}^d$ define
strings $T_d,\dots,T_0$ over $\Gamma\cup\{E_0,\dots,E_{n-1}\}$ by
$T_d=\Lce E_{n-d-1}\Rce$ and, for $1\le j\le d$,
\[
T_{j-1}=\Lfl T_jE_{n-j}\Rfl\ \text{ if }\pi_j=l,\qquad
T_{j-1}=\Lfl E_{n-j}T_j\Rfl\ \text{ if }\pi_j=r,
\]
and write $T_\pi=T_0$.  Let $F_n$ consist of the nonterminals
$E_0,\dots,E_{n-1}$ with their productions in $G_n$, a new initial
nonterminal $S$, and the productions $S\to T_\pi$ for all such $\pi$.  Then
$L(F_n)=M_n$, and relative to $M_n$ the grammar $F_n$ has the strong
$1$-FCP with the context $(\Lce^{n-k},\Rce^{n-k})$ for $E_k$ and
$(\eps,\eps)$ for $S$.  It has $n+1$ nonterminals, $2^n+2n-2$ productions and
$\rho=3n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nonterminals $E_k$ generate $L(G_n,E_k)$.  By induction on $d-j$, every
terminal word derived from $T_j$ lies in $L(G_n,E_{n-j})$ and contains
$\Lce$; so $L(F_n)\subseteq M_n$.  Conversely, let $m\in M_n$.  Its
derivation tree in $G_n$ has a node with a production
$E_i\to\Lce E_{i-1}\Rce$; let $\nu$ be such a node of least depth $d$.  Then
$d\le n-1$, and every proper ancestor of $\nu$ uses a binary production.
Recording at the ancestors of $\nu$ whether the path to $\nu$ turns left or
right gives $\pi\in\{l,r\}^d$, and $m$ is derived from $T_\pi$, the subtrees
off the path and the subtree below $\nu$ being derived from the $E_k$.  For
$k<n$, the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(b) gives
$\{(\Lce^{n-k},\Rce^{n-k})\}\Lc=L(G_n,E_k)=L(F_n,E_k)$ relative to $M_n$, and
$\{(\eps,\eps)\}\Lc=M_n=L(F_n,S)$.  There are $\sum_{d<n}2^d=2^n-1$ productions
for $S$, one for $E_0$ and two for each $E_k$ with $1\le k<n$, and
$|T_\pi|=3d+3\le3n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[kernel capture]\label{lem:capture}
Let $L'$ be a language, $z=RT\in L'$, and $T^\circ$ a word such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capture}
\{R':\ R'T\in L'\ \text{and}\ R'T^\circ\notin L'\}=\{R\}.
\end{equation}
Let $G$ be a CFG with $L(G)=L'$ and an SPP $(K_A\RL)_A$ relative to $L'$.  If,
in some derivation tree of $z$, a node labelled $B$ has a yield occurrence
$x$ lying inside $R$, then $x\in K_B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $z=pxq$; then $(p,q)\in\mathcal C(G,B)$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:reach},
$pK_Bq\subseteq pK_B\RL q\subseteq L'$.  Since $x$ lies in $R$, $q=q_0T$ for
some $q_0$; put $q'=q_0T^\circ$, so that $pxq'=RT^\circ\notin L'$
by~\eqref{eq:capture}.  Now $x\in L(G,B)\subseteq K_B\RL$, so
$K_B\R\subseteq\D(x)\not\ni(p,q')$, and some $e\in K_B$ has $peq'\notin L'$.  As
$peq=(peq_0)T\in L'$, \eqref{eq:capture} gives $peq_0=R$, i.e.\ $e=x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[one-word deletions]\label{lem:one-word}
Let $L$ be a language, $w=RT^\circ\in L$, and $T\ne T^\circ$ a word with
$RT\in L$ such that $yT\in L$ implies $yT^\circ\in L$ for every word $y$.
Then $z=RT$ and $T^\circ$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:capture} for $L'=L\setminus\{w\}$.
For $n\ge2$ this applies to $L=L_n$, $w=w^*_n$,
$T^\circ=\Lfl\ta\ta\Rfl\Rfl^{n-1}$ and $T=\Lce\ta\Rce\Rfl^{n-1}$, with
$|R|=|w^*_n|-(n+3)=3\cdot2^n-n-5$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$z\ne w$ since $T\ne T^\circ$, so $z\in L'$.  If $R'T\in L'$, then
$R'T^\circ\in L$, and $R'T^\circ\notin L'$ iff $R'T^\circ=w$ iff $R'=R$.  For
$L_n$: the last $E_1$-node of $w^*_n$ is $\Lfl\ta\ta\Rfl$, followed by the
closing brackets of its $n-1$ ancestors, so $w^*_n$ ends with $T^\circ$, and
$RT\in L_n$ replaces this node by the $E_1$-word $\Lce\ta\Rce$.  If
$yT\in L_n$, the occurrence of $\Lce\ta\Rce$ in $yT$ is an $E_1$-node
(Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes}), and replacing it by $\Lfl\ta\ta\Rfl$ gives
$yT^\circ\in L_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[kernel lower bound]\label{thm:general-lower}
Let $L'$ be a finite language, and let $z=RT\in L'$ and $T^\circ$
satisfy~\eqref{eq:capture}.  Let $G=(N,\Sigma,P,I)$ be any CFG with $L(G)=L'$
that has an SPP $(K_A\RL)_A$ with finite kernels, $\rho$ the maximal number
of symbols on a right-hand side and $\kappa=\max\{|e|:e\in\bigcup_AK_A\}$.
Then $\rho\,|N|\,\max(\kappa,1)\ge|R|$.  The same bound holds for every
$s$-typed CFG for $L'$ with the typed very weak FKP, for every typing $s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a derivation tree $\mathcal T$ of $z$ with the least number of nodes.
No nonterminal repeats on a path of $\mathcal T$: if $A$ labels a node and a proper
descendant, with yields $\alpha y\beta$ and $y$, then $\alpha\beta\ne\eps$
would give infinitely many words $u\alpha^ty\beta^tv\in L(G)$, contradicting
finiteness of $L'$, and $\alpha\beta=\eps$ would allow a smaller tree.  Call a
node a \emph{chain node} if its yield contains both positions $|R|$ and
$|R|+1$ of $z$; chain nodes form a path from the root, so there are at most
$|N|$ of them.  For every position $j\le|R|$, the deepest chain node
containing $j$ either has $j$ as a terminal of its right-hand side or has a
child containing $j$ that is not a chain node; such a child lies inside
$R$.  Hence $R$ is covered by at most $\rho|N|$ pieces, each a single terminal
or the yield of a node lying inside $R$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:capture} such
a yield belongs to a kernel, so has length at most $\kappa$.  Therefore
$|R|\le\rho|N|\max(\kappa,1)$.  For typed grammars use
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}, which produces an untyped SPP of the same grammar
with the same kernels.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[kernel lower bound for $M_n$]\label{thm:deletion}
Let $n\ge2$.  Every CFG for $M_n$ with an SPP $(K_A\RL)_A$ with finite
kernels, and every $s$-typed CFG for $M_n$ with the typed very weak FKP, for
every typing $s$, satisfies
\[
\rho\,|N|\,\max(\kappa,1)\ \ge\ 3\cdot2^n-n-5 .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:one-word} and Theorem~\ref{thm:general-lower}.
\end{proof}

The bound is a trade-off between the parameters, and it is tight up to a
factor $O(n)$ in two opposite ways.  The presentation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(c) has small $\rho$ and $|N|$ and
$\rho|N|\kappa\le8n\max(3\cdot2^{n-1}-2,2n+1)$.  The listing grammar, with one
nonterminal $S$, a production $S\to w$ for every $w\in M_n$ and the kernel
$K_S=\{u_n\}$, has the strong $1$-FKP (the only context of $u_n$ is
$(\eps,\eps)$, so $\{u_n\}\RL=M_n$), small $|N|$ and $\kappa$, and
$\rho|N|\kappa=(3\cdot2^n-3)(2n+1)$, but doubly exponentially many productions.
So no single parameter is exponential in every presentation; what the
theorem excludes is a presentation small in every parameter: if
$\rho|N|\le p(n)$, then $\kappa\ge(3\cdot2^n-n-5)/p(n)$.  On the frame side,
$F_n$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:untyped-frames}) has $\rho|N|\le3n(n+1)$ and
frames of length at most $2n$ with no observer, though $2^n+2n-2$
productions.

\begin{theorem}[kernel--context gap]\label{thm:gap}
Let $n\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item The grammar $F_n$ for $M_n$ has $n+1$ nonterminals, $2^n+2n-2$
productions, $\rho\le3n$, and the strong $1$-FCP with contexts of length at
most $2n$.
\item The grammar $H_n$ for $M_n$ has $2n$ nonterminals, $6n-4$ productions,
$\rho\le4$, and the very weak $1$-FKP with kernel words of length at most
$\max(3\cdot2^{n-1}-2,2n+1)$.
\item Every CFG for $M_n$ with an SPP $(K_A\RL)_A$, $K_A$ finite, and every
$s$-typed CFG for $M_n$ with the typed very weak FKP for any typing $s$,
satisfies
$\rho\,|N|\max(\kappa,1)\ge3\cdot2^n-n-5$.
\item $L_n$ has an untyped CFG of size $O(n)$ with the strong $1$-FKP and the
strong $1$-FCP, with kernels and contexts of length at most $2n+1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) is Proposition~\ref{prop:untyped-frames}.  (ii) The grammar $H_n$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(b) has the $n$ nonterminals $(E_i,1)$, $0\le i<n$, and the $n$ nonterminals $(E_i,\zeta)$,
$1\le i\le n$.  It has $n$ productions whose left-hand side has type $1$;
each $(E_i,\zeta)$ with $i\ge2$ has three binary and two unary productions,
while $(E_1,\zeta)$ has only $\Lce(E_0,1)\Rce$, because $(E_0,\zeta)$ does not
exist and the binary pair $(1,1)$ is excluded.  So there are
$n+5(n-1)+1=6n-4$ productions, and $\rho\le4$ for $\Lfl XY\Rfl$.  Its very
weak $1$-FKP is Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(c).  (iii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion}.  (iv) is Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(a).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the mark observer and the exemplars of $L_n$]\label{rem:Ln-sU}
Typed kernels are type-homogeneous, so the observer gives the exemplar side
of $L_n$ no advantage, and in fact a disadvantage.  Let $L$ be finite, $s$ a
typing and $(G,\tau)$ an $s$-typed CFG for $L$ with a typed SPP
$(\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$.  For $\mu\in\widehat s(L)$, some initial $S$ of type
$\mu$ generates a word of $L\cap F_\mu$, so $\cl_\mu(K_S)\ne\emptyset$; as
$L$ is finite, a context $(u,\eps)$ with $u$ longer than every word of $L$
accepts nothing, so $\emptyset\RL=\emptyset$ and $K_S\ne\emptyset$; and
$K_S\subseteq\cl_\mu(K_S)\subseteq L\cap F_\mu$ by soundness.  Hence
$\kappa\ge\max_{\mu\in\widehat s(L)}\min\{|w|:w\in L\cap F_\mu\}$, the
quantity by which \citet[Eq.~($\spadesuit$), Cor.~6.6]{kuriyama-tcs1} bounds
the yields of typed grammars.  For $L_n$ under $s_U$ we have
$L_n\cap F_1=\{w^*_n\}$, so every $s_U$-typed exemplar presentation of $L_n$
lists $w^*_n$ and has $\kappa\ge3\cdot2^n-2$, whereas refining $G_n$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:product} and Proposition~\ref{prop:duality}(c) gives an
$s_U$-typed strong $1$-FCP grammar for $L_n$ of size $O(n)$ with contexts of
length at most $2n$.  The disadvantage depends on the observer.  Let $p$ map
a nonempty word to its length modulo $2$, a typing onto the group of order
two.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes} all words of $L(G_n,E_i)$ have the same
contexts in $L_n$, so $\{y\}\RL=L(G_n,E_i)$ for each of them
(Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(a)), and refining $G_n$ by $p$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:product}) gives a $p$-typed strong $1$-FKP grammar for $L_n$
of size $O(n)$, with the kernel $u_i$ for the odd and
$\Lfl u_{i-1}u_{i-1}\Rfl$, of length $4i$, for the even words of
$L(G_n,E_i)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences for learning algorithms}\label{sec:applications}

This section turns the lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} into lower
bounds for the author's positive-data reconstruction and for the primal
learner of \citet{ky2017}; for the dual learner, whose frames are short, a
separate refutation argument is needed (Proposition~\ref{prop:dual-refutation}).
The learners are Algorithms~1 and~2 of \citet{ky2017}; for Clark's original
rule test and its correction see \citet{leiss2014}.

\subsection*{Learning from positive data}

Recall from \citet[Sec.~4.1]{kuriyama-tcs1}
that $\mathsf{Rec}_s(K)$ has a nonterminal $[\![x]\!]$ for every nonempty
factor $x$ of a word of the finite sample $K$, a start symbol $\widehat S$,
and productions
(B) $[\![xy]\!]\to[\![x]\!][\![y]\!]$,
(U) $[\![x]\!]\to[\![y]\!]$ for $x\approx_{K,s}y$ (that is, $s(x)=s(y)$ and
$\D_K(x)\cap\D_K(y)\ne\emptyset$, where $\D_K$ is $\D$ relative to $K$),
(L) $[\![a]\!]\to a$, and
(S) $\widehat S\to[\![w]\!]$ for $w\in K\cap\Sigma^+$, plus
$\widehat S\to\eps$ if $\eps\in K$.

\begin{lemma}[reconstructed grammars carry kernel SPPs]\label{lem:rec-kernel}
Let $s$ be a typing, $L\in\Ccf s$ nonempty with $\eps\notin L$, and
$K\subseteq L$ finite with $L(\mathsf{Rec}_s(K))=L$; write
$\|K\|=\sum_{w\in K}(|w|+1)$.  Let $G_K$ be obtained from
$\mathsf{Rec}_s(K)$ by deleting $\widehat S$ and its productions and
declaring every $[\![w]\!]$ with $w\in K$ initial.  Then $L(G_K)=L$, and
$(\{x\}\RL)_{[\![x]\!]}$, relative to $L$, is an SPP of $G_K$.  Hence $G_K$
has the very weak $1$-FKP with $\rho_{G_K}\le2$,
$|N_{G_K}|\le\|K\|^2/2$ and $\kappa_{G_K}\le\max_{w\in K}|w|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The pre-fixed point property is the soundness invariant in the proof of
\citet[Thm.~4.2]{kuriyama-tcs1}, $[\![x]\!]\Rightarrow^*w$ implies
$x\equiv_Lw$, in closure form; we give the short argument.
$\widehat S$ occurs on no right-hand side and $\eps\notin K$, so
$L(G_K)=L(\mathsf{Rec}_s(K))=L$.  Pre-fixed point: for (B),
$\{xy\}\RL\supseteq\{x\}\RL\{y\}\RL$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:clark}; for (L),
$a\in\{a\}\RL$; for (U), $K\subseteq L$ gives $\D_K(x)\subseteq\D_L(x)$, so
$x$ and $y$ have equal types and a common context in $L$, and
$\sim_s$-substitutability gives $\D_L(x)=\D_L(y)$, i.e.\
$\{x\}\RL=\{y\}\RL$.  Soundness: for $w\in K\subseteq L$,
$(\eps,\eps)\in\D_L(w)$, so $\{w\}\RL\subseteq L$, while the union over the
initial nonterminals contains $L(G_K)=L$.  The bounds: every right-hand
side has at most two symbols; a word $w$ has at most $|w|(|w|+1)/2$
nonempty factors; kernels are single observed factors.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[characteristic data for the deleted-word target]
\label{cor:rec-data}
Let $n\ge2$ and let $s$ be any typing with $M_n\in\Ccf s$, for instance
$s_U$ \citep[Prop.~6.4(iv)]{kuriyama-tcs1} or any refinement of $s_U$.  If
$K\subseteq M_n$ is finite and $L(\mathsf{Rec}_s(K))=M_n$, then
\[
\|K\|^3\ \ge\ 3\cdot2^n-n-5 .
\]
In particular every characteristic sample $\mathcal F$ of $M_n$ for
$\mathsf{Rec}_s$, in the sense of \citet[Sec.~2]{kuriyama-tcs1}, satisfies
$\|\mathcal F\|\ge(3\cdot2^n-n-5)^{1/3}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion} to $G_K$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:rec-kernel}:
$3\cdot2^n-n-5\le\rho|N|\max(\kappa,1)\le2\cdot(\|K\|^2/2)\cdot\|K\|$.
A characteristic sample $\mathcal F$ satisfies
$L(\mathsf{Rec}_s(\mathcal F))=M_n$ (take $K=\mathcal F$).  For a refinement
$h$ of $s_U$, equal $h$-types imply equal $s_U$-types, so
$\sim_{s_U}$-substitutability implies $\sim_h$-substitutability and
$M_n\in\Ccf h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[relation to the fixed-typing paper]\label{rem:rec-data}
\citet[Prop.~6.4]{kuriyama-tcs1} proves that every characteristic sample
of $L_n$ for $\mathsf{Rec}_{s_U}$ contains $w^*_n$ but states no bound for
$M_n$ itself; Corollary~\ref{cor:rec-data} gives one for every $s$ with
$M_n\in\Ccf s$ ($s_0$ is excluded, as $M_n\notin\Ccf{s_0}$ for $n\ge2$
\citep[proof of Prop.~6.4]{kuriyama-tcs1}).  The exponent $1/3$ is not
claimed to be optimal.  A characteristic sample need not contain the
factor $w^*_{n-1}$: for $n=3$, eleven words of $M_3$, none of which contains
$w^*_2$, form a characteristic sample for $\mathsf{Rec}_{s_U}$ (found by
computer, \texttt{tools/check\_rec\_samples.py}).  Long words are still
needed for small $n$: since $\mathsf{Rec}_s$ is monotone in the sample,
every characteristic sample of $M_2$ contains a word of length at least $9$,
and every characteristic sample of $M_3$ one of length at least $16$.  We do
not know whether every characteristic sample of $M_n$ must contain a word
of length $2^{\Omega(n)}$.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Learners with membership queries}

Since $M_n$ is finite, both learners converge on every positive
presentation of $M_n$: once every word has appeared, $D_i$ no longer
changes, the sets $J_i$, $H_i$, $\mathcal E_i$ change at most once more, and
by Proposition~\ref{prop:learners} the limit grammar is correct.  The
question is how much data they need.

\begin{corollary}[data at convergence of the primal learner]\label{cor:primal-data}
If $\mathrm{Primal}_{r,k}$ (typed for any $s$, or untyped) converges on a
positive presentation of $M_n$ at stage $i$, then the data $D_i$ seen so far
satisfy
\[
\|D_i\|\ \ge\ \Bigl(\frac{3\cdot2^n-n-5}{2r+1}\Bigr)^{1/(2k+2)},
\qquad \|D\|=\textstyle\sum_{d\in D}(|d|+1).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The limit grammar $G=G_i$ has the SPP $(K\RL)_K$ indexed by its
nonterminals (Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(b), Proposition~\ref{prop:learners}).  Its
kernels are sets of at most $k$ substrings of $D_i$, so $\kappa\le\|D_i\|$ and
$|N|\le|\Sub(D_i)|^k\le\|D_i\|^{2k}$; its right-hand sides consist of at most
$r$ nonterminals and $r+1$ factors of data words, so
$\rho\le(r+1)\|D_i\|+r\le(2r+1)\|D_i\|$.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion}.
\end{proof}
For fixed $r,k$ the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:primal-data} is
$2^{\Omega(n)}$; if $k$ grows with $n$ the displayed bound is all we claim.

\begin{proposition}[refutation lower bound for the dual learners]
\label{prop:dual-refutation}
Let $n\ge2$, $r\ge2$, $k\ge1$.  If the untyped dual learner
$\mathrm{Dual}_{r,k}$, or the typed dual learner $\mathrm{Dual}^{s_U}_{r,k}$,
converges on a positive presentation of $M_n$ at stage $i$, then $w^*_{n-1}$
is a factor of a word in $D_i$; in particular some datum has length at least
$3\cdot2^{n-1}-2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $t\le i$ be the last stage at which $J$ and $H$ were reset, so the limit
grammar is built from $J_t=\Con(D_t)$, $H_t=\Sub^{\le r+1}(D_t)$ and tested on
$\mathcal E_i=\Sub(D_i)$.  By the proof of \citet[Thm.~3]{ky2017} (and
Proposition~\ref{prop:learners}), every production of the limit grammar is
valid, so every invalid candidate production must fail the test on $\mathcal E_i$.

Every word of $M_n$ is $\Lce y\Rce$ or $\Lfl yy'\Rfl$ with at least one of
$y,y'$ of type $\zeta$; take $c=(\Lce,\Rce)$, $c=(\Lfl,y'\Rfl)$ with $y'$ of type
$\zeta$, or $c=(\Lfl y,\Rfl)$ with $y$ of type $\zeta$, for some datum of
$D_t$.  In each case $c\in J_t$ and $\{c\}\Lc=L(G_n,E_{n-1})$ relative to
$M_n$.  Since the limit grammar generates words containing $\Lfl$, some
datum of $D_t$ contains $\Lfl$, hence also a later $\Rfl$, so
$(\Lfl,\eps,\Rfl)\in H_t$.

Untyped case: the candidate $\{(\eps,\eps)\}\to\Lfl\{c\}\{c\}\Rfl$ is invalid,
as $w^*_n=\Lfl w^*_{n-1}w^*_{n-1}\Rfl\notin M_n=\{(\eps,\eps)\}\Lc$.  It fails the
test on $\mathcal E_i$ only if there are $x_1,x_2\in \mathcal E_i\cap\{c\}\Lc$ with
$\Lfl x_1x_2\Rfl\notin M_n$, i.e.\ $x_1=x_2=w^*_{n-1}$.

Typed case: the type-consistent candidate
$(\{c\},\zeta)\to\Lfl(\{c\},1)(\{c\},\zeta)\Rfl$ is invalid, since
$\Lfl w^*_{n-1}y\Rfl$ (with $y\in L(G_n,E_{n-1})$ of type $\zeta$) has nesting
depth $n$ and so is not in $\{c\}\Lc$.  The test can only fail with
$x_1\in \mathcal E_i\cap\{c\}\Lc\cap F_1=\mathcal E_i\cap\{w^*_{n-1}\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[reading the deletion family]\label{rem:deletion-table}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the presentation rows, $O(n)$ refers separately to grammar size and
maximum witness length, and $\tilde\Theta(2^n)$ means up to a factor $O(n)$;
the kernel bounds for $M_n$ are on $\rho|N|\max(\kappa,1)$ and hold for every
typing.  The untyped context presentation of $M_n$ has $2^n+2n-2$

linear kernel presentation and the length-parity observer keeps it
(Remark~\ref{rem:Ln-sU}).  The last line concerns Algorithm~1 of
\citet{ky2017} without additional pruning: a polynomial typed context
presentation does not give polynomial data for the dual learner, because
\emph{refuting} candidate productions needs $w^*_{n-1}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[one primal learner for all fixed observers]\label{rem:uniform-known}
Let $s$ be any typing and $L\in\Ccf s$ nonempty with $\eps\notin L$.  By
the main theorem of \citet{kuriyama-tcs1}, $L$ has a characteristic sample
$K$ for $\mathsf{Rec}_s$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:rec-kernel} the grammar $G_K$
has at most two nonterminals per right-hand side and the very weak
$1$-FKP.  (If $\eps\in L$, apply this to $L\setminus\{\eps\}$, which lies in
$\Ccf s$ because no context of a nonempty word changes, and add an initial
nonterminal $A$ with the production $A\to\eps$ and the kernel $\{\eps\}$.)
The same follows by the known route: starting the proof of
\citet[Prop.~9.7]{kuriyama-tcs1} from a binary grammar gives a congruential
grammar for $L$ of rank $2$, and congruential grammars have the $1$-FKP
(\citealp[Sec.~6.3]{yoshinaka2015}; \citealp[Sec.~6.5.3]{clark-yoshinaka2016}).  So the
untyped learner $\mathrm{Primal}_{2,1}$ identifies every language of
$\bigcup_s\Ccf s$ with membership queries (Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(b),
\citealp[Thm.~6]{ky2017}), although this union contains every regular language
\citep[Prop.~3.1]{kuriyama-tcs1} and so, by \citet{gold1967}, is not
identifiable from positive data alone: for these classes, membership
queries make the observer dispensable.  The inclusion of $\bigcup_s\Ccf s$ in $\FKP1{w}$ is
strict: the language of Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-context} has the strong
$1$-FKP \citep[Prop.~2(i)]{ky2017} but lies in no $\Ccf s$, since for any $s$
there are $1\le i<j$ with $s(a^i)=s(a^j)$, the words $a^i,a^j$ share the
context $(a^\ell,db^\ell)$ for every $\ell\ge j$ (both $\ell+i$ and $\ell+j$
lie in $[\ell,2\ell]$), and only $a^i$ admits $(\eps,cb^{2i})$.  Nothing is
claimed about the amount of data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[frames for substitutable languages]\label{rem:subst-fcp}
Substitutability also has a frame-side reading.  For a typing $s$, $L$ is
$\sim_s$-substitutable if and only if, for every $x\in\Sigma^+$ and every
$(u,v)\in\D_L(x)$,
\[
\{(u,v)\}\Lc\cap F_{s(x)}=[x]_s,\qquad
[x]_s=\{y\in F_{s(x)}:\D_L(y)=\D_L(x)\}:
\]
a word of the type of $x$ that fits one frame of $x$ shares a context with
$x$, and conversely.  Hence every $L\in\Ccf s$ lies in $\tFCP1{vw}{s}$ (for
the empty language this is trivial).
Refine the congruential grammar of Remark~\ref{rem:uniform-known} by
Lemma~\ref{lem:product} with $h=s$ and trim it; give each nonterminal
$(A,\mu)$ with $\mu\ne\one$ the set $[x]_s$ for a word $x$ of its yield,
named by one context of $x$, and each $(A,\one)$ the set $F_{\one}=\{\eps\}$.
This family is a pre-fixed point, since words with equal distributions and
equal types concatenate to words with equal distributions and equal types,
and it is sound, since every word of $[w]_s$ with $w\in L$ has the context
$(\eps,\eps)$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:duality}(c) the same holds under
every refinement of $s$.  So every language of $\Ccf s$ has one-frame and,
by Remark~\ref{rem:uniform-known}, one-exemplar presentations;
Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} shows that within $\Ccf{s_U}$ their sizes can still
differ exponentially.  Whether the inclusion holds without types is Open
Problem~\ref{op:further}.
\end{remark}

\section{The role of the observer}
\label{sec:duality}

The following observation follows from the untyped rule-validity
equivalence in \citet[Sec.~4]{ky2017} and the restricted Galois connection.
\begin{proposition}[context--kernel duality]\label{prop:duality}
Let $s\preceq h$ be typings of $\Sigma^+$ and $k\ge1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item $\tFKP{k}{vw}{s}=\tFKP{k}{w}{s}$.
\item $\tFKP{k}{vw}{h}\subseteq\tFKP{k}{vw}{s}\subseteq\FKP{k}{vw}=\FKP{k}{w}$.
\item $\FCP{k}{vw}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{vw}{s}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{vw}{h}$ and
$\FCP{k}{st}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{st}{s}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{st}{h}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) For $\supseteq$, note that $(\cl_{\tau(A)}(L(G,A)))_A$ is an SPP of every
$s$-typed $(G,\tau)$: for a production $A\to w_0A_1\cdots A_nw_n$ the set
$P=w_0\cl(L(G,A_1))w_1\cdots\cl(L(G,A_n))w_n$ lies in $F_{\tau(A)}$, and
$P\R=(w_0L(G,A_1)w_1\cdots L(G,A_n)w_n)\R\supseteq L(G,A)\R$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:star}, so $P\subseteq\cl_{\tau(A)}(L(G,A))$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:typed-galois}; for $S\in I$, $(\eps,\eps)\in L(G,S)\R$ gives
$\cl(L(G,S))\subseteq L$.  For $\subseteq$, let $(G,\tau)$ have the
typed SPP $(\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction},
$(K_A\RL)_A$ is an untyped SPP.  The grammar $G'$ of Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(a),
obtained by adding $A\to w$ for $w\in K_A$, is still $s$-typed because
$K_A\subseteq F_{\tau(A)}$, and satisfies $L(G')=L$ and $L(G',A)\RL=K_A\RL$.
Intersecting with $F_{\tau(A)}$ gives
$\cl_{\tau(A)}(L(G',A))=\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A)$.

(b) Let $(G,\tau)$ be $h$-typed with typed SPP $(\cl^h_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} (for $h$), $(K_A\RL)_A$ is an untyped SPP; this
proves membership in $\FKP{k}{vw}$.  Moreover $(G,\pi\circ\tau)$ is
$s$-typed and $K_A\subseteq F^h_{\tau(A)}\subseteq F^s_{\pi\tau(A)}$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} (for $s$) turns the same untyped SPP into the
typed SPP $(\cl^s_{\pi\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$.  The equality is
Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(a).

(c) Let $G$ be untyped or $s$-typed with SPP $(X_A)_A$, $X_A=C_A\Lc$ or
$X_A=C_A\Lc\cap F^s_{\sigma(A)}$.  Form $G^h$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:product}
and put $X_{(A,\nu)}=X_A\cap F^h_\nu=C_A\Lc\cap F^h_\nu$ (in the typed case
$F^h_\nu\subseteq F^s_{\sigma(A)}$).  For a production of $G^h$ the right-hand
side $w_0X_{(A_1,\nu_1)}\cdots w_n$ is contained in $X_A$ (pre-fixed point
of $G$) and in $F^h_\nu$ (types multiply), so the family is a pre-fixed
point; it is sound since $\bigcup_\nu X_S\cap F^h_\nu=X_S$.  The same
computation with $X_A=L(G,A)$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:product} gives the strong
case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[observers never enlarge the primal learner's class]\label{cor:primal-dominated}
If the typed primal learner $\mathrm{Primal}^s_{r,k}$ converges to a grammar
for $L_*$ on some positive presentation of $L_*$, then the untyped learner
$\mathrm{Primal}_{r,k}$ converges to a grammar for $L_*$ on every positive
presentation of $L_*$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:learners}, convergence of $\mathrm{Primal}^s_{r,k}$
yields an $s$-typed grammar for $L_*$ with at most $r$ nonterminals per
right-hand side and an untyped SPP $(K\RL)_K$ with $|K|\le k$.  So $L_*$ has a
CFG of rank at most $r$ with the very weak $k$-FKP, and
Fact~\ref{fact:ky}(b) applies.
\end{proof}
The corollary compares the classes of languages identified; it says
nothing about how fast the learners converge or how much data they use.

\subsection*{Strictness at the two-element semilattice}

The typed inclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:duality} are strict, for every
$k\ge1$, already between $s_0$ and an indicator typing into $U_1$: the
witnesses below have the strong $1$-property on one side and no
very weak property for any $k$ on the other.  We make no strictness claim
for the endpoint inclusions between untyped and $s_0$-typed classes.

\begin{proposition}[observation helps contexts]\label{prop:strict-context}
Let $\Sigma=\{a,b,c,d\}$ and
$L=\{a^mcb^{2m}:m\ge0\}\cup\{a^mdb^n:n\le m\le2n\}$
(\citealp[Prop.~2(i)]{ky2017}).  Then $L\in\tFCP1{st}{h_{\{c\}}}$, but
$L\notin\FCP{}{vw}$ and $L\notin\tFCP{}{vw}{s_0}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $h=h_{\{c\}}$.  The grammar with nonterminals $S_1$ (type $\zeta$) and
$S_2$ (type $1$), both initial, and productions
$S_1\to aS_1bb\mid c$, $S_2\to aS_2b\mid aaS_2b\mid d$ is $h$-typed,
$L(G,S_1)=L\cap F_\zeta$ and $L(G,S_2)=L\cap F_1$.  Since $\{(\eps,\eps)\}\Lc=L$,
both components are $\{(\eps,\eps)\}\Lc\cap F_\mu$, so $G$ has the typed strong
$1$-FCP.

$L\notin\FCP{}{vw}$ is \citet[Prop.~2(i)]{ky2017}.  We recall the argument to
see that it also excludes $s_0$-typed grammars.  Let $G'$ be any CFG for $L$
(typed or not).  The pumping lemma applied to $a^pcb^{2p}$, $p$ large, gives
an initial $S$, a nonterminal $E$ and $S\Rightarrow^*a^{i_1}Eb^{j_1}$,
$E\Rightarrow^+a^iEb^{j}$ with $i+j>0$, and $E\Rightarrow^*a^{i_2}cb^{j_2}$
(the pumped factors cannot contain $c$ and cannot both lie on one side of
$c$).  Since $a^{i_1+ti+i_2}cb^{j_1+tj+j_2}\in L$ for all $t$, $j=2i$, so $i>0$.  Suppose an SPP has
$E$-component $X_E=C_E\Lc\cap F$ with $C_E$ finite and $F\in\{\Sigma^*,\Sigma^+\}$.
Since $a^{i_2}cb^{j_2}\in X_E$, every $(u,v)\in C_E$ is $(a^{n-i_2},b^{2n-j_2})$
with $n\ge i_2$, $2n\ge j_2$.  Choosing $m\ge2n$ for all such $n$, the
nonempty word $a^{3m}db^{2m}$ satisfies all contexts of $C_E$ (the $a$-count
$3m+n-i_2$ and $b$-count $2m+2n-j_2$ satisfy $b\le a\le2b$), so it lies in
$X_E$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} applied to the reaching contexts
$(a^{i_1+ti},b^{j_1+2ti})$, $a^{i_1+ti+3m}db^{j_1+2ti+2m}\in L$ for all $t$,
which forces $j_1+2ti+2m\le i_1+ti+3m$ for all $t$, contradicting $i>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[observation hurts kernels]\label{prop:strict-kernel}
Let $\Sigma=\{a,b,y,z\}$, $P=\{a^mb^n:m\ne n\}$ and
\[
L_K=P\cup\{a^mzb^n:n\le m\}\cup\{a^myb^n:m\le n\}.
\]
Then $L_K\in\tFKP1{st}{s_0}\subseteq\FKP1{vw}$, but
$L_K\notin\tFKP{k}{vw}{h_{\{y,z\}}}$ for every $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Upper bound.}  Use two initial nonterminals $S_+,S_-$, both of
$s_0$-type $0$, and productions
\[
S_+\to a\mid z\mid aS_+\mid aS_+b,\qquad
S_-\to b\mid y\mid S_-b\mid aS_-b.
\]
Their languages are respectively
\[
L_+=\{a^mb^n:m>n\}\cup\{a^mzb^n:m\ge n\},\qquad
L_-=\{a^mb^n:m<n\}\cup\{a^myb^n:m\le n\}.
\]
They have union $L_K$.  Relative to $L_K$,
$\D(z)=\{(a^p,b^q):p\ge q\}$ and
$\D(y)=\{(a^p,b^q):p\le q\}$.
We show $\{z\}\RL=L_+$; the proof of $\{y\}\RL=L_-$ is symmetric.  A word
$x$ lies in $\{z\}\RL$ iff $a^pxb^q\in L_K$ for all $p\ge q\ge0$; taking
$p=q=0$, $x\in L_K$.  If $x=a^mb^n$ with $m\ne n$, then $a^{m+p}b^{n+q}\in L_K$
for all $p\ge q$ iff $m+p\ne n+q$ for all $p-q\ge0$, iff $m>n$.  If
$x=a^mzb^n$ with $n\le m$, then $n+q\le m+p$ for all $p\ge q$.  If
$x=a^myb^n$, then $a^{m+p}yb^{n}\notin L_K$ for $p>n-m$.  Hence
$\{z\}\RL=L_+$.  Thus both generated components are singleton-kernel
closures.  Since neither contains $\eps$, the grammar
has the $s_0$-typed strong $1$-FKP.

\emph{Lower bound.}  Let $h=h_{\{y,z\}}$ and suppose an $h$-typed $(G,\tau)$
for $L_K$ has a typed SPP $(\cl_{\tau(A)}(K_A))_A$.  Every word of $P$ has
type $1$, so $P\subseteq\bigcup\{\cl_1(K_S):S\in I,\ \tau(S)=1\}$.  For such
$S$, $K_S\subseteq F_1\cap\cl_1(K_S)\subseteq F_1\cap L_K=P$ by soundness.  Let
$p$ bound $|m-n|$ over all $a^mb^n$ in the finitely many such $K_S$
($p=0$ if they are all empty; an empty kernel has empty closure here,
since $(ba,\eps)$ is a context of no word).  If
$|i-j|>p$ then $(a^i,b^j)\in K_S\R$, because $a^{i+m}b^{n+j}\in P$ for
$|m-n|\le p$.  A word $a^mb^n$ with $|m-n|>p$ fails the context
$(a^i,b^j)$ with $i-j=n-m$, so it is not in $K_S\RL$.  Hence the union
misses $a^{p+1}$, a word of $P$, a contradiction.  This cancellation
argument is that of \citet[Prop.~2(ii)]{ky2017}.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:duality}(b), kernel failures are
observer-invariant: the language $\{a^mb^n:m\ne n\}$, which has the strong
$1$-FCP but no CFG with the very weak FKP \citep[Prop.~2(ii)]{ky2017}, has no
$s$-typed CFG with the typed very weak FKP, for any typing $s$.
The weak FCP behaves differently from both the strong and the very weak
FCP of Proposition~\ref{prop:duality}(c): it is not monotone under refinement.
We use the languages of \citet[Secs.~5--6]{ky2017}: over
$\Sigma_0=\{a,b,c,d,e,f,\#,\$,\%\}$, with $V=\{a,b,\#\}^*$ and $V_0=\{a,b\}^*$,
\begin{align*}
L_1&=\{v\$z: v\in V,\ z=(\mathrm{erase}_\#(v))^R\},\\
L_2&=\{wyc^id^ie^jz: w,z\in V_0,\ y\in(\#V_0)^*,\ i,j\ge0,\ |w|_a\ge|w|_b\},\\
L_3&=\{wyc^id^je^jz: w,z\in V_0,\ y\in(\#V_0)^*,\ i,j\ge0,\ |w|_a\le|w|_b\},\\
L_4&=\{v\$zc^k\%f^l: v\in V,\ z\in V_0,\ k,l\ge0\},\\
L_5&=\{vc^id^ie^jzc^k\%f^l: v\in V,\ z\in V_0,\ i,j,k,l\ge0,\ j\ne k\},
\end{align*}
$L_{123}=L_1\cup L_2\cup L_3$ and $L'=L_{123}\cup L_4\cup L_5$; we also put
$J_0=\{vc^id^ie^iz: v\in V,\ z\in V_0,\ i\ge0\}$.  Note $V\subseteq L_2\cup L_3$
and $J_0\subseteq L_2\cup L_3$.

\begin{proposition}[the weak FCP is not monotone]\label{prop:weak-not-preserved}
Let $L_1^@$ be $L_1$ with $\$$ replaced by a fresh letter $@$, put
$\Sigma=\Sigma_0\cup\{@\}$, $L^+=L'\cup L_1^@$ and $h=h_{\{@\}}$, so that
$s_0\preceq h$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item $L^+\in\FCP2{w}$ and $L^+\in\tFCP2{w}{s_0}$;
\item $L^+\notin\tFCP{k}{w}{h}$ for every $k$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence $\tFCP{k}{w}{s_0}\not\subseteq\tFCP{k}{w}{h}$ for every $k\ge2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proof checks the context sets of \citet[Lemmas~8 and~12]{ky2017}
against the new letter $@$; it is given in Appendix~\ref{app:weak}.
\end{proof}

So, unlike the strong and the very weak FCP, the typed weak FCP is not
monotone under refinement, already between $s_0$ and a two-element
indicator.  The case $k=1$ remains open (Open Problem~\ref{op:weak-fcp}).

In contrast, the context-side failure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-context} is repaired by one bit of
observation.

\subsection*{Observers that help frames}

A \emph{two-sided ideal} of $\mathsf S^\sharp$ is a set $\mathsf I$ with
$\mathsf S^\sharp\mathsf I\mathsf S^\sharp\subseteq\mathsf I$.  A context $(u,v)$
\emph{forces} $\mathsf I$ if $\widehat s(u)\,\mathsf S^\sharp\,\widehat s(v)
\subseteq\mathsf I$: whatever fills it, the result has a type in $\mathsf I$.

\begin{proposition}[ideal restriction]\label{prop:ideal}
Let $s$ be a typing, $\mathsf I$ a two-sided ideal of $\mathsf S^\sharp$, and
$(G,(C_A)_A)$ an untyped $k$-context presentation of $L$ in which no initial
nonterminal occurs on a right-hand side and every context of every
non-initial nonterminal forces $\mathsf I$.  Let $G'$ be the refinement $G^s$
of Lemma~\ref{lem:product} with the initial nonterminals $(S,\mu)$, $S$ initial
in $G$ and $\mu\in\mathsf I$.  Then $L(G')=L'=L\cap\widehat s^{-1}(\mathsf I)$,
and relative to $L'$ the family $(C_A\Lc\cap F_\mu)_{(A,\mu)}$ is an SPP of
$G'$; if the given presentation is strong, so is this one.  Thus $L'$ has an
$s$-typed $k$-context presentation with the same contexts, at most
$|N||\mathsf S^\sharp|$ nonterminals and the same $\rho$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $C\Lc$ and $C^{\lhd'}$ for the operators relative to $L$ and $L'$;
$L(G')=L'$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:product}.  (i) If $(u,v)$ forces $\mathsf I$,
then $uxv\in L$ implies $uxv\in L'$; so $C_B^{\lhd'}=C_B\Lc$ for every
non-initial $B$.  (ii) If $\mu\in\mathsf I$ and $x\in F_\mu$, then
$\widehat s(uxv)\in\mathsf S^\sharp\mu\mathsf S^\sharp\subseteq\mathsf I$ for every
$(u,v)$; so $C_S^{\lhd'}\cap F_\mu=C_S\Lc\cap F_\mu$ for initial $S$.  For a
production $(A,\mu)\to w_0(A_1,\mu_1)w_1\cdots(A_m,\mu_m)w_m$ of $G'$, the
product of the right-hand components lies in
$w_0C_{A_1}\Lc w_1\cdots C_{A_m}\Lc w_m\cap F_\mu\subseteq C_A\Lc\cap F_\mu$ by
(i), the given pre-fixed point and type consistency, and
$C_A\Lc\cap F_\mu=C_A^{\lhd'}\cap F_\mu$ by (i) or (ii).  The initial
components $C_S\Lc\cap F_\mu$, $\mu\in\mathsf I$, lie in
$L\cap\widehat s^{-1}(\mathsf I)=L'$ by soundness of the given presentation, so
the family is an SPP.  In the strong case (i) and (ii) give
$C_A^{\lhd'}\cap F_\mu=L(G,A)\cap F_\mu=L(G',(A,\mu))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[two instances]\label{rem:ideal-examples}
Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(b) is the case $L=L_n$, $G=G_n$ with
initial nonterminal $E_n$, $s=s_U$ and $\mathsf I=\{\zeta\}$: the context
$(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})$ of each $E_i$, $i<n$, contains a mark and so forces
$\{\zeta\}$.  For a second observer, let $t(w)=\min(\#_{\Lce}(w),2)$ in
$\{0,1,2\}$ with saturating addition and $\mathsf I=\{2\}$.  With the
contexts $(\Lce^{n-i},\Rce^{n-i})$ for $i\le n-2$ and $(\Lfl,u_{n-1}\Rfl)$ for
$E_{n-1}$ ($n\ge3$), which contain at least two marks and give the strong
$1$-FCP of $G_n$, the structures with at least two marks receive a
$t$-typed strong $1$-FCP presentation with contexts of length at most
$2n+1$.  This language is not a one-word deletion of $L_n$, and we have no
lower bound for its exemplars.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The mark observer on the deletion family}

\begin{theorem}[the mark observer]\label{thm:observer}
Let $n\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item The $s_U$-typed grammar $H_n$ for $M_n$ has $2n$ nonterminals, $6n-4$
productions, $\rho\le4$, and the typed strong $1$-FCP with contexts of length
at most $2n$.
\item For every typing $s$, every $s$-typed CFG for $M_n$ with the typed very
weak FKP satisfies $\rho|N|\max(\kappa,1)\ge3\cdot2^n-n-5$.
\item Every $s_U$-typed CFG for $L_n$ with the typed very weak FKP has the
kernel word $w^*_n$, of length $3\cdot2^n-2$, whereas $G_n$ has the untyped
strong $1$-FKP with kernel words of length at most $2n+1$.
\item The typed frame presentation of (a) has total length $O(n^2)$.  Every
exemplar presentation of $M_n$, untyped or $s$-typed for any $s$, has total
length at least $2\sqrt{3\cdot2^n-n-5}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) is Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(b); the productions are counted
in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}(ii).  (b) is the typed part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:deletion}.  (c) is Remark~\ref{rem:Ln-sU} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:deletion-upper}(a).  (d) The presentation of (a) has
$6n-4$ productions with right-hand sides of length at most $4$ and $2n$
contexts of length at most $2n$.  For the second claim let
$R=3\cdot2^n-n-5$; for a typed presentation pass first to the untyped SPP
of the same grammar with the same kernels (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}).  In
the derivation tree of $z$ used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:general-lower}
(with $R$, $T$, $T^\circ$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:one-word}),
the chain nodes carry distinct nonterminals and hence use distinct
productions; let $q$ be the sum of the lengths of their right-hand sides.
The covering argument of that proof charges each piece of $R$ to a symbol of
one of these right-hand sides, so $R\le q\max(\kappa,1)$.  The total length
$\Lambda$ counts these productions, at least $q$, and separately a kernel word of
maximal length, at least $\max(\kappa,1)$; some kernel is nonempty, since
$\emptyset\RL=\emptyset$ for the finite language $M_n$.  Hence
$\Lambda\ge q+\max(\kappa,1)\ge2\sqrt{q\max(\kappa,1)}\ge2\sqrt R$.
\end{proof}

Comparing (a) with Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}(i), the mark observer reduces the
frame presentation of $M_n$ from $2^n+2n-2$ to $6n-4$ productions, with frames
of length at most $2n$ in both.  By (b) and (c) it never helps exemplars and
can hurt them, and (a) is an instance of Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal}.  By
(d), under $s_U$ the gap holds also in total length,
$O(n^2)$ against $2^{\Omega(n)}$.  Without an observer we know only the
upper bounds $\Theta(n2^n)$ for $F_n$ and $\Theta(2^n)$ for the exemplars of
$H_n$; whether untyped frame presentations of $M_n$ can have polynomial
total length is Open Problem~\ref{op:Mn-context}.

The two halves of Theorems~A and~C hold in general.

\begin{theorem}[a general gap]\label{thm:general-gap}
Let $L$ be a finite language, $s$ a typing and $\mathsf I$ a two-sided ideal
of $\mathsf S^\sharp$.  Suppose that $L$ has an untyped $k$-context
presentation as in Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal}, with contexts of length at
most $\ell$; that $L\setminus\widehat s^{-1}(\mathsf I)=\{w\}$; and that $w$, $R$,
$T$, $T^\circ$ are as in Lemma~\ref{lem:one-word}.  Then $L\setminus\{w\}$ has an
$s$-typed $k$-context presentation with contexts of length at most $\ell$,
the same $\rho$ and at most $|N||\mathsf S^\sharp|$ nonterminals, whereas every
exemplar presentation of $L\setminus\{w\}$, untyped or typed under any
observer, satisfies $\rho|N|\max(\kappa,1)\ge|R|$ and has total length at
least $2\sqrt{|R|}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$L\cap\widehat s^{-1}(\mathsf I)=L\setminus\{w\}$, so the first claim is
Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal}.  The second is Lemma~\ref{lem:one-word} with
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-lower}, and the argument for (d) above.
\end{proof}
Theorems~A(ii) and~C are the case $L=L_n$, $s=s_U$, $\mathsf I=\{\zeta\}$,
$w=w^*_n$, $\ell=2n$ and $|R|=3\cdot2^n-n-5$.

\section{Discussion: frames, exemplars and observation}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why exemplars fail.}
An exemplar-named category $K\RL$ is closed upwards in distribution: it
contains everything that occurs in all frames shared by the exemplars.  The
licensing condition of $M_n$ removes a single structure, $w^*_n$, whose parts
all occur elsewhere, so a category containing a large unmarked part $x$ of
a marked structure can exclude the completion of $x$ to $w^*_n$ only by
listing $x$ itself (Lemma~\ref{lem:capture}).  A frame-named category needs
no such list: in $F_n$ every category below the root is a whole level of
$L_n$, and the root productions place the mark.

\paragraph{Observation as morphology.}
A typing is a finite, compositional classification of expressions, of the
kind supplied by morphological marking or agreement features.  In
Example~\ref{ex:toy} the plural observer stands in for the agreement frame;
in $M_n$ the mark observer lets short frames name the categories with $O(n)$
productions (Theorem~\ref{thm:observer}).  Frames gain from observation and
exemplars do not, because a typed exemplar presentation is an untyped one
with homogeneous exemplars (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}); under the mark
observer the exemplars of $L_n$ even become long, under the length-parity
observer they do not (Remark~\ref{rem:Ln-sU}).  The weak finite context
property does not follow this pattern
(Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-not-preserved}).

\paragraph{The programming-language example.}
The classification of the introduction is syntactic, not a property of the
executed path:
\begin{verbatim}
def f():
    if False:
        yield 0
    return 0
\end{verbatim}
is a generator function, and replacing \texttt{yield 0} by \texttt{pass}
makes it an ordinary one (as is easily checked).  The presence of a
\texttt{yield} is a compositional bit only in fragments without nested
scopes, since a \texttt{yield} inside a nested function belongs to that
function; the idealised fragment of the introduction has none.

\paragraph{Limits.}
The linguistic examples illustrate the mathematical notions; we make no
empirical claim about natural languages.  The general forms require a finite language and a single deleted word; for
several deleted words, such as the structures with at least two marks, the
frame side carries over (Remark~\ref{rem:ideal-examples}), but we have no
exemplar lower bound.  The results are worst-case separations: the two ways of
naming can differ exponentially, not that they do so for natural languages
or for every learner.  Discontinuous constituents (multiple context-free
grammars), cross-serial dependencies and the categorial grammars of
\citet{kanazawa1998} are beyond the context-free setting of this paper.

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{openproblem}[monotonicity of the weak $1$-FCP]\label{op:weak-fcp}
Is $\tFCP1{w}{s}\subseteq\tFCP1{w}{h}$ for $s\preceq h$?
Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-not-preserved} answers the question negatively
for every $k\ge2$.  Finite languages cannot give a counterexample for
$k=1$, since every finite language has, for every $s$, a typed strong
$1$-FCP grammar listing its words.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[robust separation]\label{op:robust}
Is $\tFCP{}{vw}{s}\ne\tFCP{}{w}{s}$ for every typing $s$ over an alphabet of
size at least two?  For the witness $L'$ of \citet[Thm.~14]{ky2017}, the
indicator of $\$$ separates the yield $a^{m_6}\$a^{l_6}$ from the words
$c^id^ie^i$ used in the proof of \citet[Lemma~13]{ky2017}, so that proof no
longer applies; we do not know whether $L'$ then has the typed weak FCP.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[total length of untyped frame presentations of $M_n$]\label{op:Mn-context}
Let $f(n)$ be the least total length of an untyped frame presentation of
$M_n$.  Is $f(n)=2^{\Omega(n)}$?  The grammar $F_n$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:untyped-frames} gives $f(n)=O(n2^n)$, with $\rho|N|$ and
frame length polynomial in $n$ but $2^n+2n-2$ productions, and under the
mark observer total length $O(n^2)$ suffices
(Theorem~\ref{thm:observer}(d)).  A lower bound would have to bound the
number of productions, not only $\rho$, $|N|$ and the frame length.
Proposition~\ref{prop:Mn-constituent} below is a partial result: if frames
have length at most $\ell$, every node whose yield contains the whole
occurrence of $v$ in $z$ leaves at most $\ell$ symbols of $z$ outside its
yield (as $|z|=|w^*_n|-|w^*_{j-1}|$).  It does not bound the depth of such a
node, since unit productions can be inserted, and it does not locate the
productions that introduce the brackets of $v$.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{proposition}[constituents around a marked node]\label{prop:Mn-constituent}
Let $1\le j<n$ and let $z\in M_n$ be obtained from $w^*_n$ by replacing one
occurrence of the $E_j$-node $w^*_j$ by $v=\Lce w^*_{j-1}\Rce$, so that $z$
contains exactly one pair $\Lce\ \Rce$.  Let $G$ be a CFG for $M_n$ with an SPP
$(C_A\Lc)_A$ whose contexts have length at most $\ell$ (as words $uv$).  Then
every node of every derivation tree of $z$ whose yield $x$ contains this
occurrence of $v$ satisfies $|x|\ge|w^*_n|-\ell-|w^*_{j-1}|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the node be labelled $A$ with terminal context $(p,q)$, and let $x'$ be
$x$ with the occurrence of $v$ replaced by $w^*_j$; then $px'q=w^*_n$.  If
$x'\in C_A\Lc$, Lemma~\ref{lem:reach} would give $w^*_n\in M_n$.  So some
$c\in C_A$ accepts $x$ (as $x\in L(G,A)\subseteq C_A\Lc$) but rejects $x'$.
Now $c[x]\in M_n\subseteq L_n$, the occurrence of $v$ in $c[x]$ is an
$E_j$-node (Lemma~\ref{lem:nodes}), and so $c[x']\in L_n\setminus M_n$, i.e.\
$c[x']=w^*_n$.  Since $|x'|=|x|+|w^*_j|-|v|=|x|+|w^*_{j-1}|$, we get
$|x|=|w^*_n|-|c|-|w^*_{j-1}|\ge|w^*_n|-\ell-|w^*_{j-1}|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{openproblem}[further questions]\label{op:further}
Is $\bigcup_s\Ccf s\subseteq\FCP{}{vw}$ without types (with types, see
Remark~\ref{rem:subst-fcp}), and is the typed strong FKP
anti-monotone under refinement?  Kernels filtered by type, i.e.\
nonterminals $(K,\mu)$ with arbitrary $K$ and denotation $K\RL\cap F_\mu$,
give a class containing $\FKP{k}{vw}$ and the language $L_K$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-kernel}; is it strictly larger?
\end{openproblem}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Frames and exemplars can differ exponentially in naming the same syntactic
categories.  For a global licensing condition, short frames name every
category of a grammar with few nonterminals and short right-hand sides,
while every exemplar presentation has an
exponential product of $\rho$, $|N|$ and $\kappa$; under the mark observer
the gap also holds for total length, on one and the same grammar.  The gap
bounds the data of the author's positive-data reconstruction and of the
primal learner, and a separate argument bounds the dual learner.  Refining
the observer helps frames and hurts exemplars, except for the weak FCP.
Both sides hold for observers given by ideals of types and for finite
one-word deletions in general.  Whether untyped frames need exponential
total length remains open.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-not-preserved}}\label{app:weak}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-not-preserved}]
Let $G^+$ be the grammar $G'$ of \citet[Lemma~12]{ky2017} with the extra
production $S_1\to@$.  Since $S_1$ occurs on no right-hand side other than
its own, $L(G^+,S_1)=L_1\cup L_1^@$, every other nonterminal keeps its
language, and $L(G^+)=L^+$.  All closures are relative to $L^+$ unless said
otherwise.  We use three facts about words of $L^+$: a word containing $c$,
$d$ or $e$ is not in $L_1\cup L_1^@$; a word ending in $a$, $b$, $d$ or $e$
is not in $L_4\cup L_5$; and a word containing $d$ is not in
$L_1\cup L_1^@\cup L_4$.

The nonterminals of $G'$ and the context sets $C_A$ listed for them in
\citet[proofs of Lemmas~8 and~12]{ky2017} are as follows, with
$\mathcal B=\{w\in V_0:|w|_a=|w|_b\}$; the third column is $C_A\Lc$ relative to
$L^+$, as shown below (and checked by machine for all words of length at
most $5$).
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
$A$ & $C_A$ & $C_A\Lc$ & characterisation\\
\midrule
$Q$ & $(\eps,\#cd)$, $(a,b\#de)$ & $\mathcal B$ & strong\\
$F$ & $(\eps,cd)$, $(\eps,ade)$ & $\mathcal B\#V$ & strong\\
$H$ & $(a\#c,d)$ & $\{c^id^i:i\ge0\}$ & strong\\
$E$ & $(a\#cd,e)$ & $e^*$ & strong\\
$C$ & $(b\#c,de)$ & $c^*$ & strong\\
$J$ & $(b\#d,e)$ & $\{d^ie^i:i\ge0\}$ & strong\\
$T$ & $(\$,a\%)$ & $V_0$ & strong\\
$U$ & $(e,c\%)$ & $\{e^jzc^k:z\in V_0,\ j\ne k\}$ & strong\\
$S_{4,5}$ & $(\eps,f)$ & $L_4\cup L_5$ & strong\\
$S_2$ & $(\eps,\eps)$, $(a\#,b)$ & $L_2\cup J_0$ & weak\\
$S_3$ & $(\eps,\eps)$, $(b\#,a)$ & $L_3\cup J_0$ & weak\\
$S_1$ & $(a\#,a)$, $(b\#,b)$ & $L_1\cup L_1^@\cup J_0$ & weak\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\emph{Claim.} For $Y\in\{L_2,L_3\}$, every context $(u,v)$ with
$u(Y\setminus\{\eps\})v\subseteq L^+$ satisfies $u\in V$, $v\in V_0$ and
$uv\in L^+$.  In particular $(Y\setminus\{\eps\})\R=Y\R$, and
$uJ_0v\subseteq J_0$ for every $(u,v)\in Y\R$.

For $Y=L_2$ use the words $x_t=\#cde^ta\in L_2$ ($t\ge0$).  Each $ux_tv$
contains $d$, so it lies in $L_2\cup L_3\cup L_5$.  If $v$ contained $\%$,
every $ux_tv$ would lie in $L_5$; there the $\#$ of $x_t$ belongs to the
prefix in $V$, so $cd$ is the block $c^id^i$ with $i=1$, $e^t$ is the block
$e^j$ with $j=t$, and $v=z'c^k\%f^l$ with $k$ fixed by $v$; $t=k$ violates
$j\ne k$.  A $\%$ in $u$ is impossible, since only $f$ may follow $\%$.  So
$ux_tv\in L_2\cup L_3\subseteq V\,c^*d^*e^*V_0$, and as $x_t$ begins with $\#$
and ends with $a$, $u\in V$ and $v\in V_0$.  For $Y=L_3$ use
$x=\#cd^2e^2a\in L_3$: in $L_5$ the $c$ after $\#$ and the two $d$s would
have to form a block $c^id^i$, which is impossible, so the same conclusion
follows.  Then $uv\in V\subseteq L_2\cup L_3$, so $(u,v)$ also accepts $\eps$;
antitonicity gives the other inclusion.  Finally
$u(v'c^id^ie^iz')v=(uv')c^id^ie^i(z'v)\in J_0$.

\emph{Strong nonterminals.}  For $A\in\{Q,F,H,E,C,J\}$ (resp.\
$A\in\{T,U,S_{4,5}\}$), \citet[proofs of Lemmas~8 and~12]{ky2017} give
$L(G^+,A)=C_A\Lc$ relative to $L_{123}$ (resp.\ $L'$) with $|C_A|\le2$.  Each
context listed for $Q,F,H,E,C,J$ contains $c$, $d$ or $e$ and has right
component ending in $d$ or $e$, so its completions in $L^+$ lie in $L_{123}$; each
context listed for $T$, $U$, $S_{4,5}$ contains $\%$ or ends in $f$, so its
completions in $L^+$ lie in $L_4\cup L_5$.  Hence $L(G^+,A)=C_A\Lc$ relative
to $L^+$: these nonterminals are strongly characterised.

\emph{$S_2$ and $S_3$.}  Let $C_2=\{(\eps,\eps),(a\#,b)\}$.  A completion
$a\#xb\in L^+$ ends in $b$ and has first block $a$, so it lies neither in
$L_4\cup L_5$ nor in $L_1\cup L_1^@$; hence $a\#xb\in L_2\cup L_3$, $x$
contains none of $\$,\%,f,@$, and $x\in L^+$ gives $x\in L_{123}$.  So $C_2\Lc$
relative to $L^+$ equals $C_2\Lc$ relative to $L_{123}$, which is $L_2\cup J_0$
\citep[proof of Lemma~8]{ky2017}.  Since $L_2\subseteq C_2\Lc$, we get
$L_2\RL\subseteq C_2\Lc$; conversely $L_2\subseteq L_2\RL$, and
$J_0\subseteq L_2\RL$ by the Claim.  So $L(G^+,S_2)\RL=C_2\Lc$, and
symmetrically $L(G^+,S_3)\RL=\{(\eps,\eps),(b\#,a)\}\Lc=L_3\cup J_0$.

\emph{$S_1$.}  Let $M$ be the set of mirror contexts
$(w_1\#\cdots\#w_n,w_n^R\cdots w_1^R)$, $n\ge1$, $w_i\in V_0$.  The words
of $L^+$ containing $@$ are exactly those of $L_1^@$, so a context of $L_1^@$
relative to $L^+$ is a context of $L_1^@$ relative to $L_1^@$; renaming $@$ to
$\$$ and using $L(G,S_1)\R=M$ relative to $L_{123}$
\citep[proof of Lemma~8]{ky2017}, this set is $M$.  Relative to $L'$ the
contexts of $L_1$ are $M$ together with the contexts $(v,zc^k\%f^l)$
\citep[proof of Lemma~12]{ky2017}, and the latter are not contexts of
$L_1^@$.  Hence $L(G^+,S_1)\R=M$ relative to $L^+$.  For words without $@$,
the closure $M\Lc$ relative to $L^+$ agrees with the one relative to $L'$,
which is $L_1\cup J_0=\{(a\#,a),(b\#,b)\}\Lc$
\citep[proof of Lemma~12]{ky2017}, because the
contexts involved contain no $@$.  A word $x$ containing $@$ lies in $M\Lc$
iff $x\in L_1^@$, and it lies in $\{(a\#,a),(b\#,b)\}\Lc$ iff
$a\#xa,b\#xb\in L_1^@$, i.e.\ again iff $x\in L_1^@$.  So
$L(G^+,S_1)\RL=L_1\cup L_1^@\cup J_0=\{(a\#,a),(b\#,b)\}\Lc$.  This proves
$L^+\in\FCP2{w}$.

\emph{The $s_0$-typed grammar.}  Refine $G^+$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:product}
for $s_0$ and keep the useful nonterminals.  A nonterminal $(A,\one)$
generates $\{\eps\}=\emptyset\Lc\cap F_{\one}$.  A nonterminal $(A,0)$ generates
$Y_A\setminus\{\eps\}$, $Y_A=L(G^+,A)$.  If $A$ is strongly characterised,
$C_A\subseteq Y_A\R\subseteq(Y_A\setminus\{\eps\})\R$ and
$Y_A\setminus\{\eps\}=C_A\Lc\cap\Sigma^+$ give
$\cl_0(Y_A\setminus\{\eps\})=C_A\Lc\cap\Sigma^+$.  The nonterminal $S_1$ is not
nullable, so $\cl_0(Y_{S_1})=L(G^+,S_1)\RL\cap\Sigma^+$.  For $S_2,S_3$ the
Claim gives $(Y_A\setminus\{\eps\})\RL=Y_A\RL=C_A\Lc$, so
$\cl_0(Y_A\setminus\{\eps\})=C_A\Lc\cap\Sigma^+$.  So every nonterminal of the
refinement is characterised by at most two contexts, and
$L^+\in\tFCP2{w}{s_0}$.

(b) Suppose an $h$-typed $(G,\tau)$ generates $L^+$ and has the typed weak
FCP.  Since $\zeta$ is absorbing, a production with left-hand side of type
$1$ or $\one$ has no child of type $\zeta$.  Deleting the nonterminals of
type $\zeta$ and the initial ones among them leaves a CFG $G_0$ with
$L(G_0)=L^+\cap\Sigma_0^*=L'$, in which every remaining nonterminal keeps its
language.  The proof of \citet[Lemma~13]{ky2017}, which applies to any CFG
for $L'$ and uses Ogden's lemma \citep{ogden1968}, yields for $G_0$ a
nonterminal $B$ (of type $1$) with a yield $a^{m}\$a^{l}$ and a number $k_1$
such that $L(G_0,B)\RL$ contains no word of $Vc^id^ie^iV_0$
for $i>k_1$, whereas for every finite $W\subseteq L(G_0,B)\R$ there is $k_2$
with $c^id^ie^i\in W\Lc$ for all $i>k_2$ (all relative to $L'$).  So no finite
$W\subseteq L(G_0,B)\R$ satisfies
$L(G_0,B)\RL\cap\Sigma_0^+=W\Lc\cap\Sigma_0^+$.  Relative to
$L^+$, every context of $L(G,B)$ accepts a word containing $\$$, hence
contains no $@$; on words without $@$ the two languages agree.  So
$\cl_1(L(G,B))$ relative to $L^+$ is $L(G_0,B)\RL\cap\Sigma_0^+$ relative to
$L'$.  A typed weak characterisation $\cl_1(L(G,B))=C\Lc\cap F_1$ with $C$
finite gives $L(G,B)\subseteq C\Lc$, i.e.\ $C\subseteq L(G,B)\R$ relative to
$L^+$; so $C$ contains no $@$, $C\subseteq L(G_0,B)\R$ relative to $L'$, and
$W=C$ contradicts the choice of $B$.

The last assertion follows from (a), (b) and
$\tFCP2{w}{s_0}\subseteq\tFCP{k}{w}{s_0}$ for $k\ge2$.
\end{proof}
